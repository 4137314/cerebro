Synchronous counters

A counter is a register stepping through a prescribed sequence on each clock edge (with no inputs the transitions are unconditional); a mod-16 binary counter has next-state equations , , … or simply a register fed by an incrementer; a BCD counter treats codes 10–15 as don't-cares; terminal count and enable let counters be cascaded into clocks and timers.

Count enable : synchronous, the counter advances only if (a mux or an AND in the toggle condition). Terminal count : 1 in the cycle in which the counter is at its last value (15, or 9 for BCD); a Moore output. Cascade: — the tens digit advances in the cycle in which the units are at 9 and enabled. Variants: up/down (adder–subtractor), parallel load (mux in front, disabled while loading), modulo selected by an input, arbitrary sequences from a state diagram.

Example

Stopwatch seconds 00–59: units BCD counter enabled by the 1 Hz tick; tens counter mod 6 enabled by ; minutes enabled by . At 09 → 10 the units roll to 0 and the tens step in the same edge. Counter mod 3 or 5 chosen by input : 5 states need 3 bits; is an input of the next-state table (or kept as a "map-entered variable" inside the cells).

Intuition

A counter is the simplest FSM with no inputs at all: its diagram is a single ring, and its state is its output.

Pitfall

Cascading with alone (without the enable of the lower stage) makes the upper digit count every cycle in which the lower one sits at 9 while stopped. A ripple counter (each flip-flop clocked by the previous output) is smaller but asynchronous: its bits change at different times and decoding them glitches.
