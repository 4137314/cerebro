I²C

A two-wire, half-duplex, multi-device bus: SDA (data) and SCL (clock) are open-drain lines with pull-ups, so any device can pull them low; SDA changes only while SCL is low, except for START (SDA falls with SCL high) and STOP (SDA rises with SCL high); each byte is followed by an acknowledge bit driven by the receiver.

Construction

Transaction:

  1. START: SDA while SCL = 1.
  2. Address: 7 bits, MSB first, plus R/ (1 = read).
  3. ACK: the master releases SDA during the ninth clock; the addressed slave pulls it to 0.
  4. Data bytes: 8 bits + ACK each; when reading, the master acknowledges each byte and sends NACK (SDA left high) after the last.
  5. STOP: SDA while SCL = 1. A repeated START instead of STOP keeps the bus to switch direction (write a register address, then read).

Slaves can hold SCL low to slow the master (clock stretching).

VHDL: open-drain lines

In a testbench, sda < 'H';= models the pull-up; resolution gives 0 when a device drives low.

Picture

START and STOP are the only SDA edges allowed while SCL is high.

Intuition

A party line: everyone listens, anyone can say "no" by pulling the wire down, and the address decides who answers.

Pitfall

Driving SDA or SCL high actively ('1' instead of 'Z') fights the device pulling low: a short circuit on real hardware and an X in simulation.
