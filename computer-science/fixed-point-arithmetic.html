Fixed-point arithmetic

Hardware DSP stores real numbers as integers with an implicit binary point: in Qm.n a word of bits represents ; addition aligns points, multiplication adds fraction bits (Qa.b × Qc.d → Q(a+c+1).(b+d)), and every step must choose rounding versus truncation and saturation versus wrap-around.

Definition

Signed Qm.n: 1 sign bit, integer bits, fraction bits. Range , resolution .

  • Addition: same format in, one extra integer bit out to avoid overflow.
  • Multiplication: Q. × Q. = Q.; the product is then shifted right by the excess fraction bits.
  • Quantization error: truncation (drop bits) has bias on average; rounding to nearest has zero mean and error uniform in , variance .
  • Overflow: wrap-around (two's complement default) gives huge errors; saturation clips to the extremes.

Example

Q1.6 (8 bits): is 00110000, is 11100000. Product: in Q3.12; shift right by 6: in Q1.6, i.e. ✓.

Intuition

Fixed point is doing all your sums in, say, cents: integers everywhere, and you just remember where the decimal point is.

Pitfall

Word lengths grow at every multiplication and accumulation (a FIR with taps needs guard bits in the accumulator); forgetting them gives silent wrap-around, the worst possible error.
