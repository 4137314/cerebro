Datapath subsystems: detectors, shifters, counters

Small blocks recur in every datapath: zero/one detectors (wide AND/OR trees), shifters (a barrel shifter shifts by any amount in multiplexer levels), thermometer encoders, and counters — binary, ring (one-hot) and Johnson (twisted ring), which trade flip-flops for decoding simplicity and speed.

Construction

  • Zero / one detector: , : a tree of levels.
  • Barrel shifter: stage shifts by or not, controlled by bit of the shift amount; levels of 2:1 muxes, muxes. Rotations wrap the bits around; arithmetic right shifts replicate the sign.
  • Thermometer code: ones followed by zeros ( for 6); used in flash ADCs and priority logic.
  • Binary counter: flip-flops, an incrementer (ripple carry: slow for wide counters; fast counters use lookahead on the toggle condition ).
  • Ring counter: flip-flops in a ring holding a single 1: one-hot states, no decoding, states.
  • Johnson counter: a ring with the last output inverted: flip-flops, states, one bit changes per step, glitch-free decoding of each state with a 2-input gate.

Example

Sequence: 0000, 0001, 0011, 0111, 1111, 1110, 1100, 1000.

Intuition

Choosing a counter is choosing where to pay: in flip-flops (ring), in logic (binary), or in between (Johnson).

Pitfall

Ring and Johnson counters must be reset into a legal state: from an illegal pattern (two 1s in a ring) they cycle through illegal states for ever.
