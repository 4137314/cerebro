Condition variables and the producer–consumer problem

A mutex makes a thread wait for access; a condition variable makes it wait for a condition (buffer not empty, not full): wait(lock, pred) atomically releases the mutex and sleeps until notified, then re-acquires it and re-checks the predicate, which also handles spurious wake-ups.

Method

Shared state protected by one mutex; one condition variable per condition waited on.

  1. the waiter takes a std::unique_lock, calls cv.wait(lock, [&]{ return condition; });
  2. the notifier changes the state under the lock, then calls cv.notify_one() (or notify_all()).

wait with a predicate is a loop: while (!pred()) cv.wait(lock);, so a wake-up without the condition (spurious, or another consumer was faster) just goes back to sleep.

Example: bounded buffer

Intuition

A mutex is a door; a condition variable is a waiting room next to it, where you sleep until someone inside says "your condition may now be true — come and check".

Pitfall

Waiting without a predicate (if instead of while) breaks on spurious wake-ups, and changing the condition without holding the mutex can lose a notification: the waiter checks, the notifier changes and notifies, then the waiter sleeps for ever.
