Power gating and leakage reduction

Clock gating stops dynamic power but not leakage; power gating disconnects an idle block from the supply with a high-threshold sleep transistor (header or footer), cutting leakage by orders of magnitude, at the cost of wake-up time and energy, isolation cells on the outputs and retention registers for state that must survive.

Construction

  • Sleep transistor: a wide high- PMOS between and the block's virtual supply (header) or NMOS to ground (footer). Off: leakage limited by the high- device. On: small series resistance, slight speed loss.
  • Multi-threshold CMOS: low- (fast, leaky) cells on critical paths, high- cells elsewhere.
  • Isolation cells: clamp the outputs of a powered-down block to known values so that floating nodes do not cause short-circuit current in powered blocks.
  • Retention registers: a small always-on latch saves the state before shutdown.
  • Break-even time: gating pays only if the block stays off longer than , the time in which saved leakage equals the energy spent turning off and on.

Intuition

Clock gating is putting a machine on pause; power gating is unplugging it — far more saving, but restarting takes time and you lose what was in its memory unless you saved it.

Pitfall

Turning a large block on draws a current surge (in-rush) that disturbs neighbouring blocks' supply; switches are turned on gradually, which adds wake-up latency.
