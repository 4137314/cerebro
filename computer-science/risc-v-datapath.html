Construction

  • Fetch: instr < imem(pc)=; with a synchronous BRAM the instruction is available one cycle after the address.
  • Decode: register file read (distributed RAM, asynchronous read or latched output); immediate generator by format; opcode decoded into control signals.
  • Execute: ALU on (rs1, rs2 or imm); comparator for branches (beq, bne, blt, bge, unsigned variants); branch target , jump targets (jal) and (jalr).
  • Memory: loads read dmem(rs1+imm) (with byte/half selection and sign extension), stores write rs2.
  • Write back: ALU result, loaded value, (jumps) or immediate (lui), unless .
  • Next PC: , branch target if taken, jump target.

A control FSM (FETCH → DECODE → EXECUTE → MEM → WB) handles the BRAM latencies: loads and jumps take extra states.

Picture

Verification

Assemble small programs, load their hex into the instruction memory, and compare register and memory contents after simulation with the expected values; test every format and the corner cases (x0 as destination, negative immediates, taken and not-taken branches).

Intuition

Five questions per instruction: what is it, what are its operands, what does it compute, does it touch memory, where does the result go — and where is the next instruction.

Pitfall

Synchronous block RAM returns data one cycle after the address: a design that expects the instruction or the loaded word in the same cycle works in a zero-delay mental model and fails in simulation and hardware.
