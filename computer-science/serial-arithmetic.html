Bit-serial arithmetic

A serial adder processes one bit per clock, least significant first, with one full adder and a flip-flop holding the carry: cycles per -bit addition, constant area; serial and digit-serial units trade throughput for area and wiring, and fit serial data streams.

Construction

Shift registers present and at cycle ; a full adder computes and ; a D flip-flop stores for the next cycle (reset to at the start of each word). Sum bits are shifted out. Area: one full adder and one flip-flop, independent of ; time: cycles, but each cycle is a single full-adder delay, so the clock can be fast.

Digit-serial: process bits per cycle with a -bit adder: cycles. Serial multipliers combine a serial operand with a parallel one ( cycles, adders).

Intuition

The ripple-carry adder unrolled in time instead of space: the carry waits in a flip-flop instead of travelling along a wire.

Pitfall

Serial units need control (word boundaries, carry reset) and conversion to and from parallel form; for short words the overhead eats the area saving.
