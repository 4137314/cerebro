#+TITLE: Regular curves, arc length and curvature
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:differential_geometry:
#+MSC: 53A04
#+HASH: 0aef1d19e6bb

A curve is given by a parametrisation, and a parametrisation is a
choice. Differential geometry begins where one separates what belongs to
the curve from what belongs to the choice: arc length is the
parametrisation nothing else can prefer, and curvature is what is left
once it is used -- a function of the point, not of the description.

#+DEPENDS: def-differentiable-multivariable, def-riemann-integral
#+NAME: def-regular-curve
#+begin_definition
A /parametrised curve/ is a map \(\gamma \colon I \to \R^{n}\) on an
interval, of class \(C^{k}\) with \(k\) as large as needed; it is
/regular/ if \(\gamma'(t) \ne 0\) for every \(t\). Its /arc length/
between \(a\) and \(b\) in \(I\) is
\(L(a,b) = \int_{a}^{b}\norm{\gamma'(t)}\dd t\), and \(\gamma\) has
/unit speed/ if \(\norm{\gamma'} \equiv 1\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-regular-curve, thm-ftc-first, thm-continuous-inverse, thm-chain-rule, thm-substitution
#+NAME: thm-arc-length-parametrisation
#+begin_theorem
Every regular curve admits a reparametrisation by arc length: there is
a \(C^{1}\) bijection \(\varphi\) with \(C^{1}\) inverse such that
\(\gamma \circ \varphi\) has unit speed, and the arc length between two
points is unchanged by any such reparametrisation.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix \(t_{0} \in I\) and put \(s(t) = \int_{t_{0}}^{t}\norm{\gamma'(u)}\dd u\).
The integrand is continuous and strictly positive by regularity, so by
the fundamental theorem of calculus \(s\) is \(C^{1}\) with
\(s'(t) = \norm{\gamma'(t)} > 0\); hence \(s\) is strictly increasing,
and its image is an interval on which it has a continuous, indeed
\(C^{1}\), inverse \(\varphi\) with
\(\varphi'(s) = 1/\norm{\gamma'(\varphi(s))}\). By the chain rule
\((\gamma\circ\varphi)'(s) = \gamma'(\varphi(s))\varphi'(s)\), a vector
of norm \(1\).

For invariance, let \(\psi\) be any \(C^{1}\) change of parameter with
\(\psi' > 0\). The substitution rule gives
\(\int \norm{(\gamma\circ\psi)'(u)}\dd u = \int \norm{\gamma'(\psi(u))}\psi'(u)\dd u = \int\norm{\gamma'(t)}\dd t\)
over the corresponding intervals.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-arc-length-parametrisation
#+NAME: def-curvature
#+begin_definition
Let \(\gamma\) have unit speed. Its /unit tangent/ is
\(T = \gamma'\), its /curvature/ is \(\kappa = \norm{T'}\), and where
\(\kappa > 0\) its /principal normal/ is \(N = T'/\kappa\). In
\(\R^{3}\) the /binormal/ is \(B = T \times N\) and the /torsion/
\(\tau\) is defined by \(B' = -\tau N\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-curvature, prop-pythagoras
#+NAME: prop-tangent-and-normal-are-orthogonal
#+begin_proposition
For a unit-speed curve, \(T'\) is orthogonal to \(T\); so \(N\) is a
unit vector orthogonal to \(T\) wherever it is defined.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Differentiating the constant function \(\inner{T}{T} = 1\) gives
\(2\inner{T'}{T} = 0\). Where \(\kappa > 0\), \(N = T'/\kappa\) has norm
\(1\) by construction and inherits the orthogonality.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-curvature, prop-tangent-and-normal-are-orthogonal
#+NAME: thm-frenet-serret
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\gamma\) be a unit-speed curve in \(\R^{3}\) with \(\kappa > 0\).
Then \((T, N, B)\) is an orthonormal basis at each point and
\[ T' = \kappa N, \qquad N' = -\kappa T + \tau B, \qquad B' = -\tau N. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
\(T\) and \(N\) are orthonormal by the previous proposition, and
\(B = T\times N\) is a unit vector orthogonal to both, so the triple is
an orthonormal basis. The first equation is the definition of \(\kappa\)
and \(N\), and the third is the definition of \(\tau\) once it is known
that \(B'\) is a multiple of \(N\): differentiating
\(\inner{B}{B} = 1\) gives \(\inner{B'}{B} = 0\), and differentiating
\(\inner{B}{T} = 0\) gives
\(\inner{B'}{T} = -\inner{B}{T'} = -\kappa\inner{B}{N} = 0\).

For the middle equation, expand \(N'\) in the basis. Its \(T\)- and
\(B\)-components are obtained by differentiating \(\inner{N}{T} = 0\)
and \(\inner{N}{B} = 0\):
\(\inner{N'}{T} = -\inner{N}{T'} = -\kappa\) and
\(\inner{N'}{B} = -\inner{N}{B'} = \tau\); the \(N\)-component vanishes
because \(\inner{N}{N} = 1\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-frenet-serret, cor-vanishing-derivative
#+NAME: cor-zero-curvature-is-a-straight-line
#+begin_corollary
A unit-speed curve with \(\kappa \equiv 0\) on an interval is a segment
of a straight line.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
\(\kappa = \norm{T'} = 0\) means \(\gamma'' \equiv 0\), so each
coordinate of \(\gamma'\) has vanishing derivative and is therefore
constant, say \(\gamma' \equiv v\) with \(\norm{v} = 1\). Integrating,
\(\gamma(s) = \gamma(s_{0}) + (s - s_{0})v\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Curvature and torsion determine the curve up to a Euclidean isometry --
the Frenet system is a linear differential equation whose solution is
fixed by its initial frame, and the existence and uniqueness theorem
does the rest. That is the pattern of the whole subject: a geometric
object is reconstructed from invariants by solving an equation the
invariants define.
#+end_remark
