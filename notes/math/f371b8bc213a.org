#+TITLE: Sigma-algebras, measures and measurable functions
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:measure_theory:
#+MSC: 28A05, 28A20
#+HASH: f371b8bc213a

The [[id:29d7ea7b-5f6b-48e4-949b-553595dfa9a3][Riemann integral]] partitions the domain, which forces the integrand
to be nearly continuous and makes limits fragile: a pointwise limit of
Riemann integrable functions need not be integrable. Measure theory
partitions the /range/ instead, and to do so needs a notion of size for
the preimages -- a set function that is countably additive on a family
closed under countable operations. Everything else follows from those
two words.

#+DEPENDS: def-set-partition, def-supremum
#+NAME: def-sigma-algebra
#+begin_definition
A /\(\sigma\)-algebra/ on a set \(X\) is a family \(\mathcal{A}\) of
subsets containing \(X\) and closed under complement and countable
union. The pair \((X,\mathcal{A})\) is a /measurable space/ and the
members of \(\mathcal{A}\) are /measurable/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-sigma-algebra
#+NAME: prop-sigma-algebras-are-closed-under-intersection
#+begin_proposition
A \(\sigma\)-algebra contains \(\emptyset\) and is closed under
countable intersections and set differences. The intersection of any
non-empty family of \(\sigma\)-algebras on \(X\) is one, so for every
family \(\mathcal{S}\) of subsets there is a smallest \(\sigma\)-algebra
\(\sigma(\mathcal{S})\) containing it.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
\(\emptyset = X^{c}\); \(\bigcap_{n}A_{n} = (\bigcup_{n}A_{n}^{c})^{c}\);
\(A \setminus B = A \cap B^{c}\). The three closure conditions each hold
in an intersection of \(\sigma\)-algebras because they hold in every
member. The family of \(\sigma\)-algebras containing \(\mathcal{S}\) is
non-empty, the power set being one, so their intersection is the
smallest.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-sigma-algebras-are-closed-under-intersection, def-topological-space
#+NAME: def-borel-sigma-algebra
#+begin_definition
The /Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra/ of a topological space is the
\(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the open sets. Its members are the
/Borel sets/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-sigma-algebra, def-series-convergence
#+NAME: def-measure
#+begin_definition
A /measure/ on \((X,\mathcal{A})\) is a map
\(\mu \colon \mathcal{A}\to[0,+\infty]\) with \(\mu(\emptyset)=0\) that
is /countably additive/: \(\mu(\bigcup_{n}A_{n}) = \sum_{n}\mu(A_{n})\)
for every sequence of pairwise disjoint measurable sets. The triple
\((X,\mathcal{A},\mu)\) is a /measure space/, and \(\mu\) is /finite/ if
\(\mu(X)<\infty\), /probability/ if \(\mu(X)=1\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-measure, def-sequence-limit, def-supremum
#+NAME: thm-basic-properties-of-a-measure
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\mu\) be a measure. Then \(\mu\) is monotone
(\(A\subseteq B\) implies \(\mu(A)\le\mu(B)\)), countably subadditive
(\(\mu(\bigcup_{n}A_{n})\le\sum_{n}\mu(A_{n})\) with no disjointness),
continuous from below (\(A_{n}\uparrow A\) implies
\(\mu(A_{n})\to\mu(A)\)) and continuous from above when
\(\mu(A_{1})<\infty\) (\(A_{n}\downarrow A\) implies
\(\mu(A_{n})\to\mu(A)\)).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Monotonicity: \(\mu(B) = \mu(A)+\mu(B\setminus A)\ge\mu(A)\), by
additivity on the disjoint pair.

Continuity from below: put \(B_{1}=A_{1}\) and
\(B_{n} = A_{n}\setminus A_{n-1}\), pairwise disjoint with
\(\bigcup_{k\le n}B_{k} = A_{n}\) and \(\bigcup_{k}B_{k} = A\). Then
\(\mu(A) = \sum_{k}\mu(B_{k}) = \lim_{n}\sum_{k\le n}\mu(B_{k}) = \lim_{n}\mu(A_{n})\).

Subadditivity: with \(C_{n} = A_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{k<n}A_{k}\),
disjoint with the same union and \(C_{n}\subseteq A_{n}\),
\(\mu(\bigcup_{n}A_{n}) = \sum_{n}\mu(C_{n}) \le \sum_{n}\mu(A_{n})\).

Continuity from above: \(A_{1}\setminus A_{n}\) increases to
\(A_{1}\setminus A\), so
\(\mu(A_{1})-\mu(A_{n}) \to \mu(A_{1})-\mu(A)\) by the first part and
additivity, the finiteness of \(\mu(A_{1})\) making the subtraction
legitimate.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-sigma-algebra, def-continuous-map, def-borel-sigma-algebra
#+NAME: def-measurable-function
#+begin_definition
A function \(f \colon X \to \R\) on a measurable space is /measurable/
if \(f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{A}\) for every Borel set \(B\); functions with
values in \([-\infty,+\infty]\) are measurable if in addition the
preimages of \(\pm\infty\) are measurable.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-measurable-function, prop-continuity-tested-on-a-subbasis
#+NAME: prop-measurability-tested-on-rays
#+begin_proposition
\(f\) is measurable if and only if \(\setst{x}{f(x)>a}\) is measurable
for every \(a \in \R\). Continuous functions on a topological space are
Borel measurable.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
The family \(\setst{B}{f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{A}}\) is a
\(\sigma\)-algebra, preimage commuting with complements and unions. If
it contains every ray \((a,\infty)\) it contains the
\(\sigma\)-algebra they generate, which is the Borel one -- every open
subset of \(\R\) is a countable union of open intervals, and an interval
is a difference of two rays. The converse is immediate, a ray being
Borel. For a continuous \(f\), preimages of open sets are open, hence
Borel.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-measurability-tested-on-rays, def-limsup-liminf, thm-limit-algebra
#+NAME: thm-measurability-is-preserved-by-limits
#+begin_theorem
If \(f, g\) are measurable so are \(f+g\), \(fg\), \(\abs{f}\) and
\(\max(f,g)\); and if \((f_{n})\) are measurable so are
\(\sup_{n}f_{n}\), \(\inf_{n}f_{n}\), \(\limsup_{n}f_{n}\) and
\(\liminf_{n}f_{n}\), as functions into \([-\infty,+\infty]\). In
particular a pointwise limit of measurable functions is measurable.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For the supremum, \(\setst{x}{\sup_{n}f_{n}(x)>a} = \bigcup_{n}\setst{x}{f_{n}(x)>a}\),
a countable union of measurable sets; the infimum is the supremum of
the \(-f_{n}\), and
\(\limsup_{n}f_{n} = \inf_{N}\sup_{n\ge N}f_{n}\) combines the two.

For sums, \(\setst{x}{f(x)+g(x)>a} = \bigcup_{q\in\Q}\bigl(\setst{x}{f(x)>q}\cap\setst{x}{g(x)>a-q}\bigr)\),
a countable union, since a real sum exceeds \(a\) exactly when a
rational separates the terms appropriately. Writing
\(E_{h,a} = \setst{x}{h(x)>a}\), the function \(\abs{f}\) is measurable
because \(E_{\abs{f},a}\) is \(X\) for \(a<0\) and
\(E_{f,a}\cup E_{-f,a}\) otherwise; \(\max(f,g)\) because
\(E_{\max(f,g),a} = E_{f,a}\cup E_{g,a}\); and \(fg\) from
\(fg = \tfrac{1}{4}\bigl((f+g)^{2}-(f-g)^{2}\bigr)\) once \(h^{2}\) is
known measurable, which holds since \(E_{h^{2},a}\) is \(X\) for
\(a<0\) and \(E_{h,\sqrt{a}}\cup E_{-h,\sqrt{a}}\) for \(a \ge 0\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The last theorem is the whole point. The measurable functions are closed
under every countable limiting operation, which the Riemann integrable
ones are not, and that closure is what makes the convergence theorems of
the next note possible. The price is that no explicit description of a
general measurable set exists -- \(\sigma(\mathcal{S})\) is defined from
above, by intersection, exactly like a generated subgroup or an ideal.
#+end_remark
