#+TITLE: Linear maps, quotient spaces and the rank-nullity theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:linear_algebra:
#+MSC: 15A04, 15A03
#+HASH: 023f63eea88e

For groups, a homomorphism is measured by one subgroup, its kernel. For
vector spaces the same is true and, because dimension exists, the
measurement becomes a number: what the map loses plus what it keeps is
what it started with. Rank-nullity is the first isomorphism theorem with
a dimension count attached.

#+DEPENDS: def-vector-space, def-span-and-independence
#+NAME: def-linear-map
#+begin_definition
A map \(f \colon V \to W\) between vector spaces over \(K\) is /linear/
if \(f(u + v) = f(u) + f(v)\) and \(f(\lambda v) = \lambda f(v)\). Its
/kernel/ is \(\ker f = \setst{v \in V}{f(v) = 0}\) and its /image/ is
\(\im f = f(V)\); the /rank/ of \(f\) is \(\dim \im f\) and its
/nullity/ is \(\dim \ker f\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-linear-map
#+NAME: prop-kernel-and-image-are-subspaces
#+begin_proposition
\(\ker f\) is a subspace of \(V\) and \(\im f\) is a subspace of \(W\);
and \(f\) is injective if and only if \(\ker f = \set{0}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Both contain \(0\) and are closed under sums and scalar multiples, by
linearity. If \(f\) is injective and \(f(v) = 0 = f(0)\) then \(v = 0\);
conversely if the kernel is trivial and \(f(u) = f(v)\) then
\(f(u - v) = 0\), so \(u = v\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-linear-map, thm-universal-property-of-a-quotient-set, prop-kernel-and-image-are-subspaces
#+NAME: thm-quotient-vector-space
#+begin_theorem
Let \(U \subseteq V\) be a subspace. The quotient group \(V/U\) is a
vector space under \(\lambda(v + U) = \lambda v + U\), the projection
\(\pi \colon V \to V/U\) is linear with kernel \(U\), and every linear
\(f \colon V \to W\) vanishing on \(U\) factors uniquely as
\(f = \bar{f} \circ \pi\) with \(\bar{f}\) linear.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Scalar multiplication is well defined: if \(v + U = v' + U\) then
\(v - v' \in U\), so \(\lambda v - \lambda v' \in U\). The vector space
axioms are inherited coset by coset, and \(\pi\) is linear with kernel
\(U\) by construction. If \(f\) vanishes on \(U\) it is constant on
cosets, so the universal property of the quotient set gives a unique
\(\bar{f}\) with \(\bar{f}\circ\pi = f\); it is linear because
\(\bar{f}(\lambda(v+U) + (v'+U)) = f(\lambda v + v') = \lambda \bar{f}(v+U) + \bar{f}(v'+U)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-quotient-vector-space, cor-extension-to-a-basis, thm-dimension-is-well-defined
#+NAME: thm-rank-nullity
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f \colon V \to W\) be linear with \(V\) finite-dimensional. Then
\(V/\ker f \iso \im f\) and
\[ \dim \ker f + \dim \im f = \dim V. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The induced \(\bar{f} \colon V/\ker f \to \im f\) is linear, surjective
by construction, and injective because \(\bar{f}(v + \ker f) = 0\) means
\(v \in \ker f\); so it is an isomorphism.

For the count, take a basis \(u_{1}, \dots, u_{k}\) of \(\ker f\) and
extend it to a basis \(u_{1},\dots,u_{k},v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\) of \(V\).
The vectors \(f(v_{1}), \dots, f(v_{r})\) span \(\im f\), since the
\(u_{i}\) contribute nothing; and they are independent, because
\(\sum \lambda_{j} f(v_{j}) = 0\) puts \(\sum \lambda_{j} v_{j}\) in
\(\ker f\), hence expresses it in the \(u_{i}\), and independence of the
full basis forces every \(\lambda_{j} = 0\). So
\(\dim \im f = r = \dim V - k\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-rank-nullity, prop-kernel-and-image-are-subspaces, prop-bijective-iff-invertible
#+NAME: cor-injective-iff-surjective-in-finite-dimension
#+begin_corollary
Let \(f \colon V \to V\) be linear with \(\dim V = n\) finite. Then
\(f\) is injective if and only if it is surjective, if and only if it is
an isomorphism.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Injectivity is \(\dim\ker f = 0\), which by rank-nullity is
\(\dim \im f = n\), which for a subspace of \(V\) means
\(\im f = V\), that is surjectivity. A bijective linear map has a
linear inverse: the set-theoretic inverse \(g\) satisfies
\(f(g(u)+g(v)) = u+v = f(g(u+v))\), and \(f\) is injective, so \(g\) is
additive; homogeneity is the same argument.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The corollary is false in infinite dimensions -- the shift
\((a_{1}, a_{2}, \dots) \mapsto (0, a_{1}, a_{2}, \dots)\) is injective
and not surjective -- and the failure is not a curiosity: it is why
operator theory exists as a subject separate from linear algebra, and
why the spectrum of an operator is more than its set of eigenvalues.
#+end_remark
