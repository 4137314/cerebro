#+TITLE: Tychonoff's theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54D30, 54B10
#+HASH: 72ac2b2ac74c

A product of compact spaces is compact -- for arbitrarily many factors,
with no countability and no metric. The statement is strong enough to be
equivalent to the axiom of choice, and the shortest honest proof is the
one that says what compactness /is/ in terms of ultrafilters: a product
is compact because an ultrafilter can be pushed into every coordinate at
once, and reassembled.

#+DEPENDS: def-product-topology, def-compact, thm-compactness-by-ultrafilters
#+NAME: lem-pushforward-of-an-ultrafilter
#+begin_lemma
Let \(\mathcal{U}\) be an ultrafilter on a set \(I\) and
\(f \colon I \to Y\) any map. Then
\(f_{*}\mathcal{U} = \setst{B \subseteq Y}{f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{U}}\) is
an ultrafilter on \(Y\).
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
Preimage commutes with intersection and complement and preserves
inclusion, so \(f_{*}\mathcal{U}\) is closed under finite intersections
and upward closed, contains \(Y\), and omits \(\emptyset\). For any
\(B\), either \(f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{U}\) or its complement
\(f^{-1}(Y\setminus B)\) is, by the ultrafilter criterion; so
\(f_{*}\mathcal{U}\) satisfies that criterion too.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: lem-pushforward-of-an-ultrafilter, thm-compactness-by-ultrafilters, thm-product-universal-property, def-filter-convergence
#+NAME: thm-tychonoff
#+begin_theorem
An arbitrary product \(X = \prod_{i\in I}X_{i}\) of compact spaces is
compact in the product topology.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By the ultrafilter characterisation of compactness it suffices to show
that every ultrafilter \(\mathcal{U}\) on \(X\) converges.

For each \(i\), the pushforward \((\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{U}\) is an
ultrafilter on \(X_{i}\), which is compact, so it converges to some
point; choose one and call it \(x_{i}\). Let \(x = (x_{i})_{i}\).

We claim \(\mathcal{U}\to x\). A basic neighbourhood of \(x\) is a
finite intersection \(\bigcap_{k\le n}\pi_{i_{k}}^{-1}(V_{k})\) with
\(V_{k}\) an open neighbourhood of \(x_{i_{k}}\). Since
\((\pi_{i_{k}})_{*}\mathcal{U}\to x_{i_{k}}\), we have
\(V_{k}\in(\pi_{i_{k}})_{*}\mathcal{U}\), that is
\(\pi_{i_{k}}^{-1}(V_{k})\in\mathcal{U}\); a filter is closed under
finite intersections, so the basic neighbourhood lies in
\(\mathcal{U}\), and hence so does every neighbourhood of \(x\) by
upward closure.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-tychonoff, prop-hausdorff-is-inherited, cor-heine-borel-in-euclidean-space
#+NAME: cor-cubes-are-compact
#+begin_corollary
For any set \(I\), the cube \([0,1]^{I}\) is compact Hausdorff.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
\([0,1]\) is closed and bounded in \(\R\), hence compact, and it is
Hausdorff as a metric space. Tychonoff's theorem gives compactness of
the product, and a product of Hausdorff spaces is Hausdorff.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The proof used a choice of limit in each coordinate, and that use is not
an artefact: Tychonoff's theorem implies the axiom of choice. Given
non-empty sets \(X_{i}\), topologise \(X_{i}\cup\set{\infty_{i}}\) so
that it is compact, apply the theorem to the product, and a point of a
suitable closed subset of it is a choice function. For /Hausdorff/
factors the theorem is strictly weaker -- it is equivalent to the
ultrafilter lemma -- which is a rare case where the exact strength of a
standard theorem is known.
#+end_remark
