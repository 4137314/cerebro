#+TITLE: Noether's theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:classical_mechanics:
#+MSC: 70H03, 70G65
#+HASH: 0bdd1cd532be

A cyclic coordinate conserves its momentum, but "cyclic" is a statement
about a chart, and a symmetry need not be aligned with any coordinate.
Noether's theorem removes the chart: to every one-parameter family of
transformations leaving the Lagrangian invariant it attaches a quantity
constant along every solution, and the correspondence is explicit rather
than existential.

#+DEPENDS: def-action-functional, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-variational-symmetry
#+begin_definition
A continuously differentiable family of maps
\(\Phi_{s} \colon \R^{n} \to \R^{n}\), \(s\) in a neighbourhood of
\(0\), with \(\Phi_{0} = \mathrm{id}\), is a /symmetry/ of \(L\) if
\[ L\bigl( \Phi_{s}(q), \, D\Phi_{s}(q) \dot q, \, t \bigr) = L(q, \dot q, t) \]
for all \(q, \dot q, t\) and all \(s\). Its /generator/ is the vector
field \(X(q) = \left. \frac{\partial}{\partial s} \Phi_{s}(q) \right|_{s=0}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-variational-symmetry, thm-euler-lagrange, def-conjugate-momentum, cor-vanishing-derivative
#+NAME: thm-noether
#+begin_theorem
If \(\Phi_{s}\) is a symmetry of \(L\) with generator \(X\), then
\[ I(q, \dot q) = \sum_{i} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q_{i}}(q, \dot q, t) \, X_{i}(q) = p \cdot X(q) \]
is constant along every solution of the Euler-Lagrange equations.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Differentiate the invariance identity with respect to \(s\) at
\(s = 0\). Writing \(q^{s} = \Phi_{s}(q)\), the chain rule gives
\[ 0 = \sum_{i} \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_{i}} X_{i}(q) + \sum_{i} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q_{i}} \frac{\dd}{\dd t} X_{i}(q), \]
the second term because differentiating \(D\Phi_{s}(q)\dot q\) in \(s\)
at \(0\) produces exactly the time derivative of \(X_{i}\) along the
path. Now let \(q\) solve the Euler-Lagrange equations, so that
\(\partial L/\partial q_{i} = \dd p_{i}/\dd t\). Substituting,
\[ 0 = \sum_{i} \Bigl( \frac{\dd p_{i}}{\dd t} X_{i} + p_{i} \frac{\dd X_{i}}{\dd t} \Bigr) = \frac{\dd}{\dd t} \sum_{i} p_{i} X_{i}, \]
by the product rule. A function with vanishing derivative on an
interval is constant.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
A cyclic coordinate is the special case \(\Phi_{s}(q) = q + s e_{i}\),
whose generator is the constant field \(e_{i}\) and whose conserved
quantity is \(p_{i}\). The theorem's content is that nothing depended on
the symmetry being a translation of a coordinate: invariance is a
property of \(L\), and the conserved quantity is read off the generator.
#+end_remark
