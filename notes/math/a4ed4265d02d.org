#+TITLE: Nearest points and the separating hyperplane theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:convex_geometry:
#+MSC: 52A20, 52A35
#+HASH: a4ed4265d02d

A [[id:29371f24-12c6-4151-80ae-3f459e7c83f6][convex set]] is one that contains the segment between any two of its
points, which is a statement about lines. The theorem below turns it
into a statement about linear functionals: a closed convex set is the
intersection of the half-spaces containing it, so convexity can always be
certified by inequalities. Every duality argument in optimisation is
this theorem.

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-set, def-inner-product-space
#+NAME: def-hyperplane-and-half-space
#+begin_definition
For \(u \in \R^{n}\) non-zero and \(\alpha \in \R\), the /hyperplane/ is
\(H = \setst{y}{\inner{u}{y} = \alpha}\) and the two closed
/half-spaces/ it bounds are \(\setst{y}{\inner{u}{y} \le \alpha}\) and
\(\setst{y}{\inner{u}{y} \ge \alpha}\). The hyperplane /separates/ two
sets if they lie in opposite half-spaces, and /strictly separates/ them
if the inequalities are strict.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-set, thm-euclidean-space-complete, prop-closed-iff-sequentially-closed, prop-parallelogram-identity
#+NAME: thm-nearest-point-in-a-closed-convex-set
#+begin_theorem
Let \(C \subseteq \R^{n}\) be non-empty, closed and convex, and let
\(x \in \R^{n}\). Then there is exactly one \(p \in C\) with
\(\norm{x - p} = \inf_{c \in C}\norm{x - c}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(d\) be that infimum and pick \(c_{k} \in C\) with
\(\norm{x - c_{k}} \to d\). The parallelogram identity, applied to
\(x - c_{k}\) and \(x - c_{l}\), gives
\[ \norm{c_{k}-c_{l}}^{2} = 2\norm{x-c_{k}}^{2} + 2\norm{x-c_{l}}^{2} - 4\norm{x - \tfrac{c_{k}+c_{l}}{2}}^{2}. \]
The midpoint lies in \(C\) by convexity, so the last term is at least
\(4d^{2}\), and the right-hand side tends to
\(2d^{2}+2d^{2}-4d^{2} = 0\). Hence \((c_{k})\) is Cauchy, so it
converges to some \(p\), which lies in \(C\) because \(C\) is closed;
and \(\norm{x-p} = d\) by continuity of the norm.

Uniqueness: if \(p, p'\) both attain \(d\), the same identity with
\(c_{k} = p\), \(c_{l} = p'\) gives
\(\norm{p-p'}^{2} \le 4d^{2}-4d^{2} = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-nearest-point-in-a-closed-convex-set, def-hyperplane-and-half-space
#+NAME: prop-obtuse-angle-criterion
#+begin_proposition
With the notation above, \(p\) is the nearest point of \(C\) to \(x\) if
and only if \(\inner{x - p}{c - p} \le 0\) for every \(c \in C\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Assume \(p\) is nearest and let \(c \in C\), \(t \in (0,1]\). Convexity
puts \(p + t(c-p)\) in \(C\), so
\(\norm{x-p-t(c-p)}^{2} \ge \norm{x-p}^{2}\); expanding,
\(-2t\inner{x-p}{c-p} + t^{2}\norm{c-p}^{2} \ge 0\), so
\(\inner{x-p}{c-p} \le \tfrac{t}{2}\norm{c-p}^{2}\) for every
\(t \in (0,1]\), and letting \(t \to 0\) gives the inequality.
Conversely, if it holds then for \(c \in C\),
\(\norm{x-c}^{2} = \norm{(x-p) - (c-p)}^{2} = \norm{x-p}^{2} - 2\inner{x-p}{c-p} + \norm{c-p}^{2} \ge \norm{x-p}^{2}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-obtuse-angle-criterion, def-hyperplane-and-half-space
#+NAME: thm-separating-hyperplane
#+begin_theorem
Let \(C \subseteq \R^{n}\) be non-empty, closed and convex, and
\(x \notin C\). Then there are \(u \ne 0\) and \(\alpha \in \R\) with
\(\inner{u}{c} < \alpha < \inner{u}{x}\) for every \(c \in C\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(p\) be the nearest point of \(C\) to \(x\) and put \(u = x - p\),
non-zero since \(x \notin C\). By the previous proposition
\(\inner{u}{c - p} \le 0\), that is \(\inner{u}{c} \le \inner{u}{p}\),
for every \(c \in C\), while
\(\inner{u}{x} = \inner{u}{p} + \norm{u}^{2} > \inner{u}{p}\). Any
\(\alpha\) strictly between \(\inner{u}{p}\) and \(\inner{u}{x}\) works.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-separating-hyperplane, thm-bolzano-weierstrass, prop-closed-iff-sequentially-closed
#+NAME: cor-supporting-hyperplane
#+begin_corollary
Let \(C\) be closed and convex and let \(p\) be a boundary point of
\(C\). Then there is \(u \ne 0\) with
\(\inner{u}{c} \le \inner{u}{p}\) for every \(c \in C\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Since \(p\) is on the boundary there are \(x_{k} \notin C\) with
\(x_{k} \to p\). For each \(k\) the theorem gives a unit vector
\(u_{k}\) with \(\inner{u_{k}}{c} \le \inner{u_{k}}{x_{k}}\) for all
\(c \in C\) (normalise the separating vector). The unit sphere is
bounded, so by Bolzano-Weierstrass a subsequence \(u_{k_{j}}\) converges
to some \(u\), which has \(\norm{u} = 1\) and hence is non-zero.
Passing to the limit in
\(\inner{u_{k_{j}}}{c} \le \inner{u_{k_{j}}}{x_{k_{j}}}\), using
continuity of the inner product and \(x_{k_{j}} \to p\), gives
\(\inner{u}{c} \le \inner{u}{p}\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Closedness is what the nearest point needs and convexity is what turns
the nearest point into a separating direction; drop either and the
conclusion fails. The corollary is the geometric content of a Lagrange
multiplier: at a boundary point there is a direction in which the set
does not extend, and it is normal to a hyperplane touching the set
there.
#+end_remark
