#+TITLE: The Chinese remainder theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:commutative_algebra:
#+MSC: 13A15, 11A05
#+HASH: 1d4d52c18532

Solving congruences one modulus at a time is possible exactly when the
moduli are unrelated, and the theorem says what "unrelated" means for
ideals: their sum is everything. The statement is an isomorphism of
rings, so it does not merely produce a solution but identifies the whole
ring with a product -- which is how a computation is split into
independent ones.

#+DEPENDS: def-ideal, def-ring
#+NAME: def-comaximal
#+begin_definition
Ideals \(I, J\) of a commutative ring \(R\) are /comaximal/ if
\(I + J = R\), where \(I + J = \setst{a+b}{a \in I, b\in J}\). The
/product/ \(IJ\) is the ideal generated by the products \(ab\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-comaximal
#+NAME: prop-comaximal-product-is-intersection
#+begin_proposition
If \(I\) and \(J\) are comaximal then \(IJ = I \cap J\); and if
\(I\) is comaximal with each of \(J_{1},\dots,J_{n}\) then it is
comaximal with their product.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Always \(IJ \subseteq I\cap J\), each generator lying in both. For the
converse write \(1 = u + v\) with \(u \in I\), \(v \in J\). For
\(x \in I \cap J\), \(x = xu + xv\), and \(xu \in JI\) since
\(x \in J\), while \(xv \in IJ\) since \(x \in I\); so \(x \in IJ\).

For the second claim, write \(1 = u_{k}+v_{k}\) with \(u_{k}\in I\),
\(v_{k}\in J_{k}\). Then
\(1 = \prod_{k}(u_{k}+v_{k})\), and expanding, every term but
\(\prod_{k}v_{k}\) contains some \(u_{k}\) and hence lies in \(I\). So
\(1 \in I + \prod_{k}J_{k}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-comaximal-product-is-intersection, thm-quotient-ring, thm-first-isomorphism-rings
#+NAME: thm-chinese-remainder
#+begin_theorem
Let \(I_{1},\dots,I_{n}\) be pairwise comaximal ideals of a commutative
ring \(R\). Then
\[ R/(I_{1}\cap\cdots\cap I_{n}) \iso R/I_{1} \times \cdots \times R/I_{n}, \]
by \(x + \bigcap_{k}I_{k} \mapsto (x+I_{1},\dots,x+I_{n})\), and the
intersection equals the product \(I_{1}\cdots I_{n}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The map \(\varphi \colon R \to \prod_{k}R/I_{k}\) sending \(x\) to the
tuple of its classes is a ring homomorphism with kernel
\(\bigcap_{k}I_{k}\); by the homomorphism theorem it suffices to prove
it surjective.

Fix \(k\). By the previous proposition \(I_{k}\) is comaximal with
\(\prod_{l\ne k}I_{l}\), so there are \(u \in I_{k}\) and
\(w \in \prod_{l\ne k}I_{l}\) with \(u + w = 1\). Then \(w \equiv 1\)
modulo \(I_{k}\) and \(w \equiv 0\) modulo every \(I_{l}\), \(l\ne k\);
so \(\varphi(w)\) is the \(k\)-th standard idempotent. Given a target
tuple \((x_{k}+I_{k})_{k}\), the element \(\sum_{k}x_{k}w_{k}\) maps to
it.

Finally the intersection is the product: by induction on \(n\) using the
proposition, \(I_{1}\cap(I_{2}\cdots I_{n}) = I_{1}(I_{2}\cdots I_{n})\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-chinese-remainder, thm-euclidean-is-a-pid, thm-pid-is-a-ufd
#+NAME: cor-crt-for-integers
#+begin_corollary
Let \(m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\) be pairwise coprime positive integers with
product \(m\). Then \(\Z/m\Z \iso \prod_{k}\Z/m_{k}\Z\) as rings; in
particular the system of congruences \(x \equiv a_{k} \pmod{m_{k}}\) has
a solution, unique modulo \(m\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
In \(\Z\) every ideal is \(d\Z\). The ideals \(m_{k}\Z\) are pairwise
comaximal: \(m_{j}\Z + m_{k}\Z\) is the ideal generated by
\(\gcd(m_{j},m_{k}) = 1\), hence all of \(\Z\). Their intersection is
\(m\Z\) by the theorem, the product of the \(m_{k}\Z\) being
\(m\Z\). The isomorphism is the statement about congruences, its
injectivity the uniqueness modulo \(m\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Comaximality is essential and not merely convenient: \(2\Z\) and
\(4\Z\) are not comaximal, and \(\Z/8\Z\) is not a product of
\(\Z/2\Z\) and \(\Z/4\Z\) -- it has an element of order \(8\) and the
product has none. The idempotents \(\varphi(w_{k})\) constructed in the
proof are the real content: a decomposition of a ring into a product is
the same thing as a family of orthogonal idempotents summing to \(1\).
#+end_remark
