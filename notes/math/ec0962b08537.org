#+TITLE: Geometry
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:
#+MSC: 51-XX, 52-XX, 53-XX
#+HASH: ec0962b08537

A geometry is fixed by choosing which transformations are allowed; its
theorems are the statements invariant under them. That is the organising
principle of everything below, and it is why the same object appears
several times with different content: a conic is one thing projectively,
three affinely, and infinitely many metrically.

Part of [[id:c2fcb100-fc5a-4670-9b5d-c068c0c325ff][Mathematics]].

* The geometries, by the group allowed

- *Affine* -- [[id:734ca2fe-0727-4155-992a-7b46244de19e][affine spaces and affine maps]]: a vector space with the
  origin forgotten. Invariants: parallelism, ratios along a line,
  barycentres.
- *Euclidean* -- [[id:c2edf7f5-d5ac-4e50-8734-a00b2da22cf4][orthogonality and Gram-Schmidt]] and [[id:f830b644-837d-4fda-8384-8dad0d143c2f][the isometries of
  \(\R^{n}\)]], which are exactly the affine maps with orthogonal linear
  part. Invariants: length and angle.
- *Projective* -- [[id:844044ec-e5a0-41e4-9a47-639bb67e36b2][projective space and its charts]] and [[id:a0c5f254-0252-4136-85e7-c5436092ee67][the
  cross-ratio]]. Adding one point per direction removes the exception
  from "two lines meet"; four points on a line have exactly one
  invariant, and that is the whole of the metric content that survives.
- *Convex* -- [[id:29371f24-12c6-4151-80ae-3f459e7c83f6][convex sets]], [[id:203494e0-5225-4540-9a81-f61cab21d41b][the separating hyperplane theorem]],
  [[id:3b9421b3-cb06-43d8-bd6a-aae10da6fd6f][Caratheodory's theorem]] and [[id:b9de97b3-9d38-43ee-9037-395e86c390c1][extreme points]]. Not invariance under a
  group but a condition on a set, and the one part of geometry that is
  also optimisation.
- *Differential* -- [[id:e2341c10-784e-4f50-9fce-429f9f8e9e89][curves]], [[id:bcd05411-fa96-4d39-b18a-1d2d65669fff][surfaces]] and [[id:e8ee2fda-0c4d-43f2-9fe8-f18e423a8010][smooth manifolds]]. Here the
  allowed transformations are the diffeomorphisms, and the invariants
  are found by differentiating.

* Classification is linear algebra

Twice in this area a geometric classification is settled by a normal
form for a symmetric bilinear form. [[id:63688469-ed04-4016-a5ce-54cb91443094][Sylvester's law of inertia]]
classifies [[id:ba1be74f-3a05-45ce-a429-7fe14ad45170][conics and quadrics]] by signature, and the [[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][spectral
theorem]] diagonalises [[id:bcd05411-fa96-4d39-b18a-1d2d65669fff][the shape operator of a surface]], producing the
principal curvatures. The two are different theorems on purpose:
Sylvester allows any change of basis and can preserve only signs, the
spectral theorem allows only orthonormal ones and preserves the
eigenvalues. Which one applies is exactly the question of which group
the geometry is over.

* Where geometry meets the rest

[[id:b9de97b3-9d38-43ee-9037-395e86c390c1][Minkowski's convex body theorem]] proves statements about integers by
choosing a lattice and a symmetric body -- geometry used as number
theory. [[id:e8ee2fda-0c4d-43f2-9fe8-f18e423a8010][The tangent space]] is defined algebraically, as derivations of
the ring of smooth functions, so that it needs no coordinates. And
[[id:c1d8ebd6-723e-4272-9cce-66ef33bdea2e][canonical transformations]] are the symplectic geometry of
[[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mechanics]].

* What is missing

Riemannian metrics and geodesics; the Theorema Egregium, which is stated
as a remark and not proved; differential forms and Stokes' theorem;
curvature of manifolds rather than of surfaces in \(\R^{3}\); algebraic
geometry beyond the projective linear case -- there are no varieties and
no Nullstellensatz, although the [[id:e423d662-1f19-48be-a1f8-8bdfba79235b][Hilbert basis theorem]] is in place; and
hyperbolic geometry.
