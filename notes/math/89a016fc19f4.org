#+TITLE: Continuous functions on a compact interval
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A15
#+HASH: 89a016fc19f4

Two properties of a continuous function on \([a,b]\) that fail on an
open or unbounded interval, and both come from Bolzano--Weierstrass: it
is bounded, and it attains its bounds.

#+DEPENDS: def-continuity-at-point, thm-bolzano-weierstrass
#+NAME: thm-boundedness
#+begin_theorem
A continuous function \(f \colon [a,b] \to \R\) is bounded.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose not. Then for every \(n\) there is \(x_{n} \in [a,b]\) with
\(\abs{f(x_{n})} > n\). By Bolzano--Weierstrass a subsequence
\(x_{n_{k}} \to c\), and \(c \in [a,b]\) since the interval is closed.
Continuity at \(c\) gives \(f(x_{n_{k}}) \to f(c)\), so
\((f(x_{n_{k}}))\) is bounded -- contradicting
\(\abs{f(x_{n_{k}})} > n_{k} \to \infty\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-boundedness, prop-supremum-characterisation, thm-bolzano-weierstrass
#+NAME: thm-extreme-value
#+begin_theorem
A continuous function \(f \colon [a,b] \to \R\) attains a maximum and a
minimum: there are \(p, q \in [a,b]\) with \(f(p) \le f(x) \le f(q)\)
for every \(x \in [a,b]\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By the previous theorem \(M = \sup_{x \in [a,b]} f(x)\) exists. By the
characterisation of the supremum, for every \(n\) there is
\(x_{n} \in [a,b]\) with \(f(x_{n}) > M - 1/n\). By
Bolzano--Weierstrass a subsequence \(x_{n_{k}} \to q \in [a,b]\), and
continuity gives \(f(q) = \lim_{k} f(x_{n_{k}}) = M\). Applying this to
\(-f\) produces the minimum.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Both fail without compactness: \(f(x) = 1/x\) is continuous and unbounded
on \((0,1]\), and \(f(x) = x\) attains no maximum on \([0,1)\).
#+end_remark
