#+TITLE: Equilibria, stability and the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:classical_mechanics:
#+MSC: 70H14, 34D20
#+HASH: 7b31a99b859f

An equilibrium is a constant solution; the question is whether motions
starting nearby stay nearby. For a conservative system the answer is
read off the potential alone: a strict minimum is stable, because energy
conservation traps the motion in a level set of the energy. That
argument is the prototype of every Lyapunov function.

#+DEPENDS: cor-conservative-forces-give-a-variational-principle, prop-energy-and-cyclic-coordinates, def-metric-space
#+NAME: def-equilibrium-and-stability
#+begin_definition
Consider a natural mechanical system with Lagrangian
\(L = \tfrac{1}{2}\inner{A(q)\dot{q}}{\dot{q}} - V(q)\), where
\(A(q)\) is symmetric positive definite and depends continuously on
\(q\). A point \(q_{0}\) is an /equilibrium/ if \(q(t)\equiv q_{0}\)
solves the equations of motion. It is /stable/ (in the sense of
Lyapunov) if for every neighbourhood \(U\) of \((q_{0},0)\) in phase
space there is a neighbourhood \(W\) such that every motion starting in
\(W\) stays in \(U\) for all \(t \ge 0\), and /unstable/ otherwise.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-equilibrium-and-stability, thm-lagrange-equations-from-dalembert, lem-interior-extremum
#+NAME: prop-equilibria-are-critical-points
#+begin_proposition
\(q_{0}\) is an equilibrium if and only if \(\nabla V(q_{0}) = 0\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For the constant curve \(q \equiv q_{0}\) all velocities and
accelerations vanish, so the Lagrange equations reduce to
\(\partial L/\partial q_{k} = 0\); the kinetic term is quadratic in
\(\dot{q}\) and hence contributes nothing at \(\dot{q} = 0\), leaving
\(-\partial V/\partial q_{k}(q_{0}) = 0\). Conversely, if
\(\nabla V(q_{0}) = 0\) the constant curve satisfies the equations.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-equilibria-are-critical-points, prop-energy-and-cyclic-coordinates, cor-extreme-value-on-a-compact-space, cor-heine-borel-in-euclidean-space
#+NAME: thm-lagrange-dirichlet
#+begin_theorem
If \(V\) has a strict local minimum at \(q_{0}\), then \(q_{0}\) is a
stable equilibrium.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Normalise \(V(q_{0}) = 0\), so \(V>0\) on a punctured neighbourhood, and
let \(E(q,\dot{q}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\inner{A(q)\dot{q}}{\dot{q}} + V(q)\),
which is conserved and vanishes exactly at \((q_{0},0)\) near it, both
terms being non-negative and the kinetic one positive definite.

Let \(U\) be a neighbourhood of \((q_{0},0)\); shrink it to a product
\(\overline{B}(q_{0},r)\times\overline{B}(0,\rho)\) contained in \(U\)
and inside the region where \(V>0\) off \(q_{0}\). The boundary
\(\Sigma\) of that product is compact and misses \((q_{0},0)\), so the
continuous \(E\) attains on it a minimum \(\eta>0\). Let
\(W = \setst{(q,\dot{q}) \in U}{E(q,\dot{q})<\eta}\), an open set
containing \((q_{0},0)\) since \(E\) is continuous and vanishes there.

A motion starting in \(W\) keeps \(E<\eta\) for all time by
conservation, so it can never reach \(\Sigma\); being continuous and
starting inside, it remains in the product, hence in \(U\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-lagrange-dirichlet, prop-equilibria-are-critical-points, thm-spectral-theorem
#+NAME: prop-instability-from-a-maximum
#+begin_proposition
If \(V\) is \(C^{2}\) and its Hessian at a critical point \(q_{0}\) has
a negative eigenvalue, then \(q_{0}\) is unstable.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Diagonalise the Hessian by the spectral theorem and let \(v\) be a unit
eigenvector with eigenvalue \(-\mu<0\). Along the line
\(q_{0}+sv\), Taylor's theorem gives
\(V(q_{0}+sv) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu s^{2}+o(s^{2})\), so \(V<0\) on a
punctured segment. Take an initial condition on that segment with zero
velocity and energy \(E = V(q(0))<0\). Since the kinetic term is
non-negative, \(V(q(t)) \le E<0\) for all time, so the motion can never
return to any neighbourhood of \(q_{0}\) on which \(V \ge E/2\) -- and
such a neighbourhood exists by continuity of \(V\) at \(q_{0}\), where
\(V = 0\). Hence motions starting arbitrarily close to the equilibrium
leave a fixed neighbourhood.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The converse of Lagrange-Dirichlet is false in general -- an equilibrium
can be stable at a non-strict minimum, and the question of whether a
critical point that is not a minimum is always unstable was open for a
century and is settled only under regularity hypotheses. What the proof
does show is the mechanism: the conserved energy is a function
positive away from the equilibrium and non-increasing along motions, and
abstracting exactly that gives [[id:5bd80b77-add7-48ce-9967-0a71a33bfc00][Lyapunov's method]] for systems with no
energy at all.
#+end_remark
