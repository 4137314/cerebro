#+TITLE: Splitting fields and their uniqueness
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:field_theory:
#+MSC: 12F05
#+HASH: 083ef2f33403

A polynomial with no root can be given one, by a construction that costs
nothing: quotient the polynomial ring by an irreducible factor. Iterating
produces a field in which the polynomial factors completely, and the
substance of this note is that the field so produced does not depend on
the choices made -- which is what makes "the" splitting field, and with
it the Galois group, well defined.

#+DEPENDS: def-field-extension, thm-minimal-polynomial, thm-maximal-iff-quotient-is-a-field
#+NAME: thm-kronecker
#+begin_theorem
Let \(K\) be a field and \(f \in K[x]\) non-constant. Then there is an
extension \(L/K\) with \([L:K] \le \deg f\) in which \(f\) has a root.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(p\) be an irreducible factor of \(f\), which exists because
\(K[x]\) is a unique factorisation domain. The ideal \((p)\) is maximal,
so \(L = K[x]/(p)\) is a field; the composite \(K \to K[x] \to L\) is a
ring homomorphism between fields, hence injective, so \(L\) is an
extension of \(K\). The class \(\alpha\) of \(x\) satisfies
\(p(\alpha) = 0\), hence \(f(\alpha) = 0\), and
\([L:K] = \deg p \le \deg f\) by the description of a simple algebraic
extension.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-kronecker, thm-tower-law
#+NAME: def-splitting-field
#+begin_definition
Let \(f \in K[x]\) be non-constant. An extension \(L/K\) is a
/splitting field/ of \(f\) over \(K\) if \(f\) factors into linear
factors in \(L[x]\) and \(L\) is generated over \(K\) by the roots of
\(f\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-splitting-field, thm-kronecker, thm-tower-law
#+NAME: thm-splitting-field-exists
#+begin_theorem
Every non-constant \(f \in K[x]\) has a splitting field \(L\), with
\([L:K] \le (\deg f)!\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Induct on \(n = \deg f\). For \(n = 1\), \(K\) itself works. Otherwise
Kronecker's theorem gives \(K_{1} = K(\alpha)\) with \(f(\alpha) = 0\)
and \([K_{1}:K]\le n\); in \(K_{1}[x]\) we may write
\(f = (x-\alpha)g\) with \(\deg g = n - 1\), by the factor theorem. By
induction \(g\) has a splitting field \(L\) over \(K_{1}\) with
\([L:K_{1}] \le (n-1)!\). Then \(f\) splits in \(L\), and \(L\) is
generated over \(K\) by \(\alpha\) and the roots of \(g\), which are the
roots of \(f\). The tower law gives \([L:K] \le n \cdot (n-1)! = n!\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-minimal-polynomial, thm-first-isomorphism-rings
#+NAME: lem-extension-of-an-isomorphism
#+begin_lemma
Let \(\sigma \colon K \to K'\) be a field isomorphism, extended to
\(K[x]\to K'[x]\) coefficientwise, let \(p \in K[x]\) be irreducible and
\(p' = \sigma(p)\). If \(\alpha\) is a root of \(p\) in some extension
of \(K\) and \(\alpha'\) a root of \(p'\) in some extension of \(K'\),
then \(\sigma\) extends to an isomorphism
\(K(\alpha)\to K'(\alpha')\) sending \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha'\).
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
The coefficientwise map is a ring isomorphism \(K[x]\to K'[x]\) carrying
\((p)\) onto \((p')\), so it induces an isomorphism
\(K[x]/(p) \to K'[x]/(p')\) of the quotients. By the description of a
simple algebraic extension, \(K[x]/(p) \iso K(\alpha)\) by sending the
class of \(x\) to \(\alpha\), and likewise on the other side. Composing
the three isomorphisms gives the claim; it restricts to \(\sigma\) on
\(K\) and sends \(\alpha\) to \(\alpha'\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: lem-extension-of-an-isomorphism, thm-splitting-field-exists, def-splitting-field
#+NAME: thm-splitting-field-is-unique
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\sigma \colon K\to K'\) be an isomorphism, \(f \in K[x]\)
non-constant and \(f' = \sigma(f)\). Any splitting field \(L\) of \(f\)
over \(K\) and any splitting field \(L'\) of \(f'\) over \(K'\) are
isomorphic by an isomorphism extending \(\sigma\). In particular the
splitting field of \(f\) over \(K\) is unique up to a \(K\)-isomorphism.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Induct on \([L:K]\). If \([L:K] = 1\) then \(f\) already splits in
\(K\), so \(f'\) splits in \(K'\) and \(L' = K'\); \(\sigma\) itself
serves.

Otherwise some irreducible factor \(p\) of \(f\) in \(K[x]\) has degree
at least \(2\). Let \(\alpha \in L\) be a root of \(p\) and
\(\alpha'\in L'\) a root of \(p' = \sigma(p)\), which exists because
\(p'\) divides \(f'\) and \(f'\) splits in \(L'\). By the lemma
\(\sigma\) extends to \(\tau \colon K(\alpha)\to K'(\alpha')\). Now
\(L\) is a splitting field of \(f\) over \(K(\alpha)\) and \(L'\) one of
\(f'\) over \(K'(\alpha')\), while
\([L : K(\alpha)] = [L:K]/\deg p < [L:K]\) by the tower law. The
induction hypothesis extends \(\tau\) to an isomorphism
\(L \to L'\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The same induction, run with \(K = K'\) and \(\sigma = \id\), counts the
\(K\)-automorphisms of \(L\): at each step the extension can send
\(\alpha\) to any root of its minimal polynomial, so the number of
automorphisms is \([L:K]\) exactly when every such polynomial has
distinct roots. That condition is separability, and the count is the
first half of the Galois correspondence.

Iterating the construction over all polynomials at once produces an
algebraically closed extension -- an algebraic closure -- but the
iteration is transfinite and needs Zorn's lemma, since there is no
bound on the degrees involved.
#+end_remark
