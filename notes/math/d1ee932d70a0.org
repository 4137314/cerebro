#+TITLE: Least squares and the Gauss-Markov theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:statistics:
#+MSC: 62J05, 15A06
#+HASH: d1ee932d70a0

Fitting a linear model to data is not a statistical idea but a
geometric one: the fitted values are the [[id:c2edf7f5-d5ac-4e50-8734-a00b2da22cf4][orthogonal projection]] of the
observations onto the space the model can produce, and the normal
equations are that projection written in coordinates. Statistics enters
only afterwards, to say in what sense the resulting estimator is best.

#+DEPENDS: def-random-variable, def-variance-and-covariance, def-linear-map
#+NAME: def-linear-model
#+begin_definition
A /linear model/ is \(Y = A\beta + \eps\), where \(A\) is a known
\(n \times p\) matrix of rank \(p\), \(\beta \in \R^{p}\) is unknown,
and the error \(\eps\) is a random vector with \(\E[\eps] = 0\) and
covariance matrix \(\sigma^{2}I\) -- the components uncorrelated with
common variance. The observation is the random vector \(Y \in \R^{n}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-linear-model, thm-orthogonal-decomposition, cor-orthogonal-projection-is-nearest
#+NAME: thm-normal-equations
#+begin_theorem
The function \(b \mapsto \norm{Y - Ab}^{2}\) has a unique minimiser,
\(\hat{\beta} = (A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}A^{\mathsf{T}}Y\), characterised
by the /normal equations/
\(A^{\mathsf{T}}A\hat{\beta} = A^{\mathsf{T}}Y\); and
\(A\hat{\beta}\) is the orthogonal projection of \(Y\) onto the column
space of \(A\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The column space \(V = \im A\) is a subspace of \(\R^{n}\), so
\(\R^{n} = V \oplus V^{\perp}\) and the nearest point of \(V\) to \(Y\)
is its orthogonal projection \(PY\), unique. A vector \(Ab\) equals
\(PY\) exactly when \(Y - Ab \perp V\), that is
\(A^{\mathsf{T}}(Y-Ab) = 0\), which is the normal equations. Since
\(A\) has rank \(p\), \(A^{\mathsf{T}}A\) is invertible -- if
\(A^{\mathsf{T}}Ab = 0\) then \(\norm{Ab}^{2} = b^{\mathsf{T}}A^{\mathsf{T}}Ab = 0\),
so \(Ab = 0\) and \(b = 0\) by injectivity of \(A\) -- and the solution
is the displayed formula, unique because \(b \mapsto Ab\) is injective.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-normal-equations, def-linear-model, prop-properties-of-expectation
#+NAME: prop-least-squares-is-unbiased
#+begin_proposition
\(\E[\hat{\beta}] = \beta\), and the covariance matrix of
\(\hat{\beta}\) is \(\sigma^{2}(A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Write \(M = (A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}A^{\mathsf{T}}\), so
\(\hat{\beta} = MY = M(A\beta+\eps) = \beta + M\eps\), using
\(MA = I\). Taking expectations componentwise and using
\(\E[\eps] = 0\) gives unbiasedness. The covariance of \(M\eps\) is
\(M(\sigma^{2}I)M^{\mathsf{T}} = \sigma^{2}MM^{\mathsf{T}} = \sigma^{2}(A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}\),
by bilinearity of covariance and the identity
\(MM^{\mathsf{T}} = (A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}A^{\mathsf{T}}A(A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-least-squares-is-unbiased, def-linear-model, prop-variance-identities
#+NAME: thm-gauss-markov
#+begin_theorem
Let \(c \in \R^{p}\). Among all estimators of \(c^{\mathsf{T}}\beta\)
that are linear in \(Y\) and unbiased for every \(\beta\), the estimator
\(c^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{\beta}\) has the smallest variance.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
A linear estimator is \(a^{\mathsf{T}}Y\) for some \(a \in \R^{n}\), and
unbiasedness for every \(\beta\) says
\(a^{\mathsf{T}}A\beta = c^{\mathsf{T}}\beta\) for all \(\beta\), that
is \(A^{\mathsf{T}}a = c\). Write \(a = a_{0} + d\) with
\(a_{0} = AM^{\mathsf{T}}c\) the vector giving
\(c^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{\beta} = a_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}Y\); then
\(A^{\mathsf{T}}a_{0} = A^{\mathsf{T}}A(A^{\mathsf{T}}A)^{-1}c = c\), so
\(A^{\mathsf{T}}d = 0\), that is \(d \perp \im A\), and in particular
\(a_{0}^{\mathsf{T}}d = c^{\mathsf{T}}M d = 0\) since \(a_{0} \in \im A\).

The variance of \(a^{\mathsf{T}}Y\) is
\(\sigma^{2}\norm{a}^{2} = \sigma^{2}(\norm{a_{0}}^{2}+\norm{d}^{2})\)
by Pythagoras, which exceeds
\(\sigma^{2}\norm{a_{0}}^{2} = \Var(c^{\mathsf{T}}\hat{\beta})\) unless
\(d = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
No distributional assumption appears anywhere above: the errors are not
assumed normal, only uncorrelated with equal variances, and the
conclusion is optimality within the /linear/ unbiased estimators. Both
restrictions matter. Drop equal variances and the optimal estimator is
the weighted one; assume normality and the conclusion strengthens to
optimality among all unbiased estimators, by the [[id:8cf8a109-5256-46f2-9553-c27e2de0a34e][Cramer-Rao]] bound.
#+end_remark
