#+TITLE: Continuous maps, homeomorphisms and topological invariants
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54C05
#+HASH: b63830dd97b3

Continuity was defined with \(\eps\) and \(\delta\), then shown to mean
that preimages of open sets are open. Only the second formulation
survives the loss of the metric, so it becomes the definition -- and it
is a better one, because it makes continuity manifestly about the two
topologies and nothing else. A homeomorphism is then the notion of
sameness the subject is organised around.

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space, def-continuity-at-point
#+NAME: def-continuous-map
#+begin_definition
A map \(f \colon X \to Y\) between topological spaces is /continuous/ if
\(f^{-1}(V)\) is open in \(X\) for every open \(V \subseteq Y\), and
/continuous at/ \(x \in X\) if for every neighbourhood \(V\) of
\(f(x)\) there is a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\) with
\(f(U) \subseteq V\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-continuous-map, thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+NAME: thm-characterisations-of-continuity
#+begin_theorem
For \(f \colon X \to Y\) the following are equivalent: (i) \(f\) is
continuous; (ii) \(f\) is continuous at every point; (iii) the preimage
of every closed set is closed; (iv)
\(f(\overline{A}) \subseteq \overline{f(A)}\) for every
\(A \subseteq X\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
(i) implies (ii): given \(V\) a neighbourhood of \(f(x)\), take an open
\(V_{0}\) with \(f(x) \in V_{0} \subseteq V\); then
\(U = f^{-1}(V_{0})\) is open, contains \(x\), and
\(f(U) \subseteq V\). (ii) implies (i): let \(V\) be open and
\(x \in f^{-1}(V)\); continuity at \(x\) gives a neighbourhood \(U_{x}\)
of \(x\) with \(f(U_{x}) \subseteq V\), so \(f^{-1}(V)\) contains an
open set around each of its points and is therefore open.

(i) is equivalent to (iii) because \(f^{-1}(Y\setminus C) = X \setminus f^{-1}(C)\).

(iii) implies (iv): \(f^{-1}(\overline{f(A)})\) is closed and contains
\(A\), hence contains \(\overline{A}\), which is (iv). (iv) implies
(iii): let \(C \subseteq Y\) be closed and \(A = f^{-1}(C)\). Then
\(f(\overline{A}) \subseteq \overline{f(A)} \subseteq \overline{C} = C\),
so \(\overline{A} \subseteq f^{-1}(C) = A\) and \(A\) is closed.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-continuous-map, def-basis-of-a-topology, prop-intersection-of-topologies
#+NAME: prop-continuity-tested-on-a-subbasis
#+begin_proposition
If \(\mathcal{S}\) is a subbasis of the topology of \(Y\), then \(f\) is
continuous as soon as \(f^{-1}(S)\) is open for every
\(S \in \mathcal{S}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Preimage commutes with unions and intersections:
\(f^{-1}(\bigcup_{i}V_{i}) = \bigcup_{i}f^{-1}(V_{i})\) and likewise for
intersections. Every open set of \(Y\) is a union of finite
intersections of members of \(\mathcal{S}\), so its preimage is a union
of finite intersections of open sets, hence open.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-continuous-map
#+NAME: prop-composition-of-continuous-maps
#+begin_proposition
A composition of continuous maps is continuous, and every constant map
and every identity map is continuous.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
\((g\circ f)^{-1}(W) = f^{-1}(g^{-1}(W))\), open as the preimage under
\(f\) of an open set. For a constant map the preimage of any set is
\(\emptyset\) or \(X\); for the identity it is the set itself.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-characterisations-of-continuity, prop-closed-sets-of-a-topology
#+NAME: prop-pasting-lemma
#+begin_proposition
Let \(X = C_{1}\cup C_{2}\) with \(C_{1}, C_{2}\) closed, and let
\(f_{i} \colon C_{i} \to Y\) be continuous and agree on
\(C_{1}\cap C_{2}\). Then the map \(f\) they define on \(X\) is
continuous.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
The two restrictions agree on the overlap, so \(f\) is well defined. For
\(C \subseteq Y\) closed,
\(f^{-1}(C) = f_{1}^{-1}(C) \cup f_{2}^{-1}(C)\). Each \(f_{i}^{-1}(C)\)
is closed in \(C_{i}\), hence closed in \(X\) because \(C_{i}\) is; so
\(f^{-1}(C)\) is closed as a union of two closed sets, and \(f\) is
continuous.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-composition-of-continuous-maps, prop-bijective-iff-invertible
#+NAME: def-homeomorphism
#+begin_definition
A /homeomorphism/ is a continuous bijection whose inverse is
continuous; two spaces are /homeomorphic/ if one exists between them. A
property of spaces preserved by homeomorphism is a /topological
invariant/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-homeomorphism, thm-characterisations-of-continuity
#+NAME: prop-homeomorphism-criteria
#+begin_proposition
A continuous bijection \(f \colon X \to Y\) is a homeomorphism if and
only if it is /open/ (images of open sets are open), if and only if it
is /closed/ (images of closed sets are closed). Being homeomorphic is an
equivalence relation on any set of spaces.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For a bijection, the image under \(f\) of a set equals its preimage
under \(f^{-1}\); so continuity of \(f^{-1}\) is exactly the statement
that \(f\) maps open sets to open sets, and by complementation that it
maps closed sets to closed sets. The identity is a homeomorphism, the
inverse of one is one, and a composition of two is one by the previous
proposition -- reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism, and the failure is
not pathological: the identity from a set with the discrete topology to
the same set with any coarser one is continuous and bijective in one
direction only. Compactness plus the Hausdorff property is the standard
hypothesis that rules this out, which is why so many theorems end
"...hence a homeomorphism" after checking those two.
#+end_remark
