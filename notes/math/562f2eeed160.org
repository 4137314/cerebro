The largest part of the archive, and the one whose order matters most:
almost every note here is used by the next. The sequence below is that
order. It has one turning point -- the completeness axiom -- and
everything before it is definitions, everything after it is consequences.

Part of [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][Analysis]].

* The line

[[id:3df902f9-c527-40c3-994a-78fa87ef1063][Supremum and infimum]] is the definition that makes the axiom
statable; [[id:a8f6ea88-2ae8-479a-bbbd-729e7e40ae78][completeness of the real numbers]] is the axiom, and the only
thing separating \(\R\) from \(\Q\). Its first two consequences are
[[id:58132cb5-dd59-4713-b746-80a740fd394a][the Archimedean property and the density of the rationals]]; [[id:4d0d08b6-52bf-48b5-90c3-8b075bae3dcf][the absolute
value and the triangle inequality]] is the estimate every later proof
splits through.

* Sequences

[[id:43670a22-2d73-4d27-af1e-f2367c3d5476][Limit of a sequence]], then [[id:e7861508-66ab-4d49-b445-48fd09e4bca6][the algebra of limits]], which is what makes
limits computable. Then the three ways completeness produces one:
[[id:902b2920-a465-45dc-91a9-01af8e7b7c2d][monotone convergence]], [[id:db92d316-8a78-4116-8efb-89274901fdcd][Bolzano-Weierstrass]] and [[id:03e16e45-1d12-46e1-af18-9f149478b146][the Cauchy
criterion]] -- the last being the one that mentions no order and
therefore travels to [[id:ab5041dc-f782-4348-8303-01facc0b23d1][metric spaces]]. [[id:6e7af92b-7edd-4621-82f5-251a54793e81][Limit superior and limit
inferior]] exist for every bounded sequence, which is why they are the
right tool when convergence is what one is trying to prove.

* Series

[[id:eb17807e-bad5-473d-a2ec-a48309679afd][Series and convergence of the partial sums]] reduces series to
sequences, and everything after it is a criterion:
[[id:d567eeaa-269b-48eb-a081-2c75f706705f][the Cauchy criterion, the harmonic series and condensation]],
[[id:4187e02b-9197-43e5-816b-c6e8cd98425b][absolute convergence and the comparison test]], [[id:845f24c9-1f1d-4d2d-8cc1-1f7f81ab3647][the ratio and root
tests]]. [[id:5c4f48b7-803d-473b-b6f6-52e566f545f4][The alternating series test]] is the one that uses cancellation
rather than absolute values, and hence the source of every conditionally
convergent example. [[id:ffb603ac-6356-4b5a-8177-e24740c166b3][Power series and the radius of convergence]] is where
series stop being numbers and become functions.

* Continuity

[[id:1e47346b-a210-4ba3-aab7-4cc74328035e][Limits and continuity]] and [[id:d6f8d660-acf1-44ff-bd97-3b1164e69d7a][the limit of a function and its sequential
characterisation]] -- the second is what lets every theorem about
sequences be spent twice. Then [[id:827fb337-7e3b-4d0d-b912-8bf5bcc121bb][the algebra of continuous functions and
composition]], [[id:da136910-2118-46eb-a4ff-00876ad862e2][one-sided limits and the discontinuities of a monotone
function]].

The three theorems on an interval are where completeness reappears:
[[id:f0cf6f7b-7984-4e52-9ef1-6fb39bf8058b][the intermediate value theorem]], [[id:0951689a-630a-41a9-9a31-a61b2f129444][continuous functions on a compact
interval]] (bounded, and attaining their bounds), and [[id:5481ff0d-b2c8-4368-b2c9-3a0206dead8b][uniform continuity
and the Heine-Cantor theorem]]. [[id:e22b9c51-2503-4228-8e3e-0e669e374fa4][The continuous image of an interval and
the continuous inverse]] is the pair of statements that becomes, in
[[id:830a0aed-a379-4350-bd58-f776af9b87d7][topology]], "connectedness and compactness are preserved by continuous
maps".

* Differentiation

[[id:f9557e4d-84b0-4e19-942a-c87d3aaa533f][The derivative]] and [[id:d6339989-3988-4376-a81c-e2381e5e62b2][the rules of differentiation]], then the one
theorem the subject rests on: [[id:26cf8966-f610-4802-b854-ee7810173459][the mean value theorem]]. Its consequences
are [[id:d0a81ce3-163c-4582-8001-fda678f92371][monotonicity and the sign of the derivative]], [[id:00d208c3-0224-421a-a882-f9ad1627121f][Cauchy's mean value
theorem and de l'Hopital's rule]], and [[id:6b357bea-f9ad-4f0d-8fd8-013365fcee38][convex functions and the second
derivative]].

Taylor's theorem appears twice on purpose, because the two remainders
answer different questions: [[id:d6c0b313-33dd-4059-8ccc-6ed4d98093de][the Peano remainder]] is a local statement
about the order of the error, [[id:03d0aa48-fd59-4965-91e2-5dafe84ce087][the Lagrange remainder]] a global one with
a value, and [[id:7a318571-63c2-4816-a1ff-f05dce63deab][uniform bounds on the Taylor remainder]] is what turns
either into a convergent series.

* Integration

[[id:29d7ea7b-5f6b-48e4-949b-553595dfa9a3][The Riemann integral]] by upper and lower sums; [[id:c46a4920-d788-459b-a3ff-c8d736c6a605][linearity, additivity
and monotonicity]]; [[id:01edf0f1-4a90-46e1-9404-7577de287554][monotone functions are integrable, and the mean value
theorem for integrals]]. [[id:40e468eb-db21-4321-8a3d-8ef0ec032875][The fundamental theorem of calculus]] is the
statement that the two operations of the subject are inverse, and
[[id:ffb30124-1e7a-490f-97f7-dd6ecbc43e7f][integration by parts and substitution]] is that theorem read backwards as
a technique.

* Limits of functions, and several variables

[[id:0e79d289-c68d-4e86-824e-4dfa51bcad21][Pointwise and uniform convergence]] is the note that separates the two
modes and explains which theorems survive each. In several variables,
[[id:369b032a-ef2e-4959-b8aa-412b6c807220][differentiability]] is defined by approximation by a linear map -- not
by partial derivatives, which is the distinction the subject exists to
make -- and [[id:9c3c5513-7bb1-4ee1-b4b4-c04f875772f4][extrema and the second differential]] is the second-order
test. [[id:9df53a2d-7920-4887-96c2-72a1271929c8][The Legendre transform]] is filed here for its convexity, and used
in [[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mechanics]].

* Beyond one variable

The second-year continuation is filed under [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][Analysis]] rather than here:
[[id:b6f64ed6-db0e-41c8-a093-0df725e22ecf][the inverse and implicit function theorems]],
[[id:07b82d71-2c7c-4ddd-84ce-185047ee07e5][Lagrange multipliers]], [[id:b9ad54df-4188-44c8-bc78-494d9a6728c8][Fubini's theorem]], and the
[[id:ef0a7e9f-5957-4e21-8f97-59e80b04aa33][divergence and Stokes theorems]].

* What is missing

Rearrangement of series; the Riemann criterion in terms of the measure
of the discontinuity set, which would connect this subject to
[[id:9910fd2f-5f5e-4be0-8785-c2b9904dc001][measure theory]]; and the elementary functions -- the exponential,
logarithm and trigonometric functions are used throughout the archive
and defined nowhere in it.
