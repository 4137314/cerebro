#+TITLE: The algebra of limits
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 40A05
#+HASH: 2ed11dd8b1ca

A first course proves a handful of limits from the definition and then
never does so again: from here on limits are computed by combining ones
already known. These are the rules that make that legitimate, and the
reason they are theorems rather than notation is that each one needs
the triangle inequality and a boundedness argument to hold at all.

#+DEPENDS: def-sequence-limit, def-bounded-sequence, thm-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: prop-convergent-is-bounded
#+begin_proposition
Every convergent sequence is bounded.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \(a_{n} \to L\) and take \(\eps = 1\): there is \(N\) with
\(\abs{a_{n} - L} < 1\) for \(n \ge N\), hence
\(\abs{a_{n}} \le \abs{L} + 1\) for those \(n\). Then
\(C = \max(\abs{a_{1}}, \dots, \abs{a_{N-1}}, \abs{L} + 1)\) bounds every
term.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-convergent-is-bounded, thm-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: thm-limit-algebra
#+begin_theorem
Let \(a_{n} \to L\) and \(b_{n} \to M\). Then
\(a_{n} + b_{n} \to L + M\), \(a_{n} b_{n} \to LM\), and, provided
\(M \ne 0\) and \(b_{n} \ne 0\) for all \(n\),
\(a_{n}/b_{n} \to L/M\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
/Sum./ Given \(\eps > 0\), choose \(N\) beyond which both
\(\abs{a_{n} - L} < \eps/2\) and \(\abs{b_{n} - M} < \eps/2\); the
triangle inequality gives
\(\abs{(a_{n} + b_{n}) - (L + M)} < \eps\).

/Product./ Write
\(a_{n} b_{n} - LM = a_{n}(b_{n} - M) + M(a_{n} - L)\). The sequence
\((a_{n})\) is bounded, say by \(C > 0\), by the proposition above.
Given \(\eps > 0\), choose \(N\) beyond which
\(\abs{b_{n} - M} < \eps / (2C)\) and
\(\abs{a_{n} - L} < \eps / (2(\abs{M} + 1))\). Then
\[ \abs{a_{n} b_{n} - LM} \le C \abs{b_{n} - M} + \abs{M} \abs{a_{n} - L} < \frac{\eps}{2} + \frac{\eps}{2} = \eps. \]

/Quotient./ It suffices to prove \(1/b_{n} \to 1/M\) and apply the
product rule. Since \(\abs{M} > 0\), there is \(N_{0}\) with
\(\abs{b_{n} - M} < \abs{M}/2\) for \(n \ge N_{0}\), whence
\(\abs{b_{n}} > \abs{M}/2\) by the reverse triangle inequality. For
\(n \ge N_{0}\),
\[ \Bigl| \frac{1}{b_{n}} - \frac{1}{M} \Bigr| = \frac{\abs{M - b_{n}}}{\abs{b_{n}} \abs{M}} \le \frac{2}{\abs{M}^{2}} \abs{b_{n} - M}, \]
and the right-hand side is below \(\eps\) for \(n\) large.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: prop-limits-preserve-order
#+begin_proposition
If \(a_{n} \to L\), \(b_{n} \to M\) and \(a_{n} \le b_{n}\) for all
\(n\), then \(L \le M\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(L > M\) and put \(\eps = (L - M)/2 > 0\). For large \(n\) both
\(a_{n} > L - \eps\) and \(b_{n} < M + \eps\), and
\(L - \eps = M + \eps\), so \(a_{n} > b_{n}\) -- a contradiction.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-limits-preserve-order, def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: thm-squeeze
#+begin_theorem
Let \(a_{n} \le c_{n} \le b_{n}\) for all \(n\), with
\(a_{n} \to L\) and \(b_{n} \to L\). Then \(c_{n} \to L\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Given \(\eps > 0\), choose \(N\) beyond which
\(\abs{a_{n} - L} < \eps\) and \(\abs{b_{n} - L} < \eps\). For those
\(n\),
\(L - \eps < a_{n} \le c_{n} \le b_{n} < L + \eps\), so
\(\abs{c_{n} - L} < \eps\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Note what the order statement does /not/ say: strict inequality is not
preserved. Taking \(a_{n} = 0\) and \(b_{n} = 1/n\) gives
\(a_{n} < b_{n}\) for every \(n\) and equal limits. A limit is a
statement about the tail, and strictness is exactly the property a tail
can lose.
#+end_remark
