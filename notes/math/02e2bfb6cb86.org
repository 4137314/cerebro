#+TITLE: Homotopy equivalence, retracts and contractible spaces
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:algebraic_topology:
#+MSC: 55P10
#+HASH: 02e2bfb6cb86

Homeomorphism is too fine an equivalence for the invariants of algebraic
topology: a disc and a point are not homeomorphic and no functor built
from loops can tell them apart. Homotopy equivalence is the coarser
relation those invariants actually see, and recognising it is usually a
matter of contracting a space onto a subspace without tearing it.

#+DEPENDS: def-path-homotopy, def-continuous-map, def-product-topology
#+NAME: def-homotopy-of-maps
#+begin_definition
Two continuous maps \(f, g \colon X \to Y\) are /homotopic/, written
\(f \simeq g\), if there is a continuous
\(H \colon X\times[0,1]\to Y\) with \(H(\cdot,0) = f\) and
\(H(\cdot,1) = g\). A map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is a /homotopy
equivalence/ if there is \(g \colon Y \to X\) with
\(g\circ f \simeq \id_{X}\) and \(f \circ g \simeq \id_{Y}\); the
spaces are then /homotopy equivalent/. A space homotopy equivalent to a
point is /contractible/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-homotopy-of-maps, prop-pasting-lemma, thm-equivalence-partition
#+NAME: prop-homotopy-is-an-equivalence-relation
#+begin_proposition
Homotopy is an equivalence relation on the continuous maps \(X \to Y\),
and it is compatible with composition: \(f\simeq f'\) and
\(g \simeq g'\) imply \(g\circ f \simeq g'\circ f'\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Reflexivity uses \(H(x,t) = f(x)\); symmetry, \(H(x,1-t)\);
transitivity, the concatenation \(H(x,2t)\) for \(t\le1/2\) and
\(K(x,2t-1)\) for \(t\ge1/2\), continuous by the pasting lemma since
both formulas give \(g(x)\) at \(t = 1/2\).

For compatibility, if \(H\) is a homotopy from \(f\) to \(f'\) then
\(g\circ H\) is one from \(g f\) to \(g f'\); and if \(K\) is one from
\(g\) to \(g'\) then \((x,t)\mapsto K(f'(x),t)\) is one from \(gf'\) to
\(g'f'\). Composing the two homotopies by transitivity gives the claim.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-homotopy-of-maps, prop-homotopy-is-an-equivalence-relation
#+NAME: def-deformation-retract
#+begin_definition
Let \(A \subseteq X\). A /retraction/ is a continuous
\(r \colon X \to A\) with \(r\vert_{A} = \id_{A}\); it is a
/deformation retraction/ if moreover \(\iota\circ r \simeq \id_{X}\),
where \(\iota\) is the inclusion. \(A\) is then a /deformation
retract/ of \(X\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-deformation-retract, def-homotopy-of-maps
#+NAME: prop-deformation-retract-is-a-homotopy-equivalence
#+begin_proposition
If \(A\) is a deformation retract of \(X\) then the inclusion
\(\iota \colon A \to X\) is a homotopy equivalence.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Take \(g = r\). Then \(r\circ\iota = \id_{A}\) exactly, and
\(\iota\circ r\simeq\id_{X}\) by hypothesis.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-homotopy-of-maps, def-convex-set, prop-deformation-retract-is-a-homotopy-equivalence
#+NAME: prop-convex-sets-are-contractible
#+begin_proposition
A convex subset \(C\) of \(\R^{n}\) is contractible; more generally any
two maps into \(C\) are homotopic.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For continuous \(f, g \colon X \to C\), the straight-line homotopy
\(H(x,t) = (1-t)f(x)+tg(x)\) takes values in \(C\) by convexity and is
continuous, being built from continuous operations. Taking \(g\)
constant and \(X = C\), \(f = \id\), shows the identity of \(C\) is
homotopic to a constant, which together with the maps to and from a
point gives the homotopy equivalence.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-homotopy-is-an-equivalence-relation, prop-functoriality-of-the-fundamental-group, def-homotopy-of-maps
#+NAME: thm-homotopy-invariance-of-the-fundamental-group
#+begin_theorem
If \(f \colon X \to Y\) is a homotopy equivalence and \(x_{0}\in X\),
then \(f_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\to\pi_{1}(Y,f(x_{0}))\) is an
isomorphism. In particular a contractible space has trivial fundamental
group, and homotopy equivalent path-connected spaces have isomorphic
fundamental groups.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
First, homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism up to the change of
basepoint: if \(H\) is a homotopy from \(u\) to \(v\) and \(\alpha\) is
a loop at \(x_{0}\), then \((s,t)\mapsto H(\alpha(s),t)\) is a homotopy
of loops except that the basepoint traces the path
\(\gamma(t) = H(x_{0},t)\); composing with \(\gamma\) at both ends shows
\(v_{*}[\alpha] = [\overline{\gamma}]\,u_{*}[\alpha]\,[\gamma]\), so
\(v_{*}\) is \(u_{*}\) followed by the change-of-basepoint isomorphism.

Now let \(g\) be a homotopy inverse of \(f\). Then
\(g_{*}f_{*} = (g f)_{*}\) equals \((\id_{X})_{*} = \id\) composed with
an isomorphism, hence is an isomorphism; so \(f_{*}\) is injective and
\(g_{*}\) surjective. Applying the same to \(f_{*}g_{*}\) shows
\(g_{*}\) is injective too, hence bijective, and then
\(f_{*} = g_{*}^{-1}\circ(g_{*}f_{*})\) is bijective.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The theorem is what makes the fundamental group computable: one replaces
a space by any homotopy equivalent one, and a punctured plane, an
annulus and a circle become the same problem. It also fixes the limits
of the invariant -- it cannot distinguish spaces that are homotopy
equivalent, so \(\R^{n}\) and a point have the same \(\pi_{1}\) for
every \(n\), and separating those requires the higher homotopy or
homology groups.
#+end_remark
