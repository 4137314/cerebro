#+TITLE: The residue theorem and the argument principle
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:complex_analysis:
#+MSC: 30E20, 30C15
#+HASH: 1a6c0b615eb1

Cauchy's theorem says a contour integral vanishes when there is nothing
inside. The residue theorem says what it is when there is: one number
per singularity, read off the Laurent expansion. That turns contour
integration into arithmetic, and it is the reason complex analysis is a
computational tool in [[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mathematical physics]] rather than only a
structural theory.

#+DEPENDS: def-isolated-singularity, thm-laurent-expansion, thm-primitive-on-a-convex-set
#+NAME: thm-residue
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open set containing a closed disc
\(\overline{B}\) except for finitely many points
\(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\) in its interior, and let \(C = \partial B\) be
traversed once anticlockwise. Then
\[ \int_{C} f(z)\dd z = 2\pi i \sum_{j=1}^{k}\Res(f,z_{j}). \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Choose disjoint small circles \(C_{j}\) around the \(z_{j}\), inside
\(B\). Cutting the region between \(C\) and the \(C_{j}\) into finitely
many pieces each lying in a convex open subset of the domain of
holomorphy, and cancelling the cuts, gives
\(\int_{C}f = \sum_{j}\int_{C_{j}}f\).

On a small circle about \(z_{j}\), expand \(f\) in its Laurent series,
which converges uniformly there, and integrate term by term. For
\(n \ne -1\) the function \((z-z_{j})^{n}\) has the primitive
\((z-z_{j})^{n+1}/(n+1)\) on the punctured plane, so its integral over
the closed curve vanishes; for \(n = -1\) the parametrisation
\(z = z_{j}+\rho e^{i\theta}\) gives \(2\pi i\). Hence
\(\int_{C_{j}}f = 2\pi i\,c_{-1} = 2\pi i \Res(f,z_{j})\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-residue, def-isolated-singularity, thm-riemann-removability
#+NAME: prop-computing-residues
#+begin_proposition
If \(f\) has a simple pole at \(z_{0}\), then
\(\Res(f,z_{0}) = \lim_{z\to z_{0}}(z-z_{0})f(z)\); if it has a pole of
order \(m\), then
\(\Res(f,z_{0}) = \frac{1}{(m-1)!}\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\frac{\dd^{m-1}}{\dd z^{m-1}}\bigl[(z-z_{0})^{m}f(z)\bigr]\).
If \(f = g/h\) with \(g,h\) holomorphic, \(h(z_{0}) = 0\),
\(h'(z_{0})\ne0\) and \(g(z_{0})\ne0\), then
\(\Res(f,z_{0}) = g(z_{0})/h'(z_{0})\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Write \(f(z) = \sum_{n\ge-m}c_{n}(z-z_{0})^{n}\). Then
\((z-z_{0})^{m}f(z)\) is holomorphic with power series
\(\sum_{k\ge0}c_{k-m}(z-z_{0})^{k}\), whose \((m-1)\)-st derivative at
\(z_{0}\) is \((m-1)!\,c_{-1}\); that is the displayed formula, and
\(m = 1\) is the first case.

For the quotient, \(h\) has a simple zero, so \(h(z) = (z-z_{0})h_{1}(z)\)
with \(h_{1}(z_{0}) = h'(z_{0})\ne0\); then
\((z-z_{0})f(z) = g(z)/h_{1}(z) \to g(z_{0})/h'(z_{0})\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-residue, thm-zeros-are-isolated, prop-computing-residues
#+NAME: thm-argument-principle
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open set containing \(\overline{B}\)
except for finitely many poles inside, with no zeros or poles on
\(C = \partial B\). Then
\[ \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\dd z = Z - P, \]
where \(Z\) and \(P\) count the zeros and the poles inside \(B\) with
multiplicity.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The function \(f'/f\) is holomorphic except at the zeros and poles of
\(f\). Near a zero of order \(m\), \(f(z) = (z-z_{0})^{m}g(z)\) with
\(g(z_{0})\ne0\), so
\(f'/f = \frac{m}{z-z_{0}} + \frac{g'}{g}\), whose second term is
holomorphic: the residue is \(m\). Near a pole of order \(m\) the same
computation with \(f = (z-z_{0})^{-m}g\) gives residue \(-m\). Summing
by the residue theorem gives \(Z-P\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-argument-principle, prop-standard-estimate
#+NAME: thm-rouche
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f, g\) be holomorphic on an open set containing
\(\overline{B}\), with \(\abs{g(z)}<\abs{f(z)}\) for every \(z\) on
\(C = \partial B\). Then \(f\) and \(f+g\) have the same number of
zeros in \(B\), counted with multiplicity.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Neither \(f\) nor \(f+g\) vanishes on \(C\), by the strict inequality.
For \(t \in [0,1]\) put \(h_{t} = f+tg\), which also does not vanish on
\(C\), and let
\(N(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{h_{t}'}{h_{t}}\dd z\), the number
of zeros of \(h_{t}\) in \(B\) by the argument principle. The
integrand depends continuously on \(t\), uniformly in \(z\) on the
compact \(C\), since \(\abs{h_{t}}\ge\abs{f}-\abs{g}>0\) there; hence
\(N\) is continuous. It takes integer values, and a continuous
integer-valued function on the connected \([0,1]\) is constant, so
\(N(0) = N(1)\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Rouche's theorem is how one locates roots without solving: perturbing a
polynomial by something smaller on a circle does not change how many
roots are inside, which gives the fundamental theorem of algebra again
in one line and is the standard tool for stability questions, where the
zeros of a characteristic function decide whether a solution grows.
#+end_remark
