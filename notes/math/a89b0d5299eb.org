#+TITLE: Rings and commutative algebra

A ring has two operations, and the whole subject is about their
interaction -- which is why the substructure one may divide by is not a
subring but an ideal, absorption rather than closure being what the
quotient needs. After that the questions are arithmetic: does
factorisation exist, is it unique, and is every ideal finitely
generated.

Part of [[id:504cda55-cfbf-42c2-b481-8d90660ec02a][Algebra]].

* The basic constructions

[[id:f32dae57-e863-4228-b002-ca6db443f05f][Rings, ideals and the quotient ring]]; then [[id:c9c12e48-ddd2-4359-917d-138e90df9416][integral domains, fields,
prime and maximal ideals]], where a property of an ideal is read off
from its quotient -- prime means the quotient is a domain, maximal means
it is a field. That dictionary is what makes the subject geometric, and
[[id:be042679-a939-4f2e-a061-ebd148ed1692][the Chinese remainder theorem]] is its first pay-off: comaximal ideals
split a ring into a product.

[[id:cb8c1dfe-ab80-4c47-bcda-ce71f8601866][Modules, quotients and exact sequences]] repeat the vector-space theory
over a ring, and record what is lost: without inverses a spanning set
need not contain a basis, so dimension disappears and exact sequences
take its place as the bookkeeping.

* Factorisation

[[id:950b13df-a19e-4dee-9050-7f4a5e546cd1][Polynomial rings, division and roots]] gives the division algorithm, and
with it the two facts everything else uses: the degree is additive over
a domain, and a polynomial of degree \(n\) has at most \(n\) roots.
[[id:94026fa9-c00a-4e36-845c-cadacd4d94ee][Euclidean domains, principal ideals and unique factorisation]] then
separates the three hypotheses that are usually run together --
Euclidean gives principal, principal makes irreducible elements prime,
primality gives uniqueness -- and the chain is strict at every step.

* Finiteness

[[id:e423d662-1f19-48be-a1f8-8bdfba79235b][Noetherian rings and the Hilbert basis theorem]] isolate the ascending
chain condition, which was already doing the work in the existence half
of unique factorisation, and prove that adjoining a variable preserves
it. The consequence -- any system of polynomial equations is equivalent
to a finite subsystem -- is what makes the objects of algebraic geometry
finite data.

* What is missing

Localisation, which is the other half of the dictionary between rings
and spaces; the Nullstellensatz; primary decomposition; integral
extensions and the going-up theorem; Dedekind domains, which is where
this subject meets number theory; and the structure theorem for
modules over a principal ideal domain.
