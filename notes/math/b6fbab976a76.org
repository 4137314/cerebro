#+TITLE: Smooth manifolds, smooth maps and the tangent space
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:differential_topology:
#+MSC: 58A05, 57R05
#+HASH: b6fbab976a76

Calculus needs coordinates and geometry must not depend on them. A
manifold resolves that by carrying coordinates only locally, with the
requirement that any two systems differ by a diffeomorphism -- so a
notion defined in coordinates is legitimate exactly when it is invariant
under those changes. The tangent space is the first such notion, and
defining it without coordinates is what makes the definition honest.

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space, def-hausdorff, def-homeomorphism, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-smooth-manifold
#+begin_definition
A /chart/ on a topological space \(M\) is a homeomorphism
\(\varphi \colon U \to \varphi(U) \subseteq \R^{n}\) from an open
\(U \subseteq M\) onto an open subset. Two charts are /compatible/ if
the transition map \(\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}\), defined on
\(\varphi(U\cap V)\), is smooth with smooth inverse. A /smooth
\(n\)-manifold/ is a Hausdorff space with a countable basis, together
with a family of pairwise compatible charts whose domains cover it and
which is maximal with those properties.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-smooth-manifold
#+NAME: def-smooth-map
#+begin_definition
A map \(f \colon M \to N\) between smooth manifolds is /smooth/ if for
every \(p \in M\) there are charts \(\varphi\) about \(p\) and \(\psi\)
about \(f(p)\) with \(\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}\) smooth where
defined. A /diffeomorphism/ is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse.
The smooth functions \(M \to \R\) form a ring \(C^{\infty}(M)\) under
pointwise operations.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-smooth-map, def-smooth-manifold
#+NAME: prop-smoothness-is-chart-independent
#+begin_proposition
If \(f\) is smooth in one pair of charts about \(p\) and \(f(p)\), it is
smooth in every such pair; and a composition of smooth maps is smooth.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \(\varphi',\psi'\) be other charts. Where all four are defined,
\(\psi'\circ f\circ\varphi'^{-1} = (\psi'\circ\psi^{-1})\circ(\psi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1})\circ(\varphi\circ\varphi'^{-1})\),
a composition of smooth maps between open subsets of Euclidean spaces --
the outer two being transition maps, smooth by compatibility. The chain
rule in \(\R^{n}\) makes the composite smooth. The same computation
proves the statement about compositions of maps of manifolds.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-smooth-map, thm-ftc-first, thm-chain-rule
#+NAME: lem-hadamard
#+begin_lemma
Let \(f\) be smooth on a convex open \(V \subseteq \R^{n}\) and
\(a \in V\). Then there are smooth \(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\) on \(V\) with
\[ f(x) = f(a) + \sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-a_{i})\,g_{i}(x), \qquad g_{i}(a) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a). \]
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
For \(x \in V\) the segment from \(a\) to \(x\) lies in \(V\), and
\(t \mapsto f(a + t(x-a))\) is smooth on \([0,1]\) with derivative
\(\sum_{i}(x_{i}-a_{i})\,\partial_{i}f(a+t(x-a))\) by the chain rule. By
the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\(f(x)-f(a) = \int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i}(x_{i}-a_{i})\partial_{i}f(a+t(x-a))\dd t\).
Put \(g_{i}(x) = \int_{0}^{1}\partial_{i}f(a+t(x-a))\dd t\), smooth in
\(x\) because the integrand is smooth in \((t,x)\), and evaluate at
\(x = a\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-smooth-map, lem-hadamard, def-vector-space
#+NAME: def-tangent-space
#+begin_definition
A /derivation at \(p \in M\)/ is a linear map
\(X \colon C^{\infty}(M)\to\R\) with
\(X(fg) = X(f)\,g(p) + f(p)\,X(g)\). The /tangent space/ \(T_{p}M\) is
the vector space of derivations at \(p\), under pointwise operations.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-tangent-space, lem-hadamard, thm-dimension-is-well-defined
#+NAME: thm-tangent-space-dimension
#+begin_theorem
Let \(M\) be a smooth \(n\)-manifold and \(\varphi\) a chart about
\(p\) with coordinates \(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\). Then the derivations
\(\partial/\partial x_{i}\vert_{p}\), sending \(f\) to
\(\partial_{i}(f\circ\varphi^{-1})(\varphi(p))\), form a basis of
\(T_{p}M\); in particular \(\dim T_{p}M = n\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
First, a derivation kills constants: \(X(1) = X(1\cdot1) = 2X(1)\), so
\(X(1) = 0\), and linearity extends this to all constants. Second, \(X\)
is local: if \(f\) vanishes on a neighbourhood of \(p\), pick a smooth
bump \(h\) that is \(1\) at \(p\) and supported where \(f = 0\); then
\(hf = 0\), so \(0 = X(hf) = X(h)f(p) + h(p)X(f) = X(f)\). Hence \(X\)
depends only on the germ of \(f\) at \(p\), and we may compute in a
chart, taking \(V = \varphi(U)\) convex without loss of generality.

Given \(X\), apply Hadamard's lemma to \(f\circ\varphi^{-1}\) at
\(a = \varphi(p)\) and pull back: \(f = f(p) + \sum_{i}(x_{i}-a_{i})g_{i}\)
near \(p\), where \(x_{i}\) denotes the \(i\)-th coordinate function.
Applying \(X\) and using that the constants die and that
\(x_{i}-a_{i}\) vanishes at \(p\),
\(X(f) = \sum_{i}X(x_{i})\,g_{i}(p) = \sum_{i}X(x_{i})\,\partial_{i}(f\circ\varphi^{-1})(a)\).
So the coordinate derivations span. They are independent: applying a
combination \(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\partial/\partial x_{i}\vert_{p}\) to
the coordinate function \(x_{j}\) gives \(\alpha_{j}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-tangent-space-dimension, def-linear-map, prop-smoothness-is-chart-independent
#+NAME: def-differential-of-a-smooth-map
#+begin_definition
For \(f \colon M \to N\) smooth and \(p \in M\), the /differential/
\(\dd f_{p}\colon T_{p}M \to T_{f(p)}N\) sends a derivation \(X\) to the
derivation \(h \mapsto X(h \circ f)\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-differential-of-a-smooth-map, thm-tangent-space-dimension
#+NAME: prop-chain-rule-on-manifolds
#+begin_proposition
\(\dd f_{p}\) is linear, \(\dd(\id_{M})_{p} = \id\), and
\(\dd(g\circ f)_{p} = \dd g_{f(p)}\circ \dd f_{p}\). In charts, its
matrix is the Jacobian of the coordinate representation of \(f\).
Consequently the differential of a diffeomorphism is an isomorphism, and
diffeomorphic manifolds have the same dimension.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
That \(X \mapsto X(\cdot\circ f)\) lands in derivations at \(f(p)\) is
the Leibniz rule for \(X\) applied to \((hk)\circ f = (h\circ f)(k\circ f)\);
linearity and the two functoriality identities are immediate from the
definition. Applying \(\dd f_{p}(\partial/\partial x_{i})\) to a
coordinate function \(y_{j}\) of \(N\) gives
\(\partial_{i}(y_{j}\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1})\), the Jacobian entry.
Applying functoriality to \(f^{-1}\circ f = \id\) shows
\(\dd f_{p}\) is invertible, so the two tangent spaces have equal
dimension.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The derivation definition buys coordinate-independence at the cost of
needing Hadamard's lemma, which is where smoothness genuinely enters: for
\(C^{k}\) functions with \(k\) finite the same space of derivations is
infinite-dimensional, and the theorem above fails. That is a good
illustration of a definition being correct only in the category it was
written for.
#+end_remark
