#+TITLE: A proof system for propositional logic: soundness and the deduction theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:foundations:proof_theory:
#+MSC: 03B05, 03F03
#+HASH: 22b748722399

Truth assignments say which formulas /are/ true; they say nothing about
what can be /derived/. Proof theory begins by writing down a derivation
relation as a finite combinatorial object and then comparing the two
notions. Soundness is the easy direction and is proved by induction on
derivations; that induction is the reason the axioms are chosen as
schemes rather than as a list.

#+DEPENDS: def-propositional-formula, def-truth-assignment
#+NAME: def-hilbert-system
#+begin_definition
Write \(\varphi \to \psi\) for \(\neg(\varphi\wedge\neg\psi)\). The
/axioms/ are all formulas of the six shapes
1. \(\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)\);
2. \(\bigl(\varphi\to(\psi\to\chi)\bigr) \to \bigl((\varphi\to\psi)\to(\varphi\to\chi)\bigr)\);
3. \((\neg\psi\to\neg\varphi)\to(\varphi\to\psi)\);
4. \((\varphi\wedge\psi)\to\varphi\);
5. \((\varphi\wedge\psi)\to\psi\);
6. \(\varphi\to\bigl(\psi\to(\varphi\wedge\psi)\bigr)\).
A /derivation/ of \(\varphi\) from a set \(\Sigma\) is a finite sequence
of formulas ending in \(\varphi\), each of which is an axiom, a member
of \(\Sigma\), or follows from two earlier ones by /modus ponens/ -- from
\(\chi\) and \(\chi\to\theta\), infer \(\theta\). One writes
\(\Sigma \vdash \varphi\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-system
#+NAME: prop-derivations-are-finitary
#+begin_proposition
If \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi\) then \(\Sigma_{0} \vdash \varphi\) for some
finite \(\Sigma_{0}\subseteq\Sigma\); and \(\vdash\) is monotone:
\(\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma'\) and \(\Sigma\vdash\varphi\) imply
\(\Sigma'\vdash\varphi\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
A derivation is a finite sequence, so it mentions finitely many members
of \(\Sigma\); those form the required \(\Sigma_{0}\), and the same
sequence is a derivation from it. Monotonicity holds because every
derivation from \(\Sigma\) is literally a derivation from \(\Sigma'\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-system, def-satisfiable, prop-induction-on-formulas
#+NAME: thm-soundness
#+begin_theorem
If \(\Sigma \vdash \varphi\) then \(\Sigma \models \varphi\): every
truth assignment satisfying all of \(\Sigma\) satisfies \(\varphi\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Unfolding the abbreviation, \(v \models \varphi\to\psi\) holds exactly
when \(v \not\models \varphi\) or \(v \models \psi\); this is the only
semantic fact used below.

Each axiom is satisfied by every assignment. Scheme 1: if
\(v\models\varphi\) then \(v \models \psi\to\varphi\), so the
implication holds in either case. Scheme 2: assume \(v\) satisfies
\(\varphi\to(\psi\to\chi)\), \(\varphi\to\psi\) and \(\varphi\); then
\(v\models\psi\to\chi\) and \(v\models\psi\), hence \(v\models\chi\).
Scheme 3: assume \(v\models\neg\psi\to\neg\varphi\) and
\(v\models\varphi\); if \(v\not\models\psi\) then \(v\models\neg\psi\),
so \(v\models\neg\varphi\), contradicting \(v\models\varphi\); hence
\(v\models\psi\). Schemes 4 to 6 are the clause
\(\hat{v}(\varphi\wedge\psi) = \min(\hat{v}(\varphi),\hat{v}(\psi))\)
read three times.

Now let \(v\) satisfy \(\Sigma\) and induct along a derivation
\(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}\). If \(\chi_{i}\) is an axiom it is satisfied
by the previous paragraph; if \(\chi_{i}\in\Sigma\), by hypothesis; and
if \(\chi_{i}\) follows by modus ponens from \(\chi_{j}\) and
\(\chi_{j}\to\chi_{i}\) with \(j < i\), then \(v\) satisfies both by
induction, hence satisfies \(\chi_{i}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-system
#+NAME: lem-self-implication
#+begin_lemma
\(\vdash \varphi\to\varphi\) for every formula \(\varphi\).
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
Take the instance of scheme 2 with \(\psi = (\varphi\to\varphi)\) and
\(\chi = \varphi\):
\(\bigl(\varphi\to((\varphi\to\varphi)\to\varphi)\bigr)\to\bigl((\varphi\to(\varphi\to\varphi))\to(\varphi\to\varphi)\bigr)\).
Its antecedent is an instance of scheme 1, so modus ponens gives
\((\varphi\to(\varphi\to\varphi))\to(\varphi\to\varphi)\); the
antecedent of /that/ is again an instance of scheme 1, and a second
modus ponens yields \(\varphi\to\varphi\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-system, lem-self-implication
#+NAME: thm-deduction-theorem
#+begin_theorem
\(\Sigma \cup \set{\varphi} \vdash \psi\) if and only if
\(\Sigma \vdash \varphi \to \psi\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For the direction that is not immediate, induct along a derivation \(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n} = \psi\) from
\(\Sigma\cup\set{\varphi}\), showing \(\Sigma\vdash\varphi\to\chi_{i}\).
If \(\chi_{i}\) is an axiom or lies in \(\Sigma\), then scheme 1 gives
\(\chi_{i}\to(\varphi\to\chi_{i})\), and modus ponens yields the claim.
If \(\chi_{i} = \varphi\), this is the lemma. If
\(\chi_{i}\) comes by modus ponens from \(\chi_{j}\) and
\(\chi_{j}\to\chi_{i}\), then by induction \(\Sigma\) derives
\(\varphi\to\chi_{j}\) and \(\varphi\to(\chi_{j}\to\chi_{i})\); scheme 2
and two applications of modus ponens give \(\varphi\to\chi_{i}\).

The converse is one application of modus ponens: from
\(\Sigma\vdash\varphi\to\psi\) and \(\varphi\), infer \(\psi\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The deduction theorem is what makes a Hilbert system usable at all --
without it, proving an implication means guessing an axiom instance.
Note that it is a theorem /about/ the system, proved by induction on
derivations in the ambient mathematics, and not a rule of the system:
that separation between the object language and the language reasoning
about it is the whole methodological point of proof theory.
#+end_remark
