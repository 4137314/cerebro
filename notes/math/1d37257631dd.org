#+TITLE: The Tietze extension theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54C20
#+HASH: 1d37257631dd

A continuous function on a subspace can rarely be extended: \(1/x\) on
\((0,1]\) cannot reach \(0\). Tietze's theorem says the only obstruction
is the one that example shows -- if the subspace is closed and the
function bounded, an extension always exists in a normal space. The
proof is an infinite series of Urysohn functions, each correcting a
third of the remaining error.

#+DEPENDS: thm-urysohn-lemma, def-regular-and-normal, def-subspace-topology
#+NAME: lem-one-third-approximation
#+begin_lemma
Let \(X\) be normal, \(A \subseteq X\) closed, and
\(f \colon A \to [-c,c]\) continuous. Then there is a continuous
\(g \colon X \to [-c/3,c/3]\) with
\(\abs{f(a)-g(a)} \le 2c/3\) for every \(a \in A\).
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
The sets \(C = f^{-1}([-c,-c/3])\) and \(D = f^{-1}([c/3,c])\) are
closed in \(A\), hence closed in \(X\) since \(A\) is, and they are
disjoint. Urysohn's lemma gives a continuous
\(h \colon X \to [0,1]\) with \(h\equiv0\) on \(C\) and \(h\equiv1\) on
\(D\); put \(g = \tfrac{2c}{3}h - \tfrac{c}{3}\), which takes values in
\([-c/3,c/3]\).

For \(a \in C\), \(g(a) = -c/3\) and \(f(a)\in[-c,-c/3]\), so the
difference is at most \(2c/3\); for \(a \in D\) symmetrically; and for
the remaining \(a\), both \(f(a)\) and \(g(a)\) lie in
\([-c/3,c/3]\), so they differ by at most \(2c/3\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: lem-one-third-approximation, thm-weierstrass-m-test, thm-uniform-limit-continuous
#+NAME: thm-tietze-bounded
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X\) be normal, \(A \subseteq X\) closed, and
\(f \colon A \to [-c,c]\) continuous. Then \(f\) extends to a continuous
\(F \colon X \to [-c,c]\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Build \(g_{n}\) recursively. Let \(g_{1}\) be given by the lemma for
\(f\), so \(\norm{g_{1}}_{\infty}\le c/3\) and
\(\abs{f-g_{1}}\le 2c/3\) on \(A\). Given \(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\), apply
the lemma to \(f - \sum_{k\le n}g_{k}\), which is continuous on \(A\)
and bounded by \((2/3)^{n}c\); this gives \(g_{n+1}\) with
\(\norm{g_{n+1}}_{\infty}\le\tfrac{1}{3}(2/3)^{n}c\) and
\(\abs{f-\sum_{k\le n+1}g_{k}}\le(2/3)^{n+1}c\) on \(A\).

The series \(\sum_{n}g_{n}\) converges uniformly on \(X\) by the
Weierstrass test, being dominated by
\(\sum_{n}\tfrac{c}{3}(2/3)^{n-1} = c\); so its sum \(F\) is continuous
as a uniform limit of continuous functions, and \(\abs{F}\le c\). On
\(A\) the partial sums converge to \(f\), by the error bound, so
\(F\) extends \(f\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-tietze-bounded, thm-urysohn-lemma, thm-continuous-inverse
#+NAME: thm-tietze-unbounded
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X\) be normal, \(A\subseteq X\) closed, and
\(f \colon A \to \R\) continuous. Then \(f\) extends continuously to
\(X\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(\varphi \colon \R \to (-1,1)\) be a homeomorphism, for instance
\(\varphi(t) = t/(1+\abs{t})\), whose inverse is continuous. By the
bounded case, \(\varphi\circ f\) extends to a continuous
\(G \colon X \to [-1,1]\). The set \(B = G^{-1}(\set{-1,1})\) is closed
and disjoint from \(A\), since \(\varphi\circ f\) does not take the
values \(\pm1\). Urysohn's lemma gives a continuous
\(h \colon X \to [0,1]\) with \(h\equiv0\) on \(B\) and \(h\equiv1\) on
\(A\). Then \(hG\) is continuous, takes values in \((-1,1)\) -- it is
\(0\) where \(\abs{G}=1\) -- and agrees with \(\varphi\circ f\) on
\(A\). So \(F = \varphi^{-1}\circ(hG)\) is a continuous extension of
\(f\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Closedness of \(A\) is used twice and cannot be dropped: on
\(A = (0,1]\) inside \(X = [0,1]\), the function \(1/x\) has no
extension, and \(A\) is not closed. The theorem is also a converse: a
space with closed points in which every such extension exists is normal,
since the function extending \(0\) on \(C\) and \(1\) on \(D\)
separates them. So Tietze and Urysohn are two statements of one
property.
#+end_remark
