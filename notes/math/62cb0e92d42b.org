#+TITLE: Convex functions and the second derivative
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A51
#+HASH: 62cb0e92d42b

Convexity is defined without any derivative at all, which is why it
survives where differentiability does not; when the derivatives exist,
the mean value theorem identifies it with a sign condition.

#+NAME: def-convex-function
#+begin_definition
A function \(f \colon I \to \R\) on an interval is /convex/ if
\[ f(\lambda x + (1 - \lambda) y) \le \lambda f(x) + (1 - \lambda) f(y) \]
for all \(x, y \in I\) and \(\lambda \in [0,1]\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-function, thm-mean-value
#+NAME: prop-convexity-from-second-derivative
#+begin_proposition
Let \(f\) be twice differentiable on an open interval \(I\) with
\(f'' \ge 0\). Then \(f\) is convex on \(I\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Since \(f'' \ge 0\), the mean value theorem applied to \(f'\) shows
\(f'\) is increasing on \(I\). Let \(x < y\) in \(I\),
\(\lambda \in (0,1)\), and put \(z = \lambda x + (1 - \lambda) y\), so
\(x < z < y\). By the mean value theorem there are \(s \in (x, z)\) and
\(t \in (z, y)\) with
\[ f(z) - f(x) = f'(s)(z - x), \qquad f(y) - f(z) = f'(t)(y - z). \]
As \(s < t\) we have \(f'(s) \le f'(t)\), hence
\[ \frac{f(z) - f(x)}{z - x} \le \frac{f(y) - f(z)}{y - z}. \]
Substituting \(z - x = (1-\lambda)(y - x)\) and
\(y - z = \lambda (y - x)\) and clearing denominators gives
\(f(z) \le \lambda f(x) + (1 - \lambda) f(y)\).
#+end_proof
