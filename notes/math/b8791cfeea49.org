#+TITLE: Rigid body kinematics and the inertia tensor
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:classical_mechanics:
#+MSC: 70E15, 70E17
#+HASH: b8791cfeea49

A rigid body is a constraint so strong that the configuration space is a
group: the position of every point is determined by a rotation and a
translation. Its kinetic energy is then a quadratic form in the angular
velocity, and diagonalising that form by the [[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][spectral theorem]] produces
the principal axes -- the frame in which the dynamics is written.

#+DEPENDS: def-orthogonal-group, thm-classification-of-euclidean-isometries, def-holonomic-constraint
#+NAME: def-rigid-motion
#+begin_definition
A /rigid motion/ of a body is a curve \(t \mapsto (R(t), b(t))\) with
\(R(t)\in\SO(3)\) and \(b(t)\in\R^{3}\), the position of the material
point \(X\) being \(x(t) = R(t)X + b(t)\). The /angular velocity/ is the
vector \(\omega(t)\) representing the antisymmetric matrix
\(\Omega = \dot{R}R^{\mathsf{T}}\) through
\(\Omega v = \omega \times v\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-rigid-motion, def-orthogonal-group
#+NAME: prop-angular-velocity-is-well-defined
#+begin_proposition
\(\Omega = \dot{R}R^{\mathsf{T}}\) is antisymmetric, hence of the form
\(v \mapsto \omega\times v\) for a unique \(\omega\); and the velocity
of the material point \(X\) is
\(\dot{x} = \omega\times(x-b)+\dot{b}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Differentiating \(RR^{\mathsf{T}} = I\) gives
\(\dot{R}R^{\mathsf{T}} + R\dot{R}^{\mathsf{T}} = 0\), that is
\(\Omega + \Omega^{\mathsf{T}} = 0\). The antisymmetric \(3\times3\)
matrices form a three-dimensional space, and
\(\omega \mapsto (v\mapsto\omega\times v)\) is a linear bijection onto
it, by comparing entries. Finally
\(\dot{x} = \dot{R}X+\dot{b} = \Omega R X + \dot{b} = \Omega(x-b)+\dot{b}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-angular-velocity-is-well-defined, def-bilinear-form, def-measure
#+NAME: def-inertia-tensor
#+begin_definition
Let the body have mass distribution \(\mu\) in body coordinates and let
the origin be fixed, \(b \equiv 0\). The /inertia tensor/ is the
symmetric matrix
\[ I_{jk} = \int \bigl(\abs{X}^{2}\delta_{jk} - X_{j}X_{k}\bigr)\dd\mu(X), \]
and the /angular momentum/ in body coordinates is \(L = I\Omega_{b}\)
where \(\Omega_{b} = R^{\mathsf{T}}\omega\) is the angular velocity in
those coordinates.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-inertia-tensor, prop-angular-velocity-is-well-defined, thm-tonelli
#+NAME: thm-kinetic-energy-of-a-rigid-body
#+begin_theorem
For a body rotating about a fixed origin,
\(T = \tfrac{1}{2}\inner{I\Omega_{b}}{\Omega_{b}}\); the inertia tensor
is symmetric and positive semi-definite, and positive definite unless
the mass is concentrated on a line through the origin.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
With \(x = RX\), \(\dot{x} = \omega\times x\), so
\(\abs{\dot{x}}^{2} = \abs{\omega}^{2}\abs{x}^{2}-\inner{\omega}{x}^{2}\)
by the identity for the cross product. Integrating against the mass
distribution and passing to body coordinates, where \(R\) is an isometry
so \(\abs{x} = \abs{X}\) and \(\inner{\omega}{x} = \inner{\Omega_{b}}{X}\),
gives
\(2T = \int(\abs{\Omega_{b}}^{2}\abs{X}^{2}-\inner{\Omega_{b}}{X}^{2})\dd\mu = \inner{I\Omega_{b}}{\Omega_{b}}\).

Symmetry is visible in the formula. The integrand
\(\abs{X}^{2}\abs{u}^{2}-\inner{u}{X}^{2}\) is non-negative by
Cauchy-Schwarz, giving semi-definiteness; it vanishes for a given
\(u \ne 0\) exactly where \(X\) is parallel to \(u\), so
\(\inner{Iu}{u} = 0\) forces the mass to sit on that line.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-kinetic-energy-of-a-rigid-body, thm-spectral-theorem, cor-principal-axes
#+NAME: cor-principal-axes-of-inertia
#+begin_corollary
There is an orthonormal basis of body coordinates in which \(I\) is
diagonal, with entries \(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\ge0\) the /principal
moments/; in that basis
\(T = \tfrac{1}{2}(I_{1}\Omega_{1}^{2}+I_{2}\Omega_{2}^{2}+I_{3}\Omega_{3}^{2})\)
and \(L = (I_{1}\Omega_{1}, I_{2}\Omega_{2}, I_{3}\Omega_{3})\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
\(I\) is real symmetric, so the spectral theorem provides an orthonormal
eigenbasis with real eigenvalues, non-negative by semi-definiteness.
Rewriting the quadratic form and the linear map \(L = I\Omega_{b}\) in
that basis gives the displayed expressions.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The angular momentum is not parallel to the angular velocity unless the
body spins about a principal axis, and that single fact is the whole
difficulty of rigid-body dynamics: a freely rotating body about a
non-principal axis has \(L\) constant in space while \(\omega\) moves,
which is why an object thrown spinning about its intermediate axis
tumbles. [[id:4876cdf3-ca6f-4bd7-a056-0501b3add1ea][Euler's equations]] are that statement made quantitative.
#+end_remark
