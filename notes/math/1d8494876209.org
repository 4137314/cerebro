#+TITLE: The isomorphism theorems for groups
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20A05
#+HASH: 1d8494876209

The canonical factorisation of a map says every function is a quotient
followed by an injection. A [[id:86b6c9ba-76e4-49d1-b6a5-729af64e963c][homomorphism]] is a map that respects the
operation, so its factorisation must respect it too -- and the
equivalence relation involved is the one whose classes are the cosets of
the kernel. The three theorems below are that observation, plus the two
standard ways of comparing quotients.

#+DEPENDS: def-group-homomorphism, def-subgroup
#+NAME: prop-image-is-a-subgroup
#+begin_proposition
The image \(\varphi(G)\) of a homomorphism \(\varphi \colon G \to H\) is
a subgroup of \(H\), and the preimage of a subgroup of \(H\) is a
subgroup of \(G\), normal if the subgroup is.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
The image contains \(\varphi(e) = e\) and satisfies
\(\varphi(a)\varphi(b)^{-1} = \varphi(ab^{-1})\), so the subgroup
criterion applies. For \(K \le H\), the preimage contains \(e\) and
\(a, b \in \varphi^{-1}(K)\) gives
\(\varphi(ab^{-1}) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)^{-1} \in K\). If \(K\) is
normal in \(H\) and \(a \in \varphi^{-1}(K)\), \(g \in G\), then
\(\varphi(gag^{-1}) = \varphi(g)\varphi(a)\varphi(g)^{-1} \in K\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-quotient-group, prop-kernel-is-normal, thm-canonical-factorisation, prop-image-is-a-subgroup
#+NAME: thm-first-isomorphism-groups
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\varphi \colon G \to H\) be a homomorphism. Then
\(G/\ker\varphi \iso \varphi(G)\), the isomorphism being
\(a \ker\varphi \mapsto \varphi(a)\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Put \(N = \ker\varphi\), normal by the earlier proposition, so \(G/N\)
is a group. The cosets of \(N\) are exactly the fibres of \(\varphi\):
\(aN = bN\) iff \(a^{-1}b \in N\) iff \(\varphi(a) = \varphi(b)\).
Hence \(\varphi\) is constant on cosets and the induced map
\(\bar{\varphi}(aN) = \varphi(a)\) is well defined and injective, by the
canonical factorisation; it is surjective onto \(\varphi(G)\) by
construction. It is a homomorphism because
\(\bar{\varphi}(aN \cdot bN) = \bar{\varphi}(abN) = \varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-first-isomorphism-groups, prop-subgroup-criterion
#+NAME: thm-second-isomorphism-groups
#+begin_theorem
Let \(H \le G\) and \(N \normalin G\). Then \(HN = \setst{hn}{h \in H, n \in N}\)
is a subgroup of \(G\), \(H \cap N \normalin H\), and
\(HN/N \iso H/(H \cap N)\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
\(HN\) is a subgroup: for \(h n, h'n' \in HN\),
\((hn)(h'n')^{-1} = h (n n'^{-1}) h'^{-1} = (h h'^{-1}) \bigl(h' (n n'^{-1}) h'^{-1}\bigr)\),
and the second factor lies in \(N\) by normality. Also \(N \normalin HN\),
since \(N\) is normal in all of \(G\).

Let \(\psi \colon H \to HN/N\) be the restriction to \(H\) of the
projection \(HN \to HN/N\). It is a homomorphism; it is surjective
because \(hnN = hN = \psi(h)\); and \(\psi(h) = N\) means \(h \in N\),
so \(\ker\psi = H \cap N\), which is therefore normal in \(H\). The
first isomorphism theorem gives \(H/(H \cap N) \iso HN/N\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-first-isomorphism-groups, thm-quotient-group
#+NAME: thm-third-isomorphism-groups
#+begin_theorem
Let \(N \normalin G\) and \(K \normalin G\) with \(N \subseteq K\). Then
\(K/N \normalin G/N\) and \((G/N)/(K/N) \iso G/K\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Define \(\varphi \colon G/N \to G/K\) by \(\varphi(aN) = aK\). It is
well defined: \(aN = bN\) gives \(a^{-1}b \in N \subseteq K\), hence
\(aK = bK\). It is a surjective homomorphism, directly from the
definitions of the two products. Its kernel is
\(\setst{aN}{a \in K} = K/N\), which is therefore a normal subgroup of
\(G/N\), and the first isomorphism theorem gives the claim.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The third theorem is the statement that quotienting twice is
quotienting once, so a tower of normal subgroups can be analysed one
step at a time -- the hypothesis under which "build a group out of
simpler pieces" is a well-posed programme, and the reason simple groups
(those with no proper non-trivial normal subgroup) are the atoms of it.
#+end_remark
