#+TITLE: Linear algebra
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:linear_algebra:
#+MSC: 15-XX
#+HASH: d4ec0e6ee64a

Linear algebra is the one algebraic theory that is finished: over a
field, every module is free, every short exact sequence splits, and a
single integer classifies a space up to isomorphism. That is why it is
the model the rest of algebra is measured against, and why so much of
[[id:38b76068-6f2f-4d67-8a78-655549f487ac][geometry]] and [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][analysis]] reduces to it.

Part of [[id:504cda55-cfbf-42c2-b481-8d90660ec02a][Algebra]].

* The structure theory

[[id:7c54b427-4d7a-4511-b374-58c7bb44fb84][Vector space]], then [[id:75bbba6c-ad62-4369-9a49-17beefdde80f][span, linear independence, bases and dimension]] --
where the exchange lemma proves that a number can be attached to the
space at all, and Zorn's lemma provides a basis when the space is
infinite-dimensional. [[id:63a183cf-d837-4b64-9987-ea0bacf0c0b1][Linear maps, quotient spaces and the rank-nullity
theorem]] is the first isomorphism theorem with a dimension count
attached, and [[id:6f3bed75-1321-4c80-b0bd-c8932e0c2837][dual spaces, annihilators and the transpose]] is the
dictionary between describing a subspace by generators and describing it
by equations.

* Operators

[[id:3618465d-d9db-497b-a389-782e812f266f][The determinant]] is defined by what it must satisfy -- multilinear and
alternating -- so that its formula is a consequence and its identities
are applications of uniqueness. It turns the search for eigenvectors
into a question about roots: [[id:87690e8a-d61d-4412-b811-169706579af9][eigenvalues, the characteristic polynomial
and Cayley-Hamilton]], where diagonalisability becomes a statement about
distinct roots and about the field containing them.

* Geometry on a vector space

An extra structure, a bilinear form, and the classification questions
begin again. [[id:c35a8f01-cbd2-433b-b9b5-a037bae4e91d][Inner product space]] and [[id:3bcfce93-cefa-48a2-b2e8-8f64d2b680cc][the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality]] give
lengths and angles; [[id:c2edf7f5-d5ac-4e50-8734-a00b2da22cf4][orthogonality, Gram-Schmidt and orthogonal
decomposition]] give coordinates adapted to them, and the best
approximation in a subspace. [[id:63688469-ed04-4016-a5ce-54cb91443094][Bilinear forms, quadratic forms and
Sylvester's law of inertia]] drop positivity and classify what remains by
signature; [[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][the spectral theorem]] keeps the inner product fixed and
diagonalises a symmetric operator by an orthonormal basis. The pair of
them is the difference between classifying a quadratic form and finding
the principal axes of an ellipsoid.

Two notes filed here are already geometry: [[id:734ca2fe-0727-4155-992a-7b46244de19e][affine spaces]] and
[[id:ba1be74f-3a05-45ce-a429-7fe14ad45170][conics and quadrics]].

* What is missing

The Jordan normal form and the theory of a single operator over a
non-algebraically-closed field; the minimal polynomial as opposed to the
characteristic one; tensor and exterior algebra, which is what the
determinant is really about; singular values; and multilinear algebra in
general.
