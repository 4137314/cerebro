#+TITLE: Nets and convergence in topological spaces
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54A20
#+HASH: 93142e5f2a04

Sequences describe the closure in a metric space and fail in general,
because \(\N\) is the wrong index set: what a limit argument needs is not
countability but the ability to go further in every direction at once. A
net is a family indexed by a directed set, and with that single change
every sequential characterisation of the metric theory becomes true
again, word for word.

#+DEPENDS: def-partial-order, def-topological-space
#+NAME: def-directed-set
#+begin_definition
A /directed set/ is a non-empty set \(D\) with a reflexive, transitive
relation \(\le\) such that any two elements have a common upper bound. A
/net/ in a space \(X\) is a map \(x \colon D \to X\), written
\((x_{d})_{d\in D}\); it is /eventually/ in \(A \subseteq X\) if
\(x_{d}\in A\) for all \(d \ge d_{0}\), for some \(d_{0}\), and
/frequently/ in \(A\) if for every \(d\) there is \(e \ge d\) with
\(x_{e}\in A\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-directed-set
#+NAME: def-net-convergence
#+begin_definition
A net \((x_{d})\) /converges/ to \(x\), written \(x_{d}\to x\), if it is
eventually in every neighbourhood of \(x\); it /clusters/ at \(x\) if it
is frequently in every neighbourhood of \(x\). A /subnet/ of
\((x_{d})_{d\in D}\) is a net \((x_{h(e)})_{e\in E}\) with \(E\) directed
and \(h \colon E \to D\) such that for every \(d\in D\) there is
\(e_{0}\) with \(h(e)\ge d\) for all \(e \ge e_{0}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-net-convergence, thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+NAME: thm-closure-by-nets
#+begin_theorem
For \(A \subseteq X\), \(x \in \overline{A}\) if and only if some net in
\(A\) converges to \(x\). Hence \(A\) is closed exactly when it contains
the limit of every convergent net of its points.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(x_{d}\to x\) with all \(x_{d}\in A\), every neighbourhood of \(x\)
eventually contains some \(x_{d} \in A\), so it meets \(A\) and
\(x \in \overline{A}\).

Conversely let \(x \in \overline{A}\) and let \(D\) be the set of
neighbourhoods of \(x\), directed by reverse inclusion -- two
neighbourhoods have their intersection as a common upper bound. Each
\(N \in D\) meets \(A\); choose \(x_{N} \in N \cap A\). Given a
neighbourhood \(N_{0}\), every \(N \ge N_{0}\) satisfies
\(N \subseteq N_{0}\), so \(x_{N}\in N_{0}\): the net converges to
\(x\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-net-convergence, thm-characterisations-of-continuity, thm-closure-by-nets
#+NAME: thm-continuity-by-nets
#+begin_theorem
\(f \colon X \to Y\) is continuous at \(x\) if and only if
\(x_{d}\to x\) implies \(f(x_{d})\to f(x)\) for every net.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(f\) is continuous at \(x\) and \(x_{d}\to x\). Given a
neighbourhood \(V\) of \(f(x)\), some neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\) has
\(f(U)\subseteq V\), and \((x_{d})\) is eventually in \(U\), hence
\((f(x_{d}))\) is eventually in \(V\).

Conversely, suppose \(f\) is not continuous at \(x\): some neighbourhood
\(V\) of \(f(x)\) contains no \(f(U)\). Directing the neighbourhoods of
\(x\) by reverse inclusion, choose for each \(U\) a point
\(x_{U}\in U\) with \(f(x_{U})\notin V\). Then \(x_{U}\to x\) as above,
while \((f(x_{U}))\) is never in \(V\), so it does not converge to
\(f(x)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-net-convergence, def-hausdorff, prop-hausdorff-limits-are-unique
#+NAME: prop-hausdorff-by-nets
#+begin_proposition
\(X\) is Hausdorff if and only if every net in \(X\) has at most one
limit.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
In a Hausdorff space, a net converging to \(x \ne y\) would be
eventually in each of two disjoint neighbourhoods, which is impossible
since a directed set has upper bounds.

Conversely, suppose \(x \ne y\) cannot be separated. Direct the pairs
\((U,V)\) of neighbourhoods of \(x\) and of \(y\) by reverse inclusion
in both entries, and choose \(z_{(U,V)} \in U \cap V\), non-empty by
assumption. This net converges to \(x\) and to \(y\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-net-convergence, def-compact, thm-compactness-by-ultrafilters
#+NAME: thm-compactness-by-nets
#+begin_theorem
A space is compact if and only if every net in it has a cluster point,
if and only if every net has a convergent subnet.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(X\) be compact and \((x_{d})_{d \in D}\) a net with no cluster
point. Then each \(x\) has an open \(U_{x}\) that the net is not
frequently in, so it is eventually outside \(U_{x}\): there is
\(d_{x}\) with \(x_{e}\notin U_{x}\) for \(e \ge d_{x}\). Finitely many
\(U_{x_{1}},\dots,U_{x_{k}}\) cover \(X\); taking \(e\) above all the
\(d_{x_{i}}\), which is possible by directedness, puts \(x_{e}\) outside
every \(U_{x_{i}}\) -- absurd.

Conversely, suppose every net clusters and let \(\mathcal{C}\) be a
family of closed sets with the finite intersection property. Direct the
finite subfamilies by inclusion and choose a point \(x_{F}\) in the
intersection of each; a cluster point \(x\) of this net lies in every
\(C \in \mathcal{C}\), because the net is frequently in every
neighbourhood of \(x\) and eventually inside \(C\) once \(F \ni C\), so
every neighbourhood of \(x\) meets \(C\), which is closed. Hence
\(\bigcap\mathcal{C}\ne\emptyset\), which is compactness.

Finally, \(x\) is a cluster point of \((x_{d})\) exactly when some
subnet converges to \(x\): given such an \(x\), let \(E\) be the set of
pairs \((d,N)\) with \(x_{d}\in N\) a neighbourhood of \(x\), directed
by \(\ge\) in \(d\) and reverse inclusion in \(N\), and
\(h(d,N) = d\); this is a subnet converging to \(x\). Conversely a
subnet converging to \(x\) is frequently in every neighbourhood.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Sequences are the nets indexed by \(\N\), and the metric theory is the
case in which they suffice, because the neighbourhoods of a point are
directed by a countable subfamily. Nets and filters are two languages
for the same content -- the net constructed above from the
neighbourhoods is the filter of neighbourhoods in disguise -- and which
to use is a matter of whether one prefers to index or to collect.
#+end_remark
