#+TITLE: Expectation, the transfer theorem and Jensen's inequality
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60A10, 60E15
#+HASH: b85ee8ddbc69

The expectation of a random variable is its integral, so linearity and
the convergence theorems come for nothing. Two statements do not: that
the expectation of \(g(X)\) can be computed on the line, knowing only
the law of \(X\), and that expectation commutes with a product when the
factors are independent. Both are proved by the same device -- check it
for indicators, extend by linearity and monotone limits -- which is the
standard machine of the subject.

#+DEPENDS: def-simple-function, def-random-variable, def-probability-space
#+NAME: def-expectation
#+begin_definition
The /expectation/ of a random variable \(X\) with
\(\E\abs{X}<\infty\) is \(\E[X] = \int_{\Omega}X \dd\Prob\); such an
\(X\) is /integrable/. For \(X \ge 0\) the expectation is defined in
\([0,\infty]\) with no integrability assumed.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, thm-the-law-of-a-random-variable, thm-monotone-convergence-of-integrals, thm-approximation-by-simple-functions
#+NAME: thm-transfer
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X\) be a random variable with law \(\Prob_{X}\) and
\(g \colon \R\to\R\) Borel measurable with \(g \ge 0\), or with
\(g(X)\) integrable. Then
\[ \E[g(X)] = \int_{\R} g \dd\Prob_{X}. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For \(g = \indicator_{B}\) with \(B\) Borel, the left side is
\(\Prob(X\in B)\) and the right side is \(\Prob_{X}(B)\), equal by
definition of the law. By linearity of both integrals the identity holds
for every non-negative simple \(g\). For general \(g \ge 0\), take
simple \(s_{n}\uparrow g\); then \(s_{n}(X)\uparrow g(X)\), and monotone
convergence applied on each side gives the identity in the limit.

For integrable \(g(X)\), apply the non-negative case to
\(g^{+} = \max(g,0)\) and \(g^{-} = \max(-g,0)\), both finite, and
subtract.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, cor-additivity-of-the-integral, thm-dominated-convergence
#+NAME: prop-properties-of-expectation
#+begin_proposition
Expectation is linear and monotone on integrable random variables,
\(\abs{\E[X]}\le\E\abs{X}\), and a constant has itself as expectation.
If \(X_{n}\to X\) almost surely with \(\abs{X_{n}}\le Y\) integrable,
then \(\E[X_{n}]\to\E[X]\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Each statement is the corresponding property of the integral, applied on
the measure space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\Prob)\); the last is dominated
convergence, and \(\abs{\E[X]}\le\E\abs{X}\) follows from monotonicity
applied to \(\pm X \le \abs{X}\). A constant \(c\) has
\(\int c \dd\Prob = c\,\Prob(\Omega) = c\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-independence-of-random-variables, thm-transfer, thm-monotone-convergence-of-integrals, def-simple-function
#+NAME: thm-expectation-of-a-product
#+begin_theorem
If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent and both integrable, then \(XY\) is
integrable and \(\E[XY] = \E[X]\,\E[Y]\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Assume first \(X, Y\ge0\). For the pair
\(\indicator_{\set{X\in B_{1}}}\) and \(\indicator_{\set{Y\in B_{2}}}\)
with \(B_{1},B_{2}\) Borel, the claim is
\(\Prob(X\in B_{1}, Y\in B_{2}) = \Prob(X\in B_{1})\Prob(Y\in B_{2})\),
which is the definition of independence. By bilinearity it holds for
any pair of non-negative simple functions of \(X\) and of \(Y\), each
being a finite combination of such indicators. Take simple \(s_{n}\uparrow X\) and
\(t_{n}\uparrow Y\) of the form \(s_{n} = \varphi_{n}(X)\),
\(t_{n} = \psi_{n}(Y)\) -- the approximation theorem produces exactly
such functions, being built from the level sets of \(X\) and \(Y\). Then
\(s_{n}t_{n}\uparrow XY\), and monotone convergence on both sides gives
\(\E[XY] = \E[X]\E[Y]\) in \([0,\infty]\).

In general apply this to \(\abs{X}\) and \(\abs{Y}\), which are
independent by the proposition on functions of independent variables;
the product of their finite expectations bounds \(\E\abs{XY}\), so
\(XY\) is integrable. Splitting \(X = X^{+}-X^{-}\) and
\(Y = Y^{+}-Y^{-}\) into four independent non-negative pairs and
expanding by linearity gives the identity.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, def-convex-function, prop-properties-of-expectation
#+NAME: thm-jensen-inequality
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\varphi \colon \R\to\R\) be convex and \(X\) integrable with
\(\varphi(X)\) integrable. Then \(\varphi(\E[X]) \le \E[\varphi(X)]\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Put \(m = \E[X]\). Convexity makes the difference quotient
\(t \mapsto (\varphi(t)-\varphi(m))/(t-m)\) non-decreasing on each side
of \(m\), so
\(a = \sup_{t<m}(\varphi(t)-\varphi(m))/(t-m)\) is finite and is at most
every quotient with \(t>m\). Hence the line
\(\ell(t) = \varphi(m)+a(t-m)\) satisfies \(\ell \le \varphi\)
everywhere -- for \(t>m\) because \(a\) is below the quotient, for
\(t<m\) because \(a\) is above it and the denominator is negative, and
at \(t = m\) with equality.

Applying monotonicity of expectation to \(\ell(X)\le\varphi(X)\) and
linearity gives
\(\varphi(m) + a(\E[X]-m) = \varphi(m) \le \E[\varphi(X)]\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The transfer theorem is what makes the underlying probability space
disappear from every computation: after it, an expectation is an
integral against a measure on \(\R\), and two random variables with the
same law are interchangeable in every formula. Jensen's inequality is
the reason variance, entropy and the moment inequalities all point the
same way.
#+end_remark
