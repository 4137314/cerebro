#+TITLE: Laurent series and isolated singularities
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:complex_analysis:
#+MSC: 30B10, 30D30
#+HASH: 4fbcc4f37e70

Away from the points where it misbehaves, a function is still
holomorphic, and the expansion adapted to an annulus rather than a disc
allows negative powers. How many of them appear classifies the
singularity completely into three cases -- removable, pole, essential --
and the classification is what makes the residue calculus mechanical.

#+DEPENDS: thm-holomorphic-implies-analytic, thm-cauchy-integral-formula, thm-weierstrass-m-test
#+NAME: thm-laurent-expansion
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on the annulus
\(A = \setst{z}{\rho<\abs{z-z_{0}}<R}\). Then
\[ f(z) = \sum_{n \in \Z} c_{n}(z-z_{0})^{n}, \qquad c_{n} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{r}}\frac{f(w)}{(w-z_{0})^{n+1}}\dd w, \]
converging locally uniformly on \(A\), where \(C_{r}\) is any circle
\(\abs{w-z_{0}} = r\) with \(\rho<r<R\); the coefficients do not depend
on \(r\) and are uniquely determined by \(f\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix \(z \in A\) and choose \(\rho<r_{1}<\abs{z-z_{0}}<r_{2}<R\). As in
the proof of the integral formula, cutting the region between the two
circles into pieces contained in convex subsets of \(A\) and cancelling
the cuts gives
\[ f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{r_{2}}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w - \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{r_{1}}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w. \]
On \(C_{r_{2}}\) expand \(1/(w-z)\) in powers of \((z-z_{0})/(w-z_{0})\),
of modulus less than \(1\), as before; on \(C_{r_{1}}\) expand instead
in powers of \((w-z_{0})/(z-z_{0})\), again of modulus less than \(1\),
giving
\(-\frac{1}{w-z} = \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(w-z_{0})^{k}}{(z-z_{0})^{k+1}}\).
Both series converge uniformly in \(w\) on the respective circles, by
the Weierstrass test, so may be integrated term by term; the first gives
the coefficients with \(n\ge0\) and the second those with \(n\le-1\).

Independence of \(r\) follows because the integrand is holomorphic in
the annulus, so the same cutting argument equates the integrals over two
circles. Uniqueness: given any such expansion converging locally
uniformly, multiply by \((z-z_{0})^{-m-1}\) and integrate over
\(C_{r}\); all terms vanish except \(n = m\), which contributes
\(2\pi i c_{m}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-laurent-expansion, thm-zeros-are-isolated
#+NAME: def-isolated-singularity
#+begin_definition
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a punctured disc
\(B(z_{0},R)\setminus\set{z_{0}}\), with Laurent coefficients
\(c_{n}\). The singularity at \(z_{0}\) is /removable/ if \(c_{n} = 0\)
for all \(n<0\); a /pole of order \(m\)/ if \(c_{-m}\ne0\) and
\(c_{n} = 0\) for \(n<-m\); and /essential/ otherwise. The /residue/ is
\(\Res(f,z_{0}) = c_{-1}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-isolated-singularity, thm-laurent-expansion, cor-cauchy-estimates
#+NAME: thm-riemann-removability
#+begin_theorem
The singularity at \(z_{0}\) is removable if and only if \(f\) is
bounded near \(z_{0}\), and then \(f\) extends holomorphically across
\(z_{0}\). It is a pole of order \(m\) if and only if
\((z-z_{0})^{m}f(z)\) has a removable singularity with non-zero limit,
and then \(\abs{f(z)}\to\infty\) as \(z\to z_{0}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If the singularity is removable the series defines a holomorphic
function on the whole disc, which is bounded near \(z_{0}\). Conversely
suppose \(\abs{f}\le M\) on \(0<\abs{z-z_{0}}<\delta\). For \(n<0\) and
\(r<\delta\) the standard estimate gives
\(\abs{c_{n}} \le \frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot 2\pi r\cdot M r^{-n-1} = M r^{-n}\),
and \(-n>0\), so letting \(r\to0\) forces \(c_{n} = 0\).

For a pole of order \(m\), the Laurent series of
\(g(z) = (z-z_{0})^{m}f(z)\) is that of \(f\) shifted by \(m\), so it has
no negative powers and constant term \(c_{-m}\ne0\); conversely such a
\(g\) gives back the stated coefficients. Then near \(z_{0}\),
\(\abs{f(z)} = \abs{g(z)}\abs{z-z_{0}}^{-m}\) with \(\abs{g}\) bounded
below, so it tends to infinity.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-riemann-removability, def-isolated-singularity, thm-identity
#+NAME: thm-casorati-weierstrass
#+begin_theorem
If \(z_{0}\) is an essential singularity, then for every
\(\delta>0\) the image of the punctured disc
\(0<\abs{z-z_{0}}<\delta\) is dense in \(\C\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose not: there are \(w_{0}\) and \(\eps>0\) with
\(\abs{f(z)-w_{0}}\ge\eps\) on the punctured disc. Then
\(g(z) = 1/(f(z)-w_{0})\) is holomorphic there and bounded by
\(1/\eps\), so by Riemann's theorem it extends holomorphically across
\(z_{0}\). If \(g(z_{0})\ne0\) then \(f = w_{0}+1/g\) is bounded near
\(z_{0}\) and the singularity is removable; if \(g\) has a zero of order
\(m\) at \(z_{0}\), then \(f - w_{0}\) has a pole of order \(m\). Either
way the singularity is not essential.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The three cases are exhaustive and the trichotomy is sharp: a
singularity is removable, or the function blows up, or it comes
arbitrarily close to every complex value in every neighbourhood.
Picard's theorem strengthens the last to "takes every value, with at
most one exception, infinitely often" -- which is not proved here and
is the natural next step.
#+end_remark
