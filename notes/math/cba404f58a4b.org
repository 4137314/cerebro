#+TITLE: The harmonic oscillator by ladder operators
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:quantum_theory:
#+MSC: 81Q05, 81Q10
#+HASH: cba404f58a4b

The quantum harmonic oscillator is solved without solving a differential
equation: factorising the Hamiltonian into two mutually adjoint pieces
turns the spectrum into a consequence of one commutation relation. The
computation is entirely algebraic, and it is the model for every later
use of raising and lowering operators -- angular momentum, and the
occupation numbers of a field.

#+DEPENDS: def-quantum-state-and-observable, cor-position-and-momentum, def-hilbert-space
#+NAME: def-ladder-operators
#+begin_definition
On \(L^{2}(\R)\) with \(Q, P\) as before, let \(m,\omega>0\) and put
\[ H = \frac{P^{2}}{2m} + \frac{m\omega^{2}}{2}Q^{2}, \qquad a = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Bigl(Q + \frac{i}{m\omega}P\Bigr), \]
with \(a\dg\) the formal adjoint, on the domain of smooth rapidly
decreasing functions. The /number operator/ is \(N = a\dg a\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-ladder-operators, cor-position-and-momentum
#+NAME: prop-commutation-relations
#+begin_proposition
On that domain, \(\comm{a}{a\dg} = \id\), \(H = \hbar\omega(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\),
\(\comm{N}{a} = -a\) and \(\comm{N}{a\dg} = a\dg\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Expanding the products and keeping the order,
\[ a a\dg = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\Bigl(Q^{2}+\frac{P^{2}}{m^{2}\omega^{2}} - \frac{i}{m\omega}\comm{Q}{P}\Bigr), \qquad a\dg a = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\Bigl(Q^{2}+\frac{P^{2}}{m^{2}\omega^{2}} + \frac{i}{m\omega}\comm{Q}{P}\Bigr). \]
Substituting \(\comm{Q}{P} = i\hbar\), the two differ by
\(\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\cdot\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega} = 1\), which is the
first identity. The second expression also gives
\(a\dg a = \frac{1}{\hbar\omega}\Bigl(\frac{P^{2}}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2}Q^{2}\Bigr) - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{H}{\hbar\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\),
which is the second identity. Then
\(\comm{N}{a} = a\dg a a - a a\dg a = (a\dg a - a a\dg)a = -a\), and the
adjoint computation gives \(\comm{N}{a\dg} = a\dg\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-commutation-relations, def-quantum-state-and-observable, prop-expectations-are-real
#+NAME: thm-spectrum-of-the-oscillator
#+begin_theorem
Every eigenvalue of \(N\) on a state in the domain is a non-negative
integer, and if \(N\psi_{n} = n\psi_{n}\) with \(\norm{\psi_{n}}=1\)
then \(a\dg\psi_{n}\) and \(a\psi_{n}\) are eigenvectors for \(n+1\) and
\(n-1\), with
\(\norm{a\dg\psi_{n}} = \sqrt{n+1}\) and \(\norm{a\psi_{n}} = \sqrt{n}\).
Consequently the eigenvalues of \(H\) are
\(E_{n} = \hbar\omega(n+\tfrac{1}{2})\), \(n \ge 0\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(N\psi = n\psi\) with \(\norm{\psi}=1\), then
\(n = \inner{a\dg a\psi}{\psi} = \norm{a\psi}^{2}\ge0\), so eigenvalues
are non-negative, and \(n = 0\) forces \(a\psi = 0\).

From \(\comm{N}{a\dg} = a\dg\),
\(N(a\dg\psi) = a\dg N\psi + a\dg\psi = (n+1)a\dg\psi\), and similarly
\(N(a\psi) = (n-1)a\psi\). The norms are
\(\norm{a\dg\psi}^{2} = \inner{aa\dg\psi}{\psi} = \inner{(N+1)\psi}{\psi} = n+1\)
and \(\norm{a\psi}^{2} = n\).

Now suppose \(n\) were not an integer. Applying \(a\) repeatedly gives
eigenvectors for \(n-1, n-2, \dots\), each non-zero because the norm
\(\sqrt{n-k}\) is non-zero as long as \(n-k>0\); since \(n\) is not an
integer the sequence passes below zero without vanishing, producing an
eigenvector with negative eigenvalue -- contradicting non-negativity.
Hence \(n \in \N\), and \(H = \hbar\omega(N+\tfrac{1}{2})\) gives the
energies.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-spectrum-of-the-oscillator, def-ladder-operators, thm-fourier-inversion
#+NAME: prop-the-ground-state
#+begin_proposition
The equation \(a\psi = 0\) has, up to a constant, the unique solution
\(\psi_{0}(x) = \bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\bigr)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^{2}/(2\hbar)}\),
a unit vector of \(L^{2}(\R)\); and \(\psi_{n} = (a\dg)^{n}\psi_{0}/\sqrt{n!}\)
are unit eigenvectors of \(H\) with energies \(E_{n}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Written out, \(a\psi = 0\) is the first-order equation
\(\psi' = -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\psi\), whose solutions are the
multiples of the displayed Gaussian; the normalising constant is the
Gaussian integral. The states \(\psi_{n}\) are eigenvectors by induction
from the previous theorem, and the normalisation follows from
\(\norm{a\dg\psi_{n}} = \sqrt{n+1}\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The argument never solves the eigenvalue equation for \(H\); it derives
the spectrum from one commutator and a positivity, which is why the same
computation gives the spectrum of angular momentum with
\(\comm{J_{3}}{J_{\pm}} = \pm\hbar J_{\pm}\). What it does not give is
completeness -- that the \(\psi_{n}\) span \(L^{2}(\R)\) -- which is a
genuine analytic theorem about Hermite functions and is not proved here.
#+end_remark
