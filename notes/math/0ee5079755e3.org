#+TITLE: Hausdorff spaces and separation
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54D10
#+HASH: 0ee5079755e3

The axioms of a topological space allow a topology so coarse that two
points cannot be told apart by open sets, and then a sequence can
converge to everything at once. Nothing in the general theory forbids
it; a separation axiom is the extra hypothesis under which the
statements one expects -- limits are unique, compact sets are closed --
become true again.

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space
#+NAME: def-hausdorff
#+begin_definition
A topological space \(X\) is /Hausdorff/ if for any two distinct points
\(x \ne y\) there are disjoint open sets \(U \ni x\) and \(V \ni y\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-hausdorff, def-metric-space, def-open-set
#+NAME: prop-metric-spaces-are-hausdorff
#+begin_proposition
Every metric space, with the topology of its open sets, is Hausdorff.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For \(x \ne y\) put \(r = d(x,y)/2 > 0\). The balls \(B(x,r)\) and
\(B(y,r)\) are open and disjoint: a common point \(z\) would give
\(d(x,y) \le d(x,z)+d(z,y) < 2r = d(x,y)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hausdorff, def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: prop-hausdorff-limits-are-unique
#+begin_proposition
In a Hausdorff space a sequence has at most one limit, where
\(x_{n} \to x\) means that every neighbourhood of \(x\) contains all but
finitely many \(x_{n}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(x_{n} \to x\) and \(x_{n} \to y\) with \(x \ne y\), and take
disjoint open \(U \ni x\), \(V \ni y\). All but finitely many terms lie
in \(U\), and all but finitely many lie in \(V\); as the sequence has
infinitely many indices, some term lies in both, contradicting
disjointness.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hausdorff, prop-interior-and-closure-are-extremal
#+NAME: prop-points-are-closed-in-a-hausdorff-space
#+begin_proposition
In a Hausdorff space every finite subset is closed.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
It suffices to treat a single point \(x\), finite unions of closed sets
being closed. For each \(y \ne x\) choose disjoint open
\(U_{y} \ni y\) and \(V_{y} \ni x\); then \(U_{y}\) misses \(x\), so
\(X \setminus \set{x} = \bigcup_{y \ne x}U_{y}\) is open.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hausdorff, def-compact, prop-points-are-closed-in-a-hausdorff-space
#+NAME: prop-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed
#+begin_proposition
A compact subset \(K\) of a Hausdorff space \(X\) is closed. More
precisely, a point \(x \notin K\) and \(K\) have disjoint open
neighbourhoods.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Fix \(x \notin K\). For each \(y \in K\) choose disjoint open
\(U_{y} \ni y\) and \(V_{y} \ni x\). The \(U_{y}\) cover \(K\), so
finitely many \(U_{y_{1}}, \dots, U_{y_{m}}\) do; then
\(V = V_{y_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap V_{y_{m}}\) is an open neighbourhood of
\(x\) disjoint from \(U = U_{y_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup U_{y_{m}} \supseteq K\).
In particular every \(x \notin K\) has a neighbourhood missing \(K\), so
the complement of \(K\) is open.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed, thm-continuous-image-of-a-compact-space, prop-homeomorphism-criteria, prop-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space
#+NAME: thm-continuous-bijection-from-compact-to-hausdorff
#+begin_theorem
A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a
homeomorphism.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be such a map and \(C \subseteq X\) closed.
Then \(C\) is compact, so \(f(C)\) is compact, hence closed in the
Hausdorff space \(Y\). So \(f\) is a closed bijection, therefore a
homeomorphism.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hausdorff, def-subspace-topology, def-product-topology
#+NAME: prop-hausdorff-is-inherited
#+begin_proposition
A subspace of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff, and any product of
Hausdorff spaces is Hausdorff.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For a subspace \(A\), intersect the separating open sets with \(A\).
For a product, let \(x \ne y\) in \(\prod_{i}X_{i}\); they differ in some
coordinate \(j\), so there are disjoint open \(U, V \subseteq X_{j}\)
around \(x_{j}\) and \(y_{j}\), and \(\pi_{j}^{-1}(U)\),
\(\pi_{j}^{-1}(V)\) are disjoint open sets separating \(x\) and \(y\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
A quotient is missing from that list, and deliberately: the quotient of
a Hausdorff space need not be Hausdorff, since the quotient topology is
defined by mapping out and separation is a statement about mapping in.
That is where the axiom stops being automatic and becomes something to
check.
#+end_remark
