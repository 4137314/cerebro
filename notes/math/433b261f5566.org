#+TITLE: Direct and semidirect products
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20E22, 20K01
#+HASH: 433b261f5566

A normal subgroup and its quotient do not determine a group: the two
pieces can be assembled in several ways, and which one occurred is
extra data. The recognition theorems below say when a group /is/ an
assembly of two of its subgroups, and the semidirect product is the
first construction in which the assembly is genuinely non-trivial -- the
action of one factor on the other.

#+DEPENDS: def-group, def-group-homomorphism
#+NAME: def-direct-product
#+begin_definition
The /direct product/ of groups \(H\) and \(K\) is \(H \times K\) with
componentwise multiplication. It contains \(H \times \set{e}\) and
\(\set{e}\times K\) as normal subgroups intersecting trivially and
generating it.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-direct-product, def-normal-subgroup, thm-first-isomorphism-groups
#+NAME: thm-internal-direct-product
#+begin_theorem
Let \(H, K \normalin G\) with \(H \cap K = \set{e}\) and \(HK = G\).
Then \(hk = kh\) for all \(h \in H\), \(k \in K\), and
\((h,k)\mapsto hk\) is an isomorphism \(H\times K \to G\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For \(h \in H\), \(k \in K\), the commutator
\(hkh^{-1}k^{-1}\) lies in \(K\) -- as \((hkh^{-1})k^{-1}\), using
normality of \(K\) -- and in \(H\) -- as \(h(kh^{-1}k^{-1})\), using
normality of \(H\); so it is \(e\) and the elements commute.

The map is then a homomorphism:
\((h_{1}k_{1})(h_{2}k_{2}) = h_{1}h_{2}k_{1}k_{2}\). It is surjective
because \(HK = G\), and injective because \(hk = e\) forces
\(h = k^{-1} \in H \cap K = \set{e}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-group-action, def-group-homomorphism, def-direct-product
#+NAME: def-semidirect-product
#+begin_definition
Let \(N\) and \(H\) be groups and \(\theta \colon H \to \Aut(N)\) a
homomorphism into the group of automorphisms of \(N\). The /semidirect
product/ \(N \rtimes_{\theta} H\) is the set \(N \times H\) with
\[ (n_{1},h_{1})(n_{2},h_{2}) = \bigl(n_{1}\,\theta(h_{1})(n_{2}),\; h_{1}h_{2}\bigr). \]
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-semidirect-product, def-normal-subgroup, thm-quotient-group
#+NAME: prop-semidirect-product-is-a-group
#+begin_proposition
\(N\rtimes_{\theta}H\) is a group, in which \(N\times\set{e}\) is normal
with quotient isomorphic to \(H\), and \(\set{e}\times H\) is a subgroup
meeting it trivially. It is the direct product exactly when \(\theta\)
is trivial.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Associativity is a computation in which \(\theta\) being a homomorphism
into automorphisms is used twice:
\(\bigl((n_{1},h_{1})(n_{2},h_{2})\bigr)(n_{3},h_{3})\) and
\((n_{1},h_{1})\bigl((n_{2},h_{2})(n_{3},h_{3})\bigr)\) both have first
component
\(n_{1}\,\theta(h_{1})(n_{2})\,\theta(h_{1}h_{2})(n_{3})\). The
identity is \((e,e)\) and \((n,h)^{-1} = (\theta(h^{-1})(n^{-1}), h^{-1})\).

The projection \((n,h)\mapsto h\) is a surjective homomorphism with
kernel \(N \times \set{e}\), which is therefore normal with quotient
\(H\); and \(\set{e}\times H\) is closed under the operation. The
product is componentwise exactly when \(\theta(h) = \id\) for all \(h\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-semidirect-product-is-a-group, thm-internal-direct-product
#+NAME: thm-recognition-of-semidirect-products
#+begin_theorem
Let \(N \normalin G\) and \(H \le G\) with \(N \cap H = \set{e}\) and
\(NH = G\). Then \(G \iso N \rtimes_{\theta} H\), where
\(\theta(h)(n) = hnh^{-1}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Conjugation by \(h\) maps \(N\) to itself by normality and is an
automorphism of it, and \(\theta\) is a homomorphism because
\((h_{1}h_{2})n(h_{1}h_{2})^{-1} = h_{1}(h_{2}nh_{2}^{-1})h_{1}^{-1}\).

Define \(\Phi(n,h) = nh\). It is surjective since \(NH = G\), and
injective since \(n_{1}h_{1} = n_{2}h_{2}\) gives
\(n_{2}^{-1}n_{1} = h_{2}h_{1}^{-1} \in N \cap H = \set{e}\). It is a
homomorphism:
\(\Phi(n_{1},h_{1})\Phi(n_{2},h_{2}) = n_{1}h_{1}n_{2}h_{2} = n_{1}(h_{1}n_{2}h_{1}^{-1})h_{1}h_{2} = \Phi\bigl((n_{1},h_{1})(n_{2},h_{2})\bigr)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-recognition-of-semidirect-products, thm-cauchy, cor-groups-of-prime-order
#+NAME: cor-groups-of-order-2p
#+begin_corollary
Let \(p\) be an odd prime. A group of order \(2p\) is either cyclic or
isomorphic to the semidirect product \(\Z/p\Z \rtimes \Z/2\Z\) in which
the generator of order \(2\) inverts the generator of order \(p\) -- the
dihedral group.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
By Cauchy's theorem there are elements \(a\) of order \(p\) and \(b\) of
order \(2\). The subgroup \(N = \langle a\rangle\) has index \(2\),
hence is normal: its two left cosets are \(N\) and \(G\setminus N\), and
the same holds for right cosets, so \(gN = Ng\) for all \(g\). Also
\(H = \langle b\rangle\) meets \(N\) trivially, by orders, and
\(NH\) has \(2p\) elements, so \(NH = G\). The recognition theorem gives
\(G \iso N \rtimes_{\theta} H\), and \(\theta(b)\) is an automorphism of
\(\Z/p\Z\) of order dividing \(2\), so \(a \mapsto a\) or
\(a \mapsto a^{-1}\). The first case makes the product direct, and then
\(ab\) has order \(2p\), so \(G\) is cyclic; the second is the dihedral
group.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The two cases are genuinely different groups with the same normal
subgroup and the same quotient, which is the point: an extension is not
determined by its ends. Classifying all the ways of assembling \(N\) and
\(H\) -- including those with no complementary subgroup at all, so not
semidirect -- is the subject of group cohomology.
#+end_remark
