#+TITLE: Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 40A05
#+HASH: 1dc0e2b343c4

Boundedness alone gives no limit: \((-1)^{n}\) is bounded and does not
converge. What it does give is a /subsequence/ that converges, and that
is enough for almost every compactness argument in a first course.

#+NAME: def-subsequence
#+begin_definition
Let \((a_{n})_{n \in \N}\) be a sequence and let
\(n_{1} < n_{2} < n_{3} < \cdots\) be a strictly increasing sequence of
indices. The sequence \((a_{n_{k}})_{k \in \N}\) is a /subsequence/ of
\((a_{n})\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-subsequence
#+NAME: lem-monotone-subsequence
#+begin_lemma
Every sequence of real numbers has a monotone subsequence.
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
Call an index \(n\) a /peak/ of \((a_{n})\) if \(a_{m} \le a_{n}\) for
every \(m > n\). If there are infinitely many peaks
\(n_{1} < n_{2} < \cdots\), then \((a_{n_{k}})\) is decreasing by the
definition of a peak. If there are only finitely many, let \(N\) exceed
them all. Since \(N\) is not a peak there is \(n_{1} > N\) with
\(a_{n_{1}} > a_{N}\); since \(n_{1}\) is not a peak either there is
\(n_{2} > n_{1}\) with \(a_{n_{2}} > a_{n_{1}}\), and inductively one
obtains a strictly increasing subsequence.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: lem-monotone-subsequence, thm-monotone-convergence, def-bounded-sequence
#+NAME: thm-bolzano-weierstrass
#+begin_theorem
Every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \((a_{n})\) be bounded. By the lemma it has a monotone subsequence
\((a_{n_{k}})\), which is bounded because \((a_{n})\) is. A bounded
monotone sequence converges, by the monotone convergence theorem, so
\((a_{n_{k}})\) converges.
#+end_proof
