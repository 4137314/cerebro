#+TITLE: Conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60A10, 46C05
#+HASH: 5ab1a81ae1fc

Conditioning on an event of positive probability is a definition;
conditioning on a random variable, whose every value may have
probability zero, is not. The construction that works is geometric: the
conditional expectation is the best square-integrable approximation to
\(X\) among the variables that depend only on the given information, and
the [[id:642c93ab-d004-4829-b5fa-899759923d63][Hilbert space]] projection theorem is what makes it exist.

#+DEPENDS: def-sigma-algebra, def-random-variable, def-l2, thm-riesz-fischer
#+NAME: def-sub-sigma-algebra-and-l2
#+begin_definition
Let \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\Prob)\) be a probability space and
\(\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\) a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra -- the
/information/. Write \(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) for the square-integrable
random variables that are \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, taken modulo
equality almost surely.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-sub-sigma-algebra-and-l2, thm-riesz-fischer, thm-measurability-is-preserved-by-limits
#+NAME: prop-l2-of-a-sub-sigma-algebra-is-closed
#+begin_proposition
\(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) is a closed subspace of \(L^{2}(\mathcal{F})\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
It is a subspace: sums and scalar multiples of \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable
functions are \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable. It is closed: if
\(Y_{n} \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) converges in \(L^{2}\) to \(Y\), then
by the Riesz-Fischer theorem some subsequence converges to \(Y\) almost
surely; a pointwise limit of \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable functions is
\(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable, and changing \(Y\) on a null set does not
change its class.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-l2-of-a-sub-sigma-algebra-is-closed, thm-orthogonal-projection-in-a-hilbert-space
#+NAME: thm-conditional-expectation-exists
#+begin_theorem
For every \(X \in L^{2}(\mathcal{F})\) there is a unique
\(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}] \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\), the /conditional
expectation/, such that
\[ \E\bigl[(X - \E[X\mid\mathcal{G}])\,Y\bigr] = 0 \qquad \text{for every } Y \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G}); \]
equivalently, it is the element of \(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) nearest to
\(X\). It is linear in \(X\) and satisfies
\(\E[\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]] = \E[X]\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
\(L^{2}(\mathcal{F})\) is a Hilbert space and \(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) a
closed subspace, so the projection theorem gives a unique nearest point
\(P X\), characterised by \(X - PX \perp L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) -- which
is the displayed identity, the inner product being
\(\inner{U}{V} = \E[UV]\). The projection is linear. Taking
\(Y = 1\), which lies in \(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) because the space has
finite measure, gives \(\E[X - PX] = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-conditional-expectation-exists
#+NAME: prop-tower-property
#+begin_proposition
Let \(\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\). Then
\(\E[\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]\mid\mathcal{H}] = \E[X\mid\mathcal{H}]\)
almost surely. If \(X\) is itself \(\mathcal{G}\)-measurable then
\(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}] = X\); and if \(X\) is independent of
\(\mathcal{G}\), in the sense that \(\E[XY] = \E[X]\E[Y]\) for every
\(Y \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\), then \(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}] = \E[X]\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For \(Y \in L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\), the
defining identity for \(\mathcal{G}\) gives
\(\E[(X-\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}])Y] = 0\), so \(\E[XY] = \E[\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]Y]\).
Hence \(X\) and \(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]\) have the same inner products
against \(L^{2}(\mathcal{H})\) and therefore the same projection onto
it, which is the tower property.

If \(X \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\) it is its own nearest point. If \(X\) is
independent of \(\mathcal{G}\), then for every
\(Y \in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\),
\(\E[(X-\E[X])Y] = \E[X]\E[Y]-\E[X]\E[Y] = 0\), and the constant
\(\E[X]\) lies in \(L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\); uniqueness identifies it as
the projection.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-conditional-expectation-exists, prop-pythagoras, def-variance-and-covariance
#+NAME: thm-variance-decomposition
#+begin_theorem
For \(X \in L^{2}\) and a sub-\(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal{G}\),
\[ \Var(X) = \Var\bigl(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]\bigr) + \E\bigl[(X - \E[X\mid\mathcal{G}])^{2}\bigr], \]
so \(\Var(\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]) \le \Var(X)\): conditioning cannot
increase the variance.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Assume \(\E[X] = 0\), which changes neither side. Write
\(X = \E[X\mid\mathcal{G}] + (X - \E[X\mid\mathcal{G}])\), an orthogonal
decomposition in \(L^{2}\) by the defining identity. Pythagoras gives
\(\E[X^{2}] = \E[\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]^{2}] + \E[(X-\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}])^{2}]\),
and both squared terms are variances because
\(\E[\E[X\mid\mathcal{G}]] = \E[X] = 0\). The inequality follows since
the second summand is non-negative.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Restricting to square-integrable \(X\) is what allows the geometric
construction, and it is a genuine restriction: the general definition,
for integrable \(X\), is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\(A \mapsto \E[X\indicator_{A}]\) on \(\mathcal{G}\), and it agrees with
this one where both apply. The variance decomposition above is the
identity that makes the [[id:2cbd79ad-4938-4b5b-a6ca-85d300cc1f79][Rao-Blackwell theorem]] a one-line consequence.
#+end_remark
