#+TITLE: Euler's equations and the stability of free rotation
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:classical_mechanics:
#+MSC: 70E17, 70E50
#+HASH: ce6d42acbb1b

Angular momentum is conserved in space; the [[id:7ee7ed76-454a-4874-89ce-b66b97c2a7ab][inertia tensor]] is constant
in the body. Writing the first fact in the second frame produces Euler's
equations, a closed nonlinear system in three unknowns, and their two
conserved quantities are enough to decide the stability of rotation
about each principal axis -- with the famous conclusion that the middle
one is unstable.

#+DEPENDS: def-inertia-tensor, cor-principal-axes-of-inertia, prop-angular-velocity-is-well-defined
#+NAME: thm-euler-equations
#+begin_theorem
Let a rigid body rotate freely about a fixed origin, with no applied
torque, and let \(\Omega = (\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3})\) be the
angular velocity in principal body coordinates. Then
\[ I_{1}\dot{\Omega}_{1} = (I_{2}-I_{3})\Omega_{2}\Omega_{3}, \quad I_{2}\dot{\Omega}_{2} = (I_{3}-I_{1})\Omega_{3}\Omega_{1}, \quad I_{3}\dot{\Omega}_{3} = (I_{1}-I_{2})\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The angular momentum in space, \(\ell = RL\) with \(L = I\Omega\) in
body coordinates, is constant, so \(0 = \dot{R}L + R\dot{L}\).
Multiplying by \(R^{\mathsf{T}}\) and using
\(R^{\mathsf{T}}\dot{R}\,v = \Omega\times v\) -- the body-frame form of
the previous note's identity -- gives
\(\dot{L} + \Omega \times L = 0\).

In principal coordinates \(L = (I_{1}\Omega_{1},I_{2}\Omega_{2},I_{3}\Omega_{3})\)
with the \(I_{k}\) constant, so \(\dot{L} = (I_{k}\dot{\Omega}_{k})_{k}\);
computing the cross product componentwise gives the three displayed
equations.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-euler-equations, thm-kinetic-energy-of-a-rigid-body
#+NAME: prop-two-conserved-quantities
#+begin_proposition
Along a solution of Euler's equations both
\(2T = \sum_{k}I_{k}\Omega_{k}^{2}\) and
\(\abs{L}^{2} = \sum_{k}I_{k}^{2}\Omega_{k}^{2}\) are constant.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Differentiate and substitute. For the energy,
\(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\sum_{k}I_{k}\Omega_{k}^{2} = 2\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}I_{k}\dot{\Omega}_{k} = 2\bigl[(I_{2}-I_{3})+(I_{3}-I_{1})+(I_{1}-I_{2})\bigr]\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\Omega_{3} = 0\).
For the momentum,
\(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\sum_{k}I_{k}^{2}\Omega_{k}^{2} = 2\sum_{k}I_{k}\Omega_{k}\,I_{k}\dot{\Omega}_{k}\), and the
same cancellation occurs with the factors \(I_{k}\) redistributed.
Alternatively, \(\abs{L}\) is constant because \(\ell = RL\) is constant
and \(R\) is an isometry.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-two-conserved-quantities, thm-euler-equations, def-equilibrium-and-stability, thm-lagrange-dirichlet
#+NAME: thm-tennis-racket
#+begin_theorem
Suppose \(I_{1}<I_{2}<I_{3}\). Steady rotation about the axis of least
or greatest moment -- the equilibria \(\Omega = (\Omega_{1},0,0)\) and
\((0,0,\Omega_{3})\) of Euler's equations -- is stable; steady rotation
about the intermediate axis is unstable.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The three coordinate axes are equilibria: on each, two of the three
right-hand sides vanish because two components are zero, and the third
vanishes because it carries the product of those two.

/Stability of the extreme axes./ Consider the function
\(F = \abs{L}^{2} - 2 I_{1}T = \sum_{k}I_{k}(I_{k}-I_{1})\Omega_{k}^{2}\),
a combination of two conserved quantities, hence conserved. Its
coefficients are \(0, I_{2}(I_{2}-I_{1}), I_{3}(I_{3}-I_{1})\), all
non-negative and the last two positive, so \(F\) is a positive definite
function of \((\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3})\) vanishing on the first axis.
Being conserved, it confines a motion starting with \(F\) small to the
set where \(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3}\) stay small, which together with
conservation of \(T\) keeps \(\Omega_{1}\) near its initial value: this
is stability, by the same trapping argument as in Lagrange-Dirichlet.
The third axis is symmetric, using \(2I_{3}T - \abs{L}^{2}\).

/Instability of the middle axis./ Linearise about
\((0,\Omega_{2}^{0},0)\) with \(\Omega_{2}^{0}\ne0\): writing
\(\Omega = (u_{1},\Omega_{2}^{0}+u_{2},u_{3})\) and dropping quadratic
terms,
\(I_{1}\dot{u}_{1} = (I_{2}-I_{3})\Omega_{2}^{0}u_{3}\),
\(I_{3}\dot{u}_{3} = (I_{1}-I_{2})\Omega_{2}^{0}u_{1}\), and
\(\dot{u}_{2} = 0\). Differentiating the first and substituting the
second gives \(\ddot{u}_{1} = \kappa u_{1}\) with
\(\kappa = \frac{(I_{2}-I_{3})(I_{1}-I_{2})}{I_{1}I_{3}}(\Omega_{2}^{0})^{2}>0\),
since both factors in the numerator are negative. So the linearisation
has a solution growing like \(e^{\sqrt{\kappa}t}\), and initial
conditions arbitrarily close to the equilibrium leave any fixed
neighbourhood.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The instability is genuinely nonlinear in its consequences but linear in
its cause -- one positive eigenvalue -- and it is the standard
demonstration that conservation laws alone do not decide stability:
energy and angular momentum are conserved on all three axes, and what
separates them is which combination of the two happens to be definite.
The intermediate axis is the one for which no combination is.
#+end_remark
