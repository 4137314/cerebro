#+TITLE: Series and convergence of the partial sums
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 40A05
#+HASH: 8c7cf1c9fc06

A series is not a new object: it is a sequence, the sequence of its
partial sums, and every statement about series is a statement about that
sequence. Keeping this in view is what makes the convergence tests
proofs rather than recipes.

#+DEPENDS: def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: def-series-convergence
#+begin_definition
Given a sequence \((a_{n})_{n \ge 1}\) of real numbers, its /partial
sums/ are \(s_{N} = \sum_{n=1}^{N} a_{n}\). The /series/
\(\sum_{n \ge 1} a_{n}\) /converges/ to \(s \in \R\) if
\(s_{N} \to s\), and /diverges/ otherwise.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-series-convergence
#+NAME: prop-term-test
#+begin_proposition
If \(\sum_{n \ge 1} a_{n}\) converges, then \(a_{n} \to 0\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \(s_{N} \to s\). Then \(a_{N} = s_{N} - s_{N-1} \to s - s = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The converse fails, and failing to notice this is the standard first
error: the harmonic series \(\sum 1/n\) has terms tending to \(0\) and
diverges, since \(s_{2^{k}} \ge 1 + k/2\).
#+end_remark

#+DEPENDS: def-series-convergence, prop-term-test
#+NAME: thm-geometric-series
#+begin_theorem
Let \(x \in \R\). The series \(\sum_{n \ge 0} x^{n}\) converges if and
only if \(\abs{x} < 1\), and then
\[ \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} x^{n} = \frac{1}{1 - x}. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For \(x \ne 1\) the partial sums satisfy
\((1 - x) s_{N} = 1 - x^{N+1}\), hence
\(s_{N} = (1 - x^{N+1})/(1 - x)\). If \(\abs{x} < 1\) then
\(x^{N+1} \to 0\), so \(s_{N} \to 1/(1-x)\). If \(\abs{x} \ge 1\) then
\(\abs{x^{n}} \ge 1\) for every \(n\), so the terms do not tend to
\(0\) and the series diverges by the term test; for \(x = 1\) the
partial sums are \(N + 1\) directly.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
This is the comparison object the ratio and root tests are calibrated
against: both work by dominating a series eventually by a geometric one
with ratio \(r < 1\), so everything they can prove is a consequence of
this identity.
#+end_remark
