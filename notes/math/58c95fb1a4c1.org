#+TITLE: Connectedness, components and path-connectedness
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54D05
#+HASH: 58c95fb1a4c1

The [[id:f0cf6f7b-7984-4e52-9ef1-6fb39bf8058b][intermediate value theorem]] was proved from the completeness of
\(\R\), but its statement mentions no supremum: a continuous function
cannot skip a value because an interval cannot be cut in two. Isolating
that as a property of spaces turns one theorem about \(\R\) into the
first topological invariant, and gives the standard way of proving two
spaces are not homeomorphic.

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space
#+NAME: def-connected
#+begin_definition
A topological space \(X\) is /connected/ if it is not the union of two
disjoint non-empty open sets. A subset is connected if it is so in the
subspace topology. A subset that is both open and closed is /clopen/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-connected, prop-interior-and-closure-are-extremal
#+NAME: prop-connected-iff-no-proper-clopen
#+begin_proposition
\(X\) is connected if and only if its only clopen subsets are
\(\emptyset\) and \(X\); equivalently, if and only if every continuous
map \(X \to \set{0,1}\), the latter discrete, is constant.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
A clopen \(A\) with \(\emptyset \ne A \ne X\) gives the disconnection
\(X = A \cup (X \setminus A)\) into non-empty open sets, and conversely
each piece of a disconnection is clopen, being the complement of the
other. A continuous \(f \colon X \to \set{0,1}\) has
\(f^{-1}(0)\) clopen, and a non-constant \(f\) makes it a proper
non-empty clopen set; conversely a proper clopen \(A\) yields the
continuous non-constant indicator of \(A\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-connected, prop-connected-iff-no-proper-clopen, def-continuous-map
#+NAME: thm-continuous-image-of-a-connected-space
#+begin_theorem
The continuous image of a connected space is connected. Connectedness is
therefore a topological invariant.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(f \colon X \to Y\) be continuous and surjective with \(X\)
connected, and let \(g \colon Y \to \set{0,1}\) be continuous. Then
\(g \circ f\) is continuous, hence constant, and \(f\) is surjective, so
\(g\) is constant. By the previous proposition \(Y\) is connected. A
homeomorphism is in particular a continuous surjection in both
directions.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-connected, prop-connected-iff-no-proper-clopen
#+NAME: prop-union-of-connected-sets
#+begin_proposition
If \((A_{i})\) are connected subsets of \(X\) with a common point \(p\),
then \(\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\) is connected. If \(A\) is connected and
\(A \subseteq B \subseteq \overline{A}\), then \(B\) is connected.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \(f\) be a continuous map from the union to \(\set{0,1}\). Its
restriction to each \(A_{i}\) is continuous, hence constant, with value
\(f(p)\); so \(f\) is constant.

For the second claim, let \(f \colon B \to \set{0,1}\) be continuous;
it is constant on \(A\), say with value \(0\), so
\(A \subseteq f^{-1}(0)\), which is closed in \(B\). Hence
\(f^{-1}(0)\) contains the closure of \(A\) in \(B\), which is
\(\overline{A}\cap B = B\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-connected, def-supremum, ax-completeness
#+NAME: thm-connected-subsets-of-the-line
#+begin_theorem
A subset of \(\R\) is connected if and only if it is an interval, that
is, if \(x < z < y\) with \(x, y \in A\) implies \(z \in A\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(A\) is not an interval, pick such an \(x < z < y\) with
\(z \notin A\); then
\(A = (A \cap (-\infty, z)) \cup (A \cap (z, \infty))\) is a
disconnection into non-empty relatively open sets.

Conversely let \(A\) be an interval and suppose
\(A = U \cup V\) with \(U, V\) non-empty, disjoint and open in \(A\).
Pick \(x \in U\), \(y \in V\), say \(x < y\); then
\([x,y] \subseteq A\). Let \(s = \sup (U \cap [x,y])\), which exists by
completeness. If \(s \in U\) then \(s < y\), and since \(U\) is open in
\(A\) a whole interval \([s, s+\delta] \subseteq U \cap [x,y]\) would
exceed the supremum. If \(s \in V\) then \(s > x\), and openness of
\(V\) puts \((s-\delta, s] \subseteq V\), so no point of
\(U\cap[x,y]\) exceeds \(s - \delta\), again contradicting the
supremum. But \(s \in [x,y] \subseteq A = U \cup V\) -- a contradiction.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-connected-subsets-of-the-line, thm-continuous-image-of-a-connected-space, def-continuous-map
#+NAME: def-path-connected
#+begin_definition
A /path/ in \(X\) from \(x\) to \(y\) is a continuous
\(\gamma \colon [0,1] \to X\) with \(\gamma(0) = x\) and
\(\gamma(1) = y\). The space is /path-connected/ if any two of its
points are joined by a path.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-path-connected, prop-union-of-connected-sets, thm-connected-subsets-of-the-line, thm-continuous-image-of-a-connected-space
#+NAME: prop-path-connected-implies-connected
#+begin_proposition
Every path-connected space is connected.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
If \(X\) is empty the claim is vacuous. Otherwise fix \(p \in X\). For
each \(x\) choose a path from \(p\) to \(x\); its image is the
continuous image of \([0,1]\), which is connected as an interval, and
contains \(p\). So \(X\) is a union of connected sets with the common
point \(p\), hence connected.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-union-of-connected-sets, thm-equivalence-partition
#+NAME: prop-connected-components
#+begin_proposition
The relation "\(x\) and \(y\) lie in a common connected subset" is an
equivalence relation; its classes, the /connected components/, are the
maximal connected subsets of \(X\), and they are closed and partition
\(X\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Reflexivity uses the connected set \(\set{x}\); symmetry is clear; and
if \(x, y \in A\) and \(y, z \in B\) with \(A, B\) connected, then
\(A \cup B\) is connected by the previous proposition, having the point
\(y\) in common. The class of \(x\) is the union of all connected sets
containing \(x\), connected for the same reason, hence the largest such
set. Classes partition \(X\), and each is closed because its closure is
connected and contains it, so equals it by maximality.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Components are closed but need not be open: in \(\Q\) every component is
a single point, and no point is open. That example is also the reason
connectedness is not the same as path-connectedness -- the standard
counterexample being the closure of the graph of \(\sin(1/x)\), which is
connected by the second half of the union proposition and admits no path
to the segment it accumulates on.
#+end_remark
