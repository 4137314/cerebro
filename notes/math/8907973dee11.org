#+TITLE: The Rao-Blackwell theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:statistics:
#+MSC: 62F10
#+HASH: 8907973dee11

Given any unbiased estimator, however crude, conditioning it on a
sufficient statistic produces another unbiased estimator that is never
worse and is usually strictly better. The proof is the variance
decomposition of [[id:3c9568ab-072c-4973-9f6b-7506e431be22][conditional expectation]] -- Pythagoras, in the space of
square-integrable random variables -- and sufficiency is used exactly
once, to guarantee that the result is a statistic at all.

#+DEPENDS: def-sufficient-statistic, thm-conditional-expectation-exists, def-estimator
#+NAME: prop-conditioning-on-a-sufficient-statistic-is-a-statistic
#+begin_proposition
Let \(T\) be sufficient and \(U\) a square-integrable estimator. Then
\(\E_{\theta}[U \mid T]\) does not depend on \(\theta\), so it is a
statistic; write it \(U^{*}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
On a countable sample space the conditional expectation given \(T\) is
computed from the conditional probabilities:
\(\E_{\theta}[U\mid T=t] = \sum_{x}U(x)\Prob_{\theta}(X=x\mid T=t)\).
By sufficiency each weight is free of \(\theta\), hence so is the sum,
and the resulting function of \(t\) is measurable with respect to the
\(\sigma\)-algebra generated by \(T\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-conditioning-on-a-sufficient-statistic-is-a-statistic, thm-variance-decomposition, prop-tower-property, prop-bias-variance-decomposition
#+NAME: thm-rao-blackwell
#+begin_theorem
Let \(U\) be a square-integrable unbiased estimator of \(g(\theta)\) and
\(T\) sufficient. Then \(U^{*} = \E[U\mid T]\) is an unbiased estimator
of \(g(\theta)\) and
\[ \Var_{\theta}(U^{*}) \le \Var_{\theta}(U) \qquad \text{for every } \theta, \]
with equality if and only if \(U = U^{*}\) almost surely under
\(\Prob_{\theta}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
\(U^{*}\) is a statistic by the previous proposition, and it is
unbiased because \(\E_{\theta}[U^{*}] = \E_{\theta}[U] = g(\theta)\) by
the tower property with the trivial \(\sigma\)-algebra.

For the variances, the decomposition of the previous note applied under
\(\Prob_{\theta}\) with \(\mathcal{G}\) the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated
by \(T\) gives
\(\Var_{\theta}(U) = \Var_{\theta}(U^{*}) + \E_{\theta}[(U-U^{*})^{2}]\).
The last term is non-negative, giving the inequality, and it vanishes
exactly when \(U = U^{*}\) almost surely. Since both estimators are
unbiased, the same comparison holds for the mean squared errors.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-rao-blackwell, def-estimator
#+NAME: cor-optimal-estimators-are-functions-of-a-sufficient-statistic
#+begin_corollary
If an unbiased estimator has smallest variance among all unbiased
estimators, then it is almost surely a function of every sufficient
statistic.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Let \(U\) be such an estimator and \(T\) sufficient. Then \(U^{*}\) is
unbiased with \(\Var_{\theta}(U^{*})\le\Var_{\theta}(U)\), and
minimality forces equality; by the equality case \(U = U^{*}\) almost
surely, and \(U^{*}\) is a function of \(T\) by construction.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The theorem says where to look for a good estimator and not how to
recognise the best one: two different unbiased functions of the same
sufficient statistic are not compared by it. What settles that is
completeness of the statistic, under which the conditioned estimator is
unique and hence optimal -- the Lehmann-Scheffe theorem, which is the
natural next note and is not written yet.
#+end_remark
