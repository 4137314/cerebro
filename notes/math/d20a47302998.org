A /binary operation/ is the single ingredient from which every algebraic
structure on this shelf is built: a rule that combines two elements of a
set into a third element /of the same set/.

#+NAME: def-binary-operation
#+begin_definition
Let \(S\) be a set. A *binary operation* on \(S\) is a function
\[
  \ast \colon S \times S \to S .
\]
One writes \(a \ast b\) for \(\ast(a, b)\). That the codomain is again
\(S\) is the /closure/ requirement: \(a \ast b\) never leaves \(S\).
#+end_definition

A binary operation \(\ast\) on \(S\) may in addition be /associative/
(\((a \ast b) \ast c = a \ast (b \ast c)\)), /commutative/
(\(a \ast b = b \ast a\)), or admit an /identity/ \(e\) with
\(e \ast a = a \ast e = a\). These optional properties are exactly the
axioms singled out one at a time by the structures that follow.
