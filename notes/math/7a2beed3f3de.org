#+TITLE: Small oscillations and normal modes
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:classical_mechanics:
#+MSC: 70J10, 15A22
#+HASH: 7a2beed3f3de

Near a stable equilibrium a mechanical system is, to first order, a
collection of independent harmonic oscillators. Producing them is a
purely algebraic statement -- two quadratic forms, one of them positive
definite, can be diagonalised simultaneously -- and it is the
[[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][spectral theorem]] applied after a change of variables that makes the
kinetic form the identity.

#+DEPENDS: def-bilinear-form, thm-spectral-theorem, thm-diagonalisation-of-symmetric-forms
#+NAME: thm-simultaneous-diagonalisation
#+begin_theorem
Let \(A, B\) be real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrices with \(A\)
positive definite. Then there is an invertible \(S\) with
\(S^{\mathsf{T}}AS = I\) and \(S^{\mathsf{T}}BS\) diagonal, with
diagonal entries the roots \(\lambda\) of \(\det(B-\lambda A) = 0\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Since \(A\) is symmetric positive definite, the spectral theorem gives
\(A = Q\Lambda Q^{\mathsf{T}}\) with \(Q\) orthogonal and \(\Lambda\)
diagonal with positive entries; put
\(A^{-1/2} = Q\Lambda^{-1/2}Q^{\mathsf{T}}\), symmetric with
\(A^{-1/2}AA^{-1/2} = I\). The matrix \(C = A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\) is
symmetric, so \(C = R D R^{\mathsf{T}}\) with \(R\) orthogonal and
\(D\) diagonal. Put \(S = A^{-1/2}R\): then
\(S^{\mathsf{T}}AS = R^{\mathsf{T}}R = I\) and
\(S^{\mathsf{T}}BS = D\).

The diagonal entries are the eigenvalues of \(C\), and
\(\det(B-\lambda A) = \det(A^{1/2})^{2}\det(C-\lambda I)\), so they are
exactly the roots of that polynomial.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-simultaneous-diagonalisation, def-equilibrium-and-stability, thm-lagrange-dirichlet, thm-taylor-peano
#+NAME: thm-normal-modes
#+begin_theorem
Let \(q_{0}\) be an equilibrium of a natural system with \(V\) of class
\(C^{2}\), and set \(A = A(q_{0})\) and \(B = D^{2}V(q_{0})\). The
linearisation of the equations of motion at \(q_{0}\) is
\(A\ddot{u} = -Bu\), and in the coordinates \(u = S\eta\) of the
previous theorem it decouples into
\(\ddot{\eta}_{k} = -\lambda_{k}\eta_{k}\). If all \(\lambda_{k}>0\) the
solutions are superpositions of /normal modes/
\(\eta_{k}(t) = c_{k}\cos(\omega_{k}t+\varphi_{k})\) with frequencies
\(\omega_{k} = \sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Write \(q = q_{0}+u\). Expanding the Lagrangian to second order in
\((u,\dot{u})\) by Taylor's theorem, the kinetic term is
\(\tfrac{1}{2}\inner{A\dot{u}}{\dot{u}}\) up to terms of higher order --
\(A(q)\) is continuous and \(\dot{u}\) already appears quadratically --
and the potential is
\(V(q_{0})+\tfrac{1}{2}\inner{Bu}{u}+o(\abs{u}^{2})\), the linear term
vanishing because \(q_{0}\) is a critical point. The Lagrange equations
of the quadratic Lagrangian are \(A\ddot{u} = -Bu\).

Substituting \(u = S\eta\) and multiplying by \(S^{\mathsf{T}}\) gives
\(S^{\mathsf{T}}AS\ddot{\eta} = -S^{\mathsf{T}}BS\eta\), that is
\(\ddot{\eta} = -D\eta\), one scalar equation per coordinate. For
\(\lambda_{k}>0\) the general solution of
\(\ddot{\eta}_{k}=-\lambda_{k}\eta_{k}\) is the displayed sinusoid, and
the general solution of the system is their superposition through
\(u = S\eta\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-normal-modes, thm-lagrange-dirichlet, prop-instability-from-a-maximum
#+NAME: cor-frequencies-and-stability
#+begin_corollary
All \(\lambda_{k}\) are positive exactly when \(D^{2}V(q_{0})\) is
positive definite, in which case \(q_{0}\) is a stable equilibrium of
the full nonlinear system; a negative \(\lambda_{k}\) makes it unstable.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
By the theorem the \(\lambda_{k}\) are the roots of
\(\det(B-\lambda A) = 0\), which are the eigenvalues of
\(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\); that matrix is positive definite exactly when
\(B\) is, since \(\inner{A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}x}{x} = \inner{B y}{y}\) with
\(y = A^{-1/2}x\) ranging over everything. A positive definite Hessian
makes \(q_{0}\) a strict local minimum of \(V\), by Taylor's theorem, so
Lagrange-Dirichlet gives stability; a negative eigenvalue gives
instability by the earlier proposition.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The normal modes are the physically meaningful objects and the
coordinates \(\eta\) are not orthonormal for anything except the kinetic
energy -- which is exactly the point of the simultaneous
diagonalisation: the two forms cannot in general be brought to the
identity together, and privileging the positive definite one is what
makes the frequencies, rather than the eigenvalues of \(B\) alone,
the invariants. The same algebra reappears whenever a quadratic energy
is minimised subject to a quadratic normalisation, from the generalised
eigenvalue problems of numerical analysis to the [[id:642c93ab-d004-4829-b5fa-899759923d63][Rayleigh quotient]].
#+end_remark
