#+TITLE: The weak and strong laws of large numbers
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60F05, 60F15
#+HASH: 8465ea9dd0d3

The statement that an average of independent measurements approaches the
mean is what makes probability a description of anything, and it comes
in two strengths that are genuinely different: the average is eventually
close /with high probability/, or it converges /for almost every
outcome/. The first is Chebyshev's inequality applied once; the second
needs the Borel-Cantelli lemma and a summable bound.

#+DEPENDS: def-independence-of-random-variables, cor-chebyshev-inequality, cor-variance-of-a-sum-of-independent-variables, def-modes-of-convergence
#+NAME: thm-weak-law-of-large-numbers
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X_{1},X_{2},\dots\) be independent, identically distributed and
square-integrable, with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^{2}\), and
let \(S_{n} = X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}\). Then \(S_{n}/n \to \mu\) in
probability: for every \(\eps>0\),
\(\Prob(\abs{S_{n}/n-\mu}\ge\eps) \le \sigma^{2}/(n\eps^{2}) \to 0\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By linearity \(\E[S_{n}/n] = \mu\), and by independence
\(\Var(S_{n}) = n\sigma^{2}\), so \(\Var(S_{n}/n) = \sigma^{2}/n\).
Chebyshev's inequality applied to \(S_{n}/n\) gives the displayed bound,
which tends to \(0\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-weak-law-of-large-numbers, lem-borel-cantelli-first, thm-expectation-of-a-product, cor-fast-convergence-in-probability-is-almost-sure
#+NAME: thm-strong-law-under-a-fourth-moment
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X_{1},X_{2},\dots\) be independent, identically distributed with
\(\E[X_{1}^{4}]<\infty\) and mean \(\mu\). Then \(S_{n}/n\to\mu\)
almost surely.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Replacing \(X_{i}\) by \(X_{i}-\mu\), which preserves independence and
the fourth-moment hypothesis, assume \(\mu = 0\).

Expand \(\E[S_{n}^{4}] = \sum \E[X_{i}X_{j}X_{k}X_{l}]\) over all
quadruples of indices. If some index occurs exactly once, the
corresponding factor is independent of the rest, so the expectation
factors and vanishes because \(\E[X_{i}] = 0\). The surviving terms are
those with all four indices equal -- there are \(n\) of them, each
\(\E[X_{1}^{4}]\) -- and those splitting into two pairs, of which there
are \(3n(n-1)\), each equal to \(\E[X_{1}^{2}]^{2}\) by independence.
Hence \(\E[S_{n}^{4}] \le Cn^{2}\) for a constant \(C\) depending only
on the law.

By Markov's inequality applied to \(S_{n}^{4}\), for every \(\eps>0\)
\[ \Prob\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{S_{n}}{n}\Bigr|>\eps\Bigr) = \Prob(S_{n}^{4}>\eps^{4}n^{4}) \le \frac{Cn^{2}}{\eps^{4}n^{4}} = \frac{C}{\eps^{4}n^{2}}, \]
and \(\sum_{n}n^{-2}<\infty\). The corollary to the first
Borel-Cantelli lemma then gives \(S_{n}/n\to0\) almost surely.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-strong-law-under-a-fourth-moment, def-modes-of-convergence
#+NAME: cor-frequencies-converge
#+begin_corollary
Let \((A_{n})\) be independent events all of the same probability
\(p\), and let \(N_{n}\) be the number of the first \(n\) that occur.
Then \(N_{n}/n \to p\) almost surely.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
The indicators \(\indicator_{A_{n}}\) are independent by the
proposition on functions of independent variables, identically
distributed, bounded and hence with finite fourth moment, and their
common mean is \(p\). Their partial sums are \(N_{n}\), so the strong
law applies.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The fourth moment is a convenience, not a necessity: Kolmogorov's strong
law needs only integrability, and Etemadi's proof needs only pairwise
independence. What is not a convenience is the distinction between the
two laws. The weak law says nothing about any single sequence of
outcomes; the strong law says that the set of outcomes along which the
averages fail to converge is null, which is the statement that justifies
reading a long run of measurements as an estimate.
#+end_remark
