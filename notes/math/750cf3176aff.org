#+TITLE: Interior, closure, boundary and density
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54A05
#+HASH: 750cf3176aff

Every subset of a topological space has a largest open set inside it and
a smallest closed set around it. The two operations are dual, they are
defined by intersections rather than by construction, and the local
characterisation below -- a point is in the closure when every
neighbourhood meets the set -- is what makes them usable in proofs.

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space, prop-closed-sets-of-a-topology
#+NAME: def-interior-closure-boundary
#+begin_definition
Let \(A \subseteq X\). The /interior/ \(A^{\circ}\) is the union of all
open sets contained in \(A\), the /closure/ \(\overline{A}\) is the
intersection of all closed sets containing \(A\), and the /boundary/ is
\(\partial A = \overline{A} \setminus A^{\circ}\). The set \(A\) is
/dense/ if \(\overline{A} = X\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-interior-closure-boundary
#+NAME: prop-interior-and-closure-are-extremal
#+begin_proposition
\(A^{\circ}\) is the largest open set contained in \(A\) and
\(\overline{A}\) the smallest closed set containing \(A\); \(A\) is open
if and only if \(A = A^{\circ}\), and closed if and only if
\(A = \overline{A}\). Moreover
\(X \setminus \overline{A} = (X\setminus A)^{\circ}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
A union of open sets is open and an intersection of closed sets is
closed, so \(A^{\circ}\) and \(\overline{A}\) are open and closed
respectively, and by construction each is extremal among the sets in its
definition. If \(A\) is open it is one of the sets united to form
\(A^{\circ}\), so \(A \subseteq A^{\circ} \subseteq A\); the converse is
immediate, and dually for closures. For the last identity, complements
turn the closed sets containing \(A\) into the open sets contained in
\(X \setminus A\), and intersection into union.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-interior-and-closure-are-extremal
#+NAME: thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+begin_theorem
\(x \in \overline{A}\) if and only if every neighbourhood of \(x\) meets
\(A\); and \(x \in A^{\circ}\) if and only if some neighbourhood of
\(x\) is contained in \(A\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose some open \(U \ni x\) misses \(A\). Then \(X \setminus U\) is a
closed set containing \(A\), so \(\overline{A} \subseteq X\setminus U\)
and \(x \notin \overline{A}\). Conversely if \(x \notin \overline{A}\)
then \(U = X \setminus \overline{A}\) is an open neighbourhood of \(x\)
missing \(A\). The statement about the interior is the definition of
\(A^{\circ}\) as a union of open sets.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+NAME: cor-dense-meets-every-open-set
#+begin_corollary
\(A\) is dense in \(X\) if and only if every non-empty open set meets
\(A\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Density says every point lies in \(\overline{A}\), which by the theorem
says every neighbourhood of every point meets \(A\); and a non-empty
open set is a neighbourhood of each of its points.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-interior-closure-boundary, prop-interior-and-closure-are-extremal
#+NAME: prop-boundary-properties
#+begin_proposition
\(\partial A\) is closed, \(\partial A = \overline{A} \cap \overline{X \setminus A}\),
and \(X\) is the disjoint union of \(A^{\circ}\), \(\partial A\) and
\((X\setminus A)^{\circ}\). A set is closed exactly when it contains its
boundary, and open exactly when it is disjoint from it.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Using \(X \setminus \overline{X\setminus A} = A^{\circ}\), we get
\(\overline{A}\cap\overline{X\setminus A} = \overline{A}\setminus A^{\circ} = \partial A\),
an intersection of closed sets, hence closed. The three sets are
pairwise disjoint by construction and cover \(X\), since a point outside
\(A^{\circ} \cup (X\setminus A)^{\circ}\) lies in
\(\overline{A}\cap\overline{X\setminus A}\).

If \(A\) is closed then \(\overline{A} = A\), so
\(\partial A \subseteq A\); conversely \(\partial A \subseteq A\) gives
\(\overline{A} = A^{\circ}\cup\partial A \subseteq A\). Applying this to
the complement gives the statement for open sets.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The closure is not "\(A\) together with its limit points of sequences":
that description is correct in a metric space and false in general,
because a sequence is indexed by \(\N\) and a neighbourhood basis need
not be countable. Nets and filters exist to repair exactly this, and the
theorem above is the statement that survives without them.
#+end_remark
