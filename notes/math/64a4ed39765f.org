#+TITLE: Affine spaces, affine subspaces and affine maps
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:linear_algebra:
#+MSC: 51N10, 15A03
#+HASH: 64a4ed39765f

A [[id:7c54b427-4d7a-4511-b374-58c7bb44fb84][vector space]] has a distinguished point, and geometry does not: no
point of the plane is the origin until one is chosen. An affine space is
what remains of a vector space when the origin is forgotten -- points,
and differences of points which are vectors -- and it is the right home
for everything stated in terms of lines, parallelism and barycentres.

#+DEPENDS: def-vector-space, def-group-action
#+NAME: def-affine-space
#+begin_definition
An /affine space/ over a field \(K\) is a non-empty set \(A\) together
with a vector space \(V\) over \(K\) and a map
\(A \times A \to V\), \((P,Q) \mapsto \vec{PQ}\), such that
\(\vec{PQ} + \vec{QR} = \vec{PR}\) for all \(P,Q,R\), and for each
\(P \in A\) the map \(Q \mapsto \vec{PQ}\) is a bijection \(A \to V\).
One writes \(Q = P + v\) for the inverse of that bijection, and
\(\dim A = \dim V\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-affine-space
#+NAME: prop-affine-elementary-identities
#+begin_proposition
In an affine space \(\vec{PP} = 0\) and \(\vec{QP} = -\vec{PQ}\); the
map \((P, v) \mapsto P + v\) is an action of the additive group of
\(V\) on \(A\), and it is transitive with trivial stabilisers.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Taking \(P = Q = R\) in the identity gives
\(\vec{PP} + \vec{PP} = \vec{PP}\), so \(\vec{PP} = 0\); then
\(\vec{PQ} + \vec{QP} = \vec{PP} = 0\). For the action,
\(P + 0 = P\) because \(\vec{PP} = 0\), and
\((P + v) + w = P + (v+w)\) because
\(\vec{P(P+v)} + \vec{(P+v)((P+v)+w)} = v + w\). Transitivity and
triviality of the stabilisers are the statement that
\(Q \mapsto \vec{PQ}\) is a bijection.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-affine-space, def-span-and-independence
#+NAME: def-affine-subspace
#+begin_definition
A subset \(S \subseteq A\) is an /affine subspace/ if it is non-empty
and \(\vec{S} = \setst{\vec{PQ}}{P,Q \in S}\) is a linear subspace of
\(V\) with \(S = P_{0} + \vec{S}\) for some (hence any)
\(P_{0} \in S\). Its /direction/ is \(\vec{S}\), and two affine
subspaces are /parallel/ if one direction contains the other.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-affine-subspace
#+NAME: prop-affine-subspaces-are-translates
#+begin_proposition
A non-empty \(S \subseteq A\) is an affine subspace if and only if
\(S = P_{0} + W\) for some \(P_{0} \in A\) and some linear subspace
\(W \subseteq V\), and then \(W = \vec{S}\) is determined by \(S\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
One direction is the definition. Conversely let \(S = P_{0} + W\). For
\(P = P_{0}+w\) and \(Q = P_{0}+w'\) in \(S\) we get
\(\vec{PQ} = w' - w \in W\), and every \(w \in W\) arises as
\(\vec{P_{0}(P_{0}+w)}\); hence \(\vec{S} = W\), a subspace, and
\(S = P_{0} + \vec{S}\). Since \(\vec{S}\) is defined from \(S\) alone,
\(W\) is determined.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-affine-space, def-linear-map
#+NAME: def-affine-map
#+begin_definition
A map \(f \colon A \to B\) between affine spaces over \(K\), with
directions \(V\) and \(W\), is /affine/ if there is a linear
\(\vec{f} \colon V \to W\) with
\(\vec{f(P)f(Q)} = \vec{f}(\vec{PQ})\) for all \(P, Q \in A\). The map
\(\vec{f}\) is the /linear part/ of \(f\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-affine-map, prop-affine-subspaces-are-translates
#+NAME: prop-affine-maps-preserve-barycentres
#+begin_proposition
The linear part of an affine map is unique; the composition of affine
maps is affine with linear part the composition of the linear parts; and
an affine map preserves barycentres:
\(f\bigl(P_{0} + \sum_{i} \lambda_{i} \vec{P_{0}P_{i}}\bigr) = f(P_{0}) + \sum_{i} \lambda_{i}\vec{f(P_{0})f(P_{i})}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Uniqueness: fixing \(P\), every \(v \in V\) is \(\vec{PQ}\) for exactly
one \(Q\), and \(\vec{f}(v)\) is then forced. Composition: if
\(\vec{f}\) and \(\vec{g}\) are the linear parts of \(f\) and \(g\),
then \(\vec{g(f(P))g(f(Q))} = \vec{g}(\vec{f}(\vec{PQ}))\). For
barycentres, put \(v = \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\vec{P_{0}P_{i}}\); then
\(f(P_{0}+v) = f(P_{0}) + \vec{f}(v)\) by definition of the linear part,
and \(\vec{f}(v) = \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\vec{f}(\vec{P_{0}P_{i}})\) by
linearity.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Choosing a point \(O \in A\) identifies \(A\) with \(V\) and every
affine map with \(x \mapsto \vec{f}(x) + b\). That is why affine
geometry can always be computed in coordinates -- and why one should
not: the statements that survive are exactly those, like the
preservation of barycentres, that never mention \(O\).
#+end_remark
