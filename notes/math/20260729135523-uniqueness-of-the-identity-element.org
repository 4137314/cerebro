#+TITLE: Uniqueness of the identity element
#+DATE: [2026-07-29 Wed 13:55]
#+FILETAGS: :evergreen:easy:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20A05

In a [[id:c74536f9-f1a8-4ce8-a26f-8ae6300aa2eb][group]] the identity is not merely assumed to exist -- it is forced
to be unique, which is what licenses the article in "/the/ identity".

#+DEPENDS: def-group
#+NAME: thm-uniqueness-of-identity
#+begin_theorem
Let \((G, \ast)\) be a [[id:c74536f9-f1a8-4ce8-a26f-8ae6300aa2eb][group]]. If \(e\) and \(e'\) are both identity
elements of \(G\), then \(e = e'\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Because \(e'\) is an identity, \(e \ast e' = e\). Because \(e\) is an
identity, \(e \ast e' = e'\). Therefore \(e = e \ast e' = e'\).
#+end_proof

The identical argument, applied to one element and two candidate
inverses, shows that inverses are unique as well.
