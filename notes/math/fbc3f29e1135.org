#+TITLE: Integration by parts and substitution
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A42
#+HASH: fbc3f29e1135

Neither of these is a technique. Each is a differentiation rule read
backwards through the fundamental theorem: the product rule becomes
integration by parts, the chain rule becomes substitution. Stating them
that way is what fixes the hypotheses -- the versions taught as
manipulations quietly assume a continuous derivative, and it is worth
seeing where that assumption enters.

#+DEPENDS: thm-derivative-algebra, thm-ftc-second, thm-integral-linearity, thm-continuous-is-integrable
#+NAME: thm-integration-by-parts
#+begin_theorem
Let \(u, v \colon [a,b] \to \R\) be differentiable with \(u'\) and
\(v'\) continuous. Then
\[ \int_{a}^{b} u(x) v'(x) \dd x = u(b) v(b) - u(a) v(a) - \int_{a}^{b} u'(x) v(x) \dd x. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The product rule gives \((uv)' = u'v + uv'\) on \([a,b]\). Both
\(u'v\) and \(uv'\) are continuous, hence integrable, and so is their
sum \((uv)'\). Since \(uv\) is an antiderivative of \((uv)'\), the
second fundamental theorem gives
\[ \int_{a}^{b} \bigl( u'(x) v(x) + u(x) v'(x) \bigr) \dd x = u(b) v(b) - u(a) v(a), \]
and linearity of the integral splits the left-hand side into the two
integrals of the claim.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-chain-rule, thm-ftc-first, thm-ftc-second, thm-continuous-is-integrable
#+NAME: thm-substitution
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\varphi \colon [a,b] \to \R\) be differentiable with
\(\varphi'\) continuous, and let \(f\) be continuous on an interval
containing \(\varphi([a,b])\). Then
\[ \int_{a}^{b} f\bigl( \varphi(x) \bigr) \varphi'(x) \dd x = \int_{\varphi(a)}^{\varphi(b)} f(y) \dd y. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(F(y) = \int_{\varphi(a)}^{y} f(t) \dd t\). Since \(f\) is
continuous, the first fundamental theorem gives \(F' = f\). By the
chain rule \(F \circ \varphi\) is differentiable with
\[ (F \circ \varphi)'(x) = F'\bigl( \varphi(x) \bigr) \varphi'(x) = f\bigl( \varphi(x) \bigr) \varphi'(x), \]
which is continuous and therefore integrable. Applying the second
fundamental theorem to \(F \circ \varphi\) on \([a,b]\),
\[ \int_{a}^{b} f\bigl( \varphi(x) \bigr) \varphi'(x) \dd x = F\bigl( \varphi(b) \bigr) - F\bigl( \varphi(a) \bigr) = \int_{\varphi(a)}^{\varphi(b)} f(y) \dd y, \]
since \(F(\varphi(a)) = 0\) by definition.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Note what the substitution theorem does /not/ require: \(\varphi\) need
not be injective, and \(\varphi(a)\) need not be less than
\(\varphi(b)\). The identity is between two numbers, and the
orientation is carried by the convention
\(\int_{\beta}^{\alpha} = -\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\). The version that
does need injectivity is the other direction -- rewriting
\(\int f(y) \dd y\) as an integral in \(x\), which requires
\(\varphi^{-1}\) to exist -- and conflating the two is the usual source
of a substitution that loses a sign.
#+end_remark
