#+TITLE: Partial differential equations
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:partial_differential_equations:
#+MSC: 35-XX
#+HASH: f7eaeb1f126b

There is no theory of partial differential equations, only theories of
particular ones, and the division that organises them is by how
information travels. Three model equations exhaust the second-order
linear cases, and each is met here through the statement that decides
uniqueness for it -- a maximum principle, an explicit formula, an energy
identity.

Part of [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][Analysis]].

* The four notes

- [[id:74f4a968-e34c-46de-a7f6-77c26d988326][First-order equations and the method of characteristics]] -- the
  equation constrains the derivative along one direction, so following
  that direction reduces it to an ordinary equation. Where the data
  surface is characteristic, the problem is ill posed, and this is
  the cleanest place to see why.
- [[id:ac600204-166d-4fc0-a452-3c3799428283][The heat equation]] (parabolic) -- separation of variables and the
  maximum principle. Instant smoothing forwards, ill posedness
  backwards, both readable in one exponential factor.
- [[id:97451c87-2d3d-4584-ad50-af7d219ae2a7][The wave equation]] (hyperbolic) -- d'Alembert's formula, finite
  propagation speed, and the energy identity that gives uniqueness with
  no formula at all.
- [[id:fe42fdf4-dd3b-4112-a197-9e3fadbacad6][Harmonic functions and the Dirichlet problem]] (elliptic) -- the mean
  value property, the maximum principle, and the rigidity that makes
  harmonic functions analytic.

* Two techniques, used throughout

Separation of variables produces a [[id:e57a531d-6f95-4eff-9ad8-7ae4b983f912][Sturm-Liouville problem]] whose
eigenfunctions are the building blocks, and reassembling the solution
needs their completeness -- which is stated there and not proved. The
[[id:f667edb9-9594-4469-a3cf-1dd0c497ae9e][Fourier transform]] does the same job on the whole line, turning
differentiation into multiplication and the equations into algebra.

* What is missing

Everything modern: weak solutions and Sobolev spaces, which is where the
subject actually lives; existence for the Dirichlet problem, of which
only uniqueness is proved here; Green's functions and fundamental
solutions; the classification of second-order equations by their
principal symbol; and nonlinear equations of any kind. What is present
is the classical core -- the three model equations and what can be said
about them with the [[id:b9ad54df-4188-44c8-bc78-494d9a6728c8][Fubini theorem]] and the fundamental theorem of
calculus.
