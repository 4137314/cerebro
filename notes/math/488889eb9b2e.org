#+TITLE: Surfaces: the two fundamental forms and Gauss curvature
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:differential_geometry:
#+MSC: 53A05
#+HASH: 488889eb9b2e

For a curve, curvature measured how fast the tangent turned. A surface
has a tangent /plane/, and how fast it turns is not a number but a
symmetric operator on that plane. The whole local theory follows from
that operator being self-adjoint: the [[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][spectral theorem]] then supplies
principal directions and two principal curvatures, and every other
curvature at the point is a function of those two.

#+DEPENDS: def-differentiable-multivariable, def-regular-curve, def-inner-product-space
#+NAME: def-regular-surface
#+begin_definition
A /parametrised surface/ is a smooth map
\(\sigma \colon U \to \R^{3}\) on an open \(U \subseteq \R^{2}\); it is
/regular/ at \(p\) if the partial derivatives \(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}\)
are linearly independent there. The /tangent plane/ \(T_{p}\) is their
span, and the /unit normal/ is
\(N = (\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v})/\norm{\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-regular-surface, def-bilinear-form, def-inner-product-space
#+NAME: def-first-fundamental-form
#+begin_definition
The /first fundamental form/ at \(p\) is the restriction of the
Euclidean inner product to \(T_{p}\); in the basis
\(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}\) its matrix has rows \((E, F)\) and
\((F, G)\), where
\(E = \inner{\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{u}}\),
\(F = \inner{\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{v}}\),
\(G = \inner{\sigma_{v}}{\sigma_{v}}\). It computes lengths of curves on
the surface and angles between them.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-regular-surface, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-shape-operator
#+begin_definition
The /Gauss map/ sends \(p\) to the unit normal \(N(p)\), a point of the
unit sphere. Its differential, viewed as a map \(T_{p}\to T_{p}\), is
minus the /shape operator/ \(S = -\dd N\); the /second fundamental
form/ is \(\mathrm{II}(X,Y) = \inner{S X}{Y}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-shape-operator, thm-continuous-partials-implies-differentiable, def-self-adjoint
#+NAME: prop-shape-operator-is-self-adjoint
#+begin_proposition
The differential of the Gauss map takes values in \(T_{p}\), and the
shape operator is self-adjoint: \(\inner{SX}{Y} = \inner{X}{SY}\) for
all \(X,Y \in T_{p}\). In the basis \(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}\),
\(\mathrm{II}\) has matrix with rows \((e,f)\) and \((f,g)\), where
\(e = \inner{\sigma_{uu}}{N}\), \(f = \inner{\sigma_{uv}}{N}\),
\(g = \inner{\sigma_{vv}}{N}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Differentiating \(\inner{N}{N} = 1\) gives \(\inner{\dd N(X)}{N} = 0\),
so \(\dd N(X) \in T_{p}\).

Differentiating \(\inner{N}{\sigma_{u}} = 0\) with respect to \(v\)
gives \(\inner{N_{v}}{\sigma_{u}} + \inner{N}{\sigma_{uv}} = 0\), and
differentiating \(\inner{N}{\sigma_{v}} = 0\) with respect to \(u\)
gives \(\inner{N_{u}}{\sigma_{v}} + \inner{N}{\sigma_{vu}} = 0\). Since
mixed partials of a smooth map agree, subtracting yields
\(\inner{N_{v}}{\sigma_{u}} = \inner{N_{u}}{\sigma_{v}}\), that is
\(\inner{S\sigma_{v}}{\sigma_{u}} = \inner{S\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{v}}\).
Bilinearity extends this from the basis to all of \(T_{p}\). The two
displayed identities also give
\(\inner{S\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{u}} = -\inner{N_{u}}{\sigma_{u}} = \inner{N}{\sigma_{uu}}\)
and similarly for the other entries.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-shape-operator-is-self-adjoint, thm-spectral-theorem, thm-determinant-is-multiplicative
#+NAME: def-principal-and-gauss-curvature
#+begin_definition
By the spectral theorem \(S\) has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
of \(T_{p}\); the eigenvalues \(k_{1} \ge k_{2}\) are the /principal
curvatures/ and their eigenvectors the /principal directions/. The
/Gauss curvature/ is \(K = \det S = k_{1}k_{2}\) and the /mean
curvature/ is \(H = \tfrac{1}{2}\tr S = \tfrac{1}{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-principal-and-gauss-curvature, def-curvature, prop-shape-operator-is-self-adjoint
#+NAME: thm-euler-normal-curvature
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X \in T_{p}\) be a unit vector and \(\gamma\) a unit-speed curve
on the surface with \(\gamma'(0) = X\). Then the component of
\(\gamma''(0)\) along \(N\) -- the /normal curvature/ in the direction
\(X\) -- equals \(\mathrm{II}(X,X)\), so it depends on \(X\) alone and
not on \(\gamma\); and writing \(X = \cos\theta\, X_{1} + \sin\theta\, X_{2}\)
in principal directions,
\(\mathrm{II}(X,X) = k_{1}\cos^{2}\theta + k_{2}\sin^{2}\theta\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Along \(\gamma\) we have \(\inner{\gamma'}{N} = 0\); differentiating,
\(\inner{\gamma''}{N} + \inner{\gamma'}{\dd N(\gamma')} = 0\), so
\(\inner{\gamma''(0)}{N} = \inner{X}{S X} = \mathrm{II}(X,X)\), which
involves \(\gamma\) only through \(X\).

For the second formula, expand in the orthonormal eigenbasis: since
\(SX_{i} = k_{i}X_{i}\) and the \(X_{i}\) are orthonormal,
\(\inner{SX}{X} = k_{1}\cos^{2}\theta + k_{2}\sin^{2}\theta\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-euler-normal-curvature, def-principal-and-gauss-curvature
#+NAME: cor-principal-curvatures-are-extremal
#+begin_corollary
The principal curvatures are the maximum and the minimum of the normal
curvature over unit tangent directions.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
By Euler's formula the normal curvature is
\(k_{2} + (k_{1}-k_{2})\cos^{2}\theta\), which ranges over
\([k_{2},k_{1}]\) as \(\theta\) varies, attaining the endpoints at the
principal directions.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Both fundamental forms are defined through the embedding in \(\R^{3}\),
so both would be expected to change if the surface were bent. Gauss'
Theorema Egregium says that \(K\), unlike \(H\), is computable from the
first form alone -- it survives any bending that preserves lengths. That
is why a sphere cannot be flattened onto a plane, and why every map of
the earth distorts something.
#+end_remark
