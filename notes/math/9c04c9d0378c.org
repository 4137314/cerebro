#+TITLE: Pointwise and uniform convergence
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 40A30, 26A15
#+HASH: 9c04c9d0378c

A sequence of functions has two obvious notions of convergence, and the
whole point of separating them is that the naive one preserves nothing.
Pointwise convergence is a statement about each point separately, made
with a different \(N\) at each; uniform convergence asks for one \(N\)
that works everywhere, and that single quantifier change is exactly what
carries continuity, integrability and the exchange of limits across the
limit.

#+DEPENDS: def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: def-pointwise-convergence
#+begin_definition
Functions \(f_{n} \colon D \to \R\) /converge pointwise/ to
\(f \colon D \to \R\) if \(f_{n}(x) \to f(x)\) for every \(x \in D\):
for each \(x\) and each \(\eps > 0\) there is \(N\), possibly depending
on \(x\), with \(\abs{f_{n}(x) - f(x)} < \eps\) for \(n \ge N\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-pointwise-convergence
#+NAME: def-uniform-convergence
#+begin_definition
The convergence is /uniform/ if the same \(N\) serves every point: for
every \(\eps > 0\) there is \(N\) such that
\(\abs{f_{n}(x) - f(x)} < \eps\) for all \(n \ge N\) and all
\(x \in D\). Equivalently
\(\sup_{x \in D} \abs{f_{n}(x) - f(x)} \to 0\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-uniform-convergence, def-continuity-at-point, thm-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: thm-uniform-limit-continuous
#+begin_theorem
A uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(f_{n} \to f\) uniformly with each \(f_{n}\) continuous, fix
\(x_{0} \in D\) and \(\eps > 0\). Choose \(n\) with
\(\abs{f_{n}(y) - f(y)} < \eps/3\) for every \(y \in D\), and then
\(\delta > 0\) from continuity of that one \(f_{n}\) at \(x_{0}\), so
that \(\abs{f_{n}(x) - f_{n}(x_{0})} < \eps/3\) for
\(\abs{x - x_{0}} < \delta\). For such \(x\),
\[ \abs{f(x) - f(x_{0})} \le \abs{f(x) - f_{n}(x)} + \abs{f_{n}(x) - f_{n}(x_{0})} + \abs{f_{n}(x_{0}) - f(x_{0})} < \eps. \]
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The three thirds are where uniformity is spent: the outer two need one
\(n\) that is simultaneously good at \(x\) and at \(x_{0}\), and
pointwise convergence supplies no such \(n\). This is the entire
difference between the two definitions, and every failure of pointwise
convergence to preserve anything is a failure to close this argument.
#+end_remark

#+DEPENDS: def-uniform-convergence, thm-cauchy-criterion
#+NAME: thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion
#+begin_theorem
Functions \(f_{n} \colon D \to \R\) converge uniformly to some limit if
and only if for every \(\eps > 0\) there is \(N\) with
\(\sup_{x \in D} \abs{f_{n}(x) - f_{m}(x)} < \eps\) for all
\(n, m \ge N\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(f_{n} \to f\) uniformly, take \(N\) beyond which the sup distance
to \(f\) is below \(\eps/2\); the triangle inequality gives the
condition. Conversely, the condition makes \((f_{n}(x))\) a Cauchy
sequence in \(\R\) for each fixed \(x\), so it converges to some
\(f(x)\) by the Cauchy criterion. Given \(\eps > 0\) take \(N\) from the
hypothesis; for \(n \ge N\) and any \(x\), letting \(m \to \infty\) in
\(\abs{f_{n}(x) - f_{m}(x)} < \eps\) gives
\(\abs{f_{n}(x) - f(x)} \le \eps\), uniformly in \(x\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion, thm-comparison-test
#+NAME: thm-weierstrass-m-test
#+begin_theorem
Let \(u_{n} \colon D \to \R\) satisfy \(\sup_{D} \abs{u_{n}} \le M_{n}\)
with \(\sum M_{n}\) convergent. Then \(\sum u_{n}\) converges uniformly
on \(D\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(s_{N} = \sum_{n \le N} u_{n}\). For \(m > n\) and every
\(x \in D\),
\(\abs{s_{m}(x) - s_{n}(x)} \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} \abs{u_{k}(x)} \le \sum_{k=n+1}^{m} M_{k}\),
and the right-hand side is below \(\eps\) for \(m > n \ge N\) because
\(\sum M_{n}\) converges and is therefore Cauchy. The bound does not
involve \(x\), so the uniform Cauchy criterion applies.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-uniform-limit-continuous, thm-continuous-is-integrable, prop-integral-monotonicity
#+NAME: thm-uniform-limit-integral
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f_{n} \to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) with every \(f_{n}\)
continuous. Then \(f\) is integrable and
\(\int_{a}^{b} f_{n} \to \int_{a}^{b} f\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The limit is continuous, hence integrable. Writing
\(\eps_{n} = \sup_{[a,b]} \abs{f_{n} - f}\), monotonicity and the
triangle inequality for the integral give
\[ \Bigl| \int_{a}^{b} f_{n} - \int_{a}^{b} f \Bigr| \le \int_{a}^{b} \abs{f_{n} - f} \le \eps_{n} (b - a), \]
and \(\eps_{n} \to 0\) by uniform convergence.
#+end_proof
