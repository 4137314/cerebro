#+TITLE: The action functional and the first variation
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:calculus_of_variations:
#+MSC: 49S05, 70H03
#+HASH: 4619c3224a7c

Mechanics can be stated as a differential law -- a second-order equation
for each coordinate -- or as a *variational* one: among all paths joining
two configurations, the physical one makes a certain integral
stationary. The second formulation is the one that generalises, because
it is coordinate-free: the integral does not know which chart it is
written in, so the equations it produces transform correctly by
construction.

#+DEPENDS: def-derivative, thm-continuous-is-integrable
#+NAME: def-action-functional
#+begin_definition
Let \(L \colon \R^{n} \times \R^{n} \times \R \to \R\) be twice
continuously differentiable -- the /Lagrangian/, written
\(L(q, \dot q, t)\). For a continuously differentiable path
\(q \colon [t_{0}, t_{1}] \to \R^{n}\), the /action/ is
\[ S[q] = \int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}} L\bigl( q(t), \dot q(t), t \bigr) \dd t. \]
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-action-functional
#+NAME: def-variation
#+begin_definition
A /variation/ of \(q\) with fixed endpoints is a family
\(q_{\eps} = q + \eps \eta\), where
\(\eta \colon [t_{0}, t_{1}] \to \R^{n}\) is continuously
differentiable with \(\eta(t_{0}) = \eta(t_{1}) = 0\). The path \(q\) is
/stationary/ for \(S\) if
\[ \left. \frac{\dd}{\dd \eps} S[q + \eps \eta] \right|_{\eps = 0} = 0 \]
for every such \(\eta\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-variation, thm-continuous-is-integrable
#+NAME: lem-fundamental-lemma-of-the-calculus-of-variations
#+begin_lemma
Let \(g \colon [t_{0}, t_{1}] \to \R^{n}\) be continuous and satisfy
\(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}} g(t) \cdot \eta(t) \dd t = 0\) for every
continuously differentiable \(\eta\) vanishing at both endpoints. Then
\(g \equiv 0\).
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
Suppose some component satisfies \(g_{i}(\tau) \ne 0\), say
\(g_{i}(\tau) > 0\), at an interior \(\tau\). By continuity there is
\([\alpha, \beta] \subseteq (t_{0}, t_{1})\) containing \(\tau\) on
which \(g_{i} > g_{i}(\tau)/2\). Take \(\eta\) with \(i\)-th component
\((t - \alpha)^{2}(\beta - t)^{2}\) on \([\alpha, \beta]\) and \(0\)
elsewhere, other components zero; it is continuously differentiable and
vanishes at the endpoints. The integral is then strictly positive,
contradicting the hypothesis. By continuity the conclusion extends to
the endpoints.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The lemma is what converts a statement quantified over *all* variations
into a pointwise equation, and it is the only place the arbitrariness of
\(\eta\) is spent. Everything after this is calculus.
#+end_remark
