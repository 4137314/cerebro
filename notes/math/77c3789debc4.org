#+TITLE: Subgroups, the subgroup criterion and generation
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20A05
#+HASH: 77c3789debc4

A [[id:c74536f9-f1a8-4ce8-a26f-8ae6300aa2eb][group]] is rarely met alone: what one has is a group together with the
subsets of it that are again groups, and almost every construction below
-- cosets, kernels, generated subgroups, orbits -- produces one of those.
Two small results make the supply manageable: a one-line test for being a
subgroup, and the fact that subgroups are closed under intersection,
which is what allows "the smallest subgroup containing \(S\)" to be
defined without ever building it.

#+DEPENDS: def-group
#+NAME: def-subgroup
#+begin_definition
A subset \(H\) of a group \(G\) is a /subgroup/, written \(H \le G\), if
it contains the identity, is closed under the operation, and contains
the inverse of each of its elements. It is then a group under the
restricted operation.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-subgroup, thm-uniqueness-of-identity
#+NAME: prop-subgroup-criterion
#+begin_proposition
A non-empty subset \(H \subseteq G\) is a subgroup if and only if
\(a b^{-1} \in H\) for all \(a, b \in H\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
If \(H\) is a subgroup the condition is immediate. Conversely, take
\(a \in H\), which exists since \(H\) is non-empty. Then
\(e = a a^{-1} \in H\); for \(b \in H\), \(b^{-1} = e b^{-1} \in H\);
and for \(a, b \in H\), \(ab = a (b^{-1})^{-1} \in H\). The identity of
\(H\) is the identity of \(G\), which by the uniqueness of an identity
element is the only candidate.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-subgroup
#+NAME: prop-intersection-of-subgroups
#+begin_proposition
The intersection of any non-empty family of subgroups of \(G\) is a
subgroup of \(G\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Each member contains \(e\), so the intersection does and is non-empty.
If \(a, b\) lie in every \(H_{i}\), then \(ab^{-1}\) lies in every
\(H_{i}\) since each is a subgroup; the criterion applies.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-intersection-of-subgroups
#+NAME: def-generated-subgroup
#+begin_definition
For \(S \subseteq G\), the /subgroup generated by/ \(S\), written
\(\langle S \rangle\), is the intersection of all subgroups of \(G\)
containing \(S\) -- a subgroup by the previous proposition, and by
construction the smallest one containing \(S\). A group is /finitely
generated/ if it is \(\langle S \rangle\) for some finite \(S\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-generated-subgroup
#+NAME: prop-generated-subgroup-as-words
#+begin_proposition
\(\langle S \rangle\) is the set \(W\) of all finite products
\(s_{1}^{\eps_{1}} \cdots s_{k}^{\eps_{k}}\) with \(k \ge 0\),
\(s_{i} \in S\) and \(\eps_{i} \in \set{1, -1}\) (the empty product
being \(e\)).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
\(W\) is a subgroup: it contains \(e\), it is closed under
multiplication by concatenation of words, and the inverse of
\(s_{1}^{\eps_{1}} \cdots s_{k}^{\eps_{k}}\) is
\(s_{k}^{-\eps_{k}} \cdots s_{1}^{-\eps_{1}}\), again such a product. It
contains \(S\), so \(\langle S \rangle \subseteq W\). Conversely any
subgroup containing \(S\) is closed under products and inverses, hence
contains every such word, so \(W \subseteq \langle S \rangle\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The two descriptions are used for opposite purposes and it is worth
keeping both: the intersection definition proves things about
\(\langle S \rangle\) with no computation ("it is contained in every
subgroup containing \(S\)"), while the word description is what one
computes with. The same pair of definitions reappears for generated
ideals, spans of vectors, generated topologies and closures.
#+end_remark
