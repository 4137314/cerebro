#+TITLE: Linearity, additivity and monotonicity of the integral
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]

These are the properties the integral is used through, and none of them
is visible in the definition: the definition is about suprema of lower
sums, and a supremum of a sum is not the sum of the suprema. Each one
therefore has to be proved, and the Riemann criterion is what makes
each proof short -- work with the gap \(U - L\), never with the
integral itself.

#+DEPENDS: def-riemann-integral, thm-riemann-criterion, def-darboux-sums
#+NAME: thm-integral-linearity
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f, g \colon [a,b] \to \R\) be Riemann integrable and
\(\lambda \in \R\). Then \(f + g\) and \(\lambda f\) are integrable, and
\[ \int_{a}^{b} (f + g) = \int_{a}^{b} f + \int_{a}^{b} g, \qquad \int_{a}^{b} \lambda f = \lambda \int_{a}^{b} f. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
On any subinterval, \(\sup (f + g) \le \sup f + \sup g\) and
\(\inf (f+g) \ge \inf f + \inf g\), so for every partition \(P\)
\[ L(f,P) + L(g,P) \le L(f+g,P) \le U(f+g,P) \le U(f,P) + U(g,P). \]
Given \(\eps > 0\), take partitions with
\(U(f,P_{1}) - L(f,P_{1}) < \eps/2\) and
\(U(g,P_{2}) - L(g,P_{2}) < \eps/2\), and let \(P\) refine
both; the displayed inequalities give
\(U(f+g,P) - L(f+g,P) < \eps\), so \(f + g\) is integrable by the
criterion. Both integrals are then squeezed between the same two
quantities, which differ by less than \(\eps\) for every \(\eps\),
hence they are equal.

For \(\lambda \ge 0\), \(L(\lambda f, P) = \lambda L(f,P)\) and
\(U(\lambda f, P) = \lambda U(f,P)\), and the claim is immediate. For
\(\lambda = -1\) the suprema and infima exchange roles:
\(L(-f,P) = -U(f,P)\) and \(U(-f,P) = -L(f,P)\), so the gap is
unchanged and the upper and lower integrals swap sign. The general
\(\lambda\) is a composition of the two cases.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-riemann-criterion, def-riemann-integral
#+NAME: thm-integral-additivity
#+begin_theorem
Let \(a < c < b\). Then \(f\) is integrable on \([a,b]\) if and only if
it is integrable on \([a,c]\) and on \([c,b]\), and then
\[ \int_{a}^{b} f = \int_{a}^{c} f + \int_{c}^{b} f. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
A partition of \([a,b]\) containing \(c\) splits into partitions
\(P_{1}, P_{2}\) of the two halves, with
\(U(f,P) = U(f,P_{1}) + U(f,P_{2})\) and likewise for \(L\); adding
\(c\) to a partition only refines it, so nothing is lost by assuming
\(c \in P\).

If \(f\) is integrable on both halves, choose \(P_{1}, P_{2}\) with gap
less than \(\eps/2\) each; their union has gap less than \(\eps\).
Conversely, if \(f\) is integrable on \([a,b]\), take \(P\) with gap
less than \(\eps\) and refine it by \(c\); since both
\(U(f,P_{i}) - L(f,P_{i})\) are non-negative and they sum to less than
\(\eps\), each is less than \(\eps\). The identity of the three
integrals is the identity \(U(f,P) = U(f,P_{1}) + U(f,P_{2})\) passed
to the infimum over such partitions.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-riemann-integral, def-darboux-sums
#+NAME: prop-integral-monotonicity
#+begin_proposition
If \(f \le g\) are integrable on \([a,b]\), then
\(\int_{a}^{b} f \le \int_{a}^{b} g\). In particular, if
\(m \le f \le M\) then
\(m(b-a) \le \int_{a}^{b} f \le M(b-a)\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
On every subinterval \(\sup f \le \sup g\), so
\(U(f,P) \le U(g,P)\) for every partition \(P\), and taking the infimum
over \(P\) gives the inequality between the upper integrals, which are
the integrals. The second claim is the first applied to the constant
functions \(m\) and \(M\), whose integrals are \(m(b-a)\) and
\(M(b-a)\) directly from the definition.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-integral-monotonicity, thm-integral-linearity, cor-reverse-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: cor-integral-triangle-inequality
#+begin_corollary
If \(f\) is integrable on \([a,b]\), then so is \(\abs{f}\), and
\[ \Bigl| \int_{a}^{b} f \Bigr| \le \int_{a}^{b} \abs{f}. \]
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
On any subinterval the oscillation of \(\abs{f}\) is at most that of
\(f\), by the reverse triangle inequality, so
\(U(\abs{f},P) - L(\abs{f},P) \le U(f,P) - L(f,P)\) and the Riemann
criterion transfers. Since \(-\abs{f} \le f \le \abs{f}\), monotonicity
and linearity give
\(-\int \abs{f} \le \int f \le \int \abs{f}\), which is the claim.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Additivity is what the first fundamental theorem uses when it writes
\(F(c+h) - F(c)\) as an integral over \([c, c+h]\), and the estimate it
follows with is monotonicity. Neither was available before this note,
which is a fair statement of how much of the theory of the integral is
spent making the notation behave.
#+end_remark
