#+TITLE: Flows, equilibria and linearisation
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:dynamics:dynamical_systems:
#+MSC: 37C10, 34D20
#+HASH: 849f18943111

An autonomous differential equation is better read as a flow: a family
of maps moving the whole space, rather than one solution at a time. The
questions then become qualitative -- where are the fixed points, what
happens near them, what happens for all time -- and the first answer is
that near a hyperbolic equilibrium the nonlinear system behaves like its
linearisation.

#+DEPENDS: thm-picard-lindelof, def-lipschitz-in-y, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-flow
#+begin_definition
Let \(f\) be \(C^{1}\) on an open \(U \subseteq \R^{n}\). The
/autonomous system/ is \(\dot{x} = f(x)\); its /flow/ is the map
\(\Phi_{t}(x_{0})\) giving the value at time \(t\) of the solution with
\(\Phi_{0}(x_{0}) = x_{0}\), defined for \(t\) in a maximal interval. A
point with \(f(x_{0}) = 0\) is an /equilibrium/, and an /orbit/ is the
image of a maximal solution.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-flow, thm-picard-lindelof
#+NAME: prop-the-flow-is-a-group
#+begin_proposition
Where both sides are defined, \(\Phi_{t+s} = \Phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{s}\) and
\(\Phi_{0} = \id\); each \(\Phi_{t}\) is injective, and two distinct
orbits are disjoint.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Fix \(s\) and \(x_{0}\), and let \(u(t) = \Phi_{t+s}(x_{0})\) and
\(v(t) = \Phi_{t}(\Phi_{s}(x_{0}))\). Both solve \(\dot{y} = f(y)\) --
the first because the system is autonomous, so a time shift of a
solution is a solution -- with the same value at \(t = 0\); by
uniqueness in the existence theorem they coincide. Injectivity follows
from \(\Phi_{-t}\circ\Phi_{t} = \id\). If two orbits meet at a point,
the solutions through it agree by uniqueness, so the orbits coincide.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-flow, def-equilibrium-and-stability, thm-taylor-peano
#+NAME: def-hyperbolic-equilibrium
#+begin_definition
Let \(x_{0}\) be an equilibrium and \(A = Df(x_{0})\). It is
/hyperbolic/ if no eigenvalue of \(A\) has zero real part, /a sink/ if
all real parts are negative, /a source/ if all are positive. The
equilibrium is /asymptotically stable/ if it is stable and every motion
starting near it converges to it.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-hyperbolic-equilibrium, thm-spectral-theorem, thm-picard-lindelof, cor-chebyshev-inequality
#+NAME: thm-linearised-stability
#+begin_theorem
If every eigenvalue of \(A = Df(x_{0})\) has real part at most
\(-\mu<0\), then \(x_{0}\) is asymptotically stable, and the convergence
is exponential: \(\abs{\Phi_{t}(x)-x_{0}} \le C e^{-\mu' t}\abs{x-x_{0}}\)
for some \(0<\mu'<\mu\) and \(x\) near \(x_{0}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Take \(x_{0} = 0\). There is an inner product on \(\R^{n}\) for which
\(\inner{Ay}{y} \le -\mu'\abs{y}^{2}\) with \(\mu'<\mu\): in a basis
bringing \(A\) to a form with the eigenvalues on the diagonal and
arbitrarily small off-diagonal entries, the symmetric part of \(A\) is
negative definite with the stated bound. Use the associated norm.

Write \(f(y) = Ay + r(y)\) with \(\abs{r(y)} \le \eps\abs{y}\) for
\(\abs{y}\le\delta\), by differentiability at \(0\); choose \(\eps\)
with \(\mu'' = \mu'-\eps>0\). Along a solution with \(\abs{y(t)}\le\delta\),
\[ \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\tfrac{1}{2}\abs{y}^{2} = \inner{f(y)}{y} \le -\mu'\abs{y}^{2}+\eps\abs{y}^{2} = -\mu''\abs{y}^{2}. \]
Hence \(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\bigl(e^{2\mu'' t}\abs{y}^{2}\bigr)\le0\), so
\(\abs{y(t)} \le e^{-\mu'' t}\abs{y(0)}\). In particular the solution
never leaves the ball of radius \(\delta\) if it starts inside, so it is
defined for all \(t \ge 0\), and it converges to \(0\) exponentially.
Equivalence of the two norms turns this into the stated estimate.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-linearised-stability, def-hyperbolic-equilibrium
#+NAME: cor-a-source-is-unstable
#+begin_corollary
If every eigenvalue of \(A\) has positive real part, then \(x_{0}\) is
unstable: motions starting arbitrarily close leave a fixed
neighbourhood.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Apply the theorem to the reversed system \(\dot{x} = -f(x)\), whose
linearisation is \(-A\) with all eigenvalues of negative real part. Its
solutions converge to \(x_{0}\) exponentially, so the solutions of the
original system, which are those run backwards, leave every ball on
which the estimate holds.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Linearisation decides the hyperbolic cases and only those: with an
eigenvalue on the imaginary axis the linear system is neutral and the
nonlinear terms take over, which is why a conservative mechanical system
-- whose linearisation at a stable equilibrium has purely imaginary
eigenvalues -- needs [[id:8b7c0d78-6337-4e7c-b510-0a1462f85a66][Lagrange-Dirichlet]] rather than this theorem. The
sharp statement in the hyperbolic case is the Hartman-Grobman theorem: the
flow is locally conjugate to its linearisation by a homeomorphism.
#+end_remark
