#+TITLE: Hilbert spaces: projection, Riesz representation and orthonormal bases
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:functional_analysis:
#+MSC: 46C05
#+HASH: ed8ef87f6dd8

A Banach space has a norm; a Hilbert space has an [[id:c35a8f01-cbd2-433b-b9b5-a037bae4e91d][inner product]], and
with it the whole geometry of orthogonality survives into infinite
dimension. Two theorems carry that geometry across: the nearest point in
a closed convex set exists, and every continuous functional is an inner
product. After them, a separable Hilbert space is coordinates again.

#+DEPENDS: def-inner-product-space, def-norm, def-metric-space, def-cauchy-sequence
#+NAME: def-hilbert-space
#+begin_definition
A /Hilbert space/ is a real or complex inner product space that is
complete for the metric \(d(u,v) = \norm{u-v}\) induced by
\(\norm{v} = \sqrt{\inner{v}{v}}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-space, thm-nearest-point-in-a-closed-convex-set, prop-parallelogram-identity, def-convex-set
#+NAME: thm-nearest-point-in-a-hilbert-space
#+begin_theorem
Let \(C\) be a non-empty closed convex subset of a Hilbert space \(H\)
and \(x \in H\). Then there is exactly one \(p \in C\) with
\(\norm{x-p} = \inf_{c\in C}\norm{x-c}\), and it is characterised by
\(\inner{x-p}{c-p}\le0\) for all \(c \in C\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(d\) be the infimum and \(c_{k}\in C\) with
\(\norm{x-c_{k}}\to d\). The parallelogram identity gives
\(\norm{c_{k}-c_{l}}^{2} = 2\norm{x-c_{k}}^{2}+2\norm{x-c_{l}}^{2}-4\norm{x-\tfrac{c_{k}+c_{l}}{2}}^{2}\),
and the midpoint lies in \(C\) by convexity, so the last term is at
least \(4d^{2}\) and the right side tends to \(0\). Hence \((c_{k})\) is
Cauchy and converges, by completeness, to some \(p\), which lies in the
closed set \(C\) and attains \(d\). Uniqueness and the variational
characterisation are the same computations as in \(\R^{n}\), using only
the inner product and convexity.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-nearest-point-in-a-hilbert-space, def-orthogonal-family, thm-orthogonal-decomposition
#+NAME: thm-orthogonal-projection-in-a-hilbert-space
#+begin_theorem
Let \(M\) be a closed subspace of a Hilbert space \(H\). Then
\(H = M \oplus M^{\perp}\), and the map sending \(x\) to its nearest
point in \(M\) is the linear /orthogonal projection/ onto \(M\), of norm
at most \(1\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(P x\) be the nearest point of \(M\) to \(x\), which exists since
\(M\) is closed and convex. For \(m \in M\) and \(t \in \R\), the
characterisation applied to \(c = Px \pm t m\) gives
\(\mp t\inner{x-Px}{m}\le0\) for all \(t>0\), so
\(\inner{x-Px}{m} = 0\): thus \(x - Px \in M^{\perp}\) and
\(x = Px + (x - Px)\). The sum is direct, since a vector in
\(M\cap M^{\perp}\) is orthogonal to itself. Linearity of \(P\) follows
from uniqueness of the decomposition, and
\(\norm{x}^{2} = \norm{Px}^{2}+\norm{x-Px}^{2}\) gives
\(\norm{Px}\le\norm{x}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-orthogonal-projection-in-a-hilbert-space, def-dual-space, def-bounded-operator
#+NAME: thm-riesz-representation
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\varphi\) be a bounded linear functional on a real Hilbert space
\(H\). Then there is exactly one \(y \in H\) with
\(\varphi(x) = \inner{x}{y}\) for all \(x\), and \(\norm{\varphi} = \norm{y}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(\varphi = 0\) take \(y = 0\). Otherwise \(M = \ker\varphi\) is a
closed proper subspace, so \(M^{\perp}\ne\set{0}\); pick a unit
\(z \in M^{\perp}\). For any \(x\), the vector
\(\varphi(x)z - \varphi(z)x\) lies in \(M\), hence is orthogonal to
\(z\), which gives \(\varphi(x) = \varphi(z)\inner{x}{z}\). So
\(y = \varphi(z)z\) works.

Uniqueness: if \(\inner{x}{y} = \inner{x}{y'}\) for all \(x\), take
\(x = y - y'\). For the norms,
\(\abs{\varphi(x)} \le \norm{x}\norm{y}\) by Cauchy-Schwarz, with
equality at \(x = y\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-hilbert-space, thm-gram-schmidt, prop-pythagoras, thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion
#+NAME: thm-orthonormal-basis-and-parseval
#+begin_theorem
Let \((e_{n})_{n\in\N}\) be an orthonormal sequence in a Hilbert space
\(H\) whose span is dense. Then every \(x \in H\) satisfies
\(x = \sum_{n}\inner{x}{e_{n}}e_{n}\), with convergence in norm, and
\(\norm{x}^{2} = \sum_{n}\abs{\inner{x}{e_{n}}}^{2}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(c_{n} = \inner{x}{e_{n}}\) and \(s_{N} = \sum_{n\le N}c_{n}e_{n}\).
Then \(x - s_{N}\) is orthogonal to each \(e_{n}\), \(n \le N\), so by
Pythagoras \(\norm{x}^{2} = \norm{s_{N}}^{2} + \norm{x-s_{N}}^{2}\) and
\(\sum_{n\le N}\abs{c_{n}}^{2} = \norm{s_{N}}^{2} \le \norm{x}^{2}\):
Bessel's inequality. Hence the series of squares converges, so
\(\norm{s_{M}-s_{N}}^{2} = \sum_{N<n\le M}\abs{c_{n}}^{2}\to0\) and
\((s_{N})\) is Cauchy, converging to some \(s \in H\).

The difference \(x - s\) is orthogonal to every \(e_{n}\), hence to
their span and, by continuity of the inner product, to its closure,
which is \(H\); so \(x = s\). Then
\(\norm{x}^{2} = \lim\norm{s_{N}}^{2} = \sum_{n}\abs{c_{n}}^{2}\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Density of the span is the hypothesis that turns Bessel's inequality
into Parseval's identity, and it is what "basis" means here: not a
Hamel basis, which for an infinite-dimensional Banach space is
uncountable and useless, but a set whose /closed/ span is everything.
The theorem says every separable Hilbert space is the sequence space
\(\ell^{2}\), which is why there is essentially one of them.
#+end_remark
