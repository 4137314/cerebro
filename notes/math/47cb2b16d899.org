#+TITLE: Topological spaces, bases and generated topologies
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:topology:general_topology:
#+MSC: 54A05
#+HASH: 47cb2b16d899

In a [[id:ab5041dc-f782-4348-8303-01facc0b23d1][metric space]] the open sets were defined from the distance, and then
every argument about continuity and convergence was rewritten in terms
of them. A topology is what is left when the distance is discarded and
only the family of open sets is kept -- which is exactly as much as those
arguments used, and strictly less than a metric, since some useful
families of open sets come from no metric at all.

#+DEPENDS: def-open-set
#+NAME: def-topological-space
#+begin_definition
A /topology/ on a set \(X\) is a family \(\mathcal{T}\) of subsets --
the /open/ sets -- such that \(\emptyset\) and \(X\) belong to
\(\mathcal{T}\), any union of members of \(\mathcal{T}\) belongs to
\(\mathcal{T}\), and the intersection of any two members does. The pair
\((X,\mathcal{T})\) is a /topological space/; a set is /closed/ if its
complement is open, and a /neighbourhood/ of \(x\) is any set
containing an open set containing \(x\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space
#+NAME: prop-closed-sets-of-a-topology
#+begin_proposition
In a topological space, \(\emptyset\) and \(X\) are closed, any
intersection of closed sets is closed, and the union of two closed sets
is closed.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Complements exchange unions with intersections: the complement of
\(\bigcap_{i} C_{i}\) is \(\bigcup_{i}(X \setminus C_{i})\), a union of
open sets, hence open; and the complement of \(C_{1}\cup C_{2}\) is
\((X\setminus C_{1})\cap(X\setminus C_{2})\), open as a finite
intersection. The two extreme cases are the complements of \(X\) and
\(\emptyset\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space
#+NAME: def-basis-of-a-topology
#+begin_definition
A /basis/ of a topology \(\mathcal{T}\) is a subfamily
\(\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{T}\) such that every open set is a
union of members of \(\mathcal{B}\). A /subbasis/ is a family whose
finite intersections form a basis.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-basis-of-a-topology
#+NAME: thm-basis-criterion
#+begin_theorem
A family \(\mathcal{B}\) of subsets of \(X\) is a basis of some topology
on \(X\) if and only if it covers \(X\) and, for all
\(B_{1}, B_{2} \in \mathcal{B}\) and \(x \in B_{1}\cap B_{2}\), there is
\(B_{3} \in \mathcal{B}\) with \(x \in B_{3} \subseteq B_{1}\cap B_{2}\).
The topology is then unique: its open sets are the unions of members of
\(\mathcal{B}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Necessity: \(X\) is open, hence a union of basis members, so
\(\mathcal{B}\) covers; and \(B_{1}\cap B_{2}\) is open, hence such a
union, so any of its points lies in one of the pieces.

Sufficiency: let \(\mathcal{T}\) be the family of unions of members of
\(\mathcal{B}\). It contains \(\emptyset\) (the empty union) and \(X\)
(by covering), and is closed under arbitrary unions, a union of unions
being a union. For two members \(U = \bigcup_{i}B_{i}\) and
\(V = \bigcup_{j}B_{j}'\), a point \(x \in U\cap V\) lies in some
\(B_{i}\cap B_{j}'\), hence in some \(B_{3} \subseteq B_{i}\cap B_{j}' \subseteq U \cap V\);
so \(U \cap V\) is the union of such \(B_{3}\) and lies in
\(\mathcal{T}\). Uniqueness is the definition of a basis.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-topological-space
#+NAME: prop-intersection-of-topologies
#+begin_proposition
The intersection of any non-empty family of topologies on \(X\) is a
topology. Consequently, for any family \(\mathcal{S}\) of subsets there
is a smallest topology containing it, the topology /generated by/
\(\mathcal{S}\), and \(\mathcal{S}\) is a subbasis of it.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Each of the three closure conditions holds in every member of the
family, hence in the intersection. The topologies containing
\(\mathcal{S}\) form a non-empty family -- the discrete topology, all
subsets, is one -- so their intersection is the smallest such topology.
It contains all finite intersections of members of \(\mathcal{S}\) and
all unions of those; that family is already a topology by the basis
criterion, the finite intersections being closed under pairwise
intersection, so it is the whole of the generated topology.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Two topologies are worth naming immediately because they bound all
others: the /discrete/ one, in which every set is open, and the
/indiscrete/ one, with only \(\emptyset\) and \(X\). Every statement
below that separates points holds vacuously in the first and fails in
the second, which makes them the standard test of whether a hypothesis
is really being used.
#+end_remark
