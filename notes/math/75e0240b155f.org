#+TITLE: Complex integration and Cauchy's theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:complex_analysis:
#+MSC: 30E20
#+HASH: 75e0240b155f

The integral of a holomorphic function around a closed curve vanishes.
Everything else in complex analysis is a corollary of that sentence, and
the sentence is a triangle inequality plus a bisection argument -- no
hypothesis on the derivative beyond its existence, which is what makes
Goursat's version of the proof the right one.

#+DEPENDS: def-holomorphic, def-line-integral, def-riemann-integral
#+NAME: def-contour-integral
#+begin_definition
For a piecewise \(C^{1}\) curve \(\gamma \colon [a,b]\to\C\) and a
continuous \(f\) on its image,
\(\int_{\gamma}f(z)\dd z = \int_{a}^{b}f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\dd t\),
the right-hand side being the integral of a complex-valued function of a
real variable, taken componentwise. The /length/ of \(\gamma\) is
\(L(\gamma) = \int_{a}^{b}\abs{\gamma'(t)}\dd t\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-contour-integral, thm-integral-linearity, cor-integral-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: prop-standard-estimate
#+begin_proposition
The contour integral is linear in \(f\), reverses sign with the
orientation, is unchanged by reparametrisation, and satisfies
\(\bigl|\int_{\gamma}f(z)\dd z\bigr| \le L(\gamma)\sup_{\gamma}\abs{f}\).
If \(f = F'\) with \(F\) holomorphic, then
\(\int_{\gamma}f = F(\gamma(b))-F(\gamma(a))\); in particular the
integral vanishes on closed curves.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Linearity and the behaviour under reparametrisation are those of the
real integral, by the substitution rule. The estimate is the triangle
inequality for integrals applied to
\(\abs{f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)} \le \sup\abs{f}\cdot\abs{\gamma'(t)}\).
For a primitive, the chain rule gives
\(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}F(\gamma(t)) = f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\), and the
fundamental theorem of calculus applies on each \(C^{1}\) piece.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-contour-integral, prop-standard-estimate, def-holomorphic, cor-heine-borel-in-euclidean-space, thm-compactness-by-nets
#+NAME: thm-goursat
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open \(U\) and let \(T \subseteq U\) be a
closed triangle. Then \(\int_{\partial T}f(z)\dd z = 0\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(I(T) = \int_{\partial T}f\). Bisect \(T\) by the midpoints of its
sides into four triangles \(T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(4)}\), coherently
oriented; the interior edges are traversed twice in opposite directions,
so \(I(T) = \sum_{k}I(T^{(k)})\) and some \(T^{(k)}\) has
\(\abs{I(T^{(k)})} \ge \abs{I(T)}/4\). Iterating gives nested triangles
\(T \supseteq T_{1}\supseteq T_{2}\supseteq\cdots\) with
\(\abs{I(T_{n})} \ge 4^{-n}\abs{I(T)}\), diameter
\(d_{n} = 2^{-n}d\) and perimeter \(\ell_{n} = 2^{-n}\ell\).

The intersection of the nested compact sets is non-empty; let \(z_{0}\)
be a point of it. Given \(\eps>0\), holomorphy at \(z_{0}\) gives
\(\delta>0\) with
\(\abs{f(z)-f(z_{0})-f'(z_{0})(z-z_{0})} \le \eps\abs{z-z_{0}}\) for
\(\abs{z-z_{0}}<\delta\). The affine function
\(z \mapsto f(z_{0})+f'(z_{0})(z-z_{0})\) has a primitive, so its
integral over \(\partial T_{n}\) vanishes; hence for \(n\) large enough
that \(T_{n}\subseteq B(z_{0},\delta)\), the standard estimate gives
\(\abs{I(T_{n})} \le \ell_{n}\cdot\eps d_{n} = \eps\,4^{-n}\ell d\).
Combining, \(\abs{I(T)} \le \eps\,\ell d\) for every \(\eps>0\), so
\(I(T) = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-goursat, def-convex-set, thm-line-integral-of-a-gradient
#+NAME: thm-primitive-on-a-convex-set
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on a convex open \(U\). Then \(f\) has a
primitive on \(U\), and consequently \(\int_{\gamma}f(z)\dd z = 0\) for
every closed piecewise \(C^{1}\) curve \(\gamma\) in \(U\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix \(z_{0}\in U\) and set \(F(z) = \int_{[z_{0},z]}f\), the integral
along the segment, which lies in \(U\) by convexity. For \(z, z+h \in U\)
the triangle with vertices \(z_{0}, z, z+h\) lies in \(U\), so Goursat's
theorem gives
\(F(z+h)-F(z) = \int_{[z,z+h]}f\). Hence
\[ \Bigl|\frac{F(z+h)-F(z)}{h} - f(z)\Bigr| = \Bigl|\frac{1}{h}\int_{[z,z+h]}\bigl(f(w)-f(z)\bigr)\dd w\Bigr| \le \sup_{w \in [z,z+h]}\abs{f(w)-f(z)}, \]
which tends to \(0\) as \(h\to0\) by continuity of \(f\). So
\(F' = f\), and the vanishing on closed curves is the previous
proposition.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Convexity is doing no essential work -- star-shaped is enough, and the
correct hypothesis is again simple connectivity. What matters is that
Goursat assumes only differentiability, not continuity of the
derivative: had the proof needed \(f'\) continuous, the theorem could
not be used to /prove/ that \(f'\) is continuous, which is exactly what
the [[id:b9814e2f-633e-49eb-afaa-cc4092244461][integral formula]] does next.
#+end_remark
