An [[id:8cd15fbc-3e1f-41a6-8fde-31963a195e78][existence theorem]] produces one solution at a time; a dynamical system
is the whole family at once, read as a flow moving the space. The
questions change accordingly -- not what the solution is, but where it
goes -- and the two answers available without solving anything are
linearisation near an equilibrium and the existence of a function that
decreases along motions.

Part of [[id:c2fcb100-fc5a-4670-9b5d-c068c0c325ff][Mathematics]].

* The two notes

- [[id:9b965797-1b43-4e3b-b244-48c5e47d83fd][Flows, equilibria and linearisation]] -- the flow as a one-parameter
  group, and the theorem that a hyperbolic sink of the linearisation is
  an asymptotically stable equilibrium of the nonlinear system.
- [[id:5bd80b77-add7-48ce-9967-0a71a33bfc00][Lyapunov functions and invariant sets]] -- what to do when the
  linearisation is neutral: find a function positive away from the
  equilibrium that does not increase, which is the energy argument with
  the mechanics removed.

* Where it connects

The neutral case is not exotic: it is exactly what a conservative
mechanical system presents, since the linearisation at a stable
equilibrium has purely imaginary eigenvalues. That is why
[[id:8b7c0d78-6337-4e7c-b510-0a1462f85a66][Lagrange-Dirichlet]] and [[id:4876cdf3-ca6f-4bd7-a056-0501b3add1ea][the stability analysis of free rotation]] are
Lyapunov arguments rather than linearisation arguments, and why
[[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mathematical physics]] is where this subject earns its keep.

* What is missing

Almost all of it: periodic orbits and the Poincare-Bendixson theorem,
which is the two-dimensional classification; stable and unstable
manifolds and the Hartman-Grobman theorem; bifurcations; chaotic
dynamics and symbolic coding; ergodic theory and the Birkhoff theorem,
which would connect this area to [[id:79a8bba8-94c4-44b9-9020-df8e30d12480][probability]] and to statistical
mechanics.
