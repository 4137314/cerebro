#+TITLE: The Cauchy integral formula and analyticity
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:complex_analysis:
#+MSC: 30E20, 30B10
#+HASH: 12a35a860551

A holomorphic function is determined inside a disc by its values on the
boundary circle, by an explicit formula. Expanding the kernel of that
formula in a geometric series turns the function into a power series,
and with that one step complex differentiability -- a hypothesis about
one derivative -- becomes analyticity, a statement about all of them.
Nothing in real analysis behaves like this.

#+DEPENDS: thm-primitive-on-a-convex-set, def-contour-integral, prop-standard-estimate
#+NAME: thm-cauchy-integral-formula
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be holomorphic on an open set containing the closed disc
\(\overline{B}(z_{0},r)\), and let \(C\) be the boundary circle,
traversed once anticlockwise. Then for every \(z\) with
\(\abs{z-z_{0}}<r\),
\[ f(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix \(z\) inside. For small \(\rho>0\) let \(C_{\rho}\) be the circle of
radius \(\rho\) about \(z\). The function \(w \mapsto f(w)/(w-z)\) is
holomorphic on the region between \(C\) and \(C_{\rho}\); cutting that
region into finitely many pieces each contained in a convex open subset
of the domain, and applying the vanishing of the integral on closed
curves in each piece, the contributions along the cuts cancel and
\(\int_{C}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w = \int_{C_{\rho}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w\).

On \(C_{\rho}\), parametrised as \(w = z+\rho e^{i\theta}\), one has
\(\dd w = i\rho e^{i\theta}\dd\theta\) and
\(\int_{C_{\rho}}\frac{\dd w}{w-z} = \int_{0}^{2\pi}i\dd\theta = 2\pi i\).
Hence
\[ \Bigl|\int_{C_{\rho}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\dd w - 2\pi i f(z)\Bigr| = \Bigl|\int_{C_{\rho}}\frac{f(w)-f(z)}{w-z}\dd w\Bigr| \le 2\pi\rho\cdot\frac{\sup_{C_{\rho}}\abs{f(w)-f(z)}}{\rho}, \]
which tends to \(0\) as \(\rho\to0\) by continuity of \(f\) at \(z\).
Since the left-hand integral does not depend on \(\rho\), it equals
\(2\pi i f(z)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-cauchy-integral-formula, thm-geometric-series, thm-weierstrass-m-test, def-radius-of-convergence
#+NAME: thm-holomorphic-implies-analytic
#+begin_theorem
A holomorphic \(f\) on an open \(U\) is /analytic/: about every
\(z_{0}\in U\) it is the sum of a power series
\(\sum_{n\ge0}a_{n}(z-z_{0})^{n}\) converging on every disc
\(B(z_{0},r)\) with \(\overline{B}(z_{0},r)\subseteq U\), and
\[ a_{n} = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}\frac{f(w)}{(w-z_{0})^{n+1}}\dd w = \frac{f^{(n)}(z_{0})}{n!}. \]
In particular \(f\) has derivatives of every order, and they are
holomorphic.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For \(w\) on \(C\) and \(\abs{z-z_{0}}<r = \abs{w-z_{0}}\), expand the
kernel as a geometric series,
\[ \frac{1}{w-z} = \frac{1}{(w-z_{0})\bigl(1-\frac{z-z_{0}}{w-z_{0}}\bigr)} = \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(z-z_{0})^{n}}{(w-z_{0})^{n+1}}, \]
which converges uniformly in \(w\) on \(C\) for fixed \(z\), the ratio
having modulus \(\abs{z-z_{0}}/r<1\); the Weierstrass test applies with
the terms dominated by \(r^{-1}(\abs{z-z_{0}}/r)^{n}\). Multiplying by
the bounded \(f(w)\) preserves uniform convergence, so the series may be
integrated term by term in the Cauchy formula, giving the power series
with the stated coefficients. A power series is infinitely
differentiable inside its disc of convergence with
\(a_{n} = f^{(n)}(z_{0})/n!\), and each derivative is again given by a
power series, hence holomorphic.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-holomorphic-implies-analytic, prop-standard-estimate
#+NAME: cor-cauchy-estimates
#+begin_corollary
With the notation above, \(\abs{f^{(n)}(z_{0})} \le n!\,M/r^{n}\), where
\(M = \sup_{C}\abs{f}\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Apply the standard estimate to the integral formula for \(a_{n}\): the
length of \(C\) is \(2\pi r\) and the integrand has modulus at most
\(M/r^{n+1}\), so \(\abs{a_{n}} \le M/r^{n}\); multiply by \(n!\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: cor-cauchy-estimates, prop-vanishing-derivative-on-a-domain, cor-number-of-roots
#+NAME: thm-liouville-and-the-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra
#+begin_theorem
A bounded entire function is constant; and every non-constant
polynomial over \(\C\) has a root, hence splits into linear factors.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(\abs{f}\le M\) on \(\C\). For any \(z_{0}\) and any \(r>0\) the
Cauchy estimate with \(n = 1\) gives \(\abs{f'(z_{0})}\le M/r\); letting
\(r\to\infty\) gives \(f'(z_{0}) = 0\). So \(f' \equiv 0\) on the
connected set \(\C\) and \(f\) is constant.

Let \(p\) be a polynomial of degree \(n\ge1\) with no root. Then
\(1/p\) is entire. Writing \(p(z) = a_{n}z^{n}(1+o(1))\) as
\(\abs{z}\to\infty\), we get \(\abs{p(z)}\to\infty\), so \(1/p\) tends to
\(0\) at infinity and is therefore bounded -- it is continuous, hence
bounded on any large closed disc, and small outside it. By the first
part \(1/p\) is constant, contradicting \(n\ge1\). So \(p\) has a root
\(\alpha\), and the factor theorem splits off \(z-\alpha\); induction
finishes.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-holomorphic-implies-analytic, thm-goursat, thm-primitive-on-a-convex-set
#+NAME: thm-morera
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be continuous on an open \(U\) with
\(\int_{\partial T}f = 0\) for every closed triangle \(T \subseteq U\).
Then \(f\) is holomorphic.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The question is local, so work on a disc \(B \subseteq U\), which is
convex. The construction of a primitive used only Goursat's conclusion
on triangles and the continuity of \(f\), both assumed here, so there is
a holomorphic \(F\) on \(B\) with \(F' = f\). By the previous theorem
\(F\) is analytic, hence infinitely differentiable, so \(f = F'\) is
holomorphic.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Morera's theorem is the converse of Cauchy's and it is what makes limits
usable: a locally uniform limit of holomorphic functions is holomorphic,
because the integral over each triangle passes to the limit. In real
analysis a uniform limit of smooth functions need not be
differentiable anywhere, and the contrast is the sharpest available
statement of how strong complex differentiability is.
#+end_remark
