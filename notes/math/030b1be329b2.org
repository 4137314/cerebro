#+TITLE: Completeness of the real numbers
#+DATE: [2026-08-06 Thu]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A03

#+DEPENDS: def-supremum

\(\Q\) is an ordered field and so is \(\R\); what separates them is one
axiom. Everything in a first analysis course that is not pure algebra --
convergence, continuity, the intermediate value theorem, the integral --
is downstream of it.
#+STATUS: assumed
#+NAME: ax-completeness
#+begin_axiom
Every non-empty subset of \(\R\) that is bounded above has a supremum in
\(\R\).
#+end_axiom

#+begin_remark
This is an /axiom/ here, and marked as such: it is part of what defines
\(\R\), not a theorem about it. In a construction of the reals (Dedekind
cuts, or equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences of rationals) it becomes
a proposition, and the construction is what one proves instead.
#+end_remark

The set \(\{q \in \Q \mid q^{2} < 2\}\) is the standard witness that
\(\Q\) fails it: bounded above, non-empty, and with no least upper bound
in \(\Q\).
