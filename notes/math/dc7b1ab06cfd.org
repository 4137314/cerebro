#+TITLE: Variance, covariance and the Markov and Chebyshev inequalities
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60E15
#+HASH: dc7b1ab06cfd

Two numbers summarise a law: where it sits and how far it spreads. The
second is useful because of one inequality -- a bound on the probability
of a large deviation in terms of a moment -- which is crude, universal
and, applied to an average, already proves the law of large numbers.

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, def-l2, thm-l2-is-an-inner-product-space
#+NAME: def-variance-and-covariance
#+begin_definition
For square-integrable \(X, Y\), the /variance/ is
\(\Var(X) = \E[(X-\E[X])^{2}]\), the /standard deviation/ is
\(\sqrt{\Var(X)}\), and the /covariance/ is
\(\Cov(X,Y) = \E[(X-\E[X])(Y-\E[Y])]\). They are /uncorrelated/ if the
covariance vanishes.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-variance-and-covariance, prop-properties-of-expectation
#+NAME: prop-variance-identities
#+begin_proposition
\(\Var(X) = \E[X^{2}]-\E[X]^{2}\) and
\(\Cov(X,Y) = \E[XY]-\E[X]\E[Y]\); \(\Cov\) is symmetric and bilinear;
\(\Var(aX+b) = a^{2}\Var(X)\); and
\(\Var(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}) = \sum_{i}\Var(X_{i}) + 2\sum_{i<j}\Cov(X_{i},X_{j})\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Expanding \((X-\E[X])^{2}\) and using linearity gives the first
identity, and the same expansion gives the second. Bilinearity and
symmetry follow from those of the product and linearity of expectation.
For an affine change, \(aX+b-\E[aX+b] = a(X-\E[X])\), and squaring pulls
out \(a^{2}\). The last identity is the expansion of the square of a
sum, with the diagonal terms giving variances.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-variance-identities, thm-expectation-of-a-product
#+NAME: cor-variance-of-a-sum-of-independent-variables
#+begin_corollary
Independent square-integrable random variables are uncorrelated, so
\(\Var(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}) = \sum_{i}\Var(X_{i})\). The converse fails.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Independence gives \(\E[X_{i}X_{j}] = \E[X_{i}]\E[X_{j}]\) for
\(i \ne j\), hence \(\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}) = 0\), and the cross terms in the
previous identity vanish.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, prop-properties-of-expectation
#+NAME: thm-markov-inequality
#+begin_theorem
Let \(X \ge 0\) and \(a>0\). Then \(\Prob(X \ge a) \le \E[X]/a\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Pointwise \(a\,\indicator_{\set{X\ge a}} \le X\): where the indicator is
\(1\) this says \(a \le X\), and where it is \(0\) it says
\(0 \le X\). Taking expectations and using monotonicity and linearity,
\(a\,\Prob(X\ge a) \le \E[X]\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-markov-inequality, def-variance-and-covariance
#+NAME: cor-chebyshev-inequality
#+begin_corollary
For square-integrable \(X\) and \(\eps>0\),
\(\Prob(\abs{X-\E[X]}\ge\eps) \le \Var(X)/\eps^{2}\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Apply Markov's inequality to the non-negative variable
\((X-\E[X])^{2}\) and the level \(\eps^{2}\): the event
\(\abs{X-\E[X]}\ge\eps\) is exactly \((X-\E[X])^{2}\ge\eps^{2}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-variance-and-covariance, thm-l2-is-an-inner-product-space, thm-cauchy-schwarz
#+NAME: prop-correlation-bound
#+begin_proposition
\(\abs{\Cov(X,Y)} \le \sqrt{\Var(X)\Var(Y)}\), with equality only if
\(X\) and \(Y\) are almost surely affinely related.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Covariance is the inner product of \(L^{2}\) applied to the centred
variables \(X-\E[X]\) and \(Y-\E[Y]\), and variance is the square of the
corresponding norm; the inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz, and its equality
case is that the two centred variables are proportional as elements of
\(L^{2}\), that is almost surely.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Markov's inequality is the whole of elementary concentration, and its
crudeness is the point: it assumes nothing about the law, so the bound
it gives is the one available before any modelling. Sharper bounds come
from applying it to a better function -- to \(X^{2}\) it is Chebyshev,
to \(e^{\lambda X}\) it is the exponential bound underlying the theory
of large deviations.
#+end_remark
