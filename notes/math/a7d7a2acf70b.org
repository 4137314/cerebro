#+TITLE: The Archimedean property and the density of the rationals
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A03
#+HASH: a7d7a2acf70b

Completeness is an axiom about suprema; the two statements below are the
first things it buys. Every later argument that manufactures an index
\(N\) out of an \(\eps\) rests on one of them, which is why they come
before anything about convergence.

#+DEPENDS: ax-completeness
#+NAME: thm-archimedean
#+begin_theorem
For every \(x \in \R\) there is \(n \in \N\) with \(n > x\).
Equivalently: for every \(\eps > 0\) there is \(n \in \N\) with
\(1/n < \eps\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose that for some \(x \in \R\) no such \(n\) exists. Then \(x\) is an
upper bound of \(\N\), so by completeness \(s = \sup \N\) exists in
\(\R\). Since \(s - 1 < s\), the number \(s - 1\) is not an upper bound
of \(\N\), so there is \(m \in \N\) with \(m > s - 1\). Then
\(m + 1 \in \N\) and \(m + 1 > s\), contradicting that \(s\) is an upper
bound of \(\N\). For the second form, apply the first to \(1/\eps\):
any \(n > 1/\eps\) satisfies \(1/n < \eps\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-archimedean
#+NAME: thm-density-of-rationals
#+begin_theorem
For all \(x, y \in \R\) with \(x < y\) there is \(q \in \Q\) with
\(x < q < y\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By the Archimedean property choose \(n \in \N\) with \(1/n < y - x\),
so that \(ny - nx > 1\). Let \(m\) be the least integer with \(m > nx\);
it exists because the set of integers exceeding \(nx\) is non-empty (again
by the Archimedean property) and bounded below. Minimality gives
\(m - 1 \le nx\), hence
\[ nx < m \le nx + 1 < nx + (ny - nx) = ny. \]
Dividing by \(n > 0\) yields \(x < m/n < y\), and \(q = m/n\) is rational.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The Archimedean property fails in some ordered fields: there are
non-Archimedean ordered fields containing an element larger than every
natural number. What excludes them here is completeness, so the two
theorems above are genuinely statements about \(\R\), not about ordered
fields in general.
#+end_remark
