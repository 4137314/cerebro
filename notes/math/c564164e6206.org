#+TITLE: Power series and the radius of convergence
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 30B10, 40A30
#+HASH: c564164e6206

A power series converges on an interval and diverges outside it, and the
radius that separates the two is not an extra hypothesis: it is
determined by the coefficients, through the same \(\limsup\) the root
test already used. Everything else -- that the sum is continuous, that
it may be integrated and differentiated term by term -- follows because
the convergence is uniform on every closed subinterval.

#+DEPENDS: def-series-convergence, def-limsup-liminf, thm-root-test
#+NAME: def-radius-of-convergence
#+begin_definition
For a power series \(\sum_{n \ge 0} a_{n} (x - c)^{n}\), the /radius of
convergence/ is
\[ R = \frac{1}{\limsup_{n \to \infty} \abs{a_{n}}^{1/n}}, \]
read as \(R = \infty\) when the \(\limsup\) is \(0\) and \(R = 0\) when
it is \(\infty\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-radius-of-convergence, thm-root-test, thm-geometric-series
#+NAME: thm-power-series-converges-inside
#+begin_theorem
The series converges absolutely for \(\abs{x - c} < R\) and diverges
for \(\abs{x - c} > R\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Apply the root test to \(u_{n} = a_{n}(x-c)^{n}\):
\(\limsup \abs{u_{n}}^{1/n} = \abs{x - c} \limsup \abs{a_{n}}^{1/n} = \abs{x-c}/R\).
This is \(< 1\) exactly when \(\abs{x - c} < R\), giving absolute
convergence, and \(> 1\) exactly when \(\abs{x - c} > R\), in which case
\(\abs{u_{n}} \ge 1\) infinitely often and the terms do not tend to
\(0\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Nothing is claimed at \(\abs{x - c} = R\), and nothing can be: the
radius is defined by a \(\limsup\) of \(n\)-th roots, which is blind to
the boundary. The three series \(\sum x^{n}\), \(\sum x^{n}/n\) and
\(\sum x^{n}/n^{2}\) all have \(R = 1\) and differ there, so the
boundary is a separate question for each series and never a corollary.
#+end_remark

#+DEPENDS: thm-power-series-converges-inside, thm-weierstrass-m-test
#+NAME: thm-power-series-uniform-on-compacts
#+begin_theorem
For every \(r < R\) the series converges uniformly on
\(\setst{x}{\abs{x - c} \le r}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Pick \(\rho\) with \(r < \rho < R\). By the previous theorem
\(\sum \abs{a_{n}} \rho^{n}\) converges, so
\(\abs{a_{n}} \rho^{n} \to 0\) and in particular
\(\abs{a_{n}} \le C \rho^{-n}\) for some \(C\) and all \(n\). On the
closed ball, \(\abs{a_{n}(x-c)^{n}} \le C (r/\rho)^{n} = M_{n}\), and
\(\sum M_{n}\) is a convergent geometric series. The Weierstrass
\(M\)-test gives uniform convergence.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-power-series-uniform-on-compacts, thm-uniform-limit-continuous
#+NAME: cor-power-series-continuous
#+begin_corollary
The sum of a power series is continuous on \(\abs{x - c} < R\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Continuity is local: a point with \(\abs{x_{0} - c} < R\) lies in the
closed ball of some radius \(r < R\), on which the partial sums --
polynomials, hence continuous -- converge uniformly. A uniform limit of
continuous functions is continuous, so the sum is continuous at
\(x_{0}\).
#+end_proof
