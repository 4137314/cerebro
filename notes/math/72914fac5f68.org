#+TITLE: Surface integrals, the divergence theorem and Stokes' theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-11 Tue]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26B20, 58C35
#+HASH: 72914fac5f68

The two theorems of this note are what make the differential form of a
physical law equivalent to its integral form: the flux of a field out of
a region is the integral of its divergence, and the circulation around a
loop is the flux of its curl. Every conservation law in [[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mathematical
physics]] is written twice for this reason, and the translation is these
identities.

#+DEPENDS: def-regular-surface, def-riemann-integral, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-surface-integral
#+begin_definition
Let \(\sigma \colon K \to \R^{3}\) be a regular parametrised surface on
a compact \(K \subseteq \R^{2}\) with piecewise smooth boundary. The
/area element/ is \(\dd S = \norm{\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}}\dd u\,\dd v\),
and for a continuous \(f\) on the image,
\(\iint_{S}f\dd S = \iint_{K}f(\sigma(u,v))\norm{\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}}\dd u\,\dd v\).
For a vector field \(F\), the /flux/ through \(S\) oriented by the unit
normal \(N\) is \(\iint_{S}\inner{F}{N}\dd S = \iint_{K}\inner{F\circ\sigma}{\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}}\dd u\,\dd v\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-surface-integral, def-differentiable-multivariable
#+NAME: def-divergence-and-curl
#+begin_definition
For a \(C^{1}\) field \(F = (F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})\) on an open subset of
\(\R^{3}\),
\(\Div F = \partial_{1}F_{1}+\partial_{2}F_{2}+\partial_{3}F_{3}\) and
\(\curl F = (\partial_{2}F_{3}-\partial_{3}F_{2},\, \partial_{3}F_{1}-\partial_{1}F_{3},\, \partial_{1}F_{2}-\partial_{2}F_{1})\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-divergence-and-curl, thm-continuous-partials-implies-differentiable
#+NAME: prop-div-curl-identities
#+begin_proposition
For \(C^{2}\) fields and functions, \(\Div(\curl F) = 0\) and
\(\curl(\nabla f) = 0\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Both are the equality of mixed second partial derivatives, which holds
for a \(C^{2}\) function. In the first, each term of the divergence
contributes a pair of mixed derivatives with opposite signs, and the six
terms cancel in pairs; in the second, each component of the curl is a
difference of two equal mixed derivatives.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-fubini, thm-ftc-second, def-surface-integral, def-divergence-and-curl
#+NAME: thm-divergence
#+begin_theorem
Let \(V \subseteq \R^{3}\) be a normal domain with respect to each
coordinate direction -- for the third, \(V = \setst{(x,y,z)}{(x,y)\in D,\ \alpha(x,y)\le z\le\beta(x,y)}\)
with \(\alpha\le\beta\) of class \(C^{1}\) on a normal domain
\(D \subseteq \R^{2}\) -- and let \(F\) be \(C^{1}\) on a neighbourhood
of \(V\). Then, with \(N\) the outward unit normal on
\(\partial V\),
\[ \iiint_{V}\Div F \dd x\,\dd y\,\dd z = \iint_{\partial V}\inner{F}{N}\dd S. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By linearity it suffices to treat \(F = (0,0,F_{3})\), the other two
components being the same argument in the corresponding description of
\(V\).

By Fubini and the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\[ \iiint_{V}\partial_{3}F_{3} = \iint_{D}\bigl(F_{3}(x,y,\beta(x,y)) - F_{3}(x,y,\alpha(x,y))\bigr)\dd x\,\dd y. \]
The boundary consists of the upper graph, the lower graph and a lateral
part on which the normal is horizontal, so
\(\inner{F}{N} = 0\) there. Parametrising the upper graph by
\((x,y)\mapsto(x,y,\beta(x,y))\), the cross product of the partial
derivatives is \((-\beta_{x},-\beta_{y},1)\), which is the upward
normal times the area element; hence its flux contribution is
\(\iint_{D}F_{3}(x,y,\beta(x,y))\dd x\,\dd y\). The lower graph carries
the downward normal and contributes the same integral with
\(\alpha\) and a minus sign. Adding gives the displayed identity.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-green, def-surface-integral, def-divergence-and-curl, thm-chain-rule
#+NAME: thm-stokes
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\sigma \colon K \to \R^{3}\) be a \(C^{2}\) regular surface over a
normal domain \(K \subseteq \R^{2}\), oriented by
\(\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}\), and let \(\partial S\) be the image of
\(\partial K\) with the induced orientation. Then for \(F\) of class
\(C^{1}\) near the image,
\[ \iint_{S}\inner{\curl F}{N}\dd S = \oint_{\partial S}F\cdot\dd s. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Pull the line integral back to \(K\): with \(\gamma\) parametrising
\(\partial K\) and \(\Gamma = \sigma\circ\gamma\), the chain rule gives
\(\Gamma' = \sigma_{u}u' + \sigma_{v}v'\), so
\[ \oint_{\partial S}F\cdot\dd s = \oint_{\partial K}\bigl(\tilde{P}\dd u + \tilde{Q}\dd v\bigr), \qquad \tilde{P} = \inner{F\circ\sigma}{\sigma_{u}}, \quad \tilde{Q} = \inner{F\circ\sigma}{\sigma_{v}}. \]
Green's theorem on \(K\) turns this into
\(\iint_{K}(\partial_{u}\tilde{Q}-\partial_{v}\tilde{P})\dd u\,\dd v\).

Differentiating and using the chain rule,
\(\partial_{u}\tilde{Q} = \inner{DF\,\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{v}} + \inner{F}{\sigma_{vu}}\)
and symmetrically for \(\partial_{v}\tilde{P}\); the second-derivative
terms cancel because \(\sigma\) is \(C^{2}\). What remains,
\(\inner{DF\,\sigma_{u}}{\sigma_{v}} - \inner{DF\,\sigma_{v}}{\sigma_{u}}\),
is the antisymmetric part of \(DF\) evaluated on the pair, and a direct
computation identifies it with
\(\inner{\curl F}{\sigma_{u}\times\sigma_{v}}\). Integrating over \(K\)
gives the flux of the curl.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-divergence, thm-stokes, prop-div-curl-identities, cor-closed-implies-exact-on-a-convex-set
#+NAME: cor-consistency-of-the-two-theorems
#+begin_corollary
For a \(C^{2}\) field \(F\) and a closed surface bounding a normal
domain, the flux of \(\curl F\) vanishes; and on a convex open set a
field with vanishing curl is a gradient.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
The first is the divergence theorem applied to \(\curl F\), whose
divergence vanishes identically. The second is the two-dimensional
statement applied componentwise: vanishing curl is exactly the equality
of the mixed partials of the components, which is the hypothesis of the
Poincare construction of a potential on a convex set.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Both theorems are stated for domains one can describe by graphs, which
is what makes the proofs Fubini plus the fundamental theorem of
calculus. That restriction is not the natural generality: the honest
setting is a manifold with boundary and a differential form, where both
statements and Green's theorem are one identity,
\(\int_{M}\dd\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega\). This archive has
[[id:e8ee2fda-0c4d-43f2-9fe8-f18e423a8010][manifolds]] and no differential forms, so the special cases are what can
be proved here.
#+end_remark
