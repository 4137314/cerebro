#+TITLE: Group theory
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20-XX
#+HASH: cf44c5146a8d

A group is one associative, invertible operation and nothing else, which
is little enough that the axioms fit on a line and strong enough that
finite groups have an arithmetic. The subject splits cleanly in two:
what a single group is made of, and how a group acts on something else
-- with the second usually answering questions about the first.

Part of [[id:504cda55-cfbf-42c2-b481-8d90660ec02a][Algebra]].

* The definitions

[[id:44abe21d-eefe-4e35-b5ba-b3dd472eb071][Binary operation]], then [[id:c74536f9-f1a8-4ce8-a26f-8ae6300aa2eb][group]] and [[id:c982302e-b3f8-40d7-b38d-73177ae644d4][abelian group]];
[[id:aebe89c4-9357-4ee0-94f5-b070f46de8cd][uniqueness of the identity element]] is the first thing the axioms
imply, and the reason one may say /the/ identity. [[id:86b6c9ba-76e4-49d1-b6a5-729af64e963c][Group homomorphism]]
is the definition that makes the rest of the subject possible: a group
is studied through the maps out of it.

* Inside one group

[[id:1ec55705-108c-487b-9e64-c83dda69590d][Subgroups, the subgroup criterion and generation]]; [[id:f77f1998-23ca-4da5-95f2-b4a77fc59169][the order of an
element and cyclic groups]], the one family that is classified
completely. Then the two structural theorems: [[id:27ec50b5-1e22-4d41-8edb-d13292004a0f][cosets, the index and
Lagrange's theorem]] -- a subgroup cuts the group into equal pieces, so
sizes divide -- and [[id:65032090-bc6b-4a06-a74a-ef155052a6df][normal subgroups and the quotient group]], which
identifies the subgroups one may divide by as exactly the kernels.
[[id:e0e0ff63-6493-4efb-9eab-b96ff4d568ea][The isomorphism theorems]] are the consequences.

* Acting on a set

[[id:d1a61e90-f2ac-4e62-9cd8-8c742d43fdf2][Group actions, orbits and the orbit-stabiliser theorem]] converts
counting problems into index computations, and it is the engine of
everything that follows: [[id:bbbee435-e5f4-4473-8ac8-ab88186ba01b][Cauchy's theorem and the Sylow theorems]] are
four applications of one fixed-point lemma. [[id:e6304fc0-553a-402f-93d0-8ab1de72983d][The symmetric group and the
sign of a permutation]] is where a group acts on a polynomial to produce
an invariant, and [[id:f830b644-837d-4fda-8384-8dad0d143c2f][the orthogonal group]] is where a group is met as
symmetries in the first place.

* Building and taking apart

[[id:3318172b-8246-4968-a640-d47e6decbf0c][Direct and semidirect products]] say when a group is assembled from two
of its subgroups, and show that a normal subgroup and its quotient do
not determine it. In the other direction, [[id:68aecb66-5e63-4fa6-bfad-5629a7a343d2][composition series,
Jordan-Holder and solvable groups]] give a well-defined list of simple
factors. [[id:0cc28992-7d7f-4b04-9dfe-5908f68bca27][Free groups and presentations]] is the opposite move again: a
group named by generators and relations, with nothing else assumed.

* What is missing

The classification of finitely generated abelian groups; the simplicity
of \(A_{n}\) for \(n \ge 5\), which is what makes the insolubility of
the quintic a theorem rather than a hope; group actions on
themselves beyond the class equation; representation theory; and the
Galois correspondence, which is the reason most of this exists.
