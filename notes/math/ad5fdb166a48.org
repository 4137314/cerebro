#+TITLE: The absolute value and the triangle inequality
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A03, 26D15
#+HASH: ad5fdb166a48

Every convergence proof in the course is an estimate on
\(\abs{a - b}\), and every one of them splits that estimate through an
intermediate point. The two lines below are the whole reason that move
is legal, so they belong before the first \(\eps\), not in a footnote to
it.

#+NAME: def-absolute-value
#+begin_definition
The /absolute value/ of \(x \in \R\) is \(\abs{x} = x\) if \(x \ge 0\)
and \(\abs{x} = -x\) if \(x < 0\). Equivalently
\(\abs{x} = \max(x, -x)\), so \(\abs{x} \ge 0\), \(\abs{x} \ge x\) and
\(\abs{x} \ge -x\), with \(\abs{x} = 0\) only for \(x = 0\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-absolute-value
#+NAME: prop-absolute-value-bound
#+begin_proposition
For \(x, c \in \R\) and \(c \ge 0\): \(\abs{x} \le c\) if and only if
\(-c \le x \le c\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
If \(\abs{x} \le c\) then \(x \le \abs{x} \le c\) and
\(-x \le \abs{x} \le c\), the latter giving \(x \ge -c\). Conversely, if
\(-c \le x \le c\) then both \(x \le c\) and \(-x \le c\), and
\(\abs{x}\) is one of the two.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-absolute-value-bound
#+NAME: thm-triangle-inequality
#+begin_theorem
For all \(x, y \in \R\),
\[ \abs{x + y} \le \abs{x} + \abs{y}. \]
Consequently \(\abs{x - z} \le \abs{x - y} + \abs{y - z}\) for all
\(x, y, z \in \R\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
By the proposition, \(-\abs{x} \le x \le \abs{x}\) and
\(-\abs{y} \le y \le \abs{y}\). Adding,
\(-(\abs{x} + \abs{y}) \le x + y \le \abs{x} + \abs{y}\), and the
proposition read the other way gives
\(\abs{x + y} \le \abs{x} + \abs{y}\). For the second form apply the
first to \(x - z = (x - y) + (y - z)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-triangle-inequality
#+NAME: cor-reverse-triangle-inequality
#+begin_corollary
For all \(x, y \in \R\),
\[ \bigl| \abs{x} - \abs{y} \bigr| \le \abs{x - y}. \]
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
From \(x = (x - y) + y\) the triangle inequality gives
\(\abs{x} \le \abs{x - y} + \abs{y}\), that is
\(\abs{x} - \abs{y} \le \abs{x - y}\). Exchanging \(x\) and \(y\) gives
\(\abs{y} - \abs{x} \le \abs{y - x} = \abs{x - y}\). The left-hand side
of the claim is one of these two quantities.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The second form of the triangle inequality is the one that gets used:
it says the distance from \(x\) to \(z\) is at most the distance
through any waypoint \(y\), and choosing that waypoint well -- the
limit, a partial sum, a point of a subsequence -- is what nearly every
proof in this course does. The corollary is its usual companion: it
makes \(x \mapsto \abs{x}\) continuous with \(\delta = \eps\), and it
is what lets an estimate on \(\abs{x - y}\) be spent directly on
\(\abs{x}\).
#+end_remark
