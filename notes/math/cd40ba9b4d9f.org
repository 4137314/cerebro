#+TITLE: Dual spaces, annihilators and the transpose
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:linear_algebra:
#+MSC: 15A03, 15A04
#+HASH: cd40ba9b4d9f

Every subspace can be described in two ways: by generators, or by the
linear equations it satisfies. The dual space is the home of those
equations, and the dictionary between the two descriptions -- subspace
against annihilator, map against transpose -- reverses inclusions and
exchanges kernels with images. In finite dimension the dictionary is
perfect, and the failure of that perfection in infinite dimension is
what makes functional analysis a subject.

#+DEPENDS: def-vector-space, def-linear-map
#+NAME: def-dual-space
#+begin_definition
The /dual space/ of a vector space \(V\) over \(K\) is
\(V^{*} = \Hom(V,K)\), the set of linear maps \(V \to K\) -- the
/linear functionals/ -- with pointwise operations.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-dual-space, prop-basis-gives-unique-coordinates, thm-dimension-is-well-defined
#+NAME: thm-dual-basis
#+begin_theorem
Let \(e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\) be a basis of \(V\). The functionals
\(e^{i}\) defined by \(e^{i}(e_{j}) = \delta_{ij}\) form a basis of
\(V^{*}\); in particular \(\dim V^{*} = \dim V\) in finite dimension.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Each \(e^{i}\) is well defined by prescribing its values on a basis.
They span: for \(\varphi \in V^{*}\) and \(v = \sum_{j}x_{j}e_{j}\),
linearity gives \(\varphi(v) = \sum_{j}x_{j}\varphi(e_{j})\), so
\(\varphi = \sum_{i}\varphi(e_{i})e^{i}\). They are independent:
applying \(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}e^{i} = 0\) to \(e_{j}\) gives
\(\alpha_{j} = 0\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-dual-space, thm-dual-basis, cor-extension-to-a-basis
#+NAME: def-annihilator
#+begin_definition
For \(U \subseteq V\) a subspace, the /annihilator/ is
\(U^{\circ} = \setst{\varphi \in V^{*}}{\varphi(u) = 0 \text{ for all } u \in U}\),
a subspace of \(V^{*}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-annihilator, cor-extension-to-a-basis, thm-rank-nullity
#+NAME: thm-annihilator-dimension
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\dim V = n\) and \(U \subseteq V\) a subspace of dimension \(k\).
Then \(\dim U^{\circ} = n - k\), and \(U\) is exactly the set of vectors
annihilated by \(U^{\circ}\). Hence a \(k\)-dimensional subspace is the
solution set of \(n-k\) independent linear equations.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Extend a basis \(e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\) of \(U\) to a basis of \(V\). A
functional \(\varphi = \sum_{i}\alpha_{i}e^{i}\) annihilates \(U\)
exactly when \(\alpha_{1} = \cdots = \alpha_{k} = 0\), so \(U^{\circ}\)
is spanned by \(e^{k+1},\dots,e^{n}\) and has dimension \(n-k\).

If \(v \notin U\), write \(v\) in the basis: some coordinate
\(x_{j}\) with \(j > k\) is non-zero, and \(e^{j} \in U^{\circ}\) does
not annihilate \(v\). So the vectors annihilated by all of
\(U^{\circ}\) are exactly those of \(U\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-dual-space, def-linear-map
#+NAME: def-transpose
#+begin_definition
For a linear \(f \colon V \to W\), the /transpose/
\(f^{*} \colon W^{*} \to V^{*}\) is \(f^{*}(\varphi) = \varphi \circ f\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-transpose, thm-annihilator-dimension, thm-rank-nullity
#+NAME: thm-transpose-kernel-and-image
#+begin_theorem
For linear \(f \colon V \to W\) between finite-dimensional spaces,
\(f^{*}\) is linear, \(\ker f^{*} = (\im f)^{\circ}\),
\(\im f^{*} = (\ker f)^{\circ}\), and \(f\) and \(f^{*}\) have the same
rank. Moreover \((g\circ f)^{*} = f^{*}\circ g^{*}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Linearity and the composition rule are immediate from the definition.

\(f^{*}(\varphi) = 0\) says \(\varphi(f(v)) = 0\) for all \(v\), that is
\(\varphi \in (\im f)^{\circ}\). For the image: every
\(\varphi\circ f\) kills \(\ker f\), so
\(\im f^{*}\subseteq(\ker f)^{\circ}\); and by rank-nullity applied
twice,
\(\dim \im f^{*} = \dim W^{*} - \dim\ker f^{*} = \dim W - \dim(\im f)^{\circ} = \dim\im f\),
while \(\dim(\ker f)^{\circ} = \dim V - \dim\ker f = \dim \im f\) as
well. Equal dimensions and an inclusion give equality, and the same
computation shows the two ranks agree.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-dual-space, thm-dual-basis, prop-kernel-and-image-are-subspaces
#+NAME: thm-double-dual
#+begin_theorem
The map \(\eta \colon V \to V^{**}\) with
\(\eta(v)(\varphi) = \varphi(v)\) is linear and injective, and it is an
isomorphism when \(V\) is finite-dimensional. It is defined without
reference to a basis.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Linearity in \(v\) is immediate. Injectivity: if \(v \ne 0\), extend
\(\set{v}\) to a basis and take the coordinate functional dual to
\(v\); it does not vanish at \(v\), so \(\eta(v)\ne0\). In finite
dimension \(\dim V^{**} = \dim V^{*} = \dim V\), so an injective linear
map between them is an isomorphism.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
That \(\eta\) needs no basis, while the isomorphism \(V \iso V^{*}\)
does, is the substance of the distinction between a vector and a
functional -- the reason physics writes indices up and down. In infinite
dimension \(\eta\) is still injective and no longer surjective, and the
spaces for which it happens to be surjective are called reflexive; the
Hahn-Banach theorem is what keeps the injectivity true there.
#+end_remark
