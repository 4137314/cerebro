#+TITLE: The Riemann integral
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A42
#+HASH: 4a6533d0fe23

The integral is defined by squeezing: approximate the area from below
and from above, and call the function integrable when the two
approximations meet. Nothing about the definition presumes continuity;
that hypothesis enters only to prove that they do meet.

#+NAME: def-partition
#+begin_definition
A /partition/ of \([a,b]\) is a finite set
\(P = \set{x_{0}, \dots, x_{n}}\) with
\(a = x_{0} < x_{1} < \cdots < x_{n} = b\). A partition \(P'\) /refines/
\(P\) if \(P \subseteq P'\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-partition, def-supremum
#+NAME: def-darboux-sums
#+begin_definition
Let \(f \colon [a,b] \to \R\) be bounded and \(P\) a partition. Writing
\(m_{i} = \inf_{[x_{i-1}, x_{i}]} f\) and
\(M_{i} = \sup_{[x_{i-1}, x_{i}]} f\), the /lower/ and /upper Darboux
sums/ of \(f\) over \(P\) are
\[ L(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_{i}(x_{i} - x_{i-1}), \qquad U(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} M_{i}(x_{i} - x_{i-1}). \]
Refining a partition increases \(L\) and decreases \(U\), so every lower
sum is at most every upper sum.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-darboux-sums
#+NAME: def-riemann-integral
#+begin_definition
The /lower/ and /upper integrals/ of a bounded
\(f \colon [a,b] \to \R\) are \(\underline{I}(f) = \sup_{P} L(f,P)\) and
\(\overline{I}(f) = \inf_{P} U(f,P)\); they satisfy
\(\underline{I}(f) \le \overline{I}(f)\). The function is
/Riemann integrable/ if they are equal, and their common value is
\(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \dd x\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-riemann-integral
#+NAME: thm-riemann-criterion
#+begin_theorem
A bounded \(f \colon [a,b] \to \R\) is Riemann integrable if and only if
for every \(\eps > 0\) there is a partition \(P\) with
\(U(f,P) - L(f,P) < \eps\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose the condition holds and let \(\eps > 0\), with \(P\) as given.
Since \(L(f,P) \le \underline{I}(f) \le \overline{I}(f) \le U(f,P)\),
the two integrals differ by less than \(\eps\); as \(\eps\) was
arbitrary they are equal. Conversely, if \(f\) is integrable with
integral \(I\), choose by the characterisation of the supremum and the
infimum partitions \(P_{1}, P_{2}\) with \(L(f,P_{1}) > I - \eps/2\) and
\(U(f,P_{2}) < I + \eps/2\). Their common refinement \(P\) satisfies
both inequalities, hence \(U(f,P) - L(f,P) < \eps\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-riemann-criterion, thm-heine-cantor
#+NAME: thm-continuous-is-integrable
#+begin_theorem
Every continuous function on \([a,b]\) is Riemann integrable.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(\eps > 0\). By the Heine--Cantor theorem \(f\) is uniformly
continuous, so there is \(\delta > 0\) with
\(\abs{f(x) - f(y)} < \eps/(b-a)\) whenever \(\abs{x - y} < \delta\).
Take any partition \(P\) with all subintervals shorter than \(\delta\).
On each subinterval \(f\) attains its supremum and infimum (extreme
value theorem), at points less than \(\delta\) apart, so
\(M_{i} - m_{i} \le \eps/(b-a)\) and
\[ U(f,P) - L(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (M_{i} - m_{i})(x_{i} - x_{i-1}) \le \frac{\eps}{b-a} (b - a) = \eps. \]
The criterion above gives integrability.
#+end_proof
