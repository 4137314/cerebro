#+TITLE: The Legendre transform
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:mathematical_physics:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A51, 70H05
#+HASH: 7e0d9e3cf01a

Passing from the Lagrangian to the Hamiltonian is not a change of
notation: it is a change of *variable*, from velocity to momentum, and
what makes it invertible is convexity. Stated for a convex function of
one variable first, because that is where the hypothesis is visible and
the geometry -- a function recovered from the family of its tangent
lines -- is the whole idea.

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-function, def-supremum
#+NAME: def-legendre-transform
#+begin_definition
Let \(f \colon \R \to \R\) be convex. Its /Legendre transform/ is
\[ f^{*}(p) = \sup_{v \in \R} \bigl( p v - f(v) \bigr), \]
defined for those \(p\) where the supremum is finite.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-legendre-transform, def-convex-function
#+NAME: prop-legendre-transform-is-convex
#+begin_proposition
\(f^{*}\) is convex on the set where it is finite.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
For each fixed \(v\) the map \(p \mapsto pv - f(v)\) is affine, hence
convex. A supremum of convex functions is convex: for
\(\lambda \in [0,1]\),
\(\lambda g_{v}(p) + (1-\lambda) g_{v}(p') \ge g_{v}(\lambda p + (1-\lambda)p')\)
for every \(v\), and taking the supremum on the left over \(v\) --
which only increases it -- preserves the inequality.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-legendre-transform, prop-convexity-from-second-derivative, lem-interior-extremum, cor-monotone-from-derivative
#+NAME: thm-legendre-stationary-point
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f\) be twice continuously differentiable with \(f'' > 0\). Then
for each \(p\) in the range of \(f'\) the supremum defining
\(f^{*}(p)\) is attained at the unique \(v\) with \(f'(v) = p\), and
\[ f^{*}(p) = p v - f(v), \qquad (f^{*})'(p) = v. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix \(p\) and put \(g(v) = pv - f(v)\), so \(g'(v) = p - f'(v)\) and
\(g'' = -f'' < 0\). Since \(f'' > 0\), \(f'\) is strictly increasing, so
\(g'\) vanishes at exactly one \(v\), and \(g\) increases before it and
decreases after: that \(v\) is the unique maximum, so the supremum is
attained there and equals \(pv - f(v)\).

For the derivative, let \(v(p)\) denote that unique point. Then
\(f^{*}(p) = p \, v(p) - f(v(p))\), and differentiating,
\[ (f^{*})'(p) = v(p) + p \, v'(p) - f'(v(p)) v'(p) = v(p), \]
because \(f'(v(p)) = p\) makes the last two terms cancel.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The transform is an involution on strictly convex functions -- the
derivative of \(f^{*}\) inverts the derivative of \(f\) -- and that is
why the passage to the Hamiltonian loses nothing. Convexity of \(L\) in
\(\dot q\) is therefore not a technical convenience: it is the exact
condition under which velocity and momentum determine each other.
#+end_remark
