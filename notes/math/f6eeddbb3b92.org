#+TITLE: Functional analysis
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:functional_analysis:
#+MSC: 46-XX, 47-XX

Functional analysis is linear algebra on spaces where the unit ball is
not compact. Every difference from the finite-dimensional theory traces
back to that one fact, and every theorem below is a substitute for
something compactness used to provide: a basis, a limit, a bound, a
separating functional.

Part of [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][Analysis]].

* The dividing line

[[id:ee85e495-a9cc-40e1-a273-076fbde3d8c0][Normed spaces and the equivalence of norms in finite dimension]] is the
note that draws it: in finite dimension all norms are equivalent, the
sphere is compact, subspaces are closed, and linear maps are
automatically continuous. Each of those four statements fails in
infinite dimension, and the subject is the list of what replaces them.

* The replacements

- For a *separating functional*: [[id:22ffdad0-babd-4070-bfce-78778aa1fb8b][the Hahn-Banach theorem]], which
  extends a dominated functional off a subspace by Zorn's lemma. It is
  the reason the [[id:6f3bed75-1321-4c80-b0bd-c8932e0c2837][dual space]] is large enough to be useful.
- For a *bound*: [[id:6e6d4450-92e9-4e21-ab0b-ac8950b9d2f0][uniform boundedness, open mapping and closed graph]] --
  three theorems, one proof scheme, all resting on [[id:44de8493-8687-4cfd-8c39-65561e38033c][Baire's theorem]] and
  therefore on completeness.
- For a *convergent subsequence*: [[id:e0a22a46-03da-4e30-a730-64bf076da027][the Arzela-Ascoli theorem]], which
  says what has to be added to boundedness -- equicontinuity -- to
  recover it in a space of functions.
- For *coordinates*: [[id:642c93ab-d004-4829-b5fa-899759923d63][Hilbert spaces]], where an inner product survives
  and with it orthogonal projection, the representation of every
  functional, and orthonormal bases.

* Why Hilbert space is the easy case

The [[id:c2edf7f5-d5ac-4e50-8734-a00b2da22cf4][orthogonal decomposition]] of a finite-dimensional inner product
space carries over verbatim once the subspace is closed, because the
[[id:203494e0-5225-4540-9a81-f61cab21d41b][nearest point in a closed convex set]] still exists -- and that argument
uses the parallelogram identity, which is exactly what a norm coming
from an inner product has and a general norm does not. Everything
special about Hilbert spaces is downstream of that identity.

* What is missing

\(L^{p}\) spaces, which are the examples the general theory was built
for and which need [[id:9910fd2f-5f5e-4be0-8785-c2b9904dc001][measure theory]] to define; weak topologies and the
Banach-Alaoglu theorem, which is where [[id:dcebacce-9383-47a7-a556-580d2ee38e5b][Tychonoff]] pays off; spectral
theory for operators, of which only [[id:dde5958e-70f1-4680-9e80-06de5649c142][the finite-dimensional symmetric
case]] is present; compact operators; and Fourier analysis, which would
be the first serious use of [[id:642c93ab-d004-4829-b5fa-899759923d63][orthonormal bases]].
