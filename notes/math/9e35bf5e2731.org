#+TITLE: Probability spaces, conditioning and independence
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60A05
#+HASH: 9e35bf5e2731

A probability is a [[id:9910fd2f-5f5e-4be0-8785-c2b9904dc001][measure]] of total mass one, and everything formal
about it -- monotonicity, continuity along increasing sequences,
countable subadditivity -- is inherited from that and needs no separate
proof. What is genuinely probabilistic begins with the two notions this
note adds, conditioning and independence, neither of which is a
measure-theoretic idea at all: they are hypotheses about how information
is distributed.

#+DEPENDS: def-measure, def-sigma-algebra, thm-basic-properties-of-a-measure
#+NAME: def-probability-space
#+begin_definition
A /probability space/ is a measure space \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\Prob)\)
with \(\Prob(\Omega) = 1\). Elements of \(\Omega\) are /outcomes/,
measurable sets are /events/, and \(\Prob(A)\) is the /probability/ of
\(A\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-probability-space, thm-basic-properties-of-a-measure
#+NAME: prop-elementary-rules
#+begin_proposition
For events \(A, B\): \(\Prob(A^{c}) = 1-\Prob(A)\);
\(\Prob(A\cup B) = \Prob(A)+\Prob(B)-\Prob(A\cap B)\); and
\(\Prob(\bigcup_{n}A_{n}) \le \sum_{n}\Prob(A_{n})\) for any sequence of
events. If \(A_{n}\uparrow A\) or \(A_{n}\downarrow A\), then
\(\Prob(A_{n})\to\Prob(A)\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Additivity on the disjoint pair \(A, A^{c}\) with total mass \(1\) gives
the first. For the second, \(A \cup B\) is the disjoint union of
\(A \setminus B\), \(A\cap B\) and \(B \setminus A\), and
\(\Prob(A) = \Prob(A\setminus B)+\Prob(A\cap B)\); substituting gives
the identity. Subadditivity and continuity from below are properties of
any measure; continuity from above needs \(\Prob(A_{1})<\infty\), which
here is automatic.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-probability-space
#+NAME: def-conditional-probability
#+begin_definition
For events \(A, B\) with \(\Prob(B)>0\), the /conditional probability/
of \(A\) given \(B\) is
\(\Prob(A \mid B) = \Prob(A\cap B)/\Prob(B)\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-conditional-probability, def-probability-space
#+NAME: prop-conditioning-is-a-probability
#+begin_proposition
For fixed \(B\) with \(\Prob(B)>0\), the map
\(A \mapsto \Prob(A\mid B)\) is a probability measure on
\((\Omega,\mathcal{F})\), concentrated on \(B\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
It is non-negative, assigns \(\Prob(B\cap\Omega)/\Prob(B) = 1\) to
\(\Omega\), and is countably additive because
\(A \mapsto \Prob(A \cap B)\) is, division by the constant
\(\Prob(B)\) preserving the sums. It assigns \(0\) to any event disjoint
from \(B\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-conditional-probability, def-set-partition, prop-elementary-rules
#+NAME: thm-total-probability-and-bayes
#+begin_theorem
Let \((B_{n})\) be a countable partition of \(\Omega\) into events of
positive probability, and \(A\) an event. Then
\[ \Prob(A) = \sum_{n}\Prob(A\mid B_{n})\,\Prob(B_{n}), \]
and if \(\Prob(A)>0\),
\(\Prob(B_{k}\mid A) = \Prob(A\mid B_{k})\Prob(B_{k}) / \sum_{n}\Prob(A\mid B_{n})\Prob(B_{n})\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
The events \(A \cap B_{n}\) are pairwise disjoint with union \(A\), so
countable additivity gives
\(\Prob(A) = \sum_{n}\Prob(A\cap B_{n})\), and each term is
\(\Prob(A\mid B_{n})\Prob(B_{n})\) by the definition. For the second
identity,
\(\Prob(B_{k}\mid A) = \Prob(A\cap B_{k})/\Prob(A)\); rewrite the
numerator as \(\Prob(A\mid B_{k})\Prob(B_{k})\) and the denominator by
the first identity.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-probability-space, def-conditional-probability
#+NAME: def-independence-of-events
#+begin_definition
Events \(A\) and \(B\) are /independent/ if
\(\Prob(A\cap B) = \Prob(A)\Prob(B)\). A family \((A_{i})_{i\in I}\) is
/independent/ if
\(\Prob(A_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap A_{i_{k}}) = \Prob(A_{i_{1}})\cdots\Prob(A_{i_{k}})\)
for every finite set of distinct indices.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-independence-of-events, prop-elementary-rules
#+NAME: prop-independence-and-complements
#+begin_proposition
If \(A\) and \(B\) are independent, so are \(A\) and \(B^{c}\); and if
\(\Prob(B)>0\), independence is equivalent to
\(\Prob(A\mid B) = \Prob(A)\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
\(\Prob(A\cap B^{c}) = \Prob(A)-\Prob(A\cap B) = \Prob(A)(1-\Prob(B)) = \Prob(A)\Prob(B^{c})\).
The second claim is the definition of conditional probability with the
factorisation substituted, in either direction.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Independence of a family is /not/ pairwise independence: three events
can be pairwise independent while the intersection of all three
factorises wrongly, which is why the definition quantifies over every
finite subfamily. The distinction is what makes the second
Borel-Cantelli lemma and the strong law true statements rather than
plausible ones.
#+end_remark
