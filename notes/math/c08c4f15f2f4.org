#+TITLE: Uniform continuity and the Heine--Cantor theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A15
#+HASH: c08c4f15f2f4

Continuity allows \(\delta\) to depend on the point; uniform continuity
does not. The distinction is invisible on a closed bounded interval --
that is the theorem below -- and decisive everywhere else: \(1/x\) on
\((0,1]\) is continuous and not uniformly so.

#+DEPENDS: def-continuity-at-point
#+NAME: def-uniform-continuity
#+begin_definition
A function \(f \colon D \to \R\) is /uniformly continuous/ if for every
\(\eps > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that
\(\abs{f(x) - f(y)} < \eps\) for all \(x, y \in D\) with
\(\abs{x - y} < \delta\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-uniform-continuity, thm-bolzano-weierstrass
#+NAME: thm-heine-cantor
#+begin_theorem
A continuous function on a closed bounded interval \([a,b]\) is
uniformly continuous.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose not. Then there is \(\eps > 0\) such that for every \(n\) the
choice \(\delta = 1/n\) fails: there are \(x_{n}, y_{n} \in [a,b]\) with
\(\abs{x_{n} - y_{n}} < 1/n\) and
\(\abs{f(x_{n}) - f(y_{n})} \ge \eps\). The sequence \((x_{n})\) is
bounded, so by Bolzano--Weierstrass a subsequence \(x_{n_{k}} \to c\),
and \(c \in [a,b]\) because the interval is closed. Since
\(\abs{x_{n_{k}} - y_{n_{k}}} \to 0\), also \(y_{n_{k}} \to c\).
Continuity at \(c\) gives \(f(x_{n_{k}}) \to f(c)\) and
\(f(y_{n_{k}}) \to f(c)\), so
\(\abs{f(x_{n_{k}}) - f(y_{n_{k}})} \to 0\), contradicting that it is at
least \(\eps\) for every \(k\).
#+end_proof
