#+TITLE: Characteristic functions
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:probability_theory:
#+MSC: 60E10
#+HASH: a9bac540f64f

Convolving two laws to find the law of a sum is unpleasant; multiplying
two functions is not. The characteristic function turns the first
operation into the second, and it does so without any integrability
hypothesis, because the integrand has modulus one. Everything after that
is the price: recovering the law from the transform, and recognising
convergence of laws in convergence of transforms.

#+DEPENDS: def-expectation, def-random-variable, thm-transfer
#+NAME: def-characteristic-function
#+begin_definition
The /characteristic function/ of a random variable \(X\) is
\[ \varphi_{X}(t) = \E[e^{itX}] = \E[\cos(tX)] + i\,\E[\sin(tX)], \qquad t \in \R, \]
which is defined for every \(t\) because the integrand is bounded.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-characteristic-function, thm-dominated-convergence, thm-expectation-of-a-product
#+NAME: prop-properties-of-the-characteristic-function
#+begin_proposition
\(\varphi_{X}(0)=1\), \(\abs{\varphi_{X}(t)}\le1\), \(\varphi_{X}\) is
uniformly continuous, \(\varphi_{aX+b}(t) = e^{itb}\varphi_{X}(at)\),
and if \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent then
\(\varphi_{X+Y} = \varphi_{X}\varphi_{Y}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
The first two are immediate from \(\abs{e^{itX}} = 1\) and
monotonicity. For continuity,
\(\abs{\varphi_{X}(t+h)-\varphi_{X}(t)} \le \E\abs{e^{ihX}-1}\), a bound
independent of \(t\); the integrand is bounded by \(2\) and tends to
\(0\) pointwise as \(h \to 0\), so dominated convergence makes it tend
to \(0\), which is uniform continuity. The affine rule is
\(e^{it(aX+b)} = e^{itb}e^{i(ta)X}\). For a sum,
\(e^{it(X+Y)} = e^{itX}e^{itY}\) and the two factors are independent
bounded variables, so the expectation of the product is the product of
the expectations, applied to real and imaginary parts.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-properties-of-the-characteristic-function, thm-dominated-convergence, thm-taylor-peano
#+NAME: thm-moments-and-the-expansion-at-zero
#+begin_theorem
If \(\E[X^{2}]<\infty\), then \(\varphi_{X}\) is twice differentiable
with \(\varphi_{X}'(0) = i\E[X]\) and
\(\varphi_{X}''(0) = -\E[X^{2}]\); consequently
\[ \varphi_{X}(t) = 1 + it\E[X] - \tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\E[X^{2}] + o(t^{2}) \qquad (t \to 0). \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
For the first derivative, the difference quotient of \(e^{itX}\) in
\(t\) is bounded in modulus by \(\abs{X}\), which is integrable since
\(\E[X^{2}]<\infty\) implies \(\E\abs{X}<\infty\); dominated convergence
allows differentiation under the expectation, giving
\(\varphi_{X}'(t) = \E[iXe^{itX}]\). The same argument with the bound
\(X^{2}\) gives \(\varphi_{X}''(t) = \E[-X^{2}e^{itX}]\). Evaluating at
\(t = 0\) gives the two values.

The expansion is Taylor's theorem with the Peano remainder applied to
the real and imaginary parts of \(\varphi_{X}\), both twice
differentiable at \(0\) with the stated derivatives.
#+end_proof

* Recovering the law                                            :folklore:

The two facts below are what make characteristic functions a tool rather
than a curiosity, and both are quoted here rather than proved: the
inversion formula is a computation with Fubini's theorem and a Dirichlet
integral, and Levy's theorem rests on it. They are standard, long, and
independent of everything else in this archive.

#+STATUS: assumed
#+DEPENDS: def-characteristic-function
#+NAME: thm-uniqueness-of-the-characteristic-function
#+begin_theorem
Two random variables with the same characteristic function have the same
law.
#+end_theorem

#+STATUS: assumed
#+DEPENDS: thm-uniqueness-of-the-characteristic-function, def-modes-of-convergence
#+NAME: thm-levy-continuity
#+begin_theorem
Let \((X_{n})\) be random variables and suppose
\(\varphi_{X_{n}}(t) \to \psi(t)\) for every \(t\), with \(\psi\)
continuous at \(0\). Then \(\psi\) is the characteristic function of a
random variable \(X\), and \(X_{n} \to X\) in distribution.
#+end_theorem

#+end_remark
