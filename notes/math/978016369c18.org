#+TITLE: Convex hulls and Caratheodory's theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:convex_geometry:
#+MSC: 52A20, 52A35
#+HASH: 978016369c18

The convex hull of a set is defined by an intersection, which says
nothing about how to reach a point of it. Caratheodory's theorem says
one never needs more than \(n+1\) of the original points, whatever the
set: convexity in dimension \(n\) is a finitary condition, and this is
the reason the theory has compactness statements at all.

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-set
#+NAME: def-convex-hull
#+begin_definition
The /convex hull/ \(\operatorname{conv} S\) of \(S \subseteq \R^{n}\) is the
intersection of all convex sets containing \(S\). A /convex combination/
of \(v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\) is \(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\) with
\(\lambda_{i} \ge 0\) and \(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i} = 1\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-convex-hull
#+NAME: prop-hull-is-the-set-of-convex-combinations
#+begin_proposition
An intersection of convex sets is convex, so \(\operatorname{conv} S\) is the smallest
convex set containing \(S\); and \(\operatorname{conv} S\) is exactly the set of
convex combinations of finitely many points of \(S\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
If each \(C_{i}\) is convex and \(x, y\) lie in all of them, then so
does \((1-t)x+ty\) for \(t \in [0,1]\); hence the intersection is
convex, and by construction it is contained in every convex set
containing \(S\).

Let \(K\) be the set of convex combinations of points of \(S\). Any
convex set containing \(S\) contains \(K\): by induction on \(k\), if
\(\lambda_{k} < 1\) then
\(\sum_{i \le k}\lambda_{i}v_{i} = (1-\lambda_{k}) w + \lambda_{k}v_{k}\)
with \(w = \sum_{i<k}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{k}}v_{i}\) a convex
combination of \(k-1\) points. So \(K \subseteq \operatorname{conv} S\). Conversely
\(K\) is itself convex -- a convex combination of two convex
combinations is one, after collecting coefficients, which are again
non-negative and sum to \(1\) -- and it contains \(S\), so
\(\operatorname{conv} S \subseteq K\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-hull-is-the-set-of-convex-combinations, def-span-and-independence
#+NAME: thm-caratheodory
#+begin_theorem
Every point of \(\operatorname{conv} S\), for \(S \subseteq \R^{n}\), is a convex
combination of at most \(n+1\) points of \(S\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(x = \sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\) be a convex combination with
\(k\) minimal, all \(\lambda_{i} > 0\), and suppose \(k > n+1\). The
\(k-1 > n\) vectors \(v_{2}-v_{1}, \dots, v_{k}-v_{1}\) lie in
\(\R^{n}\), so they are dependent: there are \(\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{k}\),
not all zero, with \(\sum_{i \ge 2}\mu_{i}(v_{i}-v_{1}) = 0\). Putting
\(\mu_{1} = -\sum_{i\ge2}\mu_{i}\) gives
\(\sum_{i}\mu_{i}v_{i} = 0\) and \(\sum_{i}\mu_{i} = 0\), with some
\(\mu_{i} \ne 0\) and therefore some \(\mu_{i} > 0\).

Let \(t = \min\setst{\lambda_{i}/\mu_{i}}{\mu_{i} > 0}\), attained at
\(i = j\). Then \(x = \sum_{i}(\lambda_{i} - t\mu_{i})v_{i}\), the new
coefficients are non-negative -- for \(\mu_{i} \le 0\) obviously, and
for \(\mu_{i} > 0\) by the choice of \(t\) -- they sum to \(1\), and the
\(j\)-th vanishes. This is a convex combination of \(k-1\) of the
points, contradicting minimality. Hence \(k \le n+1\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-caratheodory, thm-bolzano-weierstrass, prop-closed-iff-sequentially-closed
#+NAME: cor-hull-of-a-compact-set-is-closed
#+begin_corollary
If \(S \subseteq \R^{n}\) is closed and bounded, so is \(\operatorname{conv} S\).
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Boundedness is clear, a convex combination of points of norm at most
\(R\) having norm at most \(R\). For closedness, let
\(x_{m} \to x\) with \(x_{m} \in \operatorname{conv} S\). By the theorem, write
\(x_{m} = \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{i}^{(m)}v_{i}^{(m)}\) with
\(v_{i}^{(m)} \in S\) and \(\lambda^{(m)}\) in the set of non-negative
vectors summing to \(1\) (padding with repeated points if fewer than
\(n+1\) are needed). Both the \(v_{i}^{(m)}\) and the
\(\lambda^{(m)}\) range over closed bounded sets, so by
Bolzano-Weierstrass, applied finitely many times, a subsequence has
\(v_{i}^{(m)} \to v_{i} \in S\) and
\(\lambda^{(m)} \to \lambda\) with \(\lambda_{i} \ge 0\) summing to
\(1\). Passing to the limit gives \(x = \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}v_{i} \in \operatorname{conv} S\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Without Caratheodory the corollary is not available in this form: the
number of points in the combinations would grow with \(m\) and there
would be no fixed finite-dimensional compact set to extract a
convergent subsequence from. That is the sense in which the theorem is
about finiteness rather than about counting.
#+end_remark
