The rest of the archive uses sets, functions, quotients and proofs
without comment. This area is where those become objects of study rather
than instruments: what a set can be, what a proof is, and what follows
about the limits of both. It is also, in practice, where the tools live
-- Zorn's lemma and the ultrafilter lemma are used far more often here
than they are discussed.

Part of [[id:c2fcb100-fc5a-4670-9b5d-c068c0c325ff][Mathematics]].

* The subjects

- [[id:2601b3b5-f2ba-444f-a80d-7cdef30c5065][Set theory]] -- relations and quotients, order, choice, transfinite
  induction and recursion, cardinality, filters.
- [[id:483f9ad5-72a5-4e63-9623-51a677395e27][Logic]] -- formulas as objects: satisfaction, derivation, the
  relation between the two, and what no derivation can decide.

* The two theorems everything else uses

[[id:3cb0cea8-d46a-44cf-ad4f-39b88f22d50e][Zorn's lemma]] and [[id:e3c053e0-2462-4692-8b0d-ee81002ec63c][the ultrafilter lemma]] are the export products of
this area. Between them they produce a [[id:75bbba6c-ad62-4369-9a49-17beefdde80f][basis of any vector space]], a
[[id:c9c12e48-ddd2-4359-917d-138e90df9416][maximal ideal of any ring]], a [[id:22ffdad0-babd-4070-bfce-78778aa1fb8b][Hahn-Banach extension]], [[id:dcebacce-9383-47a7-a556-580d2ee38e5b][Tychonoff's
theorem]], the [[id:97912891-a007-42a1-8b9f-410b0c47e802][compactness theorem for first-order logic]] and the [[id:22a8386a-16ba-435b-a71d-26d9886886d0][Stone
representation]] of a Boolean algebra. Every one of those proofs produces
an object that no construction reaches, and it is worth knowing which
results in a subject are of that kind.

* Syntax against semantics

The characteristic move of logic is to define two relations and then
compare them: \(\Sigma \models \varphi\), which quantifies over all
assignments, and \(\Sigma\vdash\varphi\), which asks for a finite
object. [[id:36cd1905-a97d-4a57-8df6-c624798c4eb9][Soundness]] is the easy direction and [[id:77484172-ce88-4a74-8fcf-b70251b5034f][completeness]] the
substantial one; [[id:b3043ad1-2aac-4361-8f17-605013e7a12a][compactness]] is what the finiteness of derivations
forces, and it can also be proved semantically, by Zorn or by
[[id:97912891-a007-42a1-8b9f-410b0c47e802][ultraproducts]], which is the same argument seen from the other side.
Then [[id:4400af63-8a49-4b93-a45a-6dc1505f616c][Lowenheim-Skolem]] says how little a first-order theory controls,
and [[id:eb14cc58-7808-4c04-a956-042143f30664][the halting problem]] says that some questions have no decision
procedure at all.

* What is missing

The axioms of set theory are used and never written down -- there is no
note stating ZF. Godel's completeness theorem for first-order logic is
absent, its consequence (compactness) having been obtained by
ultraproducts instead; the incompleteness theorems are absent, and with
them arithmetic as an object of study; and category theory, which is
where the repeated constructions of [[id:504cda55-cfbf-42c2-b481-8d90660ec02a][algebra]] and [[id:830a0aed-a379-4350-bd58-f776af9b87d7][topology]] are properly
stated, does not exist here at all.
