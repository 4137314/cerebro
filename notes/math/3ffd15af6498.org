#+TITLE: Limit superior and limit inferior
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 40A05
#+HASH: 3ffd15af6498

A bounded sequence need not converge, but two numbers are always
attached to it, and they exist without any hypothesis at all. That is
their point: \(\limsup\) and \(\liminf\) turn "does this converge?"
into an equation between two quantities that are already there, which
is what the root test and every later oscillation argument need.

#+DEPENDS: def-subsequence, def-sequence-limit
#+NAME: prop-subsequence-limit
#+begin_proposition
If \(a_{n} \to L\), then every subsequence of \((a_{n})\) converges to
\(L\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \((a_{n_{k}})\) be a subsequence and \(\eps > 0\). Choose \(N\) with
\(\abs{a_{n} - L} < \eps\) for \(n \ge N\). Since the indices are
strictly increasing, \(n_{k} \ge k\) for every \(k\), so \(k \ge N\)
gives \(n_{k} \ge N\) and hence \(\abs{a_{n_{k}} - L} < \eps\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-bounded-sequence, ax-completeness, def-supremum
#+NAME: def-limsup-liminf
#+begin_definition
Let \((a_{n})\) be a bounded sequence. The sequences
\(s_{N} = \sup_{n \ge N} a_{n}\) and \(t_{N} = \inf_{n \ge N} a_{n}\)
are well defined by completeness, decreasing and increasing
respectively, and bounded. Their limits
\[ \limsup_{n \to \infty} a_{n} = \lim_{N \to \infty} s_{N} = \inf_{N} \sup_{n \ge N} a_{n}, \qquad \liminf_{n \to \infty} a_{n} = \lim_{N \to \infty} t_{N} = \sup_{N} \inf_{n \ge N} a_{n} \]
are the /limit superior/ and /limit inferior/ of \((a_{n})\). Always
\(\liminf a_{n} \le \limsup a_{n}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-limsup-liminf, prop-supremum-characterisation
#+NAME: prop-limsup-characterisation
#+begin_proposition
Let \((a_{n})\) be bounded and \(L = \limsup a_{n}\). Then for every
\(\eps > 0\) we have \(a_{n} < L + \eps\) for all but finitely many
\(n\), and \(a_{n} > L - \eps\) for infinitely many \(n\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Fix \(\eps > 0\). Since \(s_{N} \to L\) and \((s_{N})\) is decreasing,
there is \(N\) with \(s_{N} < L + \eps\), that is \(a_{n} < L + \eps\)
for every \(n \ge N\); only the finitely many terms before \(N\) can
fail it. For the second claim, note \(s_{N} \ge L\) for every \(N\)
because \((s_{N})\) decreases to \(L\). Given \(N\), the
characterisation of the supremum applied to
\(s_{N} = \sup_{n \ge N} a_{n}\) produces \(n \ge N\) with
\(a_{n} > s_{N} - \eps \ge L - \eps\). As \(N\) was arbitrary, such
\(n\) occur arbitrarily late.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-limsup-characterisation, thm-squeeze, prop-subsequence-limit
#+NAME: thm-limsup-criterion
#+begin_theorem
A bounded sequence \((a_{n})\) converges if and only if
\(\liminf a_{n} = \limsup a_{n}\), and then the common value is the
limit.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Write \(t_{N} = \inf_{n \ge N} a_{n}\) and
\(s_{N} = \sup_{n \ge N} a_{n}\), so that
\(t_{N} \le a_{N} \le s_{N}\) for every \(N\). If the
two limits agree, the squeeze theorem gives \(a_{N} \to\) that common
value.

Conversely let \(a_{n} \to L\) and \(\eps > 0\). Choose \(N\) with
\(L - \eps < a_{n} < L + \eps\) for \(n \ge N\). Then
\(L - \eps \le t_{N}\) and \(s_{N} \le L + \eps\), so both
\(\liminf a_{n}\) and \(\limsup a_{n}\) lie in
\([L - \eps, L + \eps]\). As \(\eps\) was arbitrary they both equal
\(L\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The first half of the characterisation is the form used in practice:
\(\limsup a_{n} < 1\) means the terms are eventually below some
\(r < 1\), which is exactly the hypothesis the root test wants. Stating
the root test with \(\limsup \abs{a_{n}}^{1/n}\) instead of a limit
therefore costs nothing and drops the assumption that the limit exists.
#+end_remark
