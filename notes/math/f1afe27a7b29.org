#+TITLE: Projective space, affine charts and the dimension formula
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:geometry:algebraic_geometry:
#+MSC: 51N15, 14A10
#+HASH: f1afe27a7b29

Two lines in a plane meet in a point, unless they are parallel. The
exception is not a fact about lines but about the plane: adding one
point per direction removes it, and the resulting space is the set of
lines through the origin of a vector space one dimension higher. Every
statement about intersections then becomes the Grassmann formula.

#+DEPENDS: def-vector-space, def-equivalence-relation, thm-equivalence-partition
#+NAME: def-projective-space
#+begin_definition
Let \(V\) be a vector space over a field \(K\). The /projective space/
\(\PP(V)\) is the set of one-dimensional subspaces of \(V\);
equivalently \((V \setminus \set{0})/{\sim}\) where \(v \sim w\) means
\(w = \lambda v\) for some \(\lambda \ne 0\). One writes
\(\PP^{n}(K) = \PP(K^{n+1})\), and \([x_{0} : \cdots : x_{n}]\) for the
class of \((x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\) -- its /homogeneous coordinates/. The
dimension of \(\PP(V)\) is \(\dim V - 1\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-projective-space, def-equivalence-relation
#+NAME: prop-projective-space-is-a-quotient
#+begin_proposition
The relation \(v \sim w\) above is an equivalence relation on
\(V \setminus \set{0}\), and its classes are exactly the sets
\(L \setminus \set{0}\) for \(L\) a one-dimensional subspace; so the two
descriptions in the definition agree.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Reflexivity uses \(\lambda = 1\), symmetry \(\lambda^{-1}\), and
transitivity the product of the two scalars -- all available since
\(K \setminus \set{0}\) is a group under multiplication. The class of
\(v \ne 0\) is \(\setst{\lambda v}{\lambda \ne 0} = \Span\set{v} \setminus \set{0}\),
and every one-dimensional subspace is \(\Span\set{v}\) for such a \(v\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-projective-space, prop-projective-space-is-a-quotient
#+NAME: prop-affine-charts
#+begin_proposition
For each \(i\) let \(U_{i} = \setst{[x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}]}{x_{i} \ne 0}\).
Then the \(U_{i}\) cover \(\PP^{n}(K)\), and
\[ \varphi_{i} \colon U_{i} \to K^{n}, \qquad [x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}] \mapsto (x_{0}/x_{i}, \dots, x_{i-1}/x_{i}, x_{i+1}/x_{i}, \dots, x_{n}/x_{i}) \]
is a well-defined bijection -- the \(i\)-th entry, which is always
\(1\), being omitted. Moreover \(\PP^{n}(K) \setminus U_{n}\) is a copy
of \(\PP^{n-1}(K)\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Every non-zero vector has some non-zero coordinate, so the \(U_{i}\)
cover. The formula is well defined because replacing \(x\) by
\(\lambda x\) multiplies numerator and denominator alike. It is
injective: from the image one recovers the point as
\([y_{0}:\cdots:1:\cdots:y_{n}]\) with \(1\) in position \(i\), and this
is a preimage, so it is also surjective. Finally \(x_{n} = 0\) cuts out
the classes of non-zero vectors of the hyperplane
\(\setst{x}{x_{n}=0} \iso K^{n}\), which is \(\PP^{n-1}(K)\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-projective-space, def-span-and-independence
#+NAME: def-projective-subspace
#+begin_definition
A /projective subspace/ of \(\PP(V)\) is \(\PP(W)\) for a linear
subspace \(W \subseteq V\); its dimension is \(\dim W - 1\). Subspaces
of dimension \(0, 1, 2\) are /points/, /lines/ and /planes/, and one of
dimension \(\dim\PP(V) - 1\) is a /hyperplane/.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-projective-subspace, prop-grassmann-formula
#+NAME: thm-projective-dimension-formula
#+begin_theorem
Let \(\PP(U)\) and \(\PP(W)\) be projective subspaces of \(\PP(V)\) with
\(V\) finite-dimensional. Then
\[ \dim \PP(U \cap W) = \dim\PP(U) + \dim\PP(W) - \dim\PP(U + W), \]
with the convention \(\dim\PP(\set{0}) = -1\). In particular, in
\(\PP^{2}(K)\) any two distinct lines meet in exactly one point.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Subtracting \(1\) from each term, the identity is exactly the Grassmann
formula \(\dim(U\cap W) = \dim U + \dim W - \dim(U+W)\). For two
distinct lines in \(\PP^{2}(K)\): they are \(\PP(U)\), \(\PP(W)\) with
\(\dim U = \dim W = 2\) inside \(\dim V = 3\), and \(U + W\) has
dimension \(3\), being strictly larger than \(U\); so
\(\dim(U \cap W) = 2 + 2 - 3 = 1\) and the intersection is a single
projective point.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The parallel case has not been handled separately anywhere above, and
that is the point of the construction: in the affine chart \(U_{2}\) the
two lines may fail to meet, and the missing intersection is a point of
the hyperplane at infinity \(\PP^{1}(K)\), which the formula counts like
any other. Removing exceptions from statements is what compactifying a
space is for.
#+end_remark
