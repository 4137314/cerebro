#+TITLE: Normed spaces and the equivalence of norms in finite dimension
#+DATE: [2026-08-09 Sun]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:functional_analysis:
#+MSC: 46B20, 15A60
#+HASH: 396ba5ec8582

A norm is a metric compatible with the linear structure, and there are
many of them on the same space. In finite dimension the choice turns out
not to matter for anything topological -- all norms are equivalent, so
convergence, continuity and compactness are properties of the vector
space alone. In infinite dimension the statement is false, and its
failure is the beginning of functional analysis.

#+DEPENDS: def-vector-space, def-metric-space
#+NAME: def-norm
#+begin_definition
A /norm/ on a real or complex vector space \(V\) is a map
\(\norm{\cdot} \colon V \to \R\) with \(\norm{v} \ge 0\) and
\(\norm{v} = 0\) only for \(v = 0\), \(\norm{\lambda v} = \abs{\lambda}\norm{v}\),
and \(\norm{u+v} \le \norm{u}+\norm{v}\). Two norms are /equivalent/ if
there are constants \(0 < m \le M\) with
\(m\norm{v}_{1} \le \norm{v}_{2} \le M \norm{v}_{1}\) for all \(v\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-norm, def-metric-space, def-metric-convergence
#+NAME: prop-a-norm-induces-a-metric
#+begin_proposition
\(d(u,v) = \norm{u-v}\) is a metric on \(V\), invariant under
translation and homogeneous of degree one. Equivalent norms induce the
same open sets, hence the same convergent sequences and the same
continuous maps. A /Banach space/ is a normed space complete for this
metric.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Positivity and symmetry are immediate, the latter from
\(\norm{-w} = \norm{w}\), and the triangle inequality for \(d\) is that
of the norm applied to \((u-w)+(w-v)\). If
\(m\norm{\cdot}_{1}\le\norm{\cdot}_{2}\le M\norm{\cdot}_{1}\), then
every ball for one norm contains a ball of proportional radius for the
other, so the two collections of open sets coincide, and with them
everything defined from them.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-norm, cor-heine-borel-in-euclidean-space, cor-extreme-value-on-a-compact-space, prop-basis-gives-unique-coordinates
#+NAME: thm-equivalence-of-norms-in-finite-dimension
#+begin_theorem
On a finite-dimensional real vector space any two norms are equivalent.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Fix a basis \(e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\) and let
\(\norm{x}_{1} = \sum_{i}\abs{x_{i}}\) in the corresponding coordinates,
a norm. It suffices to compare an arbitrary norm \(N\) with this one.

First, \(N(x) \le \sum_{i}\abs{x_{i}}N(e_{i}) \le C\norm{x}_{1}\) with
\(C = \max_{i}N(e_{i})\), by the triangle inequality and homogeneity.
Hence \(N\) is continuous for \(\norm{\cdot}_{1}\), since
\(\abs{N(x)-N(y)} \le N(x-y) \le C\norm{x-y}_{1}\).

The set \(S = \setst{x}{\norm{x}_{1} = 1}\) is closed and bounded in the
coordinates, hence compact by Heine-Borel, and non-empty for
\(n \ge 1\). So the continuous function \(N\) attains a minimum \(m\) on
\(S\), at some \(x_{0}\) with \(\norm{x_{0}}_{1} = 1\), so
\(x_{0} \ne 0\) and \(m = N(x_{0}) > 0\). For arbitrary \(x \ne 0\),
applying this to \(x/\norm{x}_{1}\) and using homogeneity gives
\(m\norm{x}_{1} \le N(x) \le C\norm{x}_{1}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-equivalence-of-norms-in-finite-dimension, thm-euclidean-space-complete, prop-a-norm-induces-a-metric
#+NAME: cor-finite-dimensional-normed-spaces-are-complete
#+begin_corollary
Every finite-dimensional normed space is a Banach space, and every
finite-dimensional subspace of a normed space is closed.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Let \(\dim V = n\) and identify \(V\) with \(\R^{n}\) through a basis.
By the theorem the given norm is equivalent to the Euclidean one, so the
two metrics have the same Cauchy sequences and the same limits;
\(\R^{n}\) is complete, hence so is \(V\). If \(W \subseteq Z\) is a
finite-dimensional subspace of a normed space, then \(W\) is complete
for the restricted norm, and a complete subspace is closed, since the
limit in \(Z\) of a convergent sequence of points of \(W\) is also its
limit in \(W\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Every hypothesis is used exactly once, and dimension is used twice: to
get a basis, and to get compactness of the unit sphere. Both fail in
infinite dimension -- the sphere of an infinite-dimensional normed space
is never compact -- and so does the conclusion: on the space of
polynomials, the supremum norm on \([0,1]\) and the sum of the absolute
values of the coefficients are not equivalent.
#+end_remark
