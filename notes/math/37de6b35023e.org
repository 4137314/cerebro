#+TITLE: The algebra of continuous functions and composition
#+DATE: [2026-08-07 Fri]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:analysis:real_analysis:
#+MSC: 26A15
#+HASH: 37de6b35023e

The sequential characterisation of the limit was proved so that the
theorems about sequences could be spent on functions without being
proved twice. This note spends them: the algebra of limits becomes the
algebra of continuous functions, and with it every polynomial and
rational function is continuous for free. Composition is the one rule
that does not come from a sequence argument in the same way, and it is
the one that needs care.

#+DEPENDS: prop-sequential-limit, thm-limit-algebra
#+NAME: thm-function-limit-algebra
#+begin_theorem
Let \(c\) be an accumulation point of \(D\), and let
\(\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L\) and \(\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = M\). Then
\(f + g \to L + M\) and \(fg \to LM\) as \(x \to c\), and
\(f/g \to L/M\) provided \(M \ne 0\) and \(g\) does not vanish on
\(D\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \((x_{n})\) be any sequence in \(D \setminus \set{c}\) with
\(x_{n} \to c\). By the sequential characterisation
\(f(x_{n}) \to L\) and \(g(x_{n}) \to M\), so by the algebra of limits
\((f+g)(x_{n}) \to L + M\), \((fg)(x_{n}) \to LM\), and
\((f/g)(x_{n}) \to L/M\) when \(M \ne 0\). Since this holds for every
such sequence, the sequential characterisation read backwards gives the
three limits.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-function-limit-algebra, def-continuity-at-point
#+NAME: thm-continuous-algebra
#+begin_theorem
If \(f, g \colon D \to \R\) are continuous at \(x_{0}\), then so are
\(f + g\), \(fg\) and, when \(g(x_{0}) \ne 0\), \(f/g\) on the set
where \(g\) does not vanish.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Continuity at \(x_{0}\) says \(\lim_{x \to x_{0}} f(x) = f(x_{0})\)
when \(x_{0}\) is an accumulation point of \(D\), so the previous
theorem gives the three limits as \(f(x_{0}) + g(x_{0})\),
\(f(x_{0}) g(x_{0})\) and \(f(x_{0})/g(x_{0})\), which are exactly the
values of the three functions at \(x_{0}\). If \(x_{0}\) is an isolated
point of \(D\) every function is continuous there and there is nothing
to prove.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-continuous-algebra
#+NAME: cor-polynomials-continuous
#+begin_corollary
Every polynomial is continuous on \(\R\), and every rational function
is continuous at every point where its denominator does not vanish.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
The constant functions and the identity \(x \mapsto x\) are continuous
directly from the definition (take \(\delta = \eps\) for the identity,
any \(\delta\) for a constant). Products and sums of continuous
functions are continuous, so by induction every monomial and hence
every polynomial is; the quotient rule gives the rational case.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-continuity-at-point
#+NAME: thm-composition-continuous
#+begin_theorem
Let \(f \colon D \to \R\) be continuous at \(x_{0}\), with
\(f(D) \subseteq E\), and let \(g \colon E \to \R\) be continuous at
\(f(x_{0})\). Then \(g \circ f\) is continuous at \(x_{0}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(\eps > 0\). By continuity of \(g\) at \(y_{0} = f(x_{0})\) there
is \(\eta > 0\) with \(\abs{g(y) - g(y_{0})} < \eps\) for every
\(y \in E\) with \(\abs{y - y_{0}} < \eta\). By continuity of \(f\) at
\(x_{0}\), applied with \(\eta\) in the role of the tolerance, there is
\(\delta > 0\) with \(\abs{f(x) - y_{0}} < \eta\) for every \(x \in D\)
with \(\abs{x - x_{0}} < \delta\). Combining the two, every \(x \in D\)
with \(\abs{x - x_{0}} < \delta\) satisfies
\(\abs{g(f(x)) - g(f(x_{0}))} < \eps\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Composition is stated for /continuity/ and not for limits, and that is
not laziness: the corresponding statement about limits is false as
usually written. With \(f\) constantly \(0\) and \(g\) equal to \(0\)
away from the origin and \(1\) at it, \(\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = 0\) and
\(\lim_{y \to 0} g(y) = 0\), but \(g(f(x))\) is constantly \(1\). What
repairs it is exactly the hypothesis continuity supplies: that the
outer limit is the value at the point.
#+end_remark
