#+TITLE: Fisher information and the Cramer-Rao bound
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:statistics:
#+MSC: 62B10, 62F12
#+HASH: 36f4f46bd0a3

[[id:2cbd79ad-4938-4b5b-a6ca-85d300cc1f79][Rao-Blackwell]] improves an estimator; the question it leaves open is how
far improvement can go. The answer is a bound depending only on the
model: the variance of any unbiased estimator is at least the reciprocal
of a quantity measuring how sharply the density responds to the
parameter. The proof is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to that
response.

#+DEPENDS: def-statistical-model, def-expectation, def-variance-and-covariance
#+NAME: def-regular-model
#+begin_definition
A dominated model with densities \(f(x;\theta)\), \(\theta\) ranging
over an open interval, is /regular/ if \(f(x;\theta)>0\) for all
\(x,\theta\), the map \(\theta \mapsto f(x;\theta)\) is differentiable,
and differentiation under the integral sign is valid in the two
identities used below -- for \(\int f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x)\) and for
\(\int T(x)f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x)\) with \(T\) the estimator considered.
The /score/ is
\(s(x;\theta) = \partial_{\theta}\log f(x;\theta)\) and the /Fisher
information/ is \(I(\theta) = \E_{\theta}[s(X;\theta)^{2}]\), assumed
finite and non-zero.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-regular-model
#+NAME: prop-the-score-has-mean-zero
#+begin_proposition
In a regular model, \(\E_{\theta}[s(X;\theta)] = 0\), so
\(I(\theta) = \Var_{\theta}(s(X;\theta))\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Since \(\partial_{\theta}\log f = (\partial_{\theta}f)/f\),
\[ \E_{\theta}[s(X;\theta)] = \int \frac{\partial_{\theta}f(x;\theta)}{f(x;\theta)}\,f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x) = \partial_{\theta}\int f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x) = \partial_{\theta} 1 = 0, \]
the middle equality being the differentiation under the integral sign
that regularity assumes. The variance of a centred variable is its
second moment.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: prop-the-score-has-mean-zero, prop-correlation-bound, def-estimator
#+NAME: thm-cramer-rao
#+begin_theorem
Let the model be regular and \(T\) an unbiased estimator of
\(g(\theta)\) with finite variance, \(g\) differentiable. Then
\[ \Var_{\theta}(T) \ge \frac{g'(\theta)^{2}}{I(\theta)}. \]
In particular an unbiased estimator of \(\theta\) itself has variance at
least \(1/I(\theta)\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Differentiating \(\E_{\theta}[T] = g(\theta)\) under the integral sign,
\[ g'(\theta) = \partial_{\theta}\int T(x)f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x) = \int T(x)\,s(x;\theta)f(x;\theta)\dd\mu(x) = \E_{\theta}[T\,s(X;\theta)], \]
again writing \(\partial_{\theta}f = s f\). Since the score has mean
zero, this expectation is the covariance:
\(g'(\theta) = \Cov_{\theta}(T, s(X;\theta))\).

The Cauchy-Schwarz bound for covariance gives
\(g'(\theta)^{2} \le \Var_{\theta}(T)\,\Var_{\theta}(s(X;\theta)) = \Var_{\theta}(T) I(\theta)\),
and dividing by \(I(\theta)>0\) gives the claim.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-cramer-rao, def-independence-of-random-variables, cor-variance-of-a-sum-of-independent-variables
#+NAME: prop-information-of-a-sample
#+begin_proposition
For a sample of size \(n\) from a regular model, the Fisher information
is \(nI(\theta)\); hence an unbiased estimator of \(\theta\) from such a
sample has variance at least \(1/(nI(\theta))\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
The joint density is \(\prod_{i}f(x_{i};\theta)\) by independence, so
the score of the sample is \(\sum_{i}s(x_{i};\theta)\), a sum of \(n\)
independent variables each of mean zero and variance \(I(\theta)\). Its
variance is \(nI(\theta)\), and the bound is Cramer-Rao applied to the
sample model.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The regularity conditions are hypotheses here rather than facts, and
they are exactly where the theorem can fail: for a model whose support
moves with the parameter, differentiation under the integral sign is
invalid and the bound is false -- there are unbiased estimators with
variance of order \(n^{-2}\). Stating the conditions as part of the
definition rather than as a footnote is the honest form, since nothing
in this archive proves them for any particular family.
#+end_remark
