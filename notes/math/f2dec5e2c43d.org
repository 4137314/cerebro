#+TITLE: Filters, ultrafilters and the ultrafilter lemma
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:foundations:set_theory:
#+MSC: 03E05, 54A20
#+HASH: f2dec5e2c43d

A sequence is a map out of \(\N\), and the whole theory of convergence
built on it inherits that accident: in a space whose points have no
countable neighbourhood basis, sequences are too few to detect the
closure. A filter carries no index set at all -- it is the family of
sets a limit would eventually be in -- and it is the object that makes
convergence work in every topological space.

#+DEPENDS: def-partial-order
#+NAME: def-filter
#+begin_definition
A /filter/ on a set \(X\) is a family \(\mathcal{F}\) of subsets such
that \(X \in \mathcal{F}\), \(\emptyset \notin \mathcal{F}\),
\(\mathcal{F}\) is closed under finite intersections, and
\(A \in \mathcal{F}\) with \(A \subseteq B\) implies
\(B \in \mathcal{F}\). An /ultrafilter/ is a filter maximal under
inclusion among filters on \(X\). The /principal/ filter at \(x\) is
\(\setst{A \subseteq X}{x \in A}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-filter
#+NAME: prop-ultrafilter-criterion
#+begin_proposition
A filter \(\mathcal{U}\) on \(X\) is an ultrafilter if and only if for
every \(A \subseteq X\) either \(A \in \mathcal{U}\) or
\(X \setminus A \in \mathcal{U}\).
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(\mathcal{U}\) is maximal and \(A \notin \mathcal{U}\). Then
some \(F \in \mathcal{U}\) has \(F \cap A = \emptyset\): otherwise the
family of sets containing some \(F \cap A\) would be a filter strictly
larger than \(\mathcal{U}\), contradicting maximality. But
\(F \cap A = \emptyset\) means \(F \subseteq X\setminus A\), so
\(X \setminus A \in \mathcal{U}\).

Conversely, assume the dichotomy and let
\(\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathcal{G}\) with \(\mathcal{G}\) a filter and
\(A \in \mathcal{G} \setminus \mathcal{U}\). Then
\(X\setminus A \in \mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathcal{G}\), so
\(\emptyset = A \cap (X\setminus A) \in \mathcal{G}\) -- impossible.
Hence \(\mathcal{U} = \mathcal{G}\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-filter, thm-zorn
#+NAME: thm-ultrafilter-lemma
#+begin_theorem
Every filter is contained in an ultrafilter. In particular, every family
of sets with the finite intersection property -- every finite subfamily
has non-empty intersection -- extends to an ultrafilter.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Order the filters containing a given \(\mathcal{F}\) by inclusion. The
union \(\mathcal{U}\) of a chain of filters is a filter: it is closed
upwards, and two of its members lie in a common member of the chain, so
their intersection lies there and is non-empty. Zorn's lemma gives a
maximal element, which is an ultrafilter containing \(\mathcal{F}\).

Given a family with the finite intersection property, the sets
containing some finite intersection of its members form a filter, to
which the first part applies.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-filter, def-topological-space, thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+NAME: def-filter-convergence
#+begin_definition
A filter \(\mathcal{F}\) on a topological space \(X\) /converges/ to
\(x\), written \(\mathcal{F} \to x\), if every neighbourhood of \(x\)
belongs to \(\mathcal{F}\); it /clusters/ at \(x\) if every
neighbourhood of \(x\) meets every member of \(\mathcal{F}\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-filter-convergence, thm-closure-by-neighbourhoods
#+NAME: thm-closure-by-filters
#+begin_theorem
For \(A \subseteq X\), \(x \in \overline{A}\) if and only if some filter
containing \(A\) converges to \(x\). A map \(f \colon X \to Y\) is
continuous at \(x\) if and only if \(\mathcal{F}\to x\) implies
\(f(\mathcal{F}) \to f(x)\), where \(f(\mathcal{F})\) is the filter
generated by the images \(f(F)\), \(F \in \mathcal{F}\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(\mathcal{F} \to x\) and \(A \in \mathcal{F}\), every neighbourhood
of \(x\) lies in \(\mathcal{F}\), so it meets \(A\) -- two members of a
filter cannot be disjoint. Hence \(x \in \overline{A}\). Conversely, if
\(x \in \overline{A}\) then the sets \(N \cap A\), with \(N\) a
neighbourhood of \(x\), are all non-empty and closed under finite
intersection, so they generate a filter containing \(A\) and every
neighbourhood of \(x\).

For continuity: if \(f\) is continuous at \(x\) and \(\mathcal{F}\to x\),
let \(V\) be a neighbourhood of \(f(x)\); some neighbourhood \(U\) of
\(x\) has \(f(U) \subseteq V\), and \(U \in \mathcal{F}\), so
\(V \in f(\mathcal{F})\). Conversely, apply the hypothesis to the filter
of all neighbourhoods of \(x\), which converges to \(x\): its image
contains every neighbourhood of \(f(x)\), which says exactly that some
neighbourhood of \(x\) is mapped into each.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-filter-convergence, prop-ultrafilter-criterion, def-compact, thm-ultrafilter-lemma
#+NAME: thm-compactness-by-ultrafilters
#+begin_theorem
A topological space is compact if and only if every ultrafilter on it
converges.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \(X\) be compact and \(\mathcal{U}\) an ultrafilter that converges
to no point. Then each \(x\) has an open neighbourhood
\(U_{x} \notin \mathcal{U}\), hence with
\(X \setminus U_{x} \in \mathcal{U}\). Finitely many \(U_{x_{i}}\) cover
\(X\), so \(\bigcap_{i}(X\setminus U_{x_{i}}) = \emptyset\) lies in
\(\mathcal{U}\) -- impossible.

Conversely, suppose every ultrafilter converges and let
\(\mathcal{C}\) be a family of closed sets with the finite intersection
property. Extend it to an ultrafilter \(\mathcal{U}\), which converges
to some \(x\). For \(C \in \mathcal{C}\), every neighbourhood of \(x\)
lies in \(\mathcal{U}\) and so meets \(C\); as \(C\) is closed,
\(x \in C\). Thus \(\bigcap\mathcal{C} \ne \emptyset\), and taking
complements this is exactly the finite subcover property.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The last theorem replaces "extract a convergent subsequence" by
"extend to an ultrafilter and it converges", with no countability
anywhere -- and it makes Tychonoff's theorem a two-line argument, since
an ultrafilter on a product pushes forward to an ultrafilter in each
coordinate.
#+end_remark
