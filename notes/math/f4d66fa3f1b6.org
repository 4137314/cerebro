#+TITLE: Composition series, Jordan-Holder and solvable groups
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:algebra:group_theory:
#+MSC: 20E15, 20F16
#+HASH: f4d66fa3f1b6

Taking a normal subgroup and its quotient splits a group into two
smaller ones. Iterating gives a list of pieces that admit no further
splitting -- and the theorem is that the list does not depend on how the
splitting was carried out. Finite groups therefore have well-defined
prime factors, with the caveat that, unlike for integers, the factors do
not determine the group.

#+DEPENDS: def-normal-subgroup, thm-quotient-group
#+NAME: def-simple-group
#+begin_definition
A group \(G \ne \set{e}\) is /simple/ if its only normal subgroups are
\(\set{e}\) and \(G\). A /composition series/ of \(G\) is a chain
\(\set{e} = G_{0} \normalin G_{1} \normalin \cdots \normalin G_{n} = G\)
in which each \(G_{i} \normalin G_{i+1}\) and each /factor/
\(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\) is simple.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-simple-group, thm-lagrange
#+NAME: prop-finite-groups-have-composition-series
#+begin_proposition
Every finite group other than the trivial one has a composition series.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Induct on \(\abs{G}\). If \(G\) is simple, \(\set{e}\normalin G\) is a
composition series. Otherwise take \(N\) a normal subgroup maximal among
the proper ones, which exists since there are finitely many. Then
\(G/N\) is simple: a normal subgroup of it is \(H/N\) for a normal
\(H\) with \(N \subseteq H \subseteq G\) -- take the preimage under the
projection -- and maximality forces \(H = N\) or \(H = G\). By induction
\(N\) has a composition series, and appending \(G\) gives one for \(G\).
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-simple-group, thm-second-isomorphism-groups, thm-third-isomorphism-groups
#+NAME: lem-zassenhaus
#+begin_lemma
Let \(A' \normalin A\) and \(B' \normalin B\) be subgroups of a group.
Then \(A'(A\cap B') \normalin A'(A\cap B)\), \(B'(B\cap A')\normalin B'(B\cap A)\),
and
\[ \frac{A'(A\cap B)}{A'(A\cap B')} \iso \frac{B'(B\cap A)}{B'(B\cap A')}. \]
#+end_lemma

#+begin_proof
Put \(D = (A\cap B')(A'\cap B)\), a subgroup of \(A \cap B\) since both
factors are normalised by \(A\cap B\): for \(x \in A\cap B\), conjugation
preserves \(A\) and \(B\) and, by normality of \(A'\) and \(B'\),
preserves \(A'\) and \(B'\). It is normal in \(A\cap B\) for the same
reason.

Consider \(\psi \colon A'(A\cap B) \to (A\cap B)/D\) sending
\(a'x\) with \(a'\in A'\), \(x \in A\cap B\) to \(xD\). It is well
defined: if \(a'_{1}x_{1} = a'_{2}x_{2}\) then
\(x_{2}x_{1}^{-1} \in A' \cap (A\cap B) = A'\cap B \subseteq D\). It is
a surjective homomorphism, using normality of \(A'\) in \(A\) to move
\(A'\)-factors to the left, and its kernel is the set of \(a'x\) with
\(x \in D\), that is \(A'D = A'(A\cap B')\) -- so that subgroup is
normal in \(A'(A\cap B)\) and the quotient is \((A\cap B)/D\). The
expression for \(D\) is symmetric in the two pairs, so the same argument
on the other side gives the stated isomorphism.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: lem-zassenhaus, def-simple-group, prop-finite-groups-have-composition-series
#+NAME: thm-jordan-holder
#+begin_theorem
Any two composition series of a group have the same length, and their
factors agree up to reordering and isomorphism.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Let \((G_{i})_{i\le n}\) and \((H_{j})_{j \le m}\) be composition
series. Refine each by inserting, between \(G_{i}\) and \(G_{i+1}\), the
subgroups \(G_{i}(G_{i+1}\cap H_{j})\) for \(j = 0,\dots,m\), and
symmetrically for the other series. By Zassenhaus' lemma each of these
chains is a chain of normal inclusions, and the two refinements have,
term by term, isomorphic successive quotients.

Now each factor \(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\) is simple, so among the inserted
quotients between \(G_{i}\) and \(G_{i+1}\) exactly one is non-trivial
and isomorphic to \(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\), the others being trivial: a chain
of normal subgroups between \(G_{i}\) and \(G_{i+1}\) with all quotients
proper would contradict simplicity. Deleting the trivial quotients from
both refinements therefore recovers the two original lists of factors,
which are in bijection by the term-by-term isomorphism.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: def-simple-group, thm-jordan-holder, cor-groups-of-prime-order
#+NAME: def-solvable-group
#+begin_definition
A group is /solvable/ if it has a chain
\(\set{e} = G_{0}\normalin\cdots\normalin G_{n} = G\) with every
quotient \(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\) abelian.
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-solvable-group, thm-second-isomorphism-groups, thm-third-isomorphism-groups
#+NAME: prop-solvability-is-inherited
#+begin_proposition
Subgroups and quotients of a solvable group are solvable; and if
\(N \normalin G\) with \(N\) and \(G/N\) solvable, then \(G\) is
solvable.
#+end_proposition

#+begin_proof
Let \((G_{i})\) witness solvability of \(G\) and \(H \le G\). Then
\(H_{i} = H \cap G_{i}\) is a chain with
\(H_{i}\normalin H_{i+1}\), and
\(H_{i+1}/H_{i} = H_{i+1}/(H_{i+1}\cap G_{i}) \iso H_{i+1}G_{i}/G_{i}\)
by the second isomorphism theorem, a subgroup of the abelian
\(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\), hence abelian.

For a quotient \(G/N\), the images \(G_{i}N/N\) form a chain of normal
inclusions whose successive quotients are quotients of
\(G_{i+1}/G_{i}\), by the third isomorphism theorem, hence abelian.

For the converse, concatenate a chain for \(N\) with the preimages in
\(G\) of a chain for \(G/N\); the quotients of the second part are
unchanged by the third isomorphism theorem.
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Jordan-Holder makes "solvable" equivalent to "every composition factor
is cyclic of prime order", which is why the word survives from the
theory of equations: a polynomial is solvable by radicals exactly when
its Galois group is solvable, and the tower of radical extensions is the
chain above read in fields. The insolubility of the general quintic is
then the statement that \(A_{5}\) is simple and not abelian.
#+end_remark
