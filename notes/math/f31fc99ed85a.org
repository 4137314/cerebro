One hypothesis -- that a difference quotient converges along every
direction of the plane rather than along a line -- and the subject
becomes rigid in a way real analysis never is. A function differentiable
once is differentiable infinitely often; its values on a small arc
determine it everywhere; it cannot have an interior maximum of modulus;
and a single number per singularity computes an integral. Each of those
is a corollary of the same integral formula.

Part of [[id:e023b747-878b-4a37-a097-35552d95cb66][Analysis]].

* The chain

1. [[id:dab1d339-e7a9-41e2-8593-910a11938ad2][Holomorphic functions and the Cauchy-Riemann equations]] -- what
   complex differentiability asks for, as two equations on the real and
   imaginary parts.
2. [[id:4ec94690-f4ea-47e3-9fcb-1ec55f3e358e][Complex integration and Cauchy's theorem]] -- the integral over a
   closed curve vanishes. Goursat's proof assumes only differentiability,
   which is what lets the next step prove more than it assumes.
3. [[id:b9814e2f-633e-49eb-afaa-cc4092244461][The Cauchy integral formula and analyticity]] -- values inside a disc
   from values on the circle, hence power series, hence Liouville's
   theorem and the fundamental theorem of algebra.
4. [[id:2113a291-a90e-4ed9-87ee-40abdd73759e][Zeros, the identity theorem and the maximum principle]] -- the two
   rigidity statements.
5. [[id:25af7de9-f4e9-4e76-ba34-5571aa837871][Laurent series and isolated singularities]] -- the expansion adapted
   to an annulus, and the trichotomy removable / pole / essential.
6. [[id:7854238d-a399-44d6-8636-512105380539][The residue theorem and the argument principle]] -- what the integral
   is when there /is/ something inside, and how to count zeros.

* Why the subject is a tool

The residue theorem turns contour integration into arithmetic, which is
why complex analysis is the working language of
[[id:b8b253f2-97fc-4461-8ee4-281c7a8202d2][mathematical physics]]: real integrals with no elementary primitive are
evaluated by closing a contour, and the poles of a transfer function
decide whether a system is stable, by [[id:7854238d-a399-44d6-8636-512105380539][Rouche's theorem]]. In the other
direction, harmonic functions in the plane are exactly the real parts of
holomorphic ones, so the [[id:fe42fdf4-dd3b-4112-a197-9e3fadbacad6][Dirichlet problem]] in two dimensions is complex
analysis.

* What is missing

Conformal mapping and the Riemann mapping theorem, which is the
geometric half of the subject and the reason it solves plane boundary
value problems; analytic continuation and Riemann surfaces; the Poisson
kernel; infinite products and the elementary special functions -- the
gamma and zeta functions in particular, with which this would begin to
touch number theory; and Picard's theorems, of which only the
Casorati-Weierstrass shadow is here.
