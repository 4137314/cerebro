#+TITLE: Sufficiency and the factorisation theorem
#+DATE: [2026-08-10 Mon]
#+FILETAGS: :growing:probability:statistics:
#+MSC: 62B05
#+HASH: 4013ab6a4c6c

A sample of size \(n\) is a great deal of data and almost all of it is
usually irrelevant to the parameter. A sufficient statistic is one that
retains everything relevant, in the precise sense that the sample
conditioned on it no longer mentions the parameter at all -- and the
factorisation theorem turns that condition, which quantifies over
conditional laws, into an identity one can check by inspection.

#+DEPENDS: def-statistical-model, def-conditional-probability, def-estimator
#+NAME: def-sufficient-statistic
#+begin_definition
Let \((\Prob_{\theta})\) be a statistical model on a countable sample
space \(\mathcal{X}\). A statistic \(T\) is /sufficient/ for \(\theta\)
if for every \(t\) with \(\Prob_{\theta}(T = t)>0\) the conditional
probabilities \(\Prob_{\theta}(X = x \mid T = t)\) do not depend on
\(\theta\).
#+end_definition

#+DEPENDS: def-sufficient-statistic, thm-total-probability-and-bayes
#+NAME: thm-factorisation
#+begin_theorem
On a countable sample space, write \(p_{\theta}(x) = \Prob_{\theta}(X=x)\).
A statistic \(T\) is sufficient if and only if there are functions
\(g_{\theta}\) and \(h\), with \(h\) not depending on \(\theta\), such
that
\[ p_{\theta}(x) = g_{\theta}(T(x))\, h(x) \qquad \text{for all } x, \theta. \]
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(T\) is sufficient and put \(t = T(x)\). Since
\(\set{X = x}\subseteq\set{T=t}\),
\(p_{\theta}(x) = \Prob_{\theta}(T=t)\,\Prob_{\theta}(X=x\mid T=t)\)
whenever \(\Prob_{\theta}(T=t)>0\). The second factor does not depend on
\(\theta\) by sufficiency; call it \(h(x)\), and put
\(g_{\theta}(t) = \Prob_{\theta}(T=t)\). Where
\(\Prob_{\theta}(T=t) = 0\) both sides vanish for every such \(x\).

Conversely, assume the factorisation. For \(t\) with
\(\Prob_{\theta}(T=t)>0\),
\[ \Prob_{\theta}(T=t) = \sum_{y \colon T(y)=t} g_{\theta}(t)h(y) = g_{\theta}(t)H(t), \qquad H(t) = \sum_{y \colon T(y)=t}h(y), \]
so for \(x\) with \(T(x)=t\),
\(\Prob_{\theta}(X=x\mid T=t) = g_{\theta}(t)h(x)/(g_{\theta}(t)H(t)) = h(x)/H(t)\),
which does not involve \(\theta\); and for \(T(x)\ne t\) the
conditional probability is \(0\). Hence \(T\) is sufficient.
#+end_proof

#+DEPENDS: thm-factorisation, def-sufficient-statistic
#+NAME: cor-functions-of-a-sufficient-statistic
#+begin_corollary
If \(T\) is sufficient and \(U = \psi(T)\) with \(\psi\) injective, then
\(U\) is sufficient; the whole sample is always sufficient; and a
sufficient statistic remains sufficient after adjoining any further
statistic to it.
#+end_corollary

#+begin_proof
Each claim is a factorisation. For an injective \(\psi\),
\(g_{\theta}(T(x)) = g_{\theta}(\psi^{-1}(U(x)))\), a function of
\(U(x)\). The identity statistic factors with \(g_{\theta} = p_{\theta}\)
and \(h = 1\). Adjoining a further statistic \(V\) gives
\(p_{\theta}(x) = g_{\theta}(T(x))h(x)\) with the first factor read as a
function of the pair \((T(x),V(x))\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
The theorem is stated for a countable sample space because that is where
it can be proved with the tools here: the general version replaces
probabilities by densities against a dominating measure, and its proof
needs the Radon-Nikodym theorem. The statement is the same, and so is
the use -- read off the dependence of the density on \(\theta\), and
whatever it comes packaged with is sufficient.

Sufficiency is what makes [[id:2cbd79ad-4938-4b5b-a6ca-85d300cc1f79][Rao-Blackwell]] work: conditioning an
estimator on a sufficient statistic yields something that is still an
estimator, because the conditional law is free of the parameter.
#+end_remark
