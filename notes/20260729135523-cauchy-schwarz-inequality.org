#+TITLE: Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
#+DATE: [2026-07-29 Wed 13:55]
#+FILETAGS: :evergreen:medium:linear_algebra:
#+MSC: 15A63

The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality is the one estimate that makes the geometry
of an [[id:c35a8f01-cbd2-433b-b9b5-a037bae4e91d][inner product space]] work: it is what lets one define the angle
between two vectors and prove the triangle inequality.

#+DEPENDS: def-inner-product-space
#+NAME: thm-cauchy-schwarz
#+begin_theorem
Let \(V\) be a real [[id:c35a8f01-cbd2-433b-b9b5-a037bae4e91d][inner product space]]. For all \(u, v \in V\),
\[
  \abs{\inner{u}{v}} \le \norm{u}\, \norm{v},
\]
with equality if and only if \(u\) and \(v\) are linearly dependent.
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
If \(v = 0\), both sides vanish. Otherwise, for every \(t \in \R\),
positive definiteness gives
\[
  0 \le \norm{u - t v}^{2} = \norm{u}^{2} - 2 t \inner{u}{v} + t^{2} \norm{v}^{2} .
\]
The right-hand side is a quadratic in \(t\) that is never negative, so its
discriminant is nonpositive:
\[
  4 \inner{u}{v}^{2} - 4 \norm{u}^{2} \norm{v}^{2} \le 0 ,
\]
which rearranges to \(\abs{\inner{u}{v}} \le \norm{u}\, \norm{v}\).
Equality makes the discriminant vanish, so \(\norm{u - t v} = 0\) for some
\(t\), i.e. \(u = t v\).
#+end_proof
