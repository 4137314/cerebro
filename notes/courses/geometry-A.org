#+begin_comment
First geometry course, which is linear algebra with a geometric ending:
the structure theory first, then the extra structure -- a bilinear form
-- that turns a vector space into a space with lengths and angles, and
finally affine and Euclidean geometry and the classification of conics.

The two diagonalisation theorems are taught together and contrasted on
purpose: Sylvester allows any change of basis and preserves only signs,
the spectral theorem allows only orthonormal changes and preserves the
eigenvalues. Which one applies is the question of which group the
geometry is over, and that is the idea the whole course exists to
install.
#+end_comment

* Description

A first course in linear algebra and the geometry built on it: vector
spaces, bases and dimension, linear maps and rank-nullity, determinants
as the alternating multilinear form, eigenvalues and diagonalisability;
then inner products, orthogonality and Gram-Schmidt, bilinear and
quadratic forms with Sylvester's law, and the spectral theorem. The
course closes with affine spaces, the isometries of Euclidean space, and
the classification of conics and quadrics by signature.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- prove that dimension is well defined, extend an independent set to a
  basis, and use the Grassmann formula;
- apply the rank-nullity theorem as the first isomorphism theorem with a
  dimension count;
- characterise the determinant by multilinearity and alternation, and
  derive its identities from uniqueness rather than by computation;
- compute a characteristic polynomial, decide diagonalisability, and
  prove the Cayley-Hamilton theorem;
- run Gram-Schmidt, decompose a space along a subspace and its
  orthogonal complement, and identify the orthogonal projection as the
  best approximation;
- classify a real quadratic form by its signature and diagonalise a
  symmetric operator by an orthonormal basis;
- prove that every isometry of \(\R^{n}\) is an orthogonal map followed
  by a translation, and classify conics and quadrics.

* Schedule

1. Vector spaces; span, independence, bases, dimension; the Grassmann
   formula.
2. Linear maps, kernels and images; quotient spaces; rank-nullity.
3. Duality: dual bases, annihilators, the transpose.
4. Determinants: existence, uniqueness, multiplicativity, Laplace
   expansion.
5. Eigenvalues and the characteristic polynomial; diagonalisability;
   Cayley-Hamilton.
6. Inner product spaces; Cauchy-Schwarz; orthogonality; Gram-Schmidt;
   orthogonal decomposition and projection.
7. Bilinear and quadratic forms; Sylvester's law of inertia; the
   spectral theorem for symmetric operators.
8. Affine spaces and affine maps; isometries of Euclidean space and the
   orthogonal group; conics and quadrics.
