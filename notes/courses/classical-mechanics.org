* Description

The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations of mechanics, developed as
mathematics. The action functional and its first variation; the
Euler-Lagrange equations as the pointwise form of stationarity; symmetry
and conservation, from a cyclic coordinate to Noether's theorem; the
Legendre transform and the convexity that makes it invertible;
Hamilton's equations on phase space; the Poisson bracket, which turns
conservation into an algebraic identity and makes the observables a Lie
algebra; canonical transformations and Liouville's theorem.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- write the action of a Lagrangian system and compute its first
  variation;
- derive the Euler-Lagrange equations, and say exactly where the
  fixed-endpoint hypothesis is used;
- state and prove Noether's theorem, and recover conservation of a
  conjugate momentum as its simplest case;
- construct the Legendre transform of a strictly convex function and
  explain why convexity is the hypothesis that makes velocity and
  momentum equivalent data;
- pass between the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations and prove the
  two systems of equations equivalent;
- compute Poisson brackets, prove the Jacobi identity, and derive
  Poisson's theorem on the bracket of two conserved quantities;
- prove Liouville's theorem and say what it forbids.

* Schedule

1. The action functional; variations; the fundamental lemma of the
   calculus of variations.
2. The Euler-Lagrange equations; conjugate momenta; cyclic coordinates.
3. Symmetry and conservation: Noether's theorem.
4. The Legendre transform and convexity.
5. Hamilton's equations; conservation of the Hamiltonian.
6. Poisson brackets; the Lie algebra of observables.
7. Canonical transformations; Liouville's theorem.
