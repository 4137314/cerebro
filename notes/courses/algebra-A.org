* Description

A first course in abstract algebra, from the set-theoretic constructions
it rests on to the structure theory of finite groups. Groups are treated
as the theory of one invertible operation and, equivalently, as the
theory of symmetry: the second half of the course is group actions, and
almost every theorem in it is a counting argument obtained by letting a
group act on a set built for the purpose.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- work with equivalence relations, quotients and the canonical
  factorisation of a map, and recognise them inside later constructions;
- prove statements about groups directly from the axioms, and use the
  subgroup criterion fluently;
- prove Lagrange's theorem and use it to constrain the possible orders of
  elements and subgroups;
- decide when a subgroup can be divided out, and prove the three
  isomorphism theorems;
- construct group actions and use the orbit-stabiliser theorem and the
  class equation as counting tools;
- prove Cauchy's theorem and the Sylow theorems, and use the numerical
  constraints to classify groups of small order;
- decompose a finite group into simple factors and state what
  Jordan-Holder does and does not determine.

* Schedule

1. Sets, relations, equivalence and quotients; maps and the canonical
   factorisation; order and Zorn's lemma.
2. Groups, subgroups, generation; the order of an element; cyclic
   groups.
3. Cosets, the index and Lagrange's theorem.
4. Normal subgroups, quotient groups, the isomorphism theorems.
5. Group actions: orbits, stabilisers, the class equation, Cayley's
   theorem.
6. The symmetric group and the sign of a permutation.
7. Cauchy's theorem and the Sylow theorems.
8. Products, composition series, solvability; free groups and
   presentations.
