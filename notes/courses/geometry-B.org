#+begin_comment
Second geometry course, continuing geometry-A. It is organised by the
group of transformations allowed: projective first, where least is
preserved and the exceptions vanish; then convexity, which is not a
group at all but a condition on a set; then the differential geometry of
curves and surfaces, where the allowed maps are the diffeomorphisms and
the invariants are found by differentiating.

A short topological chapter comes first because the later notes need
compactness and connectedness by name. It is deliberately minimal -- the
full treatment is a topology course, which this archive can support and
which is not this one.

* Description

A second course in geometry, organised by the transformations allowed.
Projective geometry: the space of lines through the origin, its affine
charts, the projective linear group, and the cross-ratio as the unique
invariant of four collinear points. Convex geometry: separation by
hyperplanes, Caratheodory's theorem, extreme points, and Minkowski's
theorem on convex bodies. Differential geometry: curves with their
curvature and torsion, surfaces with their two fundamental forms and
Gauss curvature, and smooth manifolds with their tangent spaces.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- work with homogeneous coordinates and affine charts, and prove the
  projective dimension formula;
- prove that a projective transformation is determined by its action on
  three points of a line, and compute the cross-ratio;
- prove the existence of a nearest point in a closed convex set and
  derive the separating and supporting hyperplane theorems;
- prove Caratheodory's theorem and Minkowski's theorem on extreme
  points, and apply the convex body theorem to a lattice;
- reparametrise a regular curve by arc length and derive the
  Frenet-Serret equations;
- compute the two fundamental forms of a surface, prove that the shape
  operator is self-adjoint, and obtain the principal and Gauss
  curvatures from the spectral theorem;
- define a smooth manifold and its tangent space without coordinates,
  and prove that the tangent space has the dimension of the manifold.

* Schedule

1. Topological preliminaries: spaces and continuity, connectedness,
   compactness.
2. Projective space: charts, subspaces, the dimension formula.
3. Projective transformations and the cross-ratio.
4. Convex sets: nearest points, separating and supporting hyperplanes.
5. Convex hulls, Caratheodory's theorem, extreme points, Minkowski's
   theorems.
6. Curves: arc length, curvature, torsion, Frenet-Serret.
7. Surfaces: the first and second fundamental forms, the shape operator,
   principal and Gauss curvature.
8. Smooth manifolds, smooth maps, tangent spaces and differentials.
