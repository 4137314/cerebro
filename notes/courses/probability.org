* Description

A rigorous first course in probability on a measure-theoretic
foundation: probability spaces, random variables and their laws,
expectation as an integral, independence, and the three limit theorems
that make the subject a description of anything -- the weak and strong
laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem. Conditioning is
treated last and geometrically, as an orthogonal projection in
\(L^{2}\), which is the form in which it is used in statistics.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- construct a probability space and manipulate events, conditional
  probabilities and independence with no appeal to intuition;
- compute an expectation as an integral against the law of a random
  variable, and justify every exchange of limit and expectation;
- state and prove the Markov and Chebyshev inequalities and use them as
  the standard concentration tools;
- distinguish the four modes of convergence and prove the implications
  between them;
- prove the weak law from Chebyshev and the strong law from the
  Borel-Cantelli lemma;
- prove the central limit theorem by characteristic functions, and say
  precisely which of its ingredients this treatment assumes;
- define conditional expectation as a projection and use the variance
  decomposition.

* Schedule

1. Measure and integration: sigma-algebras, measures, measurable
   functions, the Lebesgue integral, the convergence theorems.
2. Probability spaces: events, conditioning, Bayes, independence.
3. Random variables: laws, distribution functions, independence.
4. Expectation: the transfer theorem, Jensen's inequality.
5. Variance and covariance; the Markov and Chebyshev inequalities.
6. Modes of convergence; the Borel-Cantelli lemmas.
7. The laws of large numbers.
8. Characteristic functions and the central limit theorem.
9. Conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection.
