* Description

A second course in algebra: rings and their ideals, the quotient
construction and the homomorphism theorem, integral domains and fields,
factorisation in Euclidean and principal ideal domains, modules and
exact sequences as the replacement for dimension over a general ring,
the chain conditions, and field extensions up to the classification of
finite fields.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- decide which substructure of a ring can be divided out, and prove the
  homomorphism theorem for rings;
- read a property of an ideal off the quotient ring -- prime against
  domain, maximal against field -- and use Zorn's lemma to produce a
  maximal ideal;
- prove that a Euclidean domain is principal and that a principal ideal
  domain has unique factorisation, and say why neither implication
  reverses;
- work with modules, submodules and quotients, and use short exact
  sequences and the splitting lemma in place of dimension;
- state the ascending chain condition in its three equivalent forms and
  prove the Hilbert basis theorem;
- compute degrees of field extensions with the tower law and derive the
  classical impossibility results;
- construct a splitting field, prove it is unique, and classify the
  finite fields.

* Schedule

1. Rings, ideals, quotient rings; ring homomorphisms and the
   homomorphism theorem.
2. Integral domains and fields; prime and maximal ideals; Krull's
   theorem.
3. Polynomial rings: division, roots, the degree.
4. Euclidean domains, principal ideal domains, unique factorisation.
5. The Chinese remainder theorem.
6. Modules, quotients, free modules; exact sequences and splitting.
7. Noetherian rings and the Hilbert basis theorem.
8. Field extensions, the tower law, minimal polynomials.
9. Splitting fields and their uniqueness; finite fields.
