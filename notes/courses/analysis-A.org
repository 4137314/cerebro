* Description

A first rigorous course in real analysis for first-year mathematics
readers: the construction and completeness of \(\R\), convergence of
sequences and series, limits and continuity of real functions,
differential calculus with Taylor expansions, and the Riemann integral
with the fundamental theorem of calculus. The emphasis throughout is on
proof from the definitions: an \(\eps\)--\(\delta\) argument written
correctly is the skill the rest of the degree rests on.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- state the completeness axiom and use suprema and infima fluently, and
  derive the Archimedean property and the density of \(\Q\) from it;
- prove convergence and divergence of sequences and series directly from
  the definitions, and apply the standard convergence tests;
- use the Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem and the Cauchy criterion as
  existence tools, rather than as facts to be quoted;
- work with limits, continuity and uniform continuity rigorously, and
  prove the theorems on continuous functions on a compact interval;
- differentiate rigorously, prove and apply the mean value theorem, and
  write Taylor expansions with an explicit remainder;
- construct the Riemann integral and prove the fundamental theorem of
  calculus.

* Schedule

1. The real numbers: order, the absolute value and the triangle
   inequality, completeness, suprema and infima, the Archimedean
   property.
2. Sequences: convergence, the algebra of limits, monotone sequences,
   Bolzano--Weierstrass, Cauchy sequences, limit superior and inferior.
3. Series: convergence, the Cauchy criterion, absolute convergence, the
   standard tests, condensation, alternating series.
4. Limits of functions and continuity: the algebra of continuous
   functions, composition, one-sided limits, uniform continuity.
5. Continuous functions on an interval: intermediate value, Weierstrass,
   the continuous inverse.
6. Differentiation: the rules, the mean value theorem and its
   consequences, Cauchy's version and de l'Hopital's rule.
7. Taylor expansions with the Lagrange and Peano remainders; convexity.
8. The Riemann integral: its algebraic properties, classes of integrable
   functions, the fundamental theorem of calculus, parts and
   substitution.
