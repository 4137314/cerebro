* Description

What analysis looks like once the real line is no longer the ambient
space. Metric spaces and completeness; sequences and series of functions,
where uniform convergence is the hypothesis that carries continuity and
integration across a limit; power series; differential calculus in
several variables, where the derivative is a linear map and the partial
derivatives are a consequence; the contraction principle and the
existence and uniqueness theory it buys for ordinary differential
equations.

* Objectives

Having read it, one can:
- work in an arbitrary metric space, and say which arguments of the
  first course used the order of \(\R\) and which used only the distance;
- distinguish pointwise from uniform convergence, and prove which
  properties survive each;
- determine the radius of convergence of a power series and justify
  term-by-term operations inside it;
- define differentiability in several variables as approximation by a
  linear map, and give the exact relation to partial derivatives;
- prove the contraction mapping principle and derive from it the
  existence and uniqueness of solutions of an initial value problem;
- classify stationary points by the second differential.

* Schedule

1. Metric spaces: convergence, open and closed sets, completeness.
2. Sequences and series of functions: pointwise and uniform convergence,
   the uniform Cauchy criterion, the Weierstrass test.
3. Power series: radius of convergence, uniform convergence on compacts.
4. Differential calculus in several variables: the differential, partial
   and directional derivatives, continuity of the partials.
5. Stationary points and the second differential.
6. The contraction mapping principle.
7. Ordinary differential equations: Picard-Lindelof.
