* Limits of sequences

#+NAME: def-convergence
#+begin_definition
A sequence \((a_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}\) of real numbers /converges/ to
\(L \in \mathbb{R}\) if for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there exists
\(N \in \mathbb{N}\) such that \(|a_n - L| < \varepsilon\) for all \(n \geq N\).
This is the standard definition, see [cite:@rudin1976principles].
#+end_definition

#+NAME: ax-dedekind-completeness
#+begin_axiom
The real numbers \(\R\) are Dedekind-complete: every non-empty subset of
\(\R\) bounded above has a least upper bound in \(\R\).
#+end_axiom

# Uniqueness of limits rests on the definition of convergence -- and on
# nothing else (in particular not on completeness): #+DEPENDS records
# exactly that. `make results CMD=deps NAME=thm-uniqueness-of-limits'.
#+DEPENDS: def-convergence
#+NAME: thm-uniqueness-of-limits
#+begin_theorem
If \((a_n)\) converges to both \(L_1\) and \(L_2\), then \(L_1 = L_2\).
#+end_theorem

#+begin_proof
Suppose \(L_1 \neq L_2\). Let \(\eps = \abs{L_1 - L_2} / 2 > 0\). By
convergence to \(L_1\) there is \(N_1\) with \(\abs{a_n - L_1} < \eps\) for
all \(n \geq N_1\); similarly there is \(N_2\) for \(L_2\). Then for
\(n \geq \max(N_1, N_2)\),
\[\abs{L_1 - L_2} \leq \abs{L_1 - a_n} + \abs{a_n - L_2} < 2\eps = \abs{L_1 - L_2},\]
a contradiction. Hence \(L_1 = L_2\).
#+end_proof

#+begin_remark
Uniqueness of limits is why writing "\(\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = L\)" for
/the/ limit, rather than /a/ limit, is legitimate at all.
#+end_remark

#+NAME: conj-rational-limits
#+begin_conjecture
(Not a real open problem -- just here to exercise the =conjecture=
environment.) Every convergent sequence of rationals converges to a
rational.
#+end_conjecture

#+begin_exercise
Show that a sequence \((a_n)\) with \(a_n \to L\) and \(a_n \to L'\) must have
\(L = L'\) without appealing to the theorem above -- redo the argument
from the \(\eps\)--\(\delta\) definition.
#+end_exercise

#+begin_hint
Suppose \(L \neq L'\) and feed \(\eps = \abs{L - L'}/2\) to both limits.
#+end_hint

#+begin_solution
If \(L \neq L'\), set \(\eps = \abs{L - L'}/2 > 0\). For large \(n\) we get
both \(\abs{a_n - L} < \eps\) and \(\abs{a_n - L'} < \eps\), so
\(\abs{L - L'} \leq \abs{L - a_n} + \abs{a_n - L'} < 2\eps = \abs{L - L'}\),
a contradiction. Hence \(L = L'\).
#+end_solution
