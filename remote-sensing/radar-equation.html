Uses

Radars measure two quantities: distance (altimeters, ranging) and backscattered power. Imaging radars for Earth observation are interested mainly in the backscattering coefficient, which carries the physical information about the surface; they work in microwaves (1 mm–1 m), day and night, through clouds, fog and rain.

Intuition

Shouting at a wall and listening: the delay tells how far it is, the loudness of the echo what it is made of.

Pitfall

The law makes the echo extremely weak from orbit: spaceborne radars need high peak power, large antennas and the processing gain of pulse compression and synthetic aperture.
