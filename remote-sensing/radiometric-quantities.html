Radiometric quantities

Radiant energy [J], flux [W], exitance and irradiance (flux leaving or reaching a surface per unit area, W m), intensity (flux per solid angle, W sr) and radiance (flux per unit projected area and solid angle, W m sr): radiance is what a sensor measures, and it does not change with distance in a lossless medium.

Definition

quantity definition unit meaning
energy Q J energy of the wave
flux Φ = dQ/dt W power emitted or received
exitance M = dΦ/dA W m⁻² flux leaving a surface
irradiance E = dΦ/dA W m⁻² flux reaching a surface
intensity I = dΦ/dΩ W sr⁻¹ flux per solid angle
radiance L = d²Φ/(dA cosθ dΩ) W m⁻² sr⁻¹ per projected area and angle

Spectral versions are per unit wavelength (W m sr µm). Reflectance is a dimensionless ratio, the physical property of the surface that the spectral signature describes.

Derivation

A detector with area behind optics of solid angle collects . Moving the sensor away from a uniform surface, the ground area seen grows as while the solid angle of each ground element shrinks as : the collected flux, hence the measured radiance, does not depend on distance.

Intuition

Irradiance is how much light falls on a surface; radiance is how bright that surface looks from a given direction — the quantity a camera pixel records.

Pitfall

Radiance depends on the viewing and illumination geometry; reflectance does not (for a Lambertian surface). Comparing images taken at different sun angles requires converting radiance to reflectance.
