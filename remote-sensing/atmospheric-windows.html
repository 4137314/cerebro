Atmospheric transmittance and windows

The atmosphere transmits a fraction of the radiation crossing it; water vapour and CO absorb strongly in some bands (e.g. 1.4 and 1.9 µm, 2.7 µm, 6 µm), so sensors are designed in the windows where is high: visible–NIR, SWIR, 3–5 µm, 8–14 µm and microwaves.

Definition

for radiation crossing the atmosphere, on the way down (Sun–ground) and on the way up (ground–sensor). Low-transmittance regions are absorption bands of HO, CO, O, O. also varies with weather: clouds bring it to almost zero in the visible and infrared, while microwaves (except near the 1.36 cm water-vapour line) cross clouds, fog and rain.

Picture

Schematic transmittance in the reflective region: deep water-vapour bands at 1.4, 1.9 and 2.7 µm.

Consequences for design

  1. place bands inside windows, avoiding absorption features;
  2. pick acquisition conditions (season, time, cloud cover);
  3. still separate the atmospheric contribution from the surface one: atmospheric correction.

Intuition

The atmosphere is a coloured filter between the ground and the sensor; we can only look through the clear parts of the glass.

Pitfall

Absorption bands are not useless: sensors for the atmosphere itself (water vapour, ozone, CO) work exactly in them.
