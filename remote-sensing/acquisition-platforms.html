Acquisition platforms

Sensors fly on platforms at very different altitudes — UAVs below 1 km, aircraft at 5–15 km, stratospheric platforms at 15–36 km, polar satellites at 500–800 km, the ISS at 400 km, geostationary satellites at 36 000 km — trading resolution and flexibility against coverage, stability and cost.

Definition

platform altitude strengths weaknesses
UAV, helicopter < 1–5 km cm pixels, on demand small areas
aircraft 5–15 km high resolution, rapid turbulence, cost
stratospheric 15–36 km persistent regional view still maturing
polar satellite 500–800 km global, regular, stable fixed revisit
ISS ≈ 400 km crewed servicing non-polar, vibrations
geostationary 35 786 km continuous view coarse pixels

Worked example

Same camera (detector 10 µm, focal length 0.1 m, IFOV 0.1 mrad) on different platforms: drone at 100 m → 1 cm pixels; aircraft at 5 km → 0.5 m; satellite at 700 km → 70 m. A satellite needs a focal length 70 times longer (7 m, folded optics) to reach the aircraft's 0.5 m.

Intuition

Lower means sharper but smaller and more expensive per square kilometre; higher means coarser but wider, more regular and stable.

Pitfall

Aircraft images need strong geometric correction: roll, pitch and yaw change from line to line, unlike the smooth attitude of a satellite.
