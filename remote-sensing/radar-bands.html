Radar frequency bands

Imaging radars use letter-named microwave bands: Ka (26.5–40 GHz), K, Ku (12.5–18), X (8–12.5, COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X), C (4–8, Sentinel-1), S (2–4, NISAR), L (1–2, SMAP, NISAR), P (0.3–1 GHz, Biomass); longer wavelengths penetrate further into vegetation, soil and ice.

Choosing a band

Penetration grows with : X sees the top of the canopy and is good for urban detail and fine resolution; C is a robust all-round choice (agriculture, sea ice, oil spills, interferometry); L reaches branches and ground, keeping coherence over vegetation; P measures forest biomass and sees under dry sand and ice. Water vapour absorbs near 22 GHz (1.36 cm), which radiometers use and imaging radars avoid.

Worked example

Antenna size for a given beamwidth scales with : an azimuth beamwidth of 3° ( rad) needs : 0.6 m at X band (3.1 cm), 1.1 m at C band (5.6 cm), 4.6 m at L band (24 cm), 13 m at P band (69 cm) — which is why Biomass carries a 12 m deployable reflector.

Intuition

The wavelength is the radar's yardstick: it notices structures of its own size and looks through what is much smaller.

Pitfall

Longer wavelengths need larger antennas for the same beam width and suffer ionospheric effects (Faraday rotation) at L and P band.
