SAR interferometry (InSAR and DInSAR)

Multiplying one SLC image by the conjugate of another, , gives an interferogram whose phase measures path differences to a fraction of the wavelength: across-track baselines turn it into topography, repeat passes from the same orbit into ground displacement with millimetre sensitivity; coherence says how reliable it is.

Method

  • Interferogram: . The random scatterer phase cancels if the two acquisitions see the same scatterers; what remains is the geometric phase.
  • Topography: with perpendicular baseline , a height change changes the phase by ; the height of ambiguity is the height giving one fringe (TanDEM-X: single pass, no temporal change).
  • Displacement (DInSAR): after removing topography with a DEM, a motion along the line of sight gives : one fringe is of motion (2.8 cm in C band).
  • Coherence : drops with vegetation growth, water, long time intervals (temporal decorrelation) and large baselines.
  • Phase unwrapping: the phase is known modulo ; integrating it spatially recovers the absolute value, the hardest step.
  • Time series (persistent scatterers, SBAS): many acquisitions separate deformation from atmospheric delay: mm/year precision.

Example

Sentinel-1, cm: a fringe cycle in a differential interferogram is 2.8 cm of line-of-sight motion; earthquakes show tens of concentric fringes, subsiding cities a few per year.

Intuition

The phase is a ruler graduated in half-wavelengths; comparing two readings of the same ruler shows how much the ground moved.

Pitfall

Atmospheric water vapour delays the signal by centimetres and looks exactly like deformation in a single interferogram; only stacks of many dates separate the two.
