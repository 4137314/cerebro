Pansharpening

Sensors pair a fine panchromatic band with coarser multispectral bands; pansharpening injects the spatial detail of the pan band into the co-registered, upsampled multispectral image — by component substitution (IHS, PCA, Gram–Schmidt) or by adding the pan's high-pass detail — trading some spectral fidelity for resolution.

needs Geometric, radiometric, spectral and temporal resolution, Geometric correction, geocoding and registration

see also Principal component analysis of multispectral images, True and false colour composites

Method

Co-register and resample the multispectral bands to the pan grid.

  • Brovey: : each band rescaled by the ratio of pan to mean intensity.
  • IHS substitution (3 bands): transform RGB to intensity–hue–saturation, replace with the pan (histogram-matched), transform back. Equivalent to .
  • PCA / Gram–Schmidt: replace the first component (or a synthetic low-resolution pan) with the pan.
  • High-pass injection: , where is the pan blurred to the multispectral resolution: only detail is added, preserving the spectra.

Example

WorldView-3: pan 0.31 m, multispectral 1.24 m: pansharpened RGB at 0.31 m. Quality is assessed spectrally (spectral angle, ERGAS against degraded references) and spatially (correlation of high-pass details with the pan).

Intuition

Take the colours from the blurry photo and the sharpness from the black-and-white one.

Pitfall

The pan band's spectral range does not match the sum of the multispectral bands (it often extends into the NIR): substitution methods then distort colours, especially of vegetation. Sharpened products are for viewing and object delineation; radiometric analysis should use the original bands.
