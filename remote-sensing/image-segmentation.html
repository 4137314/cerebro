Image segmentation

Segmentation partitions the image into connected regions that are homogeneous by some criterion and labels each region; the criterion depends on the data (for optical images closeness of means, for SAR closeness of mean intensities given multiplicative speckle), and the main families are histogram thresholding, contour-based, split-and-merge and region growing.

Definition

A segmentation is a partition of the pixels with

  1. image, ;
  2. each connected;
  3. true for a homogeneity predicate ;
  4. false for adjacent .

Homogeneity criteria: optical — region variance below a threshold, or means of adjacent regions within a tolerance; SAR — ratio of mean intensities close to 1, coefficient of variation compatible with speckle.

Methods:

  • histogram thresholding;
  • contour-based (edges closed into boundaries);
  • split and merge;
  • region growing.

Uses

Region-based classification (object-based image analysis): classify regions instead of pixels, using shape, size and texture features; reduces salt-and-pepper noise in maps, especially in very high resolution images where single pixels are parts of objects.

Intuition

Segmentation turns pixels into objects — fields, lakes, buildings — before anyone asks what the objects are.

Pitfall

There is no single correct segmentation: the scale (minimum region size, homogeneity tolerance) decides whether a field is one region or a dozen, and the right scale depends on the application.
