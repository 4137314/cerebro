Scattergram

A scattergram plots, for two bands, the number of pixels at each pair of values (a two-dimensional histogram); it shows clusters, their correlation between bands and the classes' separability, and is the basic exploratory view of a multispectral image.

Definition

, displayed as an image with logarithmic grey scale. Typical structures in the red–NIR plane: the soil line (bare soils from dark to bright along a diagonal), vegetation above it (high NIR, low red), water near the origin.

Example

Selecting a region of the scattergram and highlighting the corresponding pixels in the image links feature space and geography.

Intuition

It is the image seen from inside the feature space: where the pixels pile up, there is a class.

Pitfall

Highly correlated bands (visible bands among themselves) put all pixels on a narrow diagonal: little separability. Choose band pairs that carry different information, such as red and NIR.
