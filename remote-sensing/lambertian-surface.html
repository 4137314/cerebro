Lambertian surfaces and the BRDF

A Lambertian surface reflects with the same radiance in every direction, so whatever the viewing angle; real surfaces are described by the bidirectional reflectance distribution function , between the Lambertian (diffuse) and specular (mirror) extremes.

Model

  • BRDF: [sr], how much of the irradiance coming from one direction leaves in another.
  • Lambertian: constant. Radiance is independent of the view angle; the irradiance on the surface follows Lambert's cosine law .
  • Specular: all energy leaves at the mirror angle (calm water, sun glint).
  • Natural surfaces mix the two and show effects such as the hot spot: maximum brightness when Sun and sensor are aligned, because shadows are hidden.

Intuition

A matte wall looks equally bright from any angle; a mirror only from one. Most land surfaces are closer to the wall, water closer to the mirror.

Pitfall

Assuming Lambertian behaviour for wide-swath or off-nadir images introduces brightness gradients across the scene (forests darker on one side of the swath); BRDF normalisation is needed for mosaics and time series.
