Construction

The compression ratio (time–bandwidth product, typically 100–1000) is the gain in peak-to-energy ratio.

Picture

A chirp: frequency grows linearly during the pulse.

Example

Sentinel-1 uses chirps of up to 100 MHz bandwidth: m in slant range, a few metres on the ground, with pulses tens of microseconds long.

Intuition

A long whistle that rises in pitch: each instant of the echo is labelled by its frequency, so the receiver can tell overlapping echoes apart and re-stack them into a sharp click.

Pitfall

Compression produces sidelobes around strong targets; they are reduced by weighting (windowing) the filter, at the cost of a slightly wider peak.
