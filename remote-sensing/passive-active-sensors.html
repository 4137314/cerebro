Passive and active sensors

Passive sensors measure radiation emitted or reflected by the target (cameras, multispectral and hyperspectral scanners, thermal cameras, microwave radiometers); active sensors illuminate the target and measure what comes back (radar, SAR, lidar, ground-penetrating radar); both can produce measurements (1-D signals) or images (2-D).

Definition

The aim of a sensor is to measure reflected or emitted radiance. Every measurement integrates the radiation over a range of wavelengths, an area on the ground and a time interval, which limits its accuracy.

  measures (non-imaging) imaging
passive radiometers, spectrometers cameras, thermal cameras, scanners
active altimeters, scatterometers, lidar side-looking radar, SAR, lidar, GPR

Comparison

  • passive optical: rich spectral information, needs sunlight (reflective bands) and clear sky;
  • active microwave: day and night, through clouds, sensitive to geometry, roughness and moisture; harder to interpret (speckle, side-looking geometry);
  • lidar: precise 3-D structure (canopy height, terrain), small swaths.

Intuition

A passive sensor is a camera in daylight; an active one brings its own flash, at a wavelength of its choice.

Pitfall

"Active" does not mean better: radar measures different physical properties than optical sensors, and the two are complementary rather than interchangeable.
