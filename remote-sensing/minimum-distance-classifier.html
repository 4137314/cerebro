Minimum-distance classifier

Each class is represented by the mean of its training pixels and every pixel goes to the nearest mean; with Euclidean distance the discriminants are linear and the boundaries are perpendicular bisectors, with Mahalanobis distance the class covariances are taken into account.

Example

Intuition

Each class is summarised by its centre of gravity, and every pixel joins the closest centre.

Pitfall

Euclidean distance ignores spread: a wide class (urban) loses pixels to a compact neighbour (water) whose mean happens to be closer, which the maximum-likelihood classifier corrects.
