#+title: Real analysis
#+summary: The largest part of the archive, and the one whose order matters most: almost every note here is used by the next. The sequence below is that order. It has one turning point -- the completeness axiom -- and everything before it is definitions, everything after it is consequences.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:analysis][Analysis]].

* The line

[[id:supremum-and-infimum][Supremum and infimum]] is the definition that makes the axiom
statable; [[id:completeness-of-the-real-numbers][completeness of the real numbers]] is the axiom, and the only
thing separating \(\R\) from \(\Q\). Its first two consequences are
[[id:the-archimedean-property-and-the-density-of-the-rationals][the Archimedean property and the density of the rationals]]; [[id:the-absolute-value-and-the-triangle-inequality][the absolute
value and the triangle inequality]] is the estimate every later proof
splits through.

* Sequences

[[id:limit-of-a-sequence][Limit of a sequence]], then [[id:the-algebra-of-limits][the algebra of limits]], which is what makes
limits computable. Then the three ways completeness produces one:
[[id:monotone-convergence-theorem][monotone convergence]], [[id:bolzano-weierstrass-theorem][Bolzano-Weierstrass]] and [[id:cauchy-sequences-and-the-completeness-criterion][the Cauchy
criterion]] -- the last being the one that mentions no order and
therefore travels to [[id:metric-spaces-and-completeness][metric spaces]]. [[id:limit-superior-and-limit-inferior][Limit superior and limit
inferior]] exist for every bounded sequence, which is why they are the
right tool when convergence is what one is trying to prove.

* Series

[[id:series-and-convergence-of-the-partial-sums][Series and convergence of the partial sums]] reduces series to
sequences, and everything after it is a criterion:
[[id:the-cauchy-criterion-for-series-the-harmonic-series-and-condensation][the Cauchy criterion, the harmonic series and condensation]],
[[id:absolute-convergence-and-the-comparison-test][absolute convergence and the comparison test]], [[id:the-ratio-and-root-tests][the ratio and root
tests]]. [[id:the-alternating-series-test][The alternating series test]] is the one that uses cancellation
rather than absolute values, and hence the source of every conditionally
convergent example. [[id:power-series-and-the-radius-of-convergence][Power series and the radius of convergence]] is where
series stop being numbers and become functions.

* Continuity

[[id:limits-and-continuity][Limits and continuity]] and [[id:the-limit-of-a-function-and-its-sequential-characterisation][the limit of a function and its sequential
characterisation]] -- the second is what lets every theorem about
sequences be spent twice. Then [[id:the-algebra-of-continuous-functions-and-composition][the algebra of continuous functions and
composition]], [[id:one-sided-limits-and-the-discontinuities-of-a-monotone-function][one-sided limits and the discontinuities of a monotone
function]].

The three theorems on an interval are where completeness reappears:
[[id:the-intermediate-value-theorem][the intermediate value theorem]], [[id:continuous-functions-on-a-compact-interval][continuous functions on a compact
interval]] (bounded, and attaining their bounds), and [[id:uniform-continuity-and-the-heine-cantor-theorem][uniform continuity
and the Heine-Cantor theorem]]. [[id:the-continuous-image-of-an-interval-and-the-continuous-inverse][The continuous image of an interval and
the continuous inverse]] is the pair of statements that becomes, in
[[id:topology][topology]], "connectedness and compactness are preserved by continuous
maps".

* Differentiation

[[id:the-derivative][The derivative]] and [[id:the-rules-of-differentiation][the rules of differentiation]], then the one
theorem the subject rests on: [[id:the-mean-value-theorem][the mean value theorem]]. Its consequences
are [[id:monotonicity-and-the-sign-of-the-derivative][monotonicity and the sign of the derivative]], [[id:cauchys-mean-value-theorem-and-de-lhopitals-rule][Cauchy's mean value
theorem and de l'Hopital's rule]], and [[id:convex-functions-and-the-second-derivative][convex functions and the second
derivative]].

Taylor's theorem appears twice on purpose, because the two remainders
answer different questions: [[id:taylors-theorem-with-the-peano-remainder][the Peano remainder]] is a local statement
about the order of the error, [[id:taylors-theorem-with-the-lagrange-remainder][the Lagrange remainder]] a global one with
a value, and [[id:uniform-bounds-on-the-taylor-remainder][uniform bounds on the Taylor remainder]] is what turns
either into a convergent series.

* Integration

[[id:the-riemann-integral][The Riemann integral]] by upper and lower sums; [[id:linearity-additivity-and-monotonicity-of-the-integral][linearity, additivity
and monotonicity]]; [[id:monotone-functions-are-integrable-and-the-mean-value-theorem-for-integrals][monotone functions are integrable, and the mean value
theorem for integrals]]. [[id:the-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus][The fundamental theorem of calculus]] is the
statement that the two operations of the subject are inverse, and
[[id:integration-by-parts-and-substitution][integration by parts and substitution]] is that theorem read backwards as
a technique.

* Limits of functions, and several variables

[[id:pointwise-and-uniform-convergence][Pointwise and uniform convergence]] is the note that separates the two
modes and explains which theorems survive each. In several variables,
[[id:differentiability-in-several-variables][differentiability]] is defined by approximation by a linear map -- not
by partial derivatives, which is the distinction the subject exists to
make -- and [[id:extrema-in-several-variables-and-the-second-differential][extrema and the second differential]] is the second-order
test. [[id:the-legendre-transform][The Legendre transform]] is filed here for its convexity, and used
in [[id:mathematical-physics][mechanics]].

* Beyond one variable

The second-year continuation is filed under [[id:analysis][Analysis]] rather than here:
[[id:the-inverse-and-implicit-function-theorems][the inverse and implicit function theorems]],
[[id:constrained-extrema-and-lagrange-multipliers][Lagrange multipliers]], [[id:product-measures-and-the-theorems-of-tonelli-and-fubini][Fubini's theorem]], and the
[[id:surface-integrals-the-divergence-theorem-and-stokes-theorem][divergence and Stokes theorems]].

* What is missing

Rearrangement of series; the Riemann criterion in terms of the measure
of the discontinuity set, which would connect this subject to
[[id:sigma-algebras-measures-and-measurable-functions][measure theory]]; and the elementary functions -- the exponential,
logarithm and trigonometric functions are used throughout the archive
and defined nowhere in it.
