#+title: Complex analysis
#+summary: One hypothesis -- that a difference quotient converges along every direction of the plane rather than along a line -- and the subject becomes rigid in a way real analysis never is. A function differentiable once is differentiable infinitely often; its values on a small arc determine it everywhere; it cannot have an interior maximum of modulus; and a single number per singularity computes an integral. Each of those is a corollary of the same integral formula.
#+date: [2026-08-11 Tue]

Part of [[id:analysis][Analysis]].

* The chain

1. [[id:holomorphic-functions-and-the-cauchy-riemann-equations][Holomorphic functions and the Cauchy-Riemann equations]] -- what
   complex differentiability asks for, as two equations on the real and
   imaginary parts.
2. [[id:complex-integration-and-cauchys-theorem][Complex integration and Cauchy's theorem]] -- the integral over a
   closed curve vanishes. Goursat's proof assumes only differentiability,
   which is what lets the next step prove more than it assumes.
3. [[id:the-cauchy-integral-formula-and-analyticity][The Cauchy integral formula and analyticity]] -- values inside a disc
   from values on the circle, hence power series, hence Liouville's
   theorem and the fundamental theorem of algebra.
4. [[id:zeros-the-identity-theorem-and-the-maximum-principle][Zeros, the identity theorem and the maximum principle]] -- the two
   rigidity statements.
5. [[id:laurent-series-and-isolated-singularities][Laurent series and isolated singularities]] -- the expansion adapted
   to an annulus, and the trichotomy removable / pole / essential.
6. [[id:the-residue-theorem-and-the-argument-principle][The residue theorem and the argument principle]] -- what the integral
   is when there /is/ something inside, and how to count zeros.

* Why the subject is a tool

The residue theorem turns contour integration into arithmetic, which is
why complex analysis is the working language of
[[id:mathematical-physics][mathematical physics]]: real integrals with no elementary primitive are
evaluated by closing a contour, and the poles of a transfer function
decide whether a system is stable, by [[id:the-residue-theorem-and-the-argument-principle][Rouche's theorem]]. In the other
direction, harmonic functions in the plane are exactly the real parts of
holomorphic ones, so the [[id:harmonic-functions-the-mean-value-property-and-the-dirichlet-problem][Dirichlet problem]] in two dimensions is complex
analysis.

* What is missing

Conformal mapping and the Riemann mapping theorem, which is the
geometric half of the subject and the reason it solves plane boundary
value problems; analytic continuation and Riemann surfaces; the Poisson
kernel; infinite products and the elementary special functions -- the
gamma and zeta functions in particular, with which this would begin to
touch number theory; and Picard's theorems, of which only the
Casorati-Weierstrass shadow is here.
