#+title: Probability
#+summary: Probability is [[id:sigma-algebras-measures-and-measurable-functions][measure theory]] with a normalisation and two extra ideas. The normalisation -- total mass one -- buys the finiteness that makes every measure continuous from above and every bounded function integrable. The two ideas are independence and conditioning, and neither is measure-theoretic: they are hypotheses about how information is distributed, and every theorem below is one of them applied to a sequence.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:mathematics][Mathematics]].

* Setting up

[[id:probability-spaces-conditioning-and-independence][Probability spaces, conditioning and independence]] and
[[id:random-variables-laws-and-distribution-functions][random variables, laws and distribution functions]]. The move that
defines the subject's style is in the second: the underlying space is
never described again, and everything is said about the measure a random
variable pushes onto the line.

[[id:expectation-the-transfer-theorem-and-jensens-inequality][Expectation, the transfer theorem and Jensen's inequality]] makes that
precise -- an expectation is an integral on \(\R\) against the law --
and [[id:variance-covariance-and-the-markov-and-chebyshev-inequalities][variance, covariance and the Markov and Chebyshev inequalities]]
supplies the one bound the rest of the subject spends.

* The limit theorems

Three notes, in increasing strength, all about the same sum:

1. [[id:modes-of-convergence-and-the-borel-cantelli-lemmas][Modes of convergence and the Borel-Cantelli lemmas]] -- four
   inequivalent notions of convergence, and the lemma that upgrades a
   summable bound on probabilities into a statement about almost every
   outcome.
2. [[id:the-weak-and-strong-laws-of-large-numbers][The weak and strong laws of large numbers]] -- the average converges
   to the mean, in probability by one application of Chebyshev, almost
   surely by Borel-Cantelli.
3. [[id:the-central-limit-theorem][The central limit theorem]] -- the error of that convergence,
   rescaled, has a limit that forgets everything except the first two
   moments. It is proved through
   [[id:characteristic-functions][characteristic functions]], which turn convolution into
   multiplication.

* Conditioning done properly

[[id:conditional-expectation-as-an-orthogonal-projection][Conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection]] is where the
subject needs [[id:hilbert-spaces-projection-riesz-representation-and-orthonormal-bases][Hilbert space]] theory: conditioning on an event of
probability zero has no elementary meaning, and the construction that
works is the projection onto the square-integrable variables measurable
with respect to the given information. [[id:square-integrable-functions-and-the-completeness-of-l2][The completeness of \(L^{2}\)]] is
what makes that projection exist.

* What is assumed and not proved

Two theorems are used and marked =assumed=: the uniqueness of a law
given its [[id:characteristic-functions][characteristic function]], and Levy's continuity theorem. Both
need Fubini's theorem, which this archive does not have. That is the
only debt in the area, and =cerebro results open= reports it.

* What is missing

[[id:statistics][Statistics]] is the applied half and has its own hub. Absent from the
theory: product measures and Fubini, hence any construction of an
infinite sequence of independent variables; conditional expectation for
merely integrable variables, which needs Radon-Nikodym; martingales and
their convergence theorem; stochastic processes of any kind, Brownian
motion in particular; and large deviations, of which only the
elementary [[id:variance-covariance-and-the-markov-and-chebyshev-inequalities][Markov bound]] is present.
