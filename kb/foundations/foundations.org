#+title: Foundations
#+summary: The rest of the archive uses sets, functions, quotients and proofs without comment. This area is where those become objects of study rather than instruments: what a set can be, what a proof is, and what follows about the limits of both. It is also, in practice, where the tools live -- Zorn's lemma and the ultrafilter lemma are used far more often here than they are discussed.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:mathematics][Mathematics]].

* The subjects

- [[id:set-theory][Set theory]] -- relations and quotients, order, choice, transfinite
  induction and recursion, cardinality, filters.
- [[id:logic][Logic]] -- formulas as objects: satisfaction, derivation, the
  relation between the two, and what no derivation can decide.

* The two theorems everything else uses

[[id:the-axiom-of-choice-and-zorns-lemma][Zorn's lemma]] and [[id:filters-ultrafilters-and-the-ultrafilter-lemma][the ultrafilter lemma]] are the export products of
this area. Between them they produce a [[id:span-linear-independence-bases-and-dimension][basis of any vector space]], a
[[id:integral-domains-fields-prime-and-maximal-ideals][maximal ideal of any ring]], a [[id:the-hahn-banach-theorem][Hahn-Banach extension]], [[id:tychonoffs-theorem][Tychonoff's
theorem]], the [[id:ultraproducts-and-the-theorem-of-los][compactness theorem for first-order logic]] and the [[id:boolean-algebras-and-stones-representation-theorem][Stone
representation]] of a Boolean algebra. Every one of those proofs produces
an object that no construction reaches, and it is worth knowing which
results in a subject are of that kind.

* Syntax against semantics

The characteristic move of logic is to define two relations and then
compare them: \(\Sigma \models \varphi\), which quantifies over all
assignments, and \(\Sigma\vdash\varphi\), which asks for a finite
object. [[id:a-proof-system-for-propositional-logic-soundness-and-the-deduction-theorem][Soundness]] is the easy direction and [[id:completeness-of-propositional-logic][completeness]] the
substantial one; [[id:propositional-logic-formulas-assignments-and-compactness][compactness]] is what the finiteness of derivations
forces, and it can also be proved semantically, by Zorn or by
[[id:ultraproducts-and-the-theorem-of-los][ultraproducts]], which is the same argument seen from the other side.
Then [[id:elementary-substructures-and-the-lowenheim-skolem-theorems][Lowenheim-Skolem]] says how little a first-order theory controls,
and [[id:computability-the-halting-problem-and-rices-theorem][the halting problem]] says that some questions have no decision
procedure at all.

* What is missing

The axioms of set theory are used and never written down -- there is no
note stating ZF. Godel's completeness theorem for first-order logic is
absent, its consequence (compactness) having been obtained by
ultraproducts instead; the incompleteness theorems are absent, and with
them arithmetic as an object of study; and category theory, which is
where the repeated constructions of [[id:algebra][algebra]] and [[id:topology][topology]] are properly
stated, does not exist here at all.
