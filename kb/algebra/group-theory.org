#+title: Group theory
#+summary: A group is one associative, invertible operation and nothing else, which is little enough that the axioms fit on a line and strong enough that finite groups have an arithmetic. The subject splits cleanly in two: what a single group is made of, and how a group acts on something else -- with the second usually answering questions about the first.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:algebra][Algebra]].

* The definitions

[[id:binary-operation][Binary operation]], then [[id:group][group]] and [[id:abelian-group][abelian group]];
[[id:uniqueness-of-the-identity-element][uniqueness of the identity element]] is the first thing the axioms
imply, and the reason one may say /the/ identity. [[id:group-homomorphism][Group homomorphism]]
is the definition that makes the rest of the subject possible: a group
is studied through the maps out of it.

* Inside one group

[[id:subgroups-the-subgroup-criterion-and-generation][Subgroups, the subgroup criterion and generation]]; [[id:the-order-of-an-element-and-cyclic-groups][the order of an
element and cyclic groups]], the one family that is classified
completely. Then the two structural theorems: [[id:cosets-the-index-and-lagranges-theorem][cosets, the index and
Lagrange's theorem]] -- a subgroup cuts the group into equal pieces, so
sizes divide -- and [[id:normal-subgroups-and-the-quotient-group][normal subgroups and the quotient group]], which
identifies the subgroups one may divide by as exactly the kernels.
[[id:the-isomorphism-theorems-for-groups][The isomorphism theorems]] are the consequences.

* Acting on a set

[[id:group-actions-orbits-and-the-orbit-stabiliser-theorem][Group actions, orbits and the orbit-stabiliser theorem]] converts
counting problems into index computations, and it is the engine of
everything that follows: [[id:cauchys-theorem-and-the-sylow-theorems][Cauchy's theorem and the Sylow theorems]] are
four applications of one fixed-point lemma. [[id:the-symmetric-group-and-the-sign-of-a-permutation][The symmetric group and the
sign of a permutation]] is where a group acts on a polynomial to produce
an invariant, and [[id:isometries-of-euclidean-space-and-the-orthogonal-group][the orthogonal group]] is where a group is met as
symmetries in the first place.

* Building and taking apart

[[id:direct-and-semidirect-products][Direct and semidirect products]] say when a group is assembled from two
of its subgroups, and show that a normal subgroup and its quotient do
not determine it. In the other direction, [[id:composition-series-jordan-holder-and-solvable-groups][composition series,
Jordan-Holder and solvable groups]] give a well-defined list of simple
factors. [[id:free-groups-and-presentations][Free groups and presentations]] is the opposite move again: a
group named by generators and relations, with nothing else assumed.

* What is missing

The classification of finitely generated abelian groups; the simplicity
of \(A_{n}\) for \(n \ge 5\), which is what makes the insolubility of
the quintic a theorem rather than a hope; group actions on
themselves beyond the class equation; representation theory; and the
Galois correspondence, which is the reason most of this exists.
