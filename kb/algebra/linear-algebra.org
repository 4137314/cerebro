#+title: Linear algebra
#+summary: Linear algebra is the one algebraic theory that is finished: over a field, every module is free, every short exact sequence splits, and a single integer classifies a space up to isomorphism. That is why it is the model the rest of algebra is measured against, and why so much of [[id:geometry][geometry]] and [[id:analysis][analysis]] reduces to it.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:algebra][Algebra]].

* The structure theory

[[id:vector-space][Vector space]], then [[id:span-linear-independence-bases-and-dimension][span, linear independence, bases and dimension]] --
where the exchange lemma proves that a number can be attached to the
space at all, and Zorn's lemma provides a basis when the space is
infinite-dimensional. [[id:linear-maps-quotient-spaces-and-the-rank-nullity-theorem][Linear maps, quotient spaces and the rank-nullity
theorem]] is the first isomorphism theorem with a dimension count
attached, and [[id:dual-spaces-annihilators-and-the-transpose][dual spaces, annihilators and the transpose]] is the
dictionary between describing a subspace by generators and describing it
by equations.

* Operators

[[id:the-determinant-as-the-alternating-multilinear-form][The determinant]] is defined by what it must satisfy -- multilinear and
alternating -- so that its formula is a consequence and its identities
are applications of uniqueness. It turns the search for eigenvectors
into a question about roots: [[id:eigenvalues-the-characteristic-polynomial-and-cayley-hamilton][eigenvalues, the characteristic polynomial
and Cayley-Hamilton]], where diagonalisability becomes a statement about
distinct roots and about the field containing them.

* Geometry on a vector space

An extra structure, a bilinear form, and the classification questions
begin again. [[id:inner-product-space][Inner product space]] and [[id:cauchy-schwarz-inequality][the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality]] give
lengths and angles; [[id:orthogonality-gram-schmidt-and-orthogonal-decomposition][orthogonality, Gram-Schmidt and orthogonal
decomposition]] give coordinates adapted to them, and the best
approximation in a subspace. [[id:bilinear-forms-quadratic-forms-and-sylvesters-law-of-inertia][Bilinear forms, quadratic forms and
Sylvester's law of inertia]] drop positivity and classify what remains by
signature; [[id:the-spectral-theorem-for-symmetric-operators][the spectral theorem]] keeps the inner product fixed and
diagonalises a symmetric operator by an orthonormal basis. The pair of
them is the difference between classifying a quadratic form and finding
the principal axes of an ellipsoid.

Two notes filed here are already geometry: [[id:affine-spaces-affine-subspaces-and-affine-maps][affine spaces]] and
[[id:conics-and-quadrics-classification-by-signature][conics and quadrics]].

* What is missing

The Jordan normal form and the theory of a single operator over a
non-algebraically-closed field; the minimal polynomial as opposed to the
characteristic one; tensor and exterior algebra, which is what the
determinant is really about; singular values; and multilinear algebra in
general.
