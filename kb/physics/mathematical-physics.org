#+title: Mathematical physics
#+summary: Classical mechanics is in this archive as geometry, not as physics: the notes below contain no experiments and no units, and what they study is a structure -- first a variational principle on a space of paths, then a symplectic form on the space of states -- that turns out to determine the motion. The physical content is the choice of Lagrangian; everything after it is a theorem.
#+date: [2026-08-10 Mon]

Part of [[id:mathematics][Mathematics]].

* The chain, in order

1. [[id:the-action-functional-and-the-first-variation][The action functional and the first variation]] -- motion is a
   stationary point of a functional on paths, which is a statement about
   the whole trajectory rather than about an instant.
2. [[id:the-euler-lagrange-equations][The Euler-Lagrange equations]] -- stationarity, made local. This is
   the point at which a variational principle becomes a differential
   equation.
3. [[id:the-legendre-transform][The Legendre transform]] -- the change of variables from velocities to
   momenta, and the reason it is invertible is convexity, not physics.
4. [[id:hamiltons-equations][Hamilton's equations]] -- the same motion as a first-order flow on
   phase space, symmetric in the two halves of the coordinates.
5. [[id:poisson-brackets][Poisson brackets]] -- the algebraic structure that flow induces on
   observables, under which the equations of motion read
   \(\dot{f} = \set{f,H}\).
6. [[id:canonical-transformations-and-liouvilles-theorem][Canonical transformations and Liouville's theorem]] -- the changes of
   coordinates that preserve the structure, and the volume they
   preserve.

* Mechanics of constrained and extended systems

The chain above starts from a Lagrangian and does not say where one
comes from. [[id:constraints-virtual-work-and-the-lagrange-equations][Constraints, virtual work and the Lagrange equations]]
supplies the derivation from Newton's law, with the one modelling
hypothesis -- ideal constraints do no virtual work -- marked as an
axiom. After it:

- [[id:central-forces-the-two-body-problem-and-keplers-laws][Central forces, the two-body problem and Kepler's laws]] -- the one
  system solved completely, and solved by symmetry rather than by
  integration.
- [[id:equilibria-stability-and-the-lagrange-dirichlet-theorem][Equilibria, stability and the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem]] -- a strict
  minimum of the potential is stable, because energy traps the motion.
- [[id:small-oscillations-and-normal-modes][Small oscillations and normal modes]] -- near such an equilibrium the
  system is a set of independent oscillators, by simultaneous
  diagonalisation of two quadratic forms.
- [[id:rigid-body-kinematics-and-the-inertia-tensor][Rigid body kinematics and the inertia tensor]] and
  [[id:eulers-equations-and-the-stability-of-free-rotation][Euler's equations]] -- the configuration space is a group, the kinetic
  energy a quadratic form, and the stability of free rotation is decided
  by which combination of the two conserved quantities is definite.

* Quantum mechanics

The same structure with the algebra of observables changed:
[[id:the-hilbert-space-formalism-of-quantum-mechanics][the Hilbert space formalism]] -- states as unit vectors, observables as
symmetric operators, the Poisson bracket replaced by a commutator, and
the uncertainty relation as a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality -- and
[[id:the-harmonic-oscillator-by-ladder-operators][the harmonic oscillator by ladder operators]], whose spectrum follows
from one commutation relation with no differential equation solved.

* Symmetry and conservation

[[id:noethers-theorem][Noether's theorem]] is the reason this chain is worth building rather
than solving the equations directly: a one-parameter symmetry of the
action yields a conserved quantity, so translation invariance /is/
conservation of momentum. In the Hamiltonian formulation the statement
becomes an identity about the [[id:poisson-brackets][Poisson bracket]] -- an observable is
conserved exactly when it commutes with the Hamiltonian -- which is the
form that survives into quantum mechanics with the bracket replaced by a
commutator.

* What is missing

Symplectic geometry as geometry -- there is no manifold here, only
coordinates, so [[id:canonical-transformations-and-liouvilles-theorem][Liouville's theorem]] is stated as a computation rather
than as a statement about a form; integrable systems and action-angle
variables; the spectral theory that quantum mechanics needs beyond
[[id:the-hilbert-space-formalism-of-quantum-mechanics][the formalism]] -- self-adjointness as opposed to symmetry, and the
spectral theorem for unbounded operators; scattering; statistical
mechanics; and relativity.

Continuum mechanics is absent as a subject, but the equations it
produces are not: they are the [[id:partial-differential-equations][partial differential equations]] of the
analysis area, and the [[id:surface-integrals-the-divergence-theorem-and-stokes-theorem][divergence theorem]] is what turns a local
conservation law into a global one.
