#+title: Dynamical systems
#+summary: An [[id:existence-and-uniqueness-for-ordinary-differential-equations][existence theorem]] produces one solution at a time; a dynamical system is the whole family at once, read as a flow moving the space. The questions change accordingly -- not what the solution is, but where it goes -- and the two answers available without solving anything are linearisation near an equilibrium and the existence of a function that decreases along motions.
#+date: [2026-08-11 Tue]

Part of [[id:mathematics][Mathematics]].

* The two notes

- [[id:flows-equilibria-and-linearisation][Flows, equilibria and linearisation]] -- the flow as a one-parameter
  group, and the theorem that a hyperbolic sink of the linearisation is
  an asymptotically stable equilibrium of the nonlinear system.
- [[id:lyapunov-functions-and-invariant-sets][Lyapunov functions and invariant sets]] -- what to do when the
  linearisation is neutral: find a function positive away from the
  equilibrium that does not increase, which is the energy argument with
  the mechanics removed.

* Where it connects

The neutral case is not exotic: it is exactly what a conservative
mechanical system presents, since the linearisation at a stable
equilibrium has purely imaginary eigenvalues. That is why
[[id:equilibria-stability-and-the-lagrange-dirichlet-theorem][Lagrange-Dirichlet]] and [[id:eulers-equations-and-the-stability-of-free-rotation][the stability analysis of free rotation]] are
Lyapunov arguments rather than linearisation arguments, and why
[[id:mathematical-physics][mathematical physics]] is where this subject earns its keep.

* What is missing

Almost all of it: periodic orbits and the Poincare-Bendixson theorem,
which is the two-dimensional classification; stable and unstable
manifolds and the Hartman-Grobman theorem; bifurcations; chaotic
dynamics and symbolic coding; ergodic theory and the Birkhoff theorem,
which would connect this area to [[id:probability][probability]] and to statistical
mechanics.
