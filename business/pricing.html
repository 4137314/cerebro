Pricing and contribution

Price is the only revenue-generating P; it lies between a cost floor and a perceived-value ceiling, adjusted to competition, with launch strategies (skimming, penetration), product-mix pricing and adjustment manoeuvres.

Definition

Price: amount of money charged for a product, or more broadly the sum of values customers exchange for the benefits of owning or using it. It is the only marketing-mix element that produces revenue; all the others are costs.

Determinant Sets
Demand (perceived value) maximum price
Costs minimum price
Market / competition where in range

Model

New-product launch

  • Market skimming: high launch price to attract buyers with strong desire, lowered gradually to reach wider bands. Example: the first iPhone, 8 GB, from USD 599 to USD 399 six months after launch.
  • Market penetration: low launch price to attract many customers and reach high volumes immediately.

Price adjustments

Discounts and allowances (time, quantity); price discrimination by customer (adult/child), product version (packaging), location (front row), time (advance booking); psychological pricing (high price signals quality); promotional (below list: "sottocosto", sales); geographic (Da Michele pizza: different prices in Naples and Milan); dynamic (continuous adjustment: online auctions, Amazon); international (economic conditions, local competition, laws, retail system).

Price changes and competitor reactions

Price cut (excess capacity, falling demand, rivals' cuts) → seen as more affordable but possibly lower quality. Price rise (cost inflation, VAT rise, excess demand, scarce materials) → seen as greedy, or as even higher quality. When a competitor cuts its price: has it cut? → will it hurt our share and profits? → can/should we act? If yes: cut our price, raise perceived value, improve quality and raise price, or launch a low-price "fighting brand".

Worked example

Contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost. Price EUR 50, variable cost EUR 30 → EUR 20. Fixed costs EUR 100,000 → break-even

A 10% price cut (EUR 45) lowers unit contribution to EUR 15: break-even rises to 6,667 units, so volume must grow 33% just to keep profit — check before matching a competitor's cut.

Pitfall

Cost sets the floor, not the price. Skimming needs buyers with high willingness to pay and barriers against quick imitation. Discrimination is by customer, product, place or time — not arbitrary.
