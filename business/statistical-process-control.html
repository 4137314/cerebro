Process variation and control charts

Natural process variation often follows a normal distribution; control limits at ±3σ separate random from assignable causes, and capability Cp compares the specification range with 6σ.

Definition

Process performance measured over time varies. Question: is a strange point random variation or a change in the process to investigate? Sampling call times day after day builds a histogram that fits a normal distribution.

Range Share of points
±1σ 68%
±2σ 95.4%
±3σ 99.7%

Natural variation = 99.7% of the distribution = ±3σ around the mean.

Model

Control chart: centre line (mean), UCL and LCL at ±3σ. A point outside → likely assignable cause → process out of control. Also investigate unlikely patterns even inside the limits:

  • alternating and erratic behaviour;
  • suspiciously average behaviour (all points hugging the centre);
  • two points near a control limit;
  • five points on one side of the centre line;
  • an apparent trend in one direction;
  • a sudden change of level.

Control limits come from the process; specification (tolerance) limits come from the customer — different things.

Worked example

Call centre: mean 100 s, σ = 20 s → natural variation 40–160 s. Specification 40–160 s → : barely capable. Cutting σ to 15 s gives . A call of 175 s is above UCL = 160 s → investigate.

Pitfall

In control ≠ capable: a stable process can still produce out of specification. Cp ignores centring (a shifted mean can fail with Cp > 1).

Recall

State the 68/95/99.7 rule; compute Cp; list six patterns to investigate.
