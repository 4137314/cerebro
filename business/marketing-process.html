Definition

Marketing is the process by which firms create value for customers and build strong relationships with them in order to capture value in return.

Concept Meaning Pizza example
Need state of felt deprivation hunger
Want need shaped by culture and personality wanting a pizza
Demand want backed by purchasing power buying the pizza

Needs are intrinsic to human nature and of three types — functional (an object/service: transport), psychological (an attitude/value: moving safely), social (a sign/symbol: being noticed while moving). Perception of a need is subjective; one product can meet several needs, one need can be met by several products.

Market offering: combination of products, services, information or experiences offered to satisfy a need. Marketing myopia: paying more attention to the products than to the benefits and experiences they produce. Exchange: obtaining a desired good from someone by offering something in return — keep and grow existing customers, not only attract new ones. Market: set of actual and potential buyers.

Model

Value and satisfaction: setting expectations

Marketers must set the right level of expectations: neither too high (disappointment) nor too low (no attraction). "What you order vs what you receive".

Expectations \ experience Poor experience Great experience
High expectations disappointed devoted
Low expectations disaffected delighted

Perceived quality is poor when expectations > perceptions, good when expectations < perceptions (gap model).

Strategic planning hierarchy

Mission → objectives → business portfolio → functional/product/market strategy → marketing strategy. The mission should be market-oriented (products and technologies become obsolete, customer needs remain). Objectives are cascaded to every manager; e.g. strategic objective "invest in advertising, strategic partnerships", marketing strategy "improve the perceived quality of McDonald's Italy". Portfolio: see BCG and Ansoff.

Customer-driven marketing strategy

Two key questions: who are our customers? (segmentation and targeting) and how can we serve them best? (differentiation and positioning, the value proposition — the set of benefits and values promised; Value Proposition Canvas).

Selling concept: factory → existing products → selling and promoting → profit through sales volume. Marketing concept: market → customer needs → integrated marketing → profit through customer satisfaction.

Capturing value

  • Marketing mix: tools implementing the strategy (4P, marketing mix); integrated marketing programme combines them.
  • CRM: creating, maintaining and growing profitable relationships by offering superior value and satisfaction.
  • Customer lifetime value: value of the whole stream of purchases of a customer loyal for life. Share of customer: share of the customer's purchases in the category won by the firm. Customer equity: total lifetime value of all the firm's customers.
  • Customer journey: awareness → consideration → conversion → loyalty → advocacy.

Worked example

Customer spends EUR 40 a week in supermarkets, 50% with our chain (share of customer), for 20 years: lifetime revenue . Raising the share to 60% adds EUR 4,160 without acquiring a single new customer.

Pitfall

A need is not created by marketing; wants and demand are shaped. Selling ≠ marketing: selling starts from the factory, marketing from the market.
