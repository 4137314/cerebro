New product development and Stage-Gate

New product development is a process from concept to final design (Stage-Gate) defining concept, package and process; launches fail for predictable reasons, and modularity, commonality and concurrent engineering reduce cost and time.

Definition

Design covers products and services and processes, which overlap. Cycle: customer expectations → marketing interprets them → design turns them into specifications → operations make the product → customer. Three key aspects:

Aspect Meaning
Concept set of benefits the customer expects — the customer's view,
  not the physical description
Package interconnected products and services delivering the concept;
  core vs supporting components
Process how package components are linked and produced

Model

Why launches fail (HBR 2011)

Problem Lesson
1. cannot sustain rapid growth plan for fast scale-up
2. product below expectations (Mosquito delay launch until really ready
Magnet)  
3. "product limbo": differences not clear test that differences convince
4. new category needs education, not given make use understandable at once
5. revolutionary but no market ask "who buys it and at what price?"

Cases of design failure: Samsung Galaxy Note 7 (battery: wrong dimensions and tolerances, faulty welding process) and Boeing 737 Max.

Stage-Gate design phases

  1. Concept generation — ideas from internal sources (R&D, staff in contact with customers, analysis of needs) and external (customers, competitors' actions, market research, marketing). Idea → concept through functions, form, benefits, purpose.
  2. Screening — feasibility, acceptability, vulnerability, judged by marketing, production and finance.
  3. Preliminary design — define package (products/services) and processes: bill of materials (structure and quantities; basis for value analysis and FMEA; e.g. HVAC system → heating, ventilation and cooling, emergency vents…), process tools: flow charts, routing sheets, flow process charts, customer-processing framework.
  4. Evaluation and improvement — QFD, FMEA, value analysis.
  5. Prototyping and final design — virtual prototyping, CAD.

Concept is fixed in 1–2, package in 3, process in 3–5; each gate is a go/kill decision.

Preliminary-design levers

Standardization, commonality, modularization, mass customization. Modularity: Fender vs Gibson guitars (bolt-on vs set neck). VW MQB platform: 200+ models; shared modular platform, engine and production programmes; VW, Skoda and SEAT models built on the same line without long changeovers — lower cost, fewer parts, faster regional variants.

Concurrent engineering

Sequential stages wait for each other; simultaneous/concurrent engineering overlaps them with early information exchange → shorter time to market, fewer late changes.

Example

A connected thermostat: concept "comfort with lower bills"; package = device + app + installation; process = assembly line + cloud service operations. A shared electronics module across three models is commonality; offering interchangeable faceplates is mass customization.

Pitfall

The concept is benefits, not specs. Screening uses three criteria, not only market appeal. Overlapping stages needs communication, otherwise rework grows.
