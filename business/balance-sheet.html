The balance sheet and the accounting equation

The balance sheet lists assets (fixed and current) against liabilities and equity; every transaction keeps assets equal to liabilities plus equity, as the Inventata sas walk-through shows.

Model

Assets (attivo) Content
A Receivables from owners capital subscribed, not yet paid in
B Fixed assets (net) investments serving several years:
1 intangible research, development, patents, licences
2 tangible land, buildings, equipment
3 financial shareholdings, long-term receivables, bonds
C Current assets return to cash within 12 months:
1 inventories raw materials, semi-finished, finished
2 receivables customers, group firms, tax authority
3 short-term investments securities not held long term
4 cash and equivalents bank and postal deposits, cheques, cash
D Accruals and deferrals ratei e risconti
Liabilities and equity Content
A Equity 1 share capital; 2 reserves; 3 retained
  earnings; 4 profit (loss) for the year
B Provisions future risks and charges, adjusted yearly
C Employee severance (TFR) owed to employees on leaving
D Payables bonds, shareholder loans, banks, other
  lenders, customer advances, suppliers
  (incl. invoices to receive), bills, tax,
  social security, other
E Accruals and deferrals  

Notes: cash + current accounts = immediate liquidity; short-term securities (e.g. Italian BOT); the bad-debt allowance (fondo svalutazione crediti) reduces receivables by the amount not expected to be collected. Equity is the ideal "debt" of the company towards its owners; share capital is permanent funding.

Worked example

Inventata sas (all EUR):

Date Event Assets after the event Total
03.01 founded with capital 20,000 cash 20,000 20,000
16.01 bank loan 5,000 cash 25,000 25,000
23.01 used vehicle 10,000 cash cash 15,000, fixed 10,000 25,000
28.01 materials 2,000 cash cash 13,000, stock 2,000 25,000
03.02 materials 2,000, pay in 30 days stock 4,000; payable 2,000 27,000
05.02 sale 500 (cost 300), on credit receivable 500, stock 3,700 27,200
10.02 building 10,000 cash cash 3,000, fixed 20,000 27,200
15.02 sale 1,000 (cost 600), cash cash 4,000, stock 3,100 27,600
03.03 pays the supplier 2,000 cash 2,000 (payable 0) 25,600

Final position: receivables 500, cash 2,000, inventory 3,100, fixed assets 20,000 = 25,600 = loan 5,000 + capital 20,000 + profit 600. Every event changes two items; the totals always balance. A sale changes equity through profit (revenue − cost of goods); paying a supplier only shrinks both sides.

Pitfall

Equity is not cash in the bank. Paying a payable is not an expense. Buying a fixed asset is not a cost of the year (only its depreciation is).
