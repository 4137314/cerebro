Kraljic and the supplier portfolio

Kraljic (1983) classifies purchase categories by strategic importance and difficulty of managing the supply situation into non-critical, leverage, bottleneck and strategic items, each with its own sourcing policy; Olsen and Ellram add relationship analysis and action plans.

Definition

An undifferentiated approach (one static sourcing strategy for everything) is wrong: items differ in importance and may need ad hoc specifications and suppliers; strategy must also be dynamic. A differentiated approach analyses all purchased items to choose the ideal relationship → segmentation of the supplier portfolio. A portfolio model simplifies a complex problem by (1) classifying variables and objects in a matrix and (2) giving strategic recommendations per cell (Gelderman and Van Weele 2000). Kraljic's unit is the purchase category.

  • Leverage: high spend, standard quality, many suppliers → the buyer can be aggressive, but mutual respect keeps the relation healthy.
  • Non-critical: periodic replenishment agreements ("vuoto per pieno": replace empties with full), catalogues, e-procurement.
  • Bottleneck: not strategic but hard; standardize, simplify with value analysis, involve the supplier in design or preferential deals.
  • Strategic: close relations, supplier involved in product development.

Olsen and Ellram (1997)

Three-step normative model: (1) analyse purchases (Kraljic); (2) analyse existing supplier relationships (relative attractiveness, strength of the relation); (3) develop action plans. The desired relationship may differ from the one actually available.

Worked example

Car maker: steel coils (high spend, many mills) → leverage: tender, dual sourcing. Office stationery → non-critical: one catalogue, e-ordering. Proprietary sensor from one supplier, low value → bottleneck: buffer stock, redesign to a standard sensor. Battery cells → strategic: long-term partnership, joint development, single or dual source.

Pitfall

The unit is the purchase category, not the supplier label. High spend does not mean high supply risk. Positions change with technology, demand and supply markets: reassess.
