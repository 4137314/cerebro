Organizations, resources and performance

An organization deliberately divides and coordinates work to reach objectives; effectiveness compares actual with expected results, efficiency compares outputs with inputs.

Definition

Tonchia (2015) defines an organization (organizzazione) as a complex social entity, deliberately designed, structured and coordinated, guided by objectives, made of people who divide work to make it more productive, according to a hierarchy, norms and precise roles, in relation with the external environment.

Organizing means four concrete tasks:

  1. define the activities to be carried out;
  2. create organizational units for them, placed on different levels;
  3. coordinate all the activities of the firm;
  4. regulate tasks, responsibilities and powers of each unit (organo).

Two dimensions of the division of labour

  • Vertical (hierarchy): assigns decision power and authority to units and defines hierarchical relations. Line units command; staff units have no formal hierarchical power, sit beside the line and give specialist advice (legal office, planning, quality assurance).

  • Horizontal (specialization): defines tasks and areas, grouping them

    • by input (function/technique) → functional (plurifunzionale) structure;
    • by output (product family, region, customer, channel) → multidivisional;
    • by input and output together → matrix.

    See the structure models.

The three organizational variables

Organizational structure, operating systems (planning, control, reward, information systems) and leadership style (stile di comando). The structure defines the units, the functions assigned to them and the relations of dependence and collaboration among them; it is drawn as an organization chart (organigramma), and is centralized or decentralized according to how much decision power and autonomy are delegated down the hierarchy.

Effectiveness and efficiency

Organizational choices aim at maximum effectiveness compatible with a high degree of efficiency: doing the right things, and doing them without waste.

Performance

Both depend on three sources:

Source Content Controllable?
Core (caratteristica) typical operating activities yes
Non-core (extra-caratt.) financial activities to raise capital partly
Context (congiuntura) institutional and market factors no

Management acts through operating levers — technological (production and IT), organizational (people, structure) and managerial (TQM, Lean, project management). Levers move operating performance:

  • internal, invisible to the customer: cost (costs, productivity) and non-cost (quality, time, flexibility);
  • external, visible to the customer: product/service performance and satisfaction.

Internal non-cost performance drives external performance, hence revenue and profit; cost performance drives profitability directly (see Picture).

Firm, enterprise, company and environment

  • Azienda: the material side, the deployment of resources;
  • Impresa: the entrepreneurial activity;
  • Società: the legal form that makes the two concrete (governance).

The firm operates in an environment (context), exchanges resources in four markets — supply (approvvigionamento), capital, labour, sales — and competes inside industries (settori), analysed with Porter's five forces.

Worked example

Planned output 1,000 units, actual 900 → effectiveness . Revenue EUR 450,000 for costs EUR 400,000 → efficiency : every euro of input returns EUR 1.125. Profit EUR 50,000 on an investment of EUR 500,000 → profitability . A help desk solves 80 requests in 40 hours (2/h); after a change, 100 in 40 hours (2.5/h) but 30 are reopened: efficiency rises, effectiveness (first-time resolution 70%) falls. Always pair one measure of each kind.

Pitfall

Efficiency is not effectiveness: a cheap process that misses its objective is ineffective. Profit (absolute) is not profitability (relative to investment), and neither guarantees liquidity.

Recall

Define organization (Tonchia), list the four organizing tasks, write both ratios, and explain the chain lever → internal non-cost performance → external performance → revenue → profit.
