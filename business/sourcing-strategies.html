Single, sole, dual and multiple sourcing

Single sourcing uses one replaceable supplier, sole sourcing one monopolist, dual sourcing two parallel sources and multi-sourcing many; the trend is fewer suppliers and closer relations, supported by TQM and JIT.

Trend: reduce the number of suppliers to build stronger, cooperative relations, supported by Japanese models — TQM (purchase quality drives product quality: close collaboration, selection and training of suppliers) and JIT (levelled production, synchronization, elimination of stocks; see JIT). The global microchip shortage (2021–2022) showed the risk of thin, concentrated supply bases: dual sourcing and buffers regained value.

Example

Tyres for a car model: dual sourcing (two brands qualified) keeps price pressure and continuity. An ECU from a company holding the only certified design: sole sourcing. Office supplies: multi sourcing or a single catalogue supplier. See transaction costs.

Pitfall

Single ≠ sole: single is a choice among alternatives; sole is a constraint (monopoly). Fewer suppliers raise dependence: pair it with risk management.

Recall
