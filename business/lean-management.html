Lean, waste and value-stream mapping

Lean (Toyota Production System) pursues zero waste, zero defects and low inventory through respect for people; seven wastes are attacked with value-stream mapping, which classifies activities as VA, NVA or BNVA and measures process efficiency.

Definition

Lean philosophy: waste is undesirable (zero waste); high quality is essential (zero defects); JIT, pull rather than push (low inventory); every difficulty is an opportunity to improve. The Toyota Production System (Taiichi Ohno) is the benchmark, based on (1) elimination of waste — muda, "a horse not carrying a load" — and (2) respect for people. Waste (Shoichiro Toyoda): "anything other than the minimum amount of equipment, materials, parts, space and workers' time absolutely essential to add value to the product".

Model

Respect for people

Workforce levelling (constant headcount, no layoffs); cooperative unions; bottom-up management; workers trained to improve continuously (kaizen, change for the better) and reach root causes; workers can stop the line for quality (jidoka, andon cord); quality circles.

The seven wastes

Group Waste Examples
Things Over-production oversized production lots
  Transport WIP moving between departments or sites
  Inventory parts waiting to be processed, finished
    goods waiting to be used
People Waiting operators waiting for parts; patients
    waiting for test results
  Motion staff walking long distances; customers
    moving between offices
  Over-processing unnecessary steps, red tape, needless tests
Quality Defects rework, recalls, hospital infections

Value-stream mapping (VSM)

Flow chart of the process with each activity labelled:

Type Meaning Example
VA customer is willing to pay for it surgery; fitting a wheel
NVA customer won't pay and firm doesn't need it nurse walking 50 m for
    materials; picking stock
BNVA not valued but required (legal, accounting) financial statements;
    reports to regulators

Add changeover time, processing time per unit (cycle time), queue time, estimated cost. Goal: remove or reduce NVA. Real case: the current-state VSM of the V8 engine block at Ford's Bridgend engine plant (supplier Kolbenschmidt, rail and truck deliveries, days of inventory between steps).

Worked example

Activity Type Min
Transport parts to inspection area NVA 5
Inspect parts NVA 3
Move parts to storage shelves NVA 10
Take parts from shelf, put in kit NVA 15
Transport parts to assembly technician NVA 5
Assemble parts VA 13
Inspect assembly NVA 3

(The course rounds the total to 56 min → 23%; summing the listed times gives 54.) Delivering parts straight to the line in kits removes storage and two moves: total 29 min, efficiency 45%.

Pitfall

Inspection is usually NVA (or BNVA), not value-adding. Lean is not only cost-cutting: respect for people is half of TPS.
