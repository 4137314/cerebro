Funding structure and fixed-asset coverage

The primary structure ratio (equity / fixed assets) measures self-coverage of long-term investments; the solidity ratio (liabilities / equity) measures dependence on external funding.

IS has liabilities in the numerator: higher = more dependence on third parties (worse). It counts all liabilities, operating payables included, not only financial debt. Why it matters: a mismatch between cash in and out could force the firm to sell investments or fixed assets to raise cash; excess liquidity could instead be invested at better returns than a current account.

Worked example

ALFA 2012: PN 646,086; AI 232,458; Pcor 1,363,449; Pcon 472,746.

The increase in IS comes mainly from bank debt, which the banks granted after their own solvency checks — combine with MT and CCN and the notes. Generic case: PN 60, AI 100, Pcor 40, Pcon 50 → ISP 0.6 (monitor), secondary coverage 1.1, IS 1.5 (monitor); total assets 150, current assets 50, CCN 10.

Pitfall

Names vary ("solidity", "autonomy", "leverage"): always write the formula. Thresholds are indicative, not guarantees; read trends, the sector and debt maturities. Negative or tiny equity makes IS meaningless.

Recall

Write ISP and IS with thresholds; compute them for ALFA; explain why financing a machine with short-term debt is risky even if profitable.
