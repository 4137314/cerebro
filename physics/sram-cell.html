The 6T SRAM cell

Two cross-coupled inverters store a bit; two NMOS access transistors connect the nodes to bit lines when the word line is high; reading must not flip the cell (pull-down stronger than access: cell ratio –2) while writing must overpower the pull-up (access stronger than pull-up: ).

Operation

  • Hold: WL = 0, the latch keeps the data (static: no refresh) with only leakage power.
  • Read: BL and BL̄ precharged to ; WL = 1; the side storing 0 discharges its bit line through access + pull-down. The node storing 0 rises to ; it must stay below the trip point of the other inverter. A sense amplifier detects ~100 mV of bit-line difference.
  • Write: drive BL = 0 on the side storing 1; the access transistor must pull that node below the trip point against the PMOS pull-up.

Engineering applications

Caches and register files in every processor (most of the transistors of a modern CPU), FPGA configuration memory, SRAM-based compute-in-memory; leakage and stability at low limit voltage scaling.

Pitfall

Read and write requirements conflict (strong access helps write, hurts read); variability (random mismatch) makes the worst cell among billions decide the design.
