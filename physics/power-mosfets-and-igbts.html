Power MOSFETs and IGBTs

A vertical MOSFET adds a drift region whose resistance dominates at high voltage: (unipolar limit); superjunctions break this limit by charge compensation, and the IGBT injects holes from a p collector to modulate the drift region, giving low conduction loss at high voltage at the price of a turn-off tail current.

IGBT

MOS gate controls an electron current that drives a wide-base pnp: holes injected from the collector flood the drift region (like a pin diode): V + small drift drop, independent of to first order (Baliga et al. 1982). Turn-off: the MOS channel stops instantly but stored holes recombine → tail current, grows with stored charge. Parasitic thyristor (pnpn) latch-up must be avoided by layout (p body under the emitter).

Worked example

Si, V, cm²/Vs, V/cm: mΩ cm² (drift only). For 20 A at 1 V drop (20 W): area cm² — impractical; an IGBT does it in ~0.2 cm² with V, a superjunction MOSFET in ~0.3 cm².

Engineering applications

Low-voltage MOSFETs in CPU VRMs and synchronous rectifiers, superjunction MOSFETs in 400 V PFC stages, IGBT modules in traction, wind and industrial drives.

Pitfall

of Si MOSFETs roughly doubles from 25 °C to 150 °C (mobility drop): design conduction loss at hot temperature. IGBTs have a knee voltage (~0.7 V): poor at light load.
