The effective index method

A 2D-confined waveguide is reduced to two 1D slab problems: solve the vertical slab to get inside and outside the ridge, then solve a horizontal slab with those indices (opposite polarisation) to get the mode index ; accurate when the field is well confined, and the starting point for numerical mode solvers.

Method

  1. Divide the cross-section into vertical regions (ridge, sides).
  2. For each region solve a vertical slab (slab waveguide) for the chosen polarisation (TE: horizontal → solve the vertical slab as TE) (Marcatili 1969).
  3. Build a horizontal slab with core index and cladding (for a strip guide the sides are just cladding).
  4. Solve it with the other polarisation (TM-like boundary conditions for the TE mode) to get .
  5. Group index by repeating at two wavelengths.

Justification

Separation of variables inserted in the scalar Helmholtz equation; the variational principle (variational method) shows the estimate is stationary, so errors are second order in the field error. It overestimates for small, high-contrast guides; full-vector solvers (finite differences, FEM) are needed for design.

Worked example

SOI strip 500×220 nm, nm, TE. Vertical slab Si(220 nm) in SiO: TE . Horizontal slab of width 500 nm, core 2.85, cladding 1.44, TM boundary conditions: (full-vector solvers give ≈ 2.44). Group index ≈ 4.2 because of strong waveguide dispersion.

Engineering applications

Quick design of ridge lasers, rib modulators, AWG slab regions, MMI couplers; sanity checks of numerical solvers; designing single-mode width limits.

Pitfall

EIM fails near cutoff and for modes with strong hybrid (TE/TM mixed) character, e.g. in slot waveguides or at sharp bends.
