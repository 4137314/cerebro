Thyristors

A pnpn structure behaves as two coupled transistors; once triggered, positive feedback () latches it on with very low voltage drop at huge currents, and it turns off only when the current falls below the holding current (line commutation) — or, in GTOs/IGCTs, by extracting gate charge.

Two-transistor model (derivation)

pnp () and npn () share the middle layers: . The 's rise with current; when the anode current is limited only by the external circuit: the device latches (Moll et al. 1956). Triggers: gate current, (displacement current through ), overvoltage (breakover), light (LTT).

Characteristics

Forward blocking and reverse blocking (symmetric); on-state –2 V at kA; latching current to stay on after the gate pulse, holding current below which it turns off. Limits: at turn-on (current crowding near the gate), in blocking.

Phase control

In an AC circuit, firing at angle after the zero crossing controls the average rectified voltage: (full-wave controlled bridge, continuous current). Turn-off occurs naturally at current zero.

Engineering applications

HVDC converter stations, static VAR compensators, soft starters, light dimmers (TRIAC), crowbar protection, pulsed power.

Pitfall

Without proper snubbers a fast turns a thyristor on unintentionally; and once on, it cannot be turned off by the gate (except GTO/IGCT).
