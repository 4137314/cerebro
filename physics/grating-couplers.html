Grating couplers

A shallow grating diffracts the guided mode out of plane when (first order); efficiency = directionality × overlap of the emitted profile with the fibre's Gaussian mode; typical SOI couplers reach −2 to −5 dB with ~30–40 nm 1 dB bandwidth, whereas edge couplers with inverse tapers are broadband and lower loss.

Model

Phase matching along the grating: (grating equation) (Dakss et al. 1970). Coupling efficiency : directionality (fraction going up rather than into the substrate, improved by BOX thickness choice or bottom mirrors), overlap (exponential decay profile vs Gaussian: max ≈ 80%; apodisation shapes it Gaussian). Fibres are tilted 8–12° to avoid second-order back-reflection.

Worked example

SOI, in the partially etched region (≈ 2.7 average), nm, in air: nm. Bandwidth: and set ≈ 35 nm (1 dB) — a fibre-angle error of 1° shifts the peak by ≈ 15 nm.

Engineering applications

Wafer-scale optical testing (no dicing), fibre arrays to transceiver chips, optical phased arrays for LiDAR (gratings as antennas, array factor), free-space coupling to atoms and ions in quantum chips.

Pitfall

Grating couplers are polarisation selective (TE design rejects TM) and narrowband; edge couplers are preferred for broadband or polarisation-diverse products.
