Eddy currents and skin effect

A conductor in a time-varying field carries induced currents that oppose the field: losses in a lamination of thickness (hence laminated cores) and current crowding near the surface within the skin depth (see plane waves in conductors).

Pitfall

The emf depends on the surface's flux, but the voltage read by a voltmeter in a time-varying field depends on the path of the leads: Kirchhoff's voltage law is only an approximation when flux links the circuit.
