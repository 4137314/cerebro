Diode circuits and ESD protection

Diode circuits are solved by guessing on/off states with a model (ideal, constant drop V, piecewise linear with ) or by iterating the Shockley equation; CMOS inputs are protected against electrostatic discharge by diodes to and ground that clamp the gate within one diode drop of the rails.

Method

  2. Solve the linear circuit.
  3. Check consistency; if violated, flip the assumption.

For accuracy use the Shockley law with a load line , iterating (Shockley 1949).

ESD protection

A human body charged to ~2 kV (Human Body Model: 100 pF through 1.5 kΩ, peak ≈ 1.3 A, ~150 ns) would rupture a 2 nm gate oxide (breakdown ≈ 2–3 V). Input pads have diodes D1 (pad → ) and D2 (ground → pad), plus a series resistor and rail clamp: the pad voltage is held within and the discharge current flows into the supply network.

Engineering applications

Rectifiers and peak detectors, clamps and level shifters, reverse-polarity protection, ESD networks on every IC pad, TVS diodes on connectors (USB, Ethernet).

Pitfall

The constant-drop model hides the temperature coefficient (−2 mV/K) and the logarithmic dependence on current; at µA currents a Si diode drops only ≈ 0.4–0.5 V.
