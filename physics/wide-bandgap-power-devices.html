Wide-bandgap power devices: SiC and GaN

A larger gap gives a ~10× higher critical field: for the same the drift region is 10× thinner and 100× more doped, so falls by –; SiC MOSFETs dominate 650 V–3.3 kV, GaN HEMTs (2DEG channel) dominate fast switching below 650 V; low intrinsic density allows operation above 200 °C.

Why (derivation)

From the unipolar limit : enters cubed (Baliga 1982). 8× higher → 512× lower drift resistance (mobility and permittivity similar). Higher also reduces by many orders (intrinsic density), so the extrinsic range extends to high temperature.

Device specifics

  • SiC MOSFET: gate-oxide interface traps lower channel mobility (channel resistance matters at 650 V), threshold drift; body diode with high (~3 V) but negligible recovery.
  • GaN HEMT: AlGaN/GaN polarisation creates a 2DEG ( cm) — normally-on; normally-off by p-GaN gate or cascode; lateral device on Si substrates; no body diode (reverse conduction through the channel); issues: dynamic (trapping), gate robustness.

Worked example

1200 V drift region: Si needs ~100 µm at cm (power diodes); SiC with MV/cm: µm, cm: mΩ cm² vs ≈ 260 mΩ cm² for Si with the same formula.

Engineering applications

EV traction inverters (SiC, 800 V batteries), on-board chargers, solar inverters, compact USB-C chargers and data-centre PSUs (GaN at MHz), RF power amplifiers (GaN HEMT for 5G and radar).

Pitfall

Faster switching ( V/ns) moves the problem to the package and layout: parasitic inductance causes ringing and EMI; device gains are lost without low-inductance design.
