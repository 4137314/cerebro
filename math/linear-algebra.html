Linear algebra

Linear algebra is the one algebraic theory that is finished: over a field, every module is free, every short exact sequence splits, and a single integer classifies a space up to isomorphism. That is why it is the model the rest of algebra is measured against, and why so much of geometry and analysis reduces to it.

The structure theory

Vector space, then span, linear independence, bases and dimension – where the exchange lemma proves that a number can be attached to the space at all, and Zorn's lemma provides a basis when the space is infinite-dimensional. Linear maps, quotient spaces and the rank-nullity theorem is the first isomorphism theorem with a dimension count attached, and dual spaces, annihilators and the transpose is the dictionary between describing a subspace by generators and describing it by equations.

Operators

The determinant is defined by what it must satisfy – multilinear and alternating – so that its formula is a consequence and its identities are applications of uniqueness. It turns the search for eigenvectors into a question about roots: eigenvalues, the characteristic polynomial and Cayley-Hamilton, where diagonalisability becomes a statement about distinct roots and about the field containing them.

Geometry on a vector space

An extra structure, a bilinear form, and the classification questions begin again. Inner product space and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give lengths and angles; orthogonality, Gram-Schmidt and orthogonal decomposition give coordinates adapted to them, and the best approximation in a subspace. Bilinear forms, quadratic forms and Sylvester's law of inertia drop positivity and classify what remains by signature; the spectral theorem keeps the inner product fixed and diagonalises a symmetric operator by an orthonormal basis. The pair of them is the difference between classifying a quadratic form and finding the principal axes of an ellipsoid.

Two notes filed here are already geometry: affine spaces and conics and quadrics.

What is missing

The Jordan normal form and the theory of a single operator over a non-algebraically-closed field; the minimal polynomial as opposed to the characteristic one; tensor and exterior algebra, which is what the determinant is really about; singular values; and multilinear algebra in general.
