A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection whose inverse is continuous; two spaces are homeomorphic if one exists between them. A property of spaces preserved by homeomorphism is a topological invariant.

A continuous bijection is a homeomorphism if and only if it is open (images of open sets are open), if and only if it is closed (images of closed sets are closed). Being homeomorphic is an equivalence relation on any set of spaces.

For a bijection, the image under of a set equals its preimage under ; so continuity of is exactly the statement that maps open sets to open sets, and by complementation that it maps closed sets to closed sets. The identity is a homeomorphism, the inverse of one is one, and a composition of two is one by the previous proposition – reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity.

A continuous bijection need not be a homeomorphism, and the failure is not pathological: the identity from a set with the discrete topology to the same set with any coarser one is continuous and bijective in one direction only. Compactness plus the Hausdorff property is the standard hypothesis that rules this out, which is why so many theorems end "…hence a homeomorphism" after checking those two.
