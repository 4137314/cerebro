Computability, the halting problem and Rice's theorem

The undecidability results do not depend on which machine model is chosen; they depend on two properties any reasonable model has – a program can be simulated by another program, and a program with a parameter frozen is again a program. Taking those two properties as the definition isolates what the diagonal argument actually uses, and makes the theorems below independent of any coding.

Rice's theorem says every non-trivial property of the function computed is undecidable: whether a program halts on all inputs, whether it computes the constant zero, whether two programs agree. Properties of the text of the program – its length, whether it contains a loop – are of course decidable, and the distinction between the two is the whole content of the statement.
