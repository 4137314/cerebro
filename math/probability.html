Probability

Probability is measure theory with a normalisation and two extra ideas. The normalisation – total mass one – buys the finiteness that makes every measure continuous from above and every bounded function integrable. The two ideas are independence and conditioning, and neither is measure-theoretic: they are hypotheses about how information is distributed, and every theorem below is one of them applied to a sequence.

Setting up

Probability spaces, conditioning and independence and random variables, laws and distribution functions. The move that defines the subject's style is in the second: the underlying space is never described again, and everything is said about the measure a random variable pushes onto the line.

Expectation, the transfer theorem and Jensen's inequality makes that precise – an expectation is an integral on against the law – and variance, covariance and the Markov and Chebyshev inequalities supplies the one bound the rest of the subject spends.

The limit theorems

Three notes, in increasing strength, all about the same sum:

  1. Modes of convergence and the Borel-Cantelli lemmas – four inequivalent notions of convergence, and the lemma that upgrades a summable bound on probabilities into a statement about almost every outcome.
  2. The weak and strong laws of large numbers – the average converges to the mean, in probability by one application of Chebyshev, almost surely by Borel-Cantelli.
  3. The central limit theorem – the error of that convergence, rescaled, has a limit that forgets everything except the first two moments. It is proved through characteristic functions, which turn convolution into multiplication.

Conditioning done properly

Conditional expectation as an orthogonal projection is where the subject needs Hilbert space theory: conditioning on an event of probability zero has no elementary meaning, and the construction that works is the projection onto the square-integrable variables measurable with respect to the given information. The completeness of is what makes that projection exist.

What is assumed and not proved

Two theorems are used and marked assumed: the uniqueness of a law given its characteristic function, and Levy's continuity theorem. Both need Fubini's theorem, which this archive does not have. That is the only debt in the area, and cerebro results open reports it.

What is missing

Statistics is the applied half and has its own hub. Absent from the theory: product measures and Fubini, hence any construction of an infinite sequence of independent variables; conditional expectation for merely integrable variables, which needs Radon-Nikodym; martingales and their convergence theorem; stochastic processes of any kind, Brownian motion in particular; and large deviations, of which only the elementary Markov bound is present.
