Set theory

Two thirds of this subject is not foundational at all but a toolbox: the quotient construction, the maximality principle and the induction that runs past are used constantly elsewhere, and are collected here because they are about sets and nothing else. The remaining third – cardinality – is where sets become objects of study rather than instruments.

The constructions everything else uses

Relations, equivalence and the quotient of a set is the single most reused note in the archive: every later quotient – of a group, a ring, a vector space, a topological space, a metric space – is this construction plus a check that the structure is constant on classes. Maps, invertibility and the canonical factorisation is the same statement for functions, and the isomorphism theorems of algebra are it with structure added.

Order

Partial orders, lattices and the Knaster-Tarski theorem shows how much of the theory of the supremum survives without an order that is total or complete – enough to produce fixed points, with no analysis anywhere. Well-ordered sets and transfinite induction and ordinals and definition by transfinite recursion separate two things usually run together: proving along a well order, and building along one.

Choice

The axiom of choice and Zorn's lemma is the export product of the subject. It is used in this archive to produce a basis of an arbitrary vector space, a maximal ideal, a Hahn-Banach extension, a maximal consistent set of formulas and an ultrafilter; every one of those objects exists and none can be exhibited. Filters, ultrafilters and the ultrafilter lemma package the last of these, and Boolean algebras and Stone's representation theorem show what they are: the points of a space built out of an algebra of propositions.

Size

Cardinality: Cantor's theorem and Cantor-Schroder-Bernstein makes comparison by injections behave like a notion of size – antisymmetric, and unbounded above. The two proofs are worth contrasting: the diagonal argument is three lines and completely explicit, the Schroder-Bernstein argument is a fixed point of a monotone operator on a power set.

What is missing

The axioms themselves – there is no note stating ZF, so "set" is used here as an undefined term with properties assumed as needed. Also absent: cardinal arithmetic, Hartogs' theorem, the continuum hypothesis and independence, and the cumulative hierarchy.
