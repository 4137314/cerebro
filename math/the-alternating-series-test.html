The alternating series test

Every test so far decides convergence by comparing absolute values, and so can only ever prove absolute convergence. This is the one test in a first course that uses cancellation, and it is therefore the source of every example of a series that converges but not absolutely.

Conditional convergence is fragile in a way absolute convergence is not: a conditionally convergent series can be rearranged to converge to any prescribed real number, or to diverge. That is why every later theorem that manipulates the order of summation assumes absolute convergence, and why the distinction is worth a definition rather than a remark.
