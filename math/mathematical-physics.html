Mathematical physics

Classical mechanics is in this archive as geometry, not as physics: the notes below contain no experiments and no units, and what they study is a structure – first a variational principle on a space of paths, then a symplectic form on the space of states – that turns out to determine the motion. The physical content is the choice of Lagrangian; everything after it is a theorem.

The chain, in order

  1. The action functional and the first variation – motion is a stationary point of a functional on paths, which is a statement about the whole trajectory rather than about an instant.
  2. The Euler-Lagrange equations – stationarity, made local. This is the point at which a variational principle becomes a differential equation.
  3. The Legendre transform – the change of variables from velocities to momenta, and the reason it is invertible is convexity, not physics.
  4. Hamilton's equations – the same motion as a first-order flow on phase space, symmetric in the two halves of the coordinates.
  5. Poisson brackets – the algebraic structure that flow induces on observables, under which the equations of motion read .
  6. Canonical transformations and Liouville's theorem – the changes of coordinates that preserve the structure, and the volume they preserve.

Mechanics of constrained and extended systems

The chain above starts from a Lagrangian and does not say where one comes from. Constraints, virtual work and the Lagrange equations supplies the derivation from Newton's law, with the one modelling hypothesis – ideal constraints do no virtual work – marked as an axiom. After it:

  • Central forces, the two-body problem and Kepler's laws – the one system solved completely, and solved by symmetry rather than by integration.
  • Equilibria, stability and the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem – a strict minimum of the potential is stable, because energy traps the motion.
  • Small oscillations and normal modes – near such an equilibrium the system is a set of independent oscillators, by simultaneous diagonalisation of two quadratic forms.
  • Rigid body kinematics and the inertia tensor and Euler's equations – the configuration space is a group, the kinetic energy a quadratic form, and the stability of free rotation is decided by which combination of the two conserved quantities is definite.

Quantum mechanics

The same structure with the algebra of observables changed: the Hilbert space formalism – states as unit vectors, observables as symmetric operators, the Poisson bracket replaced by a commutator, and the uncertainty relation as a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality – and the harmonic oscillator by ladder operators, whose spectrum follows from one commutation relation with no differential equation solved.

Symmetry and conservation

Noether's theorem is the reason this chain is worth building rather than solving the equations directly: a one-parameter symmetry of the action yields a conserved quantity, so translation invariance is conservation of momentum. In the Hamiltonian formulation the statement becomes an identity about the Poisson bracket – an observable is conserved exactly when it commutes with the Hamiltonian – which is the form that survives into quantum mechanics with the bracket replaced by a commutator.

What is missing

Symplectic geometry as geometry – there is no manifold here, only coordinates, so Liouville's theorem is stated as a computation rather than as a statement about a form; integrable systems and action-angle variables; the spectral theory that quantum mechanics needs beyond the formalism – self-adjointness as opposed to symmetry, and the spectral theorem for unbounded operators; scattering; statistical mechanics; and relativity.

Continuum mechanics is absent as a subject, but the equations it produces are not: they are the partial differential equations of the analysis area, and the divergence theorem is what turns a local conservation law into a global one.
