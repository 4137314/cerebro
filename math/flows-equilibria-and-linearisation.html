Flows, equilibria and linearisation

An autonomous differential equation is better read as a flow: a family of maps moving the whole space, rather than one solution at a time. The questions then become qualitative – where are the fixed points, what happens near them, what happens for all time – and the first answer is that near a hyperbolic equilibrium the nonlinear system behaves like its linearisation.

Linearisation decides the hyperbolic cases and only those: with an eigenvalue on the imaginary axis the linear system is neutral and the nonlinear terms take over, which is why a conservative mechanical system – whose linearisation at a stable equilibrium has purely imaginary eigenvalues – needs Lagrange-Dirichlet rather than this theorem. The sharp statement in the hyperbolic case is the Hartman-Grobman theorem: the flow is locally conjugate to its linearisation by a homeomorphism.
