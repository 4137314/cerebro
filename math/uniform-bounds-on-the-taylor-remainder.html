Taylor's theorem gives the remainder at an unproved point , which is not a number anyone can produce. What turns it into an estimate is a bound on the derivatives, and if that bound is uniform in the order the Taylor polynomials converge to the function outright. Stated for a hypothesis on the derivatives rather than for any particular function: the sine and the exponential are the cases one meets, and neither is the content.

The hypothesis is what does the work, and it is strong: one bound for all derivatives. Without it a function can be infinitely differentiable and have every Taylor coefficient at a point vanish while the function does not – so the polynomials converge, to something that is not the function. Taylor's theorem is a statement about approximation of finite order; convergence of the whole series is a separate hypothesis, never a consequence of smoothness alone.
