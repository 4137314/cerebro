Topology

Every convergence argument in the first analysis course used the distance only to say which sets are open. Topology is what remains after that observation is taken seriously: the open sets are kept, the distance is discarded, and the theorems that still hold are exactly the ones that never needed it.

mentioned in Mathematics, Foundations, Real analysis, General topology, Algebraic topology

Part of Mathematics.

The subjects

  • General topology – spaces, continuity, the three constructions, connectedness, compactness, separation, and metric spaces as the special case where sequences suffice.
  • Algebraic topology – invariants that are groups, so that a geometric impossibility becomes an algebraic one.

What the metric was doing, and what replaces it

the metric gave the topological replacement
- continuity preimages of open sets are open
sequential closure every neighbourhood meets the set
sequences nets, or filters
Bolzano-Weierstrass every open cover has a finite subcover
unique limits the Hausdorff axiom
continuous bump functions normality, via Urysohn's lemma

The right column is strictly weaker, and the point of the subject is what each weakening costs. The metric topology and the equivalence of the three compactness notions in a metric space are where the two columns are compared directly.

The two theorems that pay for the generality

Tychonoff's theorem – an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact – is false for the box topology, false for sequential compactness, and equivalent to the axiom of choice. Urysohn's lemma produces continuous real functions on a space that was given none, and Tietze's theorem extends them off a closed subspace. Neither has a metric-space analogue worth stating, because in a metric space both are one line.

What is missing

Homology, which is what replaces the fundamental group in dimensions above one; the classification of covering spaces and the Galois correspondence with subgroups of ; Seifert-van Kampen, which is how a fundamental group is actually computed; metrisation theorems; and manifolds as a topological subject – smooth manifolds are filed under geometry here, which is a choice and not a fact.
