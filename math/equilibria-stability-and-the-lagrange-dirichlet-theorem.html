Equilibria, stability and the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem

An equilibrium is a constant solution; the question is whether motions starting nearby stay nearby. For a conservative system the answer is read off the potential alone: a strict minimum is stable, because energy conservation traps the motion in a level set of the energy. That argument is the prototype of every Lyapunov function.

The converse of Lagrange-Dirichlet is false in general – an equilibrium can be stable at a non-strict minimum, and the question of whether a critical point that is not a minimum is always unstable was open for a century and is settled only under regularity hypotheses. What the proof does show is the mechanism: the conserved energy is a function positive away from the equilibrium and non-increasing along motions, and abstracting exactly that gives Lyapunov's method for systems with no energy at all.
