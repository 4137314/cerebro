Field theory

A field extension is a vector space, and that single observation makes the subject finite: questions about which numbers can be adjoined become questions about dimensions, and dimensions multiply along towers. The classical impossibility results are that multiplication and nothing else.

The three notes

Field extensions, the tower law and minimal polynomials is the foundation: the degree is multiplicative, an algebraic element generates an extension isomorphic to a quotient of a polynomial ring, and its degree is the degree of one irreducible polynomial.

Splitting fields and their uniqueness constructs, for any polynomial, a smallest field in which it factors completely, and – the substantial half – shows that field does not depend on the choices made. That uniqueness is what makes "the" splitting field, and hence the Galois group, a well-defined object.

Finite fields is the complete classification the two previous notes make possible: one field of each prime-power order, none of any other size, and a cyclic multiplicative group.

What this subject is waiting for

Every note here is a prerequisite of Galois theory and none of them is Galois theory. What is missing is the correspondence itself: separable and normal extensions, the automorphism group of a splitting field, and the theorem that its subgroups match the intermediate fields. With solvable groups already in place, the insolubility of the general quintic is two notes away – and the counting argument that begins it is sketched in the remark at the end of the splitting-field note.

Also absent: algebraic closures, whose existence needs a transfinite iteration of the splitting-field construction; transcendence degree; and the theory of separability in positive characteristic, of which only the Frobenius map appears.
