Mathematics

This is the root of the archive: one note per subject, each linking to the notes below it, down to the results themselves. It exists because the two navigations this repository already has answer different questions. cerebro note list and cerebro query by-tag say what is there; the results graph says what rests on what. Neither says what the subject is about, which is the only question a person actually asks before opening anything.

mentioned in Foundations, Algebra, Analysis, Topology, Geometry, Probability, Mathematical physics, Dynamical systems

So a hub note carries no mathematics. It states what its area is, which threads run through it, which results are load-bearing, and – as honestly as possible – what is missing. Nothing here may be depended on by a #+DEPENDS:, and nothing here is generated: a list that a script could produce belongs to the script.

The areas

  • Analysis – limits, and everything that is a limit in disguise: the real line, sequences and series, differentiation and integration, measure, and the geometry of infinite-dimensional spaces.
  • Algebra – sets with operations, studied through the maps that respect them: groups, rings, fields, vector spaces and modules.
  • Geometry – what survives a change of coordinates, once one has chosen which changes are allowed: affine, Euclidean, projective, convex and differential.
  • Topology – nearness with no distance: what continuity, connectedness and compactness mean when only the open sets remain.
  • Foundations – what the rest is written in: sets, order, choice, and the logic that makes "provable" a mathematical notion.
  • Probability – measure theory with total mass one, plus the two ideas that are not measure-theoretic at all: independence and conditioning. Its applied half is Statistics.
  • Mathematical physics – classical mechanics as geometry, from the variational principle to the symplectic structure and the rigid body, and the Hilbert space formalism of quantum mechanics.
  • Dynamical systems – a differential equation read as a flow, and the two ways of deciding where it goes without solving it.

How the areas are wired to each other

The division above is a shelving convention, not a claim about the mathematics, and the interesting notes are the ones that cross it. Three crossings carry most of the traffic here:

  • Order and choice into everything. Zorn's lemma is used to produce a basis of an arbitrary vector space, a maximal ideal of any ring, a functional extending one on a subspace and a maximal finitely satisfiable set of formulas – four theorems, one argument.
  • Completeness into fixed points. Completeness of a metric space gives the contraction principle, hence existence for differential equations; and it gives the Baire category theorem, hence the three theorems of Banach space theory.
  • Linear algebra into geometry and analysis. The spectral theorem is what diagonalises the shape operator of a surface, and Sylvester's law is what classifies the quadrics.

What this archive is not, yet

Named so that the gaps are visible rather than implied: there is no number theory, no combinatorics, no representation theory, no homological algebra, and no Galois theory – though splitting fields and solvable groups are both in place, so the two halves of it exist without the correspondence between them. Measure theory stops at the convergence theorems: Lebesgue measure itself is used and never constructed, and the absence of Fubini's theorem is what leaves two results in probability assumed rather than proved.

A hub is maintained by hand and will therefore go stale, which is the usual argument against writing one. It earns its place only by saying what cannot be derived – why a subject is one subject, and which of its results the others lean on. The moment a section here becomes a list of titles, the list belongs to cerebro note list and the section should be deleted.
