Foundations

The rest of the archive uses sets, functions, quotients and proofs without comment. This area is where those become objects of study rather than instruments: what a set can be, what a proof is, and what follows about the limits of both. It is also, in practice, where the tools live – Zorn's lemma and the ultrafilter lemma are used far more often here than they are discussed.

Part of Mathematics.

The subjects

  • Set theory – relations and quotients, order, choice, transfinite induction and recursion, cardinality, filters.
  • Logic – formulas as objects: satisfaction, derivation, the relation between the two, and what no derivation can decide.

The two theorems everything else uses

Zorn's lemma and the ultrafilter lemma are the export products of this area. Between them they produce a basis of any vector space, a maximal ideal of any ring, a Hahn-Banach extension, Tychonoff's theorem, the compactness theorem for first-order logic and the Stone representation of a Boolean algebra. Every one of those proofs produces an object that no construction reaches, and it is worth knowing which results in a subject are of that kind.

Syntax against semantics

The characteristic move of logic is to define two relations and then compare them: , which quantifies over all assignments, and , which asks for a finite object. Soundness is the easy direction and completeness the substantial one; compactness is what the finiteness of derivations forces, and it can also be proved semantically, by Zorn or by ultraproducts, which is the same argument seen from the other side. Then Lowenheim-Skolem says how little a first-order theory controls, and the halting problem says that some questions have no decision procedure at all.

What is missing

The axioms of set theory are used and never written down – there is no note stating ZF. Godel's completeness theorem for first-order logic is absent, its consequence (compactness) having been obtained by ultraproducts instead; the incompleteness theorems are absent, and with them arithmetic as an object of study; and category theory, which is where the repeated constructions of algebra and topology are properly stated, does not exist here at all.
