Dynamical systems

An existence theorem produces one solution at a time; a dynamical system is the whole family at once, read as a flow moving the space. The questions change accordingly – not what the solution is, but where it goes – and the two answers available without solving anything are linearisation near an equilibrium and the existence of a function that decreases along motions.

The two notes

  • Flows, equilibria and linearisation – the flow as a one-parameter group, and the theorem that a hyperbolic sink of the linearisation is an asymptotically stable equilibrium of the nonlinear system.
  • Lyapunov functions and invariant sets – what to do when the linearisation is neutral: find a function positive away from the equilibrium that does not increase, which is the energy argument with the mechanics removed.

Where it connects

The neutral case is not exotic: it is exactly what a conservative mechanical system presents, since the linearisation at a stable equilibrium has purely imaginary eigenvalues. That is why Lagrange-Dirichlet and the stability analysis of free rotation are Lyapunov arguments rather than linearisation arguments, and why mathematical physics is where this subject earns its keep.

What is missing

Almost all of it: periodic orbits and the Poincare-Bendixson theorem, which is the two-dimensional classification; stable and unstable manifolds and the Hartman-Grobman theorem; bifurcations; chaotic dynamics and symbolic coding; ergodic theory and the Birkhoff theorem, which would connect this area to probability and to statistical mechanics.
